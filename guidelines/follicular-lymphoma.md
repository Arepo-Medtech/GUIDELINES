# Follicular lymphoma (newly diagnosed and relapsed)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk stratification, first-line management (observation, local therapy, immunochemotherapy, maintenance) and relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma grades 1–3A in adults, as summarised in EHA's endorsement of the ESMO guideline. Grade 3B and transformed FL are excluded. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Scope and risk assessment

- **Grade IIIB follicular lymphoma behaves aggressively and, like transformed FL, is not covered** by FL treatment guidance. [S1]
- **Use the clinical scores (FLIPI, FLIPI 2, PRIMA-PI) for prognosis;** they are simple and dependable and can shape treatment. Biological, PET-based and MRD-based prognosis are still research tools. [S1]

## First-line treatment

- **Localised disease is the minority:** a recognised subgroup may be cured with local treatment, but most patients present with advanced FL. [S1]
- **Watchful waiting is still very appropriate for most advanced FL that is asymptomatic with low tumour burden.** [S1]
- **When treatment is needed, immunochemotherapy is the preferred approach.** No chemotherapy backbone has shown an overall survival advantage, so choose by the patient's condition and priorities and by how to avoid long-term toxicity. [S1]
- **Obinutuzumab-based chemotherapy lengthened PFS compared with rituximab-based chemotherapy,** but did not improve overall survival. [S1]
- **Rituximab maintenance for 2 years usually follows immunochemotherapy** because it prolongs PFS; weigh the extra COVID-19 infection risk. [S1]
- **Single-agent rituximab, or rituximab with lenalidomide, are options for selected patients.** [S1]

## Relapsed or refractory FL

- **At relapse, decide by** how many and which treatments came before, response duration (progression within 24 months, POD24), tumour burden and fitness. [S1]
- **Relapse options include immunochemotherapy and, in selected settings, high-dose chemotherapy with autologous transplant.** [S1]
- **Rituximab-lenalidomide has become one of the most attractive relapse combinations;** PI3K inhibitors also show substantial activity in relapsed or refractory FL. [S1]
- **Consider clinical trials** of new small molecules, T-cell-engaging antibodies and cellular therapies for selected patients. [S1]

## Long-term outlook

- **Survival has improved and many patients live long,** so treatment choice should respect patient priorities and prevent early and late harm, including second cancers and loss of organ function. [S1]
