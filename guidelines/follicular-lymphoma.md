# Follicular lymphoma (newly diagnosed and relapsed)

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk stratification, first-line management (observation, local therapy, immunochemotherapy, maintenance) and relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma grades 1–3A in adults, as summarised in EHA's endorsement of the ESMO guideline. Grade 3B and transformed FL are excluded. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Scope and risk assessment

- **Grade IIIB follicular lymphoma behaves aggressively and, like transformed FL, is not covered** by FL treatment guidance. [S1]
- **Use the clinical scores (FLIPI, FLIPI 2, PRIMA-PI) for prognosis;** they are simple and dependable and can shape treatment. Biological, PET-based and MRD-based prognosis are still research tools. [S1]

## First-line treatment

- **Localised disease is the minority:** a recognised subgroup may be cured with local treatment, but most patients present with advanced FL. [S1]
- **Watchful waiting is still very appropriate for most advanced FL that is asymptomatic with low tumour burden.** [S1]
- **When treatment is needed, immunochemotherapy is the preferred approach.** No chemotherapy backbone has shown an overall survival advantage, so choose by the patient's condition and priorities and by how to avoid long-term toxicity. [S1]
- **Obinutuzumab-based chemotherapy lengthened PFS compared with rituximab-based chemotherapy,** but did not improve overall survival. [S1]
- **Rituximab maintenance for 2 years usually follows immunochemotherapy** because it prolongs PFS; weigh the extra COVID-19 infection risk. [S1]
- **Single-agent rituximab, or rituximab with lenalidomide, are options for selected patients.** [S1]
- **Bendamustine-rituximab (BR), every 28 days for 6 cycles (S2):** rituximab 375 mg/m2 on day 1 and bendamustine 90 mg/m2 on days 1 and 2. ⚠️ In Australia bendamustine is a PBS authority item for previously untreated indolent NHL (and MCL) but, although TGA-registered, is not PBS-listed for relapsed or refractory NHL. [S2]
- **Bendamustine-obinutuzumab, every 28 days for 6 cycles (S2):** obinutuzumab 1,000 mg on days 1, 8 and 15 of cycle 1 and on day 1 of cycles 2 to 6, with bendamustine 90 mg/m2 on days 1 and 2. It is used for previously untreated FL and for rituximab-refractory FL, and both drugs are PBS authority items. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Infection prophylaxis with obinutuzumab-bendamustine (S2):** give PJP and varicella zoster prophylaxis during induction and for 6-12 months after it, and again for 6-12 months after obinutuzumab maintenance ends; consider G-CSF for prolonged neutropenia; use irradiated blood products. Screen for hepatitis B (HBsAg, anti-HBc, anti-HBs) before any anti-CD20 therapy. [S2]
- **Rituximab maintenance schedule (S2):** 375 mg/m2 every 2 months for up to 2 years (12 doses) after response to first-line induction for stage III/IV FL; every 3 months for up to 2 years (8 doses) after response to relapse induction. Rituximab is on the PBS general schedule. [S2]
- **Maintenance evidence (S2):** in PRIMA (1,217 untreated patients), 2 years of 8-weekly rituximab raised 36-month PFS to 74.9% from 57.6% without an overall survival difference, at the cost of more grade 3 or 4 events (24% v 17%) and more grade 2-4 infections (39% v 24%). In relapsed FL (EORTC 20981), 3-monthly maintenance also improved overall survival at 3 years (85% v 77%). [S2]

## Relapsed or refractory FL

- **At relapse, decide by** how many and which treatments came before, response duration (progression within 24 months, POD24), tumour burden and fitness. [S1]
- **Relapse options include immunochemotherapy and, in selected settings, high-dose chemotherapy with autologous transplant.** [S1]
- **Rituximab-lenalidomide has become one of the most attractive relapse combinations;** PI3K inhibitors also show substantial activity in relapsed or refractory FL. [S1]
- **Consider clinical trials** of new small molecules, T-cell-engaging antibodies and cellular therapies for selected patients. [S1]
- **Rituximab-refractory FL (the GADOLIN definition used for obinutuzumab-bendamustine, S2):** no response to, or progression during, any rituximab-containing regimen, or disease progressing less than 6 months after the final rituximab dose, whether in induction or maintenance. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Tafasitamab-lenalidomide-rituximab for relapsed or refractory FL (S2), 28-day cycles for 12 cycles:** lenalidomide 20 mg daily on days 1 to 21; tafasitamab 12 mg/kg on days 1, 8, 15 and 22 in cycles 1 to 3, then days 1 and 15 in cycles 4 to 12; rituximab 375 mg/m2 weekly in cycle 1, then day 1 in cycles 2 to 5. Tafasitamab and lenalidomide are PBS authority items and lenalidomide needs pregnancy-prevention programme registration. [S2]
- **Evidence for tafasitamab (inMIND, S2):** in 548 patients with at least one prior line, adding tafasitamab to lenalidomide-rituximab lengthened median PFS to 22.4 from 13.9 months (HR 0.43) and raised overall response to 84% from 72%, with similar serious adverse event rates (36% v 32%); overall survival data are immature. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Lenalidomide-based regimens need thromboprophylaxis and PJP cover (S2):** assess VTE risk before and during treatment; give low-dose aspirin without risk factors, or enoxaparin 40 mg daily with risk factors (dose-reduced in kidney impairment). PJP prophylaxis, for example trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole 160/800 mg one tablet twice daily on two days a week or once daily three days a week. [S2]

## Long-term outlook

- **Survival has improved and many patients live long,** so treatment choice should respect patient priorities and prevent early and late harm, including second cancers and loss of organ function. [S1]
