# Polycystic ovary syndrome

## Recognise

- **PCOS is the most common endocrine disorder of reproductive-age women (prevalence 10% to 13%)** and affects health from adolescence to after menopause. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Diagnose with two of three features:** clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovaries on ultrasound, for which anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) may now substitute. Other causes must be excluded. These are the 2018 criteria, derived from the 2003 Rotterdam consensus. [S1]
- **If irregular cycles and hyperandrogenism are both present, the diagnosis is made without ultrasound or AMH.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **In adolescents, require both hyperandrogenism and ovulatory dysfunction;** do not use ultrasound or AMH, which lack specificity at this age. [S1]
- **Insulin resistance is central to PCOS, but do not measure it clinically:** the tests in routine use are inaccurate. [S1]

## Assess long-term risk

- **After diagnosis, cover reproductive, metabolic, cardiovascular, dermatological, sleep and psychological features,** within a lifelong health plan that includes healthy lifestyle, avoiding excess weight gain and optimising fertility and preconception risk. [S1]
- **PCOS raises the rates of diabetes, cardiovascular disease, sleep disorders and other metabolic risk factors.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat PCOS as high risk in pregnancy:** identify and monitor these women. Recognise a raised premenopausal risk of endometrial cancer, although the absolute risk is still low. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Screen every woman with PCOS for depression and anxiety,** which are significantly more common, and assess and treat as needed. Stay alert to eating disorders and effects on body image and quality of life. [S1]

## Lifestyle and care

- **Support a healthy lifestyle throughout life,** aiming at overall health and preventing weight gain, with weight management if needed. No particular diet or exercise program is better than another in PCOS. [S1]
- **Avoid weight stigma:** ask the woman's permission before weighing her, and explain the health risks linked to weight. [S1]
- **Many women are dissatisfied with their diagnosis and care;** use shared decision making, good-quality education resources and integrated models of care. [S1]

## Treat cycles and hyperandrogenism

- ⚠️ **The combined oral contraceptive pill is first-line drug treatment for irregular cycles and hyperandrogenism.** No single product is preferred; favour lower-dose ethinyl estradiol and better-tolerated preparations. [S1]
- **Metformin is mainly for metabolic features** and works better than inositol, which adds little. It is not routinely advised in pregnancy. [S1]
- ⚠️ **For unwanted hair, mechanical laser works in some groups of women. Anti-androgens have only a limited role,** when other treatments have failed or cannot be used. [S1]
- **Weight-loss drugs and bariatric or metabolic surgery can be considered** as in general-population guidelines, weighing benefit against side effects. [S1]

## Infertility (summary)

- **Letrozole is the preferred first-line drug for anovulatory infertility,** alongside clomiphene combined with metformin. Gonadotrophins or ovarian surgery are mainly second line; IVF, possibly with in vitro maturation, is third line once ovulation induction has failed. Prefer single embryo transfer. [S1]
