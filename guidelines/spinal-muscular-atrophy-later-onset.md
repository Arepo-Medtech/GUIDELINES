# Spinal muscular atrophy — later-onset disease, treatment choice and switching

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** choosing, starting, changing, adding or stopping SMN-enhancing treatment (nusinersen, risdiplam, onasemnogene abeparvovec) in 5q SMA, with the emphasis on people who are not newly diagnosed, adolescents and adults: the later-onset (type IIIb/IIIc) population. Newborn screening, diagnosis and treatment initiation in screen-positive infants: see `spinal-muscular-atrophy`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 28 claims; **38 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 3 doses.** **S1** Cure SMA expert panel (Schroth M (published online 8 October 2024 (February 2025 issue); international): 22 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored · **S2** Australian Prescriber (new drug comment (published 7 July 2022; AU): 6 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ International guideline (Cure SMA expert panel, 2024). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **Written for North America and Western Europe.** The panel was 17 neurologists and a critical-care physician from the US and Europe, working by modified Delphi. In Australia only nusinersen and risdiplam are PBS-listed for type IIIb/IIIc SMA, and the PBS criteria for starting and continuing them take precedence over the switching and add-on advice here.

> 🇦🇺 **S2 is an Australian Prescriber new drug comment on risdiplam (2022),** not a guideline. It adds the dose, pharmacology, reproductive safety and a critical reading of the trial in older patients.

## Principles

- ⚠️ **Suspected SMA is now treated as an emergency:** confirm the genetic diagnosis, including the SMN2 copy number, and start treatment quickly. [S1]
- **Classifying SMA by type no longer predicts prognosis or outcome well** in the treatment era. [S1]
- **Earlier treatment gives better results, so treat as soon as is reasonably possible after diagnosis.** No current drug cures SMA. [S1]
- **Every start, change, addition or stop should weigh two things:** what the patient and family want, and the drug's safety and side effects. (89% panel agreement) [S1]

## The treatments

- **Children under 2 have up to 3 SMN-enhancing options; older people have 2.** Onasemnogene abeparvovec is a single intravenous gene-transfer dose for infants and toddlers. [S1]
- **Nusinersen** (antisense oligonucleotide) is given intrathecally: 4 loading doses across 2 months, then a maintenance dose every 4 months. [S1]
- **Risdiplam** (oral SMN2 splicing modifier) is a liquid taken once daily, by mouth or through a feeding tube. [S1]
- **Risdiplam is dosed by age and weight,** once a day as an oral solution given after a feed, never mixed into milk or formula. In the trial in older patients, those weighing 20 kg or more took 5 mg daily. [S2]
- **Risdiplam pharmacology:** it reaches the brain, has a half-life of roughly 50 hours, needs no dose change with CYP3A inhibitors such as itraconazole, and is unstudied in kidney impairment or severe liver impairment. [S2]
- **Risdiplam is simpler to give than nusinersen,** which needs a lumbar puncture for each dose. [S2]

## Deciding for people who are not newly diagnosed

- **Current clinical status drives the decision** once someone is past the initial diagnosis: comorbidities, complex spinal anatomy, and any decline in function on treatment. (100% agreement) [S1]
- **Complex spinal anatomy** can make intrathecal dosing risky or intolerable, which may favour a drug given by another route from the outset or a later switch away from intrathecal treatment. [S1]
- **Pre-existing liver disease or raised transaminases** narrows the choice, because gene therapy can worsen liver disease. [S1]
- **Loss of function after years of stability** (for example, no longer walking independently) may mean the drug slowed but did not halt progression. Changing or adding a drug can be discussed, but there are no published data to guide it. [S1]
- **Reproductive plans affect the choice** because data on these drugs in that setting are insufficient. [S1]

## Adolescents and adults

- **Factors that matter most for adolescents and adults:** tolerability, quality of life, benefit against burden, side effects, loss of function, male and female reproductive concerns, pregnancy, progression despite treatment, and treatment fatigue. [S1]
- **Adult comorbidities weigh heavily:** complex spinal anatomy, kidney disease and plans for a family. [S1]
- **Set adult goals differently from those for young children:** slower progression, keeping the motor function needed for daily living, and independence. The effect can take 12 months or more to show, and age matters less in the decision. [S1]
- **Gains in older people can be small but meaningful**, such as still driving a powered wheelchair or lifting a cup; stabilisation and better quality of life also count. Responses have been positive at every age and severity. [S1]

## Risdiplam in older patients: the Australian view

- **The benefit in older patients was small.** In SUNFISH (median age 9 years), the Motor Function Measure (0 to 96) rose by 1.36 points on risdiplam and fell by 0.19 on placebo; the youngest did best, and the 18 to 25 year group did not improve. [S2]
- **Its clinical meaning is uncertain:** a 1.55-point gap on a 96-point scale is hard to interpret, and clinicians rated 48% of the risdiplam group as improved but also 40% of the placebo group. Because younger patients respond better, starting early may give the best chance of a meaningful gain. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Reproductive safety:** risdiplam harmed the embryo and fetus in animal studies and may lower male fertility. Side effects more common than placebo were mouth ulcers, rash, diarrhoea, fever, pneumonia and urinary tract infection; whether the retinal toxicity seen in animals occurs with long-term use is not yet known. [S2]

## Changing the treatment plan

- **Give a treatment 6 to 12 months before judging it, unless the reason to change is urgent.** (89% agreement) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Urgent reasons to change sooner** (100% agreement): significant side effects or intolerance, intolerance of the route of administration, significant disease progression, or loss of motor milestones in infants and young children. [S1]
- **Other reasons to review within 6 months:** intolerance of intrathecal injections or of oral-drug gastrointestinal effects, abnormal monitoring bloods, further loss of function in an older person within 2–3 months of starting, pregnancy, or persistent symptoms with no apparent response. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Add-on (combination) treatment is unproven.** It covers any SMN-enhancing drug after gene therapy, or nusinersen with risdiplam at once; its benefit, safety and timing are unknown, and many funders will not pay for it. [S1]
- **Beware funding rules that demand ongoing absolute improvement:** they may ignore the natural history of SMA, and motor scales may miss slowed progression. [S1]

## Care model

- **Care coordination and multidisciplinary care are essential** to delivering these drugs well, with respiratory, musculoskeletal and nutrition support alongside them. [S1]
