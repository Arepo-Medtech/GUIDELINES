## ⚠️ First aid: pressure immobilisation, and when NOT to remove it

- **Pressure immobilisation is important first aid treatment for snakebite** and is usually applied before
  hospitalisation. **Ensure it is not compromising distal circulation** [M1].
- > ⚠️ **Pressure immobilisation should only be removed when antivenom is available, when an IV line is
  > in place, and blood test results are available.**
  > ⚠️ **In systemic envenoming, keep pressure immobilisation in place until antivenom treatment has
  > started** [M1].

---

## Before starting treatment

- **If possible, determine the type of snake, and seek advice from a Poisons Information Centre,
  phone 13 11 26** [M1].
- **Assess the degree of envenoming.** ⚠️ **Not all confirmed snakebites will result in systemic
  envenoming, and risk varies with the species** [M1].
- ⚠️ **Non-specific symptoms — nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, headache, abdominal pain — may indicate
  systemic envenoming** [M1]. **They are not reassurance.**

### ⚠️ Who is at increased risk of a serious outcome [M1]

**Pre-existing renal, hepatic, cardiac or respiratory impairment** · **elderly people** · **those taking
anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs**.

⚠️ **Children are also at increased risk of severe envenoming, because of smaller body mass and the
likelihood of physical activity immediately after a bite.**

---

## When to start treatment

**If systemic envenoming is present, as indicated clinically or by laboratory results** [M1]:

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Defibrination coagulopathy** | **INR above 2, low fibrinogen, elevated fibrin degradation products** — with or without clinical bleeding |
| **Anticoagulant coagulopathy** | **INR above 2, normal fibrinogen and fibrin degradation products** — with or without clinical bleeding |
| **Flaccid paralysis** | ⚠️ **including ptosis alone** |
| **Myolysis** | Major rise in creatine kinase, myoglobinuria, muscle pain |
| **Significant renal impairment** | |
| **Collapse or seizures** | |

> ⚠️ **If unsure, seek advice from a Poisons Information Centre, phone 13 11 26 — early administration of
> antivenom is important, for example within 2 hours of the bite** [M1].

**The rationale for drug use is prevention of serious complications of snakebite** [M1].

### Additional therapies in eTG

- Unidentified snake envenoming (adult and child): tiger snake antivenom 1 vial plus brown snake antivenom 1 vial, diluted 1:10 (1:5 if fluid-overload risk) in sodium chloride 0.9% or Hartmann's, IV over 15 minutes; single monovalent vial or 1 vial polyvalent in defined scenarios (taipan, death adder, mulga, significant doubt). [T1]
- No premedication before antivenom; give in resuscitation area with adrenaline available; for hypersensitivity stop infusion and give IM adrenaline if severe. [T1]
- Serum sickness (4-14 days post antivenom), moderate-severe: prednisolone 25-50 mg (child 0.5 mg/kg up to 25 mg) orally daily for up to 7 days. [T1]
- Pressure bandage with immobilisation for all suspected bites within 4 h, kept on until antivenom started or envenoming excluded; observe at least 12 h with serial bloods (at 1 h after bandage removal, 6 h and 12 h); snake venom detection kit no longer recommended; one vial usually sufficient and persistent coagulopathy is not an indication for more antivenom; FFP for active major bleeding after antivenom. [T1]
