# Psoriatic arthritis — ongoing DMARD management

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with established psoriatic arthritis on DMARDs, in specialist-shared care: tapering conventional and biologic/targeted DMARDs, laboratory safety monitoring, perioperative DMARDs and glucocorticoids, and opioids. First-line and switch choice of b/tsDMARD is not covered by S1 and comes from S2 (EULAR 2023). Perioperative detail is in `perioperative-dmard-management`; skin psoriasis is in `psoriasis`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Principles

- **Treat to target, and make every DMARD decision jointly with the patient** after discussing benefits and harms for their situation, including when reducing doses. [S1]
- **Rheumatologists should lead care of the joints and entheses;** where the skin disease matters clinically, a rheumatologist and a dermatologist should diagnose and manage together. Choose drugs on efficacy, safety, the patient's preferences and cost. [S2]

## Choosing treatment (S2, EULAR 2023)

- **NSAIDs can ease joint symptoms and local steroid injections can be added,** but most patients should not stay on NSAIDs alone. Only very mild peripheral disease, or disease that is mainly entheseal or axial, may do well on NSAIDs by themselves, and then usually briefly (about 4 weeks). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Start a conventional DMARD promptly in polyarthritis, or in oligoarthritis (up to four swollen joints) or monoarthritis with poor prognostic factors** such as structural damage, raised acute-phase reactants, dactylitis or nail disease. Methotrexate is preferred when the skin is clinically involved; leflunomide and sulfasalazine are the other first options. [S2]
- **Methotrexate dose: usually 20–25 mg a week (about 0.3 mg/kg) with folate.** Judge the response at 12 weeks: the aim is at least a 50% fall in activity by 3 months and the target by 6 months, otherwise change treatment. Do not combine methotrexate with leflunomide. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Peripheral arthritis not controlled by at least one conventional DMARD: start a biologic.** No biologic is preferred for the joints, but abatacept (CTLA4 inhibition) is a weak option. With limited disease and no poor prognostic factors, a second conventional DMARD first is reasonable if agreed. [S2]
- **Keep a tolerated conventional DMARD going with the first biologic,** especially a TNF inhibitor; for other classes there are no data either way, though continuing is usual practice. [S2]
- ⚠️ **JAK inhibitors come after at least one biologic has failed, or when a biologic is unsuitable,** with safety weighed: the regulators' warning covers age, smoking and cardiovascular, venous clot and cancer risk, and shingles is more frequent than with biologics. [S2]
- **Apremilast (a PDE4 inhibitor) is for mild disease only** (oligoarticular or entheseal, no poor prognostic factors, limited skin) after a conventional DMARD, when neither a biologic nor a JAK inhibitor suits. It has no data on preventing joint damage, and should not be combined with a TNF inhibitor. [S2]
- **Enthesitis or axial disease not settled by NSAIDs (and, for enthesitis, local injections): use a biologic.** Any class works for definite enthesitis. For axial disease use an IL-17A, TNF, IL-17A/F or JAK inhibitor; conventional DMARDs and IL-23 blockers are not recommended for the spine. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Let the other organs guide the class:** significant psoriasis → an IL-17A, IL-17A/F, IL-23 or IL-12/23 inhibitor; uveitis → an anti-TNF monoclonal antibody (adalimumab, infliximab); IBD → an anti-TNF antibody, IL-23 or IL-12/23 inhibitor, or JAK inhibitor. Avoid IL-17 inhibitors in active IBD. [S2]
- **If a biologic or JAK inhibitor fails or is not tolerated, switch to another,** within the same class once if wanted; there is no cap on the number of switches. [S2]

## Reducing conventional DMARDs

- **Consider a trial of a lower csDMARD dose** once low disease activity or remission has lasted at least 6 months, with the skin also well controlled. [S1]
- **Six months is the minimum;** a deep remission, or holding the target for at least 12 months, is the better starting point. Weigh the effect on psoriasis before reducing. [S1]
- **After reducing, review at least every 3 months with a validated composite measure (e.g. PASDAS)**, watch for new skin or nail disease, and confirm the target is held before any further change. [S1]
- **Give a flare plan** with a way to contact the prescriber between visits; if control is lost, go back to the previous effective csDMARD dose. [S1]
- **Evidence:** reducing methotrexate (alone or in sequence with other DMARDs) after 6 months of remission or low activity may increase flares and slightly raise disease activity. [S1]

## Biologic and targeted synthetic DMARDs

- ⚠️ **Do not routinely lower b/tsDMARD doses** in PsA that is in low activity or remission, and do not stop them abruptly. [S1]
- **Some patients may still prefer a lower dose.** Discuss the flare risk, the chance that remission is not regained on returning to full dose, and the lack of evidence; review 3 monthly and restore the full dose if the target slips. [S1]
- **Evidence:** there are no dose-reduction data. Stopping b/tsDMARDs after sustained minimal activity may mean fewer patients stay in remission and more flare; data cover only TNF inhibitors and ixekizumab. [S1]
- **S2 (EULAR) is more permissive:** in sustained remission, tapering of DMARDs (lowering the dose, not stopping, which usually causes flares) may be considered, by shared decision; tapering is off-label and long-term data are missing. [S2]

## Safety monitoring on csDMARDs

- **Check bloods within a month** of starting or increasing a csDMARD, during acute illness, or when a relevant new drug is added. [S1]
- **Monitor every 1-3 months if higher risk** (kidney or liver disease, diabetes, metabolic syndrome, older age, polypharmacy, toxicity signs); on a stable dose without risk factors, 6-monthly or less often by shared decision. [S1]
- **Prescribe folic acid with methotrexate,** and tell patients to withhold methotrexate during acute illness unless told otherwise. Agree monitoring and result handling between GP and rheumatologist. [S1]

## Surgery

- **Around elective surgery:** keep csDMARDs going; keep bDMARDs going too unless infection risk or its consequences are high; consider pausing tsDMARDs. [S1]
- **If withholding:** most bDMARDs miss one dosing cycle (operate around when the next dose is due); JAK inhibitors and leflunomide stop about 7 days before; methotrexate misses one cycle (surgery 1-2 weeks after the last dose). Restart once sutures are out, the wound has healed and there is no infection. [S1]
- **Do not give routine stress-dose steroids** to patients on regular glucocorticoids; continue the usual oral dose, including on the day of surgery. [S1]

## Pain

- ⚠️ **Opioids are not routinely recommended for PsA pain;** no studies address their safety or benefit in PsA. [S1]
- **Before any opioid, treat the inflammation properly** and look for fibromyalgia or nociplastic pain (where opioids are generally not advised) and missed pathology such as insufficiency fracture. [S1]
