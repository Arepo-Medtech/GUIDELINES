# Heart transplant immunosuppression and rejection

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adult heart transplant recipients: induction, maintenance with a calcineurin inhibitor plus mycophenolate or a proliferation signal inhibitor (everolimus, sirolimus), renal-sparing conversion, steroid weaning, infection prophylaxis, rejection surveillance, and treatment of rejection. Specialist transplant-unit care; primary care supports monitoring and adherence. Desensitisation protocols are summarised only in Unresolved. Kidney transplant: `kidney-transplant-immunosuppression-and-rejection`.

## Principles

- **Rejection risk is greatest soon after implantation and falls over time,** so immunosuppression is most intense straight after surgery and is stepped down over the first year to the lowest maintenance level that still prevents rejection and limits toxicity. [S1]
- **Prefer low doses of several drugs with non-overlapping toxicities** to high doses of fewer; too much immunosuppression brings infection and cancer. [S1]

## Induction

- **About 50% of heart transplant recipients get induction.** The main reason is antibody sensitisation (risk of hyperacute rejection); others are multi-organ transplant and renal sparing, to delay the calcineurin inhibitor (CNI). [S1]
- **This centre prefers rabbit anti-thymocyte globulin (rATG) over basiliximab** for efficacy, and considers it when panel reactive antibody exceeds 10%: 1.5 mg/kg daily for 5 days from the day of surgery. [S1]

## Maintenance

- **Maintenance usually pairs a CNI (tacrolimus or ciclosporin) with either an antimetabolite (mycophenolate mofetil, enteric-coated mycophenolate sodium or azathioprine) or a proliferation signal inhibitor (sirolimus or everolimus).** [S1]
- ⚠️ **CNIs remain the backbone.** The centre starts triple therapy: tacrolimus, mycophenolate mofetil and a corticosteroid (IV methylprednisolone, then oral prednisone). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tacrolimus trough targets (this centre):** first month 10–15 ng/mL; second and third months 8–12 ng/mL; after the third month 5–10 ng/mL. [S1]
- **Mycophenolate mofetil is dosed to tolerance (up to 1,500 mg twice daily) without routine levels;** gastrointestinal upset and cytopenias usually limit the dose. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid everolimus or sirolimus in the first 6 months** (wound dehiscence, poor healing, added CNI nephrotoxicity). Active fungal infection or past DVT/PE usually rules them out unless long-term anticoagulant or antifungal cover is sorted. [S1]
- **Best candidates to switch mycophenolate to a PSI:** treated rejection, new donor-specific antibodies, CMV mismatch or infection, malignancy, or cardiac allograft vasculopathy. Check proteinuria, lipids and glucose before starting and during treatment. [S1]
- **Steroid weaning in lower-risk recipients:** prednisone 10 mg daily by 3 months and 5 mg by 6 months, then down by 1 mg a month to stop by twelve months if graft function is normal, there has been no treated rejection or DSA, and adherence is good. [S1]
- **Keep prednisone 5 mg daily long term** after multi-organ transplant, and when sarcoidosis or giant cell myocarditis caused the cardiomyopathy. [S1]

## Renal sparing

- **Kidney impairment:** minimise the CNI when it is mild (creatinine 1.8–2.2 mg/dL); withdraw it when moderate (creatinine >2.0–2.5 mg/dL), but only after 6 months and not during steroid weaning, or with past PSI intolerance, LV dysfunction, humoral rejection or cellular rejection above 2R. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Switching to a CNI-free regimen:** continue mycophenolate, halve the CNI and start the PSI, keeping low-dose CNI until PSI troughs are at target: sirolimus 4–8 ng/mL, everolimus 3–8 ng/mL. [S1]
- **About 20% cannot tolerate a PSI.** Fatigue and fluid retention are commonest; others are fungal infection, pneumonitis, venous thromboembolism, high triglycerides, mouth ulcers, cytopenias and acute kidney injury. Review within 4 weeks of switching. [S1]

## Prophylaxis and monitoring

- **First-year prophylaxis (this centre):** clotrimazole troches for 3 months against thrush; valganciclovir (renally dosed) for 1 year if CMV donor-positive/recipient-negative, otherwise 6 months; co-trimoxazole for 1 year against Pneumocystis, toxoplasma and Nocardia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Keeping CNI levels on target is critical.** Recheck bloods a week after any CNI dose change; check CNI troughs twice weekly in the first month and weekly in the second. [S1]
- **Low troughs have many causes:** missed doses, side effects or cost, malabsorption, altered cytochrome P450 metabolism, drug or food interactions, infection, cytopenias, or dose cuts for intolerance. [S1]
- **Surveillance biopsy in year one:** weekly in month 1, fortnightly in month 2, monthly for months 3 to 6, then every other month to 12 months. Test for donor-specific antibodies at months 1, 3 and 6, then 6-monthly. Low-risk patients may use blood gene-expression profiling instead of some biopsies. [S1]

## Rejection

- ⚠️ **Rejection can be silent.** Clues: new LV hypertrophy (oedema), new systolic dysfunction early after transplant, low-voltage ECG, raised BNP or troponin. Symptoms: breathlessness, orthopnoea, PND, a return of pre-transplant symptoms, or atrial fibrillation or flutter. [S1]
- ⚠️ **The transplanted heart is very sensitive to adenosine:** halve the dose to 3 mg, with continuous ECG monitoring, to avoid prolonged heart block. Use IV beta-blockers cautiously early on; amiodarone is acceptable with care. [S1]
- **Biopsy when rejection is suspected.** If the biopsy is normal but there is graft dysfunction with heart failure, consider biopsy-negative rejection and cardiac MRI or molecular testing. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Symptomatic rejection (this centre):** IV methylprednisolone 500 mg daily for 3 days, then rATG for 3 to 7 days (usually 5). If donor-specific antibodies are present, add IVIG 1 g/kg daily for 2 days (maximum total 140 g). Repeat HLA antibody testing at the time of rejection. [S1]
- **Cardiogenic shock from rejection** may need pulmonary artery catheter guidance, inotropes, an intra-aortic balloon pump, and early VA ECMO; ECMO started after cardiac arrest does poorly. [S1]
- **Recurrent rejection:** photopheresis may be considered. Re-transplant is generally contraindicated within 6 months of treated rejection (hyperacute rejection risk). [S1]
