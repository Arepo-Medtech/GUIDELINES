# Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia — frontline treatment in adolescents and young adults

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** frontline treatment of B-ALL, T-ALL and T-lymphoblastic lymphoma in adolescents and young adults (15–39 years): regimen backbone, asparaginase management, transplant in first remission, rituximab, blinatumomab, Ph-positive induction, CNS prophylaxis, MRD-directed change of therapy and AYA supportive care. Relapsed/refractory disease and adults over 39 are outside the source's scope (see Unresolved). PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Frontline approach

- **The source covers adolescents and young adults (AYAs), aged 15 to 39 at diagnosis.** The panel notes that data for the whole band were limited, particularly for the youngest patients. [S1]
- **Treat AYAs with B-ALL, T-ALL or T-lymphoblastic lymphoma using a paediatric-style regimen built on asparaginase.** (Strong; moderate certainty) [S1]
- **Allogeneic transplant is not suggested as routine consolidation in first remission** (conditional). It may still benefit high-risk groups, such as those with persistent MRD, induction failure or adverse biology. [S1]

## Asparaginase

- **Pegaspargase can be capped or reduced to limit toxicity** without apparent harm to disease control (conditional). A cap means no more than 3750 IU per dose. Reduction means under 2500 IU/m2 before age 22, or under 2000 IU/m2 from age 22. [S1]
- **Premedicate before asparaginase**, with at least an IV corticosteroid, optionally adding an antihistamine or paracetamol (strong). When doses are capped or reduced, or premedication is used, check asparaginase activity. Aim for ≥0.1 IU/mL maintained for 14 days or longer. [S1]
- **Do not give cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate routinely unless there is bleeding, and do not use unfractionated heparin as routine thromboprophylaxis** (both strong). The evidence cannot settle routine LMWH or DOAC prophylaxis either way. [S1]
- **After an allergic reaction to pegaspargase, change to an Erwinia-derived asparaginase** rather than dropping asparaginase altogether. (Strong) [S1]

## B-ALL add-on therapy

- **For CD20-positive B-ALL, add rituximab to chemotherapy** (conditional). Its dose, timing, schedule and the CD20 threshold remain uncertain. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Once B-ALL is in morphological remission, add blinatumomab, whatever the MRD result.** (Conditional; very low certainty) [S1]
- **Blinatumomab carries real toxicity and burden.** In the trial, grade ≥3 neurological events were the only significant excess harm. Cytokine release syndrome, infusion reactions and hypogammaglobulinaemia also occur. It is started in hospital and given as 28-day continuous infusions. [S1]
- **Inotuzumab has no frontline recommendation** because the evidence was insufficient. [S1]
- **T-cell disease:** nelarabine is not suggested for T-lymphoblastic lymphoma, and bortezomib is not suggested for T-ALL. (Both conditional) [S1]

## Philadelphia-positive ALL

- ⚠️ **Ph-positive ALL: induce with reduced-intensity therapy plus a TKI** in preference to intensive chemotherapy plus a TKI (conditional). Post-remission treatment follows, with intensive chemotherapy and/or immunotherapy, each with a TKI. Transplant in first remission can consolidate either route. [S1]
- **The reason is fewer early deaths:** death within 60 days was lower with reduced-intensity induction plus a TKI (RR 0.25; 95% CI 0.07-0.85). [S1]
- **The guideline does not choose a TKI.** Most comparative data came from imatinib. Only one retrospective study included some dasatinib or nilotinib use, and ponatinib-based results were not yet available. [S1]

## CNS prophylaxis

- **Give CNS prophylaxis as intrathecal methotrexate or triple intrathecal therapy, either being acceptable** (conditional). Every Ph-positive patient must receive concurrent intrathecal prophylaxis. Cranial irradiation is not suggested routinely on a paediatric-style backbone (conditional). [S1]

## MRD and follow-through

- **MRD measurement is standard care.** It carries prognostic weight and sometimes sets treatment intensity. Most studies call MRD positive at ≥0.01% by quantitative flow or clone-specific methods. [S1]
- ⚠️ **If MRD persists after 3 months or more of frontline treatment** (induction and at least 1 post-remission block), **change the approach** (strong). Immunotherapy or allogeneic transplant are options; their best timing, selection and order are undefined. [S1]
- **AYA-specific care (good practice):** arrange fertility consultation before treatment starts where possible. Screen routinely for depression and anxiety, and provide multidisciplinary psychosocial support. [S1]
