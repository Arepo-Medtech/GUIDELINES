# Gyrate atrophy of the choroid and retina (ornithine aminotransferase deficiency)

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, diagnosis, metabolic treatment (arginine-restricted diet with low-protein formula, pyridoxine, creatine, lysine) and ophthalmic management of cystoid macular oedema in gyrate atrophy of the choroid and retina (GACR). Other inherited retinal dystrophies are out of scope. Urea cycle and hyperammonaemia emergencies: see `metabolic-disorders-children`. PBS access (essential amino acid formulas) is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 26 claims; **54 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 2 doses.** **S1** Aktaşoğlu EÖ (published 16 February 2026; international): 14 claims, quoted · **S2** Balfoort BM (published 6 January 2025; international): 12 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ International sources: S1 is a single-centre case series of 7 patients (Turkey, 2026) and S2 a registry cohort of 27 patients (Netherlands, 2025). **Neither is a guideline:** no consensus on treatment exists anywhere. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **What the PBS funds matches the dietary core of treatment.** The essential amino acid formulas on the PBS supply protein for the arginine-restricted diet, which the source calls the most commonly used treatment; evidence that it slows retinal degeneration is limited.

## Recognise

- **GACR is a rare autosomal recessive disorder of amino acid metabolism** caused by OAT gene variants; incidence is about 1:1,500,000. [S1]
- **OAT deficiency causes slowly progressive chorioretinal degeneration and blindness.** Myopia and night blindness are the usual presenting symptoms; newborns may instead present with hyperammonaemia. [S1]
- **Cognition may also be affected,** through secondary creatine deficiency caused by high ornithine; cataracts and vision loss also occur. [S1]
- **The fundus shows sharply demarcated mid-peripheral chorioretinal atrophy spreading toward the posterior pole;** OCT often shows outer retinal thinning and cystoid macular oedema. [S1]
- **In the larger cohort, eye symptoms began at a mean age of 10 years,** most often as poor vision from myopia or as night blindness. Twelve patients were found by targeted testing once a sibling had been diagnosed. [S2]

## Diagnose

- **High plasma ornithine is the biochemical hallmark:** every patient had it at diagnosis. Screen siblings; the youngest here (15 months) was found by family screening. [S1]
- **ERG is usually markedly reduced or extinguished at diagnosis;** use it as a diagnostic and baseline test, not to judge short-term treatment effect. [S1]
- **At diagnosis, S2 patients averaged a plasma ornithine of 735.7 µmol/L (range 341–1207), against a laboratory reference of 27–98.** [S2]
- ⚠️ **In the newborn period ornithine can be normal or low,** because the enzyme runs in reverse, and babies can present with hyperammonaemia; a neonatal proline/citrulline ratio above 32 followed by persistently high ornithine counts as diagnostic. [S2]
- **Look for cerebral creatine deficiency with MR spectroscopy:** it was present in fifteen of the 20 patients with data. Plasma creatine can be normal or high on supplements while brain creatine stays low. [S2]

## Metabolic treatment

- **There is no satisfactory treatment and no consensus;** an arginine-restricted diet with pyridoxine, lysine, proline or creatine may help. [S1]
- **Test pyridoxine (vitamin B6) responsiveness first,** before starting the diet, because OAT depends on pyridoxal phosphate. About 30% of patients respond in one review. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Arginine-restricted diet:** restrict natural protein and supplement with low-protein formulas. It is the most commonly used treatment, but whether protein restriction slows progression is not well studied. [S1]
- **Add creatine monohydrate for secondary creatine deficiency;** oral L-lysine lowered plasma ornithine in 5 pyridoxine-unresponsive patients. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Start the diet early:** the four patients who kept more than 10° of central visual field had all begun protein restriction before age 10, and the only patient free of atrophic lesions had started ornithine-lowering treatment in the first year of life. [S2]
- **Arginine-free essential amino acid supplements are added when natural protein intake is below dietary reference values** (the role of the PBS-listed formulas). Patients had kept to the diet for a median of 8 years, and nine reported struggling with it at some point. [S2]
- **Plasma ornithine usually stays high on treatment:** below 100 µmol/L only in the newborn period and in one patient on an extremely strict diet. After treatment began it fell in 12 of 27 patients and rose in 10 of 27. [S2]
- **Ornithine climbs with age, by about 8.1 µmol/L a year, independent of protein intake,** so in adults a rising level is not on its own proof of poor adherence, even though intake and reported adherence both affect it. [S2]
- **Pyridoxine was given at a mean of 260 mg a day (range 50–600 mg); three patients responded in vivo,** two with measured ornithine falls of 24% and 36%. Response probably depends on the OAT variants. [S2]
- **L-lysine at 2 to 15 g a day showed no clear effect on ornithine in this cohort, and gastrointestinal side effects made three patients stop;** loss of appetite from those effects risks catabolism and a rise in ornithine. [S2]

## Eye treatment

- **Cystoid macular oedema is treated with topical NSAIDs (e.g. nepafenac), often with a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor (acetazolamide, brinzolamide);** responses in GACR are inconsistent and were mostly unsatisfactory here. [S1]
- **Per-patient eye findings (S1 Table 2):** cystoid macular oedema in five of the six patients with data; on treatment it partly resolved in two (patients 1 and 4), stayed stable in two (2 and 3) and worsened in one (5). Two patients (1 and 2) had posterior subcapsular cataract. [S1]
- **Subtenon platelet-rich plasma and topical CoQ10/vitamin E TPGS are experimental** and cannot be recommended; neither gave a lasting benefit. [S1]

## Follow up

- **Diagnose early and follow jointly in metabolic and ophthalmology clinics;** patients here were reviewed every 3-6 months. Diet and medicine adherence was poor, and metabolic follow-up fell away as vision worsened. [S1]
- **Watch muscle mass:** four patients aged 28 to 57 met criteria for sarcopenia, and 9 of 12 measured had a low fat-free mass index; creatine deficiency or reduced mobility from poor vision may contribute. [S2]
- **The authors argue for newborn screening:** ornithine does not work as a newborn marker, but the citrulline/proline ratio could, and starting treatment in the first decade may pay off long term. [S2]
