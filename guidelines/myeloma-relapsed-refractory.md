# Relapsed and refractory multiple myeloma

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** when to treat at relapse, general salvage principles, comorbidity-driven choices, the PBS-funded salvage regimens and BCMA-directed therapy for adults with relapsed or refractory myeloma. First-line treatment is in `myeloma-transplant-eligible` and `myeloma-transplant-ineligible`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 36 claims; **78 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 2 doses.** **S1** Medical and Scientific Advisory Group (MSAG) to Myeloma Australia (Quach H) (2026 (evidence current to January 2026); AU): 22 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored · **S2** Pan-Pacific Multiple Myeloma Working Group (Chen W and others) (2025 (Delphi voting March-May 2025); international): 14 claims, quoted

> ⚠️ **Australian access differs from international practice.** Many regimens that are standard overseas (for example Dara-Pd, Isa-Pd, Isa-Kd, belantamab, talquetamab, venetoclax for t(11;14)) are not TGA-approved or not PBS-funded here, and some PBS restrictions still count prior lines rather than drug-class exposure.

> ⚠️ International consensus (Pan-Pacific Multiple Myeloma Working Group, 2025) for **S2**. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ. S2's panel is from mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Japan, Taiwan, Korea and Russia, and the authors say their advice reflects regional access and should be read in the local context.

## Definitions and when to treat

- **Refractory myeloma** means progression while on treatment, or within 60 days of stopping it, in a patient who had reached at least a minor response. [S1]
- **Not every relapse needs treatment at once.** Start promptly for clinical relapse, and also for a fast rise in paraprotein or serum free light chains even without new CRAB features. Slow, symptom-free biochemical relapse can be watched, but closely: registry data suggest organ damage that appears while relapse is only biochemical may not recover as well as once thought. [S1]
- **Clinical relapse (treat):** new plasmacytomas or bone lesions; existing ones growing by ≥50%; calcium ≥11.5 mg/dl (2.875 mmol/l); haemoglobin falling by ≥20 g/L or below 100 g/L; or creatinine up by >177 μmol/L, where due to myeloma. [S1]
- **Significant biochemical relapse (treat before organ damage):** paraprotein doubling on two consecutive tests under two months apart with an absolute rise of at least 5 g/L; or, on two consecutive tests, paraprotein up by ≥10 g/L, urine Bence Jones protein up by ≥0.5 g per 24 hours, or the involved free light chain up by ≥200 mg/L (abnormal ratio) or 25%, whichever is greater. [S1]

## General principles

- **There is no standard sequence.** Tailor therapy to earlier treatments and their toxicity, the depth and length of past responses, disease tempo and current fitness; use the most effective available option early rather than saving it. A clinical trial is strongly preferred when one is open. [S1]
- **Change drug class where possible;** another agent from the same class can be reasonable if paired with a different class. Re-using an earlier regimen is an option only if the patient was not refractory to it, stayed off therapy for about 12 months or more, and tolerated it; expect a shorter, shallower response (Grade C). [S1]
- **A second autologous transplant** can be considered in selected patients who reached at least a partial response lasting over 3 years (over 2 years if they had no maintenance after the first transplant) (Grade B, Level 4). [S1]
- **Match intensity to relapse tempo.** Rapid progression: a triplet pairing an IMiD and/or proteasome inhibitor with an antibody or chemotherapy, plus debulking chemotherapy or radiotherapy for bulky, proliferative disease. Slow progression: limit side effects; a doublet with dexamethasone, or watchful waiting in frail or elderly patients without organ damage. [S1]
- **When novel agents are exhausted,** older options remain: cyclophosphamide at moderate dose, melphalan at non-myeloablative dose, or low-dose steroids, and palliative care should be offered to those unfit for more treatment (Grade C). [S1]

## Comorbidities

- **Neuropathy and clots:** use bortezomib or thalidomide with caution, or avoid them, in pre-existing neuropathy. Anyone with past VTE or high VTE risk on an IMiD needs prophylactic or full anticoagulation; risk peaks with high-dose dexamethasone or anthracycline regimens. [S1]
- **Renal impairment:** proteasome inhibitors are usable, carfilzomib even on dialysis with close monitoring. Lenalidomide, cleared mainly by the kidney, needs dose reduction in moderate to severe impairment; thalidomide and pomalidomide are less affected. CD38 antibodies and BCMA T-cell engagers can be used, dialysis included, with careful monitoring. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Carfilzomib:** get blood pressure controlled (about 140/90 mmHg) before starting and keep monitoring it; be cautious with pulmonary hypertension or uncontrolled heart disease. Grade ≥3 hypertension affected 14%, and heart failure was about 6% v 2% with Vd in Endeavor. [S1]

## Choosing a salvage regimen (PBS-funded)

- **Drug-class exposure now matters more than the number of prior lines.** In LocoMMotion, triple-class-exposed patients on standard salvage had median PFS of 4.6 months and overall survival of 12.4 months. [S1]
- **PBS-funded options after first-line therapy:** Dara-Vd (second line only), Kd, KRd, KTd, PVd, Pd, and SVd or Sd. Most Australians relapsing for the first time have had lenalidomide and many are refractory to it, which worsens outcomes with current salvage options and must shape the choice. [S1]
- **Dara-Vd** is PBS-funded only after 1 prior line. In CASTOR it beat Vd on PFS (16.7 v 7.1 months). Bortezomib-refractory patients were excluded and it is not the choice for them; give bortezomib weekly and subcutaneously to limit neuropathy. [S1]
- **Kd** is funded after at least one prior line; Endeavor showed better PFS and survival than Vd. Endeavor gave carfilzomib twice weekly at 20/56 mg/m²; the once-weekly ARROW schedule (20/70 mg/m²) matched the twice-weekly regimen for response and PFS and was safer. [S1]
- **KRd** is funded from second line, but ASPIRE excluded lenalidomide-refractory patients, who make up most Australians with relapsed disease. **KTd** suits patients in whom renal impairment limits lenalidomide (ALLG MM018: median PFS 23 months). [S1]
- **PVd** is funded after at least 1 lenalidomide-containing line. In OPTIMISMM (all lenalidomide-exposed, 70% refractory, like Australian first relapses) it beat Vd on PFS (12.2 v 7.1 months); bortezomib-refractory patients were excluded. About 25% stopped for side effects, mostly neuropathy. PCd (median PFS 13 months) is an option when neuropathy rules out bortezomib. [S1]
- **Pd alone gives only modest control** (median PFS about 4 months). **SVd** is PBS-funded: in BOSTON it lengthened median PFS (13.93 v 9.46 months) with weekly bortezomib and less neuropathy, at the cost of manageable gastrointestinal effects that may need dose changes. [S1]

## BCMA-directed therapy

- **For triple-class-exposed disease:** PBAC recommended elranatamab (as of July 2025) after at least three prior lines, and MSAC recommended funding cilta-cel CAR-T after at least four. Teclistamab is TGA-approved but not yet PBS-funded. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Bispecific T-cell engagers carry a high risk of low immunoglobulins and infection** (nearly 70% had infections with elranatamab). Plan immunoglobulin replacement, Pneumocystis prophylaxis with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and varicella-zoster prophylaxis with valaciclovir. [S1]
- **Unfunded drugs:** if neither TGA-approved nor PBS-listed, only private purchase through the Special Access Scheme; if TGA-approved but not listed, ordinary private purchase. Compassionate-access programs may also supply some. [S1]

## International view: CD38-antibody regimens (S2)

- ⚠️ **International consensus: use a CD38-antibody regimen at first relapse unless the disease is already refractory to a CD38 antibody** (evidence A, strong; all 17 panellists agreed). In Australia only Dara-Vd is PBS-funded for this, and only after one prior line (S1). [S2]
- **Why refractoriness matters:** across 24 trials and 19 real-world studies, lenalidomide-refractory patients had median PFS of 8.8 v 13.8 months and overall survival of 21.7 v 35.9 months against the whole population; double-refractory (lenalidomide and bortezomib) patients had PFS of only 5.5 months. [S2]
- **Carfilzomib-based triplets:** Dara-Kd (CANDOR) lengthened PFS to 28.4 v 15.2 months with Kd (HR 0.64); Isa-Kd (IKEMA) to 35.7 v 19.2 months (HR 0.58), with MRD negativity 33.5% v 15.4%. Neither is PBS-funded (S1). [S2]
- **Pomalidomide-based triplets:** Isa-Pd (ICARIA-MM) improved PFS (11.5 v 6.5 months, HR 0.60) and overall survival (24.6 v 17.7 months) over Pd, with benefit in lenalidomide-, PI- and double-refractory patients. Subcutaneous Dara-Pd (APOLLO, 79.6% lenalidomide-refractory) also beat Pd, with more neutropenia (69% v 51%). Neither is PBS-funded (S1). [S2]
- **Quadruplets are only suggested (evidence B, weak; 76% agreed),** on phase I/II data such as Dara-KPd (response rate 89%, MRD negativity 65% in a mostly lenalidomide-refractory group), whose maximum tolerated dose was carfilzomib 20/27 mg/m² with pomalidomide 4 mg. [S2]
- **Retreating with a CD38 antibody:** a different CD38-antibody regimen is reasonable if the antibody was given only in induction without maintenance (evidence B, weak; 70% agreed), ideally after a washout of about 6–12 months from the last dose (76% agreed), since CD38 re-expresses over that time. S2 sees retreatment mainly as a bridge to CAR-T or bispecific antibodies. [S2]
- **Renal impairment (eGFR 30–60):** CD38-antibody regimens are preferred (evidence A, strong; all agreed), Isa-Pd or Dara-Pd first and Isa-Kd or Dara-Kd when a proteasome inhibitor is needed, with monitoring; the CD38 antibody needs no dose change. [S2]
- **Frail or over 75:** CD38-antibody regimens are appropriate (evidence A, strong). In patients 75 or older, Dara-Rd gave PFS of 28.9 v 11.4 months and Dara-Vd 17.9 v 8.1 months, with about 50% grade 3–4 neutropenia. With COPD, daratumumab needs bronchodilator prophylaxis; daratumumab trials excluded FEV1 below 50%, isatuximab trials did not. [S2]
- **Gain or amplification of 1q21:** CD38-antibody regimens are appropriate (evidence A, strong). Isa-Pd improved PFS to 9.5 v 3.8 months (HR 0.40) and Isa-Kd to 25.8 v 16.2 months (HR 0.58) in 1q21+ disease; S2 suggests isatuximab may control 1q21 amplification better than daratumumab, but this is unproven. [S2]
- **Blood counts on CD38-antibody combinations:** check the blood count at least weekly for platelets. Hold treatment if neutrophils fall below 0.5 × 10⁹/L or febrile neutropenia occurs and restart at 1 × 10⁹/L; hold or adjust if platelets fall below 10 × 10⁹/L or there is bleeding. Give G-CSF for grade 3 or worse neutropenia, and reduce the IMiD rather than stop the antibody. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Infection prevention (evidence A, strong):** aciclovir or valaciclovir for everyone; trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for Pneumocystis with high-dose dexamethasone or prolonged severe neutropenia; levofloxacin in high-risk severe neutropenia; fluconazole, escalating to posaconazole for prolonged neutropenia; inactivated influenza, pneumococcal (PCV13 and PPSV23) and recombinant zoster vaccines before starting. Monitor IgG: consider antibiotics below 400 mg/dL, and immunoglobulin replacement below 200 mg/dL or with recurrent or severe infection. [S2]
- **Grade ≥3 adverse events across the regimens S2 reviews (Table 3):** neutropenia 10.1%–69% (regular blood counts, G-CSF for grade ≥3, dose interruption or change); thrombocytopenia 13%–30% (hold or adjust doses, transfuse if severe, stop drugs that worsen it); anaemia 17.5%–25% (consider an ESA or red-cell transfusion); infections 24.3%–46% (prophylactic antibiotics, antiviral and antifungal prophylaxis when high risk, IVIG for recurrent infection with hypogammaglobulinaemia, and influenza, pneumococcal and COVID-19 vaccines). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Infection work-up and prophylaxis before therapy (S2 Table 4):** serology for CMV, HBV and EBV, and latent TB screening in high-risk groups; influenza and recombinant zoster vaccines, and pneumococcal PCV13 plus PPSV23. HBV antiviral therapy with active or past hepatitis B; dapsone or atovaquone for Pneumocystis if allergic to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole; watch for reactivation of latent infection on high-dose steroids or prolonged immunosuppression. [S2]
- **After triple-class exposure:** S2 prefers BCMA CAR-T (cilta-cel, ide-cel) for fit patients with access to a specialised centre, which beat standard regimens including Dara-Pd in CARTITUDE-4 and KarMMa-3; bispecific antibodies are the off-the-shelf alternative (teclistamab median PFS 11.3 months; elranatamab response rate 61%, 15-month PFS 50.9%), with infection monitoring. Australian access to these is set out in S1. [S2]
