# Advanced prostate cancer: mHSPC, nmCRPC and mCRPC

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** imaging and staging choices, treatment of metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC), non-metastatic and metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (nmCRPC, mCRPC), HRR/BRCA testing, and patient-centred care. Early detection and localised disease are not covered. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Assess and stage

- **Next-generation imaging (PSMA PET-CT, whole-body MRI) stages prostate cancer more accurately than conventional imaging**, and PSMA PET-CT has the strongest evidence, with less radiation and less reader variability. [S1]
- **PSMA PET-CT can give false positives**, so an experienced nuclear medicine physician should read it. Do not withhold beneficial treatment of the primary tumour just because PSMA PET shows small lymph nodes. [S1]
- **Order a test only if its result will change the management plan.** [S1]

## Metastatic hormone-sensitive disease (mHSPC)

- **Disease volume on conventional imaging (CHAARTED definition) shapes treatment, especially whether to use docetaxel** (85% of panelists). Whether metastases were present at diagnosis or appeared later also changes sequencing, metastasis-directed therapy, triplet choice and prostate radiotherapy (69%). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Intensify beyond ADT alone.** For high-volume disease 84% would combine ADT with an androgen receptor signalling inhibitor (ARSi) and/or docetaxel; for low-volume disease 69% would use a combination and only 15% ADT alone. [S1]
- **ADT alone is sub-optimal when an ARSi can be given**; the minority (15%) who use it for low-volume disease do so because of reimbursement rules. [S1]
- **Triplet therapy (ADT + ARSi + docetaxel) may not add survival over an ARSi doublet**, according to a meta-analysis of five trials. Because toxicity accumulates, reserve triplets for chemo-fit patients with high-volume, poor-prognosis disease. [S1]
- **If a patient is doing well on a doublet, adding docetaxel is hard to justify**; for some with the worst prognostic features, ADT may suffice and de-escalation may help. [S1]
- **Low-volume synchronous mHSPC: add radiotherapy to the prostate** (88% of panelists), and consider metastasis-directed therapy for selected patients (85%). [S1]
- **Cost workarounds are common in the region:** about half (54%) give lower-dose abiraterone with food, a practice changing with the 500 mg tablet. Fear of chemotherapy also limits docetaxel use in some Asian populations. [S1]

## Non-metastatic castration-resistant disease (nmCRPC)

- **PSA doubling time matters more than imaging for prognosis in nmCRPC.** Baseline PSA and doubling time predict metastasis and survival; RADAR III reserves next-generation imaging for doubling time under 6 months when M1 treatment would be appropriate. [S1]
- **Consider an ARSi when PSA doubling time is under 10 months** and there has been no local therapy (54%). With doubling time over 10 months, opinion splits between observation, prostate radiotherapy, or local therapy plus ARSi. [S1]
- **On an ARSi, most (58%) monitor PSA alone**, though the trials used imaging as well and progression can happen without a PSA rise. [S1]

## HRR and BRCA testing

- **Up to 23% of men with mCRPC carry actionable mutations.** BRCA is commonest (about half germline); ATM is also frequent but responds less well to treatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Plan testing as the disease approaches castration resistance**, since PARP inhibitors such as olaparib benefit BRCA-mutated cancers. At minimum take a family history; refer for specialist genetic counselling before germline testing or when somatic testing finds a pathogenic HRR mutation. [S1]
- **Tissue can limit testing:** archived primary biopsies often have small samples and poor DNA yield or quality; alternatives include biopsy of other sites, ctDNA and germline testing. [S1]

## Metastatic castration-resistant disease (mCRPC)

- **First-line mCRPC without a DNA-repair mutation depends on what was used for mHSPC:** after ADT alone (65%) or ADT + docetaxel (77%), an ARSi; after ADT + ARSi, docetaxel (73%); after ADT + ARSi + docetaxel, Lu-177 PSMA (50%). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not switch from one ARSi to another on progression without an actionable mutation** (the international consensus); it offers little and adds cost and toxicity, though 27% of regional panelists still switch for cost or patient preference. [S1]
- **BRCA-mutated mCRPC:** half the panel (50%) uses a PARP inhibitor alone; ARSi-plus-PARPi combinations are approved first line in some countries. [S1]
- **Docetaxel can be re-tried in mCRPC** after a reasonable gap since its use for mHSPC (62%). Cabazitaxel is an option, but worries about chemotherapy and bone-marrow suppression lead some to prefer Lu-177 PSMA where it is available. [S1]
- **Monitor mCRPC clinically and with PSA**; the usual view is to image only when PSA or symptoms suggest progression. [S1]

## Patient-centred care

- **Use geriatric assessment for older men** (77% recommend it), in practice usually when red-flag issues come up. Supportive care includes bone protection, screening for and preventing metabolic syndrome, and exercise physiology. [S1]
