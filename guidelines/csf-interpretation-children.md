# CSF interpretation in children

> ⚠️ **If the CSF is abnormal, treat for bacterial meningitis.** A normal CSF does not exclude meningitis when clinical suspicion is high.

---

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Abnormal CSF: starting empiric antimicrobials for bacterial meningitis is the safest option.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Any neutrophils in the CSF, at any age, may indicate bacterial meningitis** — discuss with a senior clinician. [S1]
- **CSF values vary with age**: white cell count and protein are higher at birth and fall over the first 2 weeks of life. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Meningitis can occur with normal CSF microscopy.** With high clinical suspicion, **treat with IV antimicrobials anyway** pending cultures or PCR. [S1]

## Normal values

- **Under 1 month:** protein under 1.0 g/L; CSF:blood glucose ratio 0.6 or more (or glucose 2.0 mmol/L or more); lactate 0.9–2.5 mmol/L. **Over 1 month:** protein under 0.4 g/L; glucose ratio 0.6 or more (or 2.5 mmol/L or more); lactate 1.0–2.0 mmol/L. [S1]
- **Neonates:** up to 5% of CSF white cells in neonates without meningitis can be neutrophils. [S1]

## Abnormal patterns

- **Bacterial meningitis:** protein over 1.0 g/L, CSF:blood glucose ratio under 0.4, lactate over 2.0 mmol/L — **over 4.0 is strongly predictive**. Each value may be normal. [S1]
- **Viral meningitis:** protein 0.4–1.0 g/L, glucose usually normal, lactate under 2.0 mmol/L. **TB meningitis:** protein 1.0–5.0 g/L, glucose ratio under 0.3, lactate over 2.0 mmol/L. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Up to 60% of bacterial meningitis may have a negative Gram stain**, even without prior antibiotics. **Lymphocyte predominance does not exclude bacterial meningitis**, and in viral meningitis neutrophils can still predominate beyond 24 hours. [S1]

## Factors that change the result

- **Antibiotics given within 24 hours before LP are unlikely to alter cell count or biochemistry**, but usually prevent culture. Partially treated meningitis (recent oral antibiotics) may lower the neutrophil count. [S1]
- **Seizures do not raise the CSF cell count.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Traumatic tap: count the total white cells and disregard the red cells.** If white cells exceed the normal range for age, **start empiric antibiotics**. Do not rely on correcting the white cell count for red cells. [S1]
- **Some guidelines correct a traumatic tap** by 1 white cell per 500–700 red cells and 0.01 g/L protein per 1000 red cells — RCH advises against relying on this. Consider **subarachnoid haemorrhage** if red cells are unexplained or persistently raised. [S1]
- **Analyse promptly**: neutrophils and lymphocytes fall progressively after 4 hours through cell lysis. [S1]

## PCR and antigen tests

- **CSF PCR is widely available** for *N. meningitidis*, *S. pneumoniae*, HSV, enterovirus and parechovirus. Extended panels exist — interpret with caution (false positives) and correlate with the cell count. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not hold back empiric treatment to wait for PCR.** Meningococcal PCR is most useful after prior antibiotics. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Request HSV PCR for suspected encephalitis** — **during the first 36–72 hours it can be negative**; repeat LP and HSV PCR after that if suspicion is high. [S1]
- **Bacterial antigen tests should never influence treatment** — low sensitivity and specificity. [S1]

## Consultation and transfer

- **Consult the local paediatric team** for all suspected encephalitis or bacterial meningitis, and any concern for non-infectious encephalopathy. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider transfer for** seizures or focal signs, altered conscious state, haemodynamic or respiratory instability, complications, or poor response to treatment. [S1]
