# Classical homocystinuria (cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognising and diagnosing CBS deficiency (classical homocystinuria), testing pyridoxine responsiveness, homocysteine targets, the methionine-restricted diet with methionine-free amino acid mixtures, betaine, monitoring, thrombosis prevention, contraception, pregnancy, illness and surgery. Remethylation disorders and nutritional causes of raised homocysteine are only excluded here. First steps for any suspected inborn error in an unwell child: `metabolic-disorders-children`. ## Recognise

- **Four organ systems are involved:** eye (ectopia lentis, severe myopia), skeleton (marfanoid habitus, osteoporosis), central nervous system (developmental delay, seizures, psychiatric problems) and vascular system (thromboembolism). [S1]
- **Children tend to present with developmental delay and lens dislocation; adults after a vascular event.** Marfan syndrome and prothrombotic disorders are key differentials. [S1]

## Diagnose

- **Plasma total homocysteine (tHcy) is the frontline test.** Pyridoxine or fortified multivitamins taken before testing can mask mild disease. [S1]
- **Separate plasma within one hour of venepuncture.** Low or low-normal methionine, or raised methylmalonic acid, points to a cause other than CBS deficiency (such as B12 or folate deficiency). [S1]
- **Confirm with CBS enzyme activity and/or CBS gene analysis;** neither detects every case. When an index case is found, offer biochemical screening to at-risk relatives. [S1]

## Treatment targets and pyridoxine

- ⚠️ **Targets:** tHcy below 50 μmol/L in pyridoxine-responsive patients; in pyridoxine-unresponsive patients the guideline recommends keeping tHcy below 100 μmol/L, with normal methionine and nutrition. [S1]
- **Test pyridoxine responsiveness after infancy with 10 mg/kg/day (maximum 500 mg/day) for 6 weeks,** measuring tHcy at least twice before and twice on treatment, with normal protein intake and folate given. Below 50 μmol/L on pyridoxine means fully responsive; a fall of more than 20% that stays above 50 means add diet and/or betaine. [S1]
- **Babies found by newborn screening rarely respond;** test them with 100 mg/day for 2 weeks. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Use the lowest effective pyridoxine dose,** up to 10 mg/kg/day, avoiding more than 500 mg/day: long-term doses above 900 mg/day cause peripheral neuropathy. Do not continue pyridoxine without a biochemical response. [S1]
- **Give all patients adequate folate;** monitor B12 and supplement if deficient. [S1]

## Diet and betaine

- **Consider diet for every patient not at target on pyridoxine alone.** Most pyridoxine-unresponsive patients need a diet very low in natural protein plus a **methionine-free amino acid mixture**, for life. [S1]
- **Most need a low-methionine diet with a cystine-enriched, methionine-free supplement;** some partial responders manage with milder protein restriction and no supplement. The degree of restriction is set by each patient's tHcy and methionine. [S1]
- **Betaine is an adjunct** for patients not at target by other means; on its own it seldom reaches target in pyridoxine-unresponsive disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Betaine dosing:** children start at 50 mg/kg twice daily, adults at 3 grams twice daily, adjusted to response; exceeding 150-200 mg/kg/day is unlikely to help. Higher doses cause a fishy odour; cerebral oedema is very rare. [S1]

## Monitor

- **Monitor tHcy, plasma amino acids (including methionine), B12 and folate in everyone;** six-monthly tHcy may be enough for fully pyridoxine-responsive adults, but children on diet need far more frequent checks. Measure B12 and folate annually. [S1]
- **DEXA every 3-5 years from adolescence;** MRI only with abnormal neurological signs. [S1]

## Thrombosis, pregnancy, illness and surgery

- ⚠️ **Venous thromboembolism risk may reach 50% by age 30 in untreated pyridoxine-unresponsive patients.** Lowering tHcy is the most important protection; keep patients hydrated. Poorly controlled patients or those with a past event may need antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid oestrogen-containing contraceptives.** [S1]
- **Pregnancy:** low molecular weight heparin in the third trimester (possibly throughout) and for at least 6 weeks postpartum; review diet often as methionine needs rise, and cut methionine back to pre-pregnancy levels after delivery. Give at least 400 micrograms folate. [S1]
- **During intercurrent illness, continue all treatment** (pyridoxine, betaine, amino acid mixture, energy) and avoid dehydration and immobility. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Surgery:** optimise control first, keep hydrated with IV fluids, use mechanical thromboprophylaxis and early mobilisation, LMWH if immobilisation is prolonged, and **avoid nitrous oxide**, which raises homocysteine. [S1]
