# Clostridioides difficile infection in adults

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with C. difficile infection (CDI): severity, first-episode treatment, severe and fulminant disease, refractory disease, recurrence (fidaxomicin, bezlotoxumab, tapered vancomycin, faecal microbiota transplant) and prevention. Primary-care drug choice from AMH is in `drug-choice-for-selected-infections`; general diarrhoea work-up in `diarrhoea`; probiotics in `probiotics`. Children: see `gastroenteritis-children`.

> ✅ **PARAPHRASED, HASH-ANCHORED.** 20 claims paraphrased from **Bishop EJ, Tiruvoipati R (Peninsula Health and Monash University, Melbourne), Journal of Antimicrobial Chemotherapy — *Management of Clostridioides difficile infection in adults and challenges in clinical practice: review and comparison of current IDSA/SHEA, ESCMID and ASID guidelines*** (published 28 November 2022 (J Antimicrob Chemother 78(1):21); origin: AU). **29 anchors re-checkable by machine; 2 doses.** The source's words are not reproduced: its licence is *CC BY-NC 4.0*.

> ⚠️ **This is a narrative review by Australian authors comparing IDSA/SHEA 2021, ESCMID 2021 and ASID 2016 guidelines**, not a guideline itself.

> ⚠️ **Australian guidance has changed since this review.** It describes ASID 2016 as still recommending metronidazole for a first non-severe episode. The **ASID 2025 update** (closed access, abstract only) replaced metronidazole with oral vancomycin, which is what `drug-choice-for-selected-infections` (AMH) now recommends. The **PBS lists oral vancomycin** for this condition (see table below).

## Assess severity

- **Non-severe CDI (IDSA/SHEA):** white cell count at or below 15 000 cells/mL and creatinine under 1.5 mg/dL. ESCMID adds temperature at or below 38.5°C, a creatinine rise of no more than 50% above baseline and no imaging signs of severity. [S1]
- **Severe CDI:** white cell count over 15 000 cells/mL or creatinine of 1.5 mg/dL or more (IDSA/SHEA). ESCMID also counts fever over 38.5°C or imaging showing colonic distension, pericolonic fat stranding or wall thickening. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fulminant (severe-complicated) CDI** means hypotension or shock, ileus or toxic megacolon; ESCMID adds raised lactate, perforation or any fulminant course. [S1]
- **Refractory CDI** is disease not responding to recommended antibiotics, that is, no response after 3–5 days. [S1]

## First episode, non-severe

- **IDSA/SHEA and ESCMID now prefer fidaxomicin for a first episode**; vancomycin is acceptable, and metronidazole only when neither is available. [S1]
- **Recurrence follows 20%–30% of courses of metronidazole or vancomycin**, and after one recurrence the chance of another is 50%–60%. Fidaxomicin is better at preventing recurrence. [S1]
- **Vancomycin beat metronidazole** in two placebo-controlled trials; a Cochrane analysis of 22 trials, mostly non-severe disease, gave moderate-quality evidence that vancomycin is superior in all cases. [S1]
- **Severity changes over time**, so reserving metronidazole for 'non-severe' cases risks under-treatment: one study found CDI-attributable 90-day mortality of 21.7% when under-treated against 8.9% when appropriately treated. [S1]
- **Fidaxomicin's main barrier to wider use is its high cost.** [S1]

## Severe CDI

- ⚠️ **Oral metronidazole is not appropriate for severe CDI**; all guidelines agree. [S1]
- **The older ASID guideline advises oral vancomycin 125 mg every 6 hours for 10 days in severe disease** and does not recommend fidaxomicin; ESCMID and IDSA/SHEA accept fidaxomicin as non-inferior. There are no data for fidaxomicin in life-threatening disease. [S1]

## Fulminant and refractory CDI

- ⚠️ **IDSA/SHEA 2021 for fulminant CDI:** no fidaxomicin; add IV metronidazole 500 mg every 8 hours to higher-dose vancomycin 500 mg every 6 hours by mouth or nasogastric tube, with rectal vancomycin retention enemas every 6 hours if there is ileus. ESCMID instead allows fidaxomicin, does not routinely add IV metronidazole and suggests adding IV tigecycline. [S1]
- **Get a surgical referral alongside medical treatment** in deteriorating patients, but avoid total colectomy where possible, particularly without toxic megacolon; ESCMID suggests partial colectomy or loop ileostomy may spare total colectomy. [S1]
- **Tigecycline can cause liver derangement, coagulopathy or pancreatitis**; monitor closely, stop early if adverse effects appear, and note courses over 14 days raise the coagulopathy risk. [S1]
- **Rescue faecal microbiota transplant (FMT)** for refractory disease: ESCMID supports considering it when surgery is not feasible, and Australasian guidelines when medical therapy fails; IDSA/SHEA has not endorsed it here. [S1]

## Recurrence and prevention

- **Bezlotoxumab** (a single infusion of anti-toxin B antibody) lowers recurrence. Both IDSA/SHEA and ESCMID support it for a second episode within 6 months; in heart failure reserve it for when benefit outweighs risk, as more deaths occurred (19.5% vs 12.5%). It is not yet endorsed in Australian guidelines. [S1]
- **Tapered-and-pulsed oral vancomycin** is an acceptable alternative to fidaxomicin (standard or extended-pulsed) for a first or later recurrence. [S1]
- **FMT:** ESCMID and Australasian guidelines agree on it for a second recurrence; IDSA/SHEA now limits it to the third and later recurrences after donor-transmitted infection alerts. A single FMT cures 65%–95% by any route. [S1]
- **Probiotics are not endorsed** by current CDI guidelines; ESCMID warns of possibly significant adverse effects and delayed microbiome recovery, and there are reports of bacteraemia and fungaemia. [S1]
- **Routine anti-CDI antibiotic prophylaxis during systemic antibiotics is not recommended (ESCMID)**; it may suit very selected patients with multiply recurrent CDI triggered by antibiotics, after specialist infectious diseases or microbiology advice. [S1]
