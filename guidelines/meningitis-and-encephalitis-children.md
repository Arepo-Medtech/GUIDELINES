# Meningitis and encephalitis in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, lumbar puncture, empiric and directed antimicrobials, steroids, fluids and follow-up for bacterial meningitis and encephalitis in children. > ⚠️ **Do not delay antimicrobials.** When LP is contraindicated, or cannot happen within 30 minutes, give empiric IV antimicrobials first (and consider steroids).

## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **Bacterial meningitis is a medical emergency needing empiric antibiotics without delay.** Keep a high index of suspicion in any unwell child, especially with altered mental state or no clear focus. [S1]
- **Viral and bacterial meningitis cannot be reliably told apart clinically** — give empiric antimicrobials until the cause is known. Presentation ranges from hours to days and may be altered by recent antibiotics. [S1]
- **Meningitis and encephalitis are hard to separate but have different pathogens** — if in doubt, cover both initially. [S1]
- **Infants with meningitis are often subtle:** poor feeding, irritability, lethargy or drowsiness, apnoea, temperature instability, vomiting and diarrhoea. On examination look for fever or hypothermia, a high-pitched cry, a full or bulging fontanelle; **neck stiffness may be absent in infants**. [S1]
- **Encephalitis:** altered mental state can be subtle — unusual behaviour, confusion, personality change, emotional lability — and seizures are common. **Bradycardia and hypertension can signal raised intracranial pressure.** [S1]
- **Risk factors for meningitis:** maternal GBS colonisation (infants <3 months), unimmunised, immunocompromised, cochlear implant, VP shunt, neurosurgery or penetrating head injury, and age <5 years. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Urgent LP**, with urgent CSF microscopy and biochemistry, preferably with a simultaneous blood glucose. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Defer LP and start empiric treatment immediately if it is unsafe:** coagulopathy, cardiovascular compromise, ongoing seizures, focal neurological signs or a markedly reduced GCS. Do the LP as soon as a senior clinician deems it safe. Tachycardia and tachypnoea with no respiratory compromise do not contraindicate LP. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Unwell child and LP more than 30 minutes away: give antimicrobials without waiting.** [S1]
- **Bloods:** blood culture, FBE (may be normal), glucose, gas for lactate, UEC for hyponatraemia; coagulation studies if shock or coagulopathy suspected. [S1]
- **Neuroimaging** for focal signs, raised ICP, encephalopathy or diagnostic uncertainty; not routine in meningitis. **A normal CT does not rule out raised ICP, and must not decide whether to do the LP.** [S1]

## Treat — empiric

- **Cover meningitis first; add encephalitis treatment** if there is altered conscious state, focal signs, seizures, signs or risk of HSV, or the child is obtunded. **Antibiotics go in no later than 30 minutes after deciding to treat.** [S1]
- **0–2 months (GBS, E. coli, Listeria):** benzylpenicillin 60 mg/kg IV — every 12 hours under 7 days old, every 6–8 hours from 7 days to <4 weeks, every 4 hours over 4 weeks — **and** cefotaxime 50 mg/kg IV — every 12 hours in week 1, every 6–8 hours from 7 days to <4 weeks, every 6 hours over 4 weeks — or ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg IV daily (max 2 g). **Dexamethasone not advised.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Where possible, do not use ceftriaxone in a neonate <41 weeks gestation**, especially one who is jaundiced or on calcium-containing solutions (TPN included). [S1]
- **2 months and over (N. meningitidis, S. pneumoniae, HiB):** ceftriaxone IV 100 mg/kg (max 4 g) once daily **or** cefotaxime IV 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) every 6 hours. **Gram-positive cocci on Gram stain → add vancomycin.** [S1]
- **Dexamethasone (2 months and over):** IV 0.15 mg/kg (max 10 mg) every 6 hours, for 4 days. [S1]
- **If hypersensitive to penicillin or cephalosporins:** vancomycin **plus** moxifloxacin IV 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg) once daily. [S1]
- **Encephalitis — add aciclovir to the antibiotics:** <30 weeks gestation 20 mg/kg IV every 12 hours; from >30 weeks gestation up to <3 months corrected age 20 mg/kg IV every 8 hours; 3 months to 12 years 20 mg/kg IV every 8 hours; >12 years 10 mg/kg IV every 8 hours. **Azithromycin can be considered as an addition.** [S1]
- **Unrecognised HSV encephalitis is devastating, but early aciclovir can lead to full recovery.** [S1]

## Steroids

- **Steroids may lower the risk of hearing loss** in bacterial meningitis (evidence mixed). **Not recommended in neonates** because of possible neurodevelopmental effects. [S1]
- **Time the first IV dexamethasone dose just before, or together with, the first antibiotic dose.** If given after, ideally within 4 hours and no more than 12 hours after starting antibiotics. [S1]

## Supportive care

- **Treat all seizures immediately.** For shock, resuscitate using **IV boluses of 0.9% sodium chloride, 10 mL/kg**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Risk of excess ADH: cap IV fluids at 2/3 of maintenance**; further restriction may be needed for hyponatraemia or raised ICP (seek senior advice). **Always maintain normal blood pressure and adequate circulating volume.** [S1]
- **Reassess hydration, weight, sodium, acid–base and neurological state 6–12-hourly during the first 48 hours.** Droplet precautions for the first 24 hours of admission. [S1]

## Directed treatment

- **Directed treatment length:** 7 days of benzylpenicillin for *N. meningitidis* · 10 days of benzylpenicillin for penicillin-sensitive *S. pneumoniae* · 10 days of cefotaxime/ceftriaxone for penicillin-resistant *S. pneumoniae* (vancomycin if also cephalosporin-resistant) · 10 days of ceftriaxone/cefotaxime for HiB. [S1]
- **Gram-negative:** ceftriaxone/cefotaxime 21 days · **organism not isolated:** 7 days minimum · **GBS, Listeria:** benzylpenicillin 14–21 days · **HSV:** aciclovir 21 days minimum. [S1]
- **Consider ID consultation** for resistant organisms or immediate penicillin/cephalosporin hypersensitivity. [S1]
- **Notify presumed or confirmed N. meningitidis and HiB immediately**; confirmed S. pneumoniae within 5 days. [S1]
- **Fever still present after 4–6 days of treatment:** think of inadequate treatment, nosocomial infection, cerebral abscess or parameningeal focus, subdural effusion or empyema. [S1]

## Disposition and follow-up

- **Transfer if** altered conscious state, seizures or focal signs, haemodynamic or respiratory instability, care beyond local comfort, or complications/poor response. [S1]
- **IV treatment can be completed via HITH** once haemodynamically stable, afebrile and directed treatment decided. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hearing loss is highest after S. pneumoniae meningitis (about 22%)** and can begin within 48 hours; do baseline audiology ideally as an inpatient. [S1]
- **Formal audiology 6–8 weeks after discharge for every child with encephalitis or bacterial meningitis**; monitor neurodevelopment; consider complement deficiency testing after more than one episode of meningococcal disease. [S1]
