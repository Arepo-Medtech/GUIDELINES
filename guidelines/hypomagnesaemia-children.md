# Hypomagnesaemia in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** causes, assessment, and oral and IV magnesium replacement and escalation for hypomagnesaemia in children. No adult hypomagnesaemia guideline is in this corpus. ## Key points

- **Oral/enteral is the preferred route.** Consider oral replacement for **asymptomatic, mild** hypomagnesaemia without GI intolerance (diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting). [S1]
- **IV is preferred for symptomatic hypomagnesaemia or significant GI intolerance.** ⚠️ **Seek specialist advice for any symptomatic child.** [S1]
- **Serum magnesium may not reflect body stores** — under 1% is extracellular. With normal renal function, 24-hour urinary magnesium may show earlier change. [S1]
- **Interpret in context:** to avoid arrhythmia after cardiac surgery, the **upper end of normal** may be the target. [S1]

## Causes and assessment

- **Drug causes:** loop and thiazide diuretics, cisplatin, tacrolimus and ciclosporin, **chronic proton pump inhibitors**, aminoglycosides. [S1]
- **GI causes:** reduced intake or absorption (malabsorption, short bowel), and losses (diarrhoea, laxative abuse, gastric suction or vomiting). Also DKA, burns and cardiopulmonary bypass. [S1]
- **Look for:** tremor, fasciculations, hyperreflexia, paraesthesia, weakness · **arrhythmia and PR/QT prolongation** · **hypokalaemia and hypocalcaemia** · nystagmus, seizures, confusion, irritability. [S1]
- **Monitor magnesium at a frequency set by the degree of abnormality and symptoms;** identify and correct the cause. [S1]

## Oral / enteral dosing

- **Oral is for asymptomatic mild hypomagnesaemia** — ⚠️ **not with significant GI intolerance such as diarrhoea, which oral magnesium will exacerbate.** [S1]
- **Dose: 0.1–0.2 mmol/kg (2.5–5 mg/kg) orally 3 times daily,** stepping up when needed to **0.4–0.8 mmol/kg (10–20 mg/kg) as often as 4 times daily.** Smaller, more frequent doses are better tolerated. [S1]
- **Products:** magnesium complex solution **50 mg/mL (2.1 mmol/mL)** · **1.55 mmol (37.4 mg)** magnesium aspartate tablet (MagMin, Mag-Sup) · Cenovis magnesium tablet **13.5 mmol (325 mg)**, which also contains manganese and pyridoxine. [S1]

## IV dosing

- ⚠️ **Severe symptoms (tetany, arrhythmia, seizures): treat with IV magnesium. Seek specialist advice for IV replacement.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Magnesium IV: 0.1–0.2 mmol/kg, as much as 0.4 mmol/kg (max 8 mmol), infused over 2–4 hours** — reduces adverse effects and improves cellular uptake. **May be given faster with severe symptoms.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Concentrated ampoules are 2 mmol/mL** — DBL magnesium sulfate 49.3% (493 mg/mL), **must be diluted first**; magnesium sulfate heptahydrate 50% (500 mg/mL). [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team** if symptomatic or severe. **Nutrition/gastroenterology/dietitian** for hypomagnesaemia on PN or with refeeding syndrome. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Renal team** for unexplained hypomagnesaemia, or with moderate–severe renal impairment — **replacement risks hypermagnesaemia.** [S1]
- **Consider transfer** if symptomatic or needing monitoring your centre cannot provide. **Discharge** when stable with a plan and any acute or chronic abnormality has resolved. [S1]
