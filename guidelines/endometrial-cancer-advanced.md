# Advanced or recurrent dMMR endometrial cancer: carboplatin, paclitaxel and dostarlimab

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** first-line carboplatin, paclitaxel and dostarlimab, then dostarlimab maintenance, for adults with advanced, metastatic or recurrent mismatch repair deficient (dMMR) endometrial cancer: eligibility, PBS status, dosing, immune-related and chemotherapy toxicity, monitoring, dose changes and the RUBY trial. The durvalumab-based first-line regimen (eviQ ID 4592), lenvatinib with pembrolizumab (ID 4322), chemotherapy alone and hormonal therapy are separate eviQ protocols and are not covered. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ⚠️ **This guideline follows a single eviQ protocol (carboplatin, paclitaxel and dostarlimab).** It is regimen-level, not a full endometrial cancer pathway, and applies to dMMR disease only.

> ⚠️ **Australian funding differs from registration:** dostarlimab is TGA-registered for proficient MMR (pMMR) disease too, but the PBS funds it only for dMMR disease.

## Who is eligible

- **For dMMR endometrial cancer (mismatch repair deficient on immunohistochemistry) that is advanced, metastatic or recurrent**, and either untreated with systemic therapy or relapsing more than 6 months after the last neoadjuvant or adjuvant systemic treatment. [S1]
- **No earlier anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 drug for this indication, and ECOG performance status 0 or 1.** [S1]
- **Talk to an expert first** if the patient has significant autoimmune disease, an organ transplant, previous viral hepatitis or past lung irradiation. [S1]
- **Only specialists experienced with immunotherapy should start and supervise treatment**, and they must be confident in managing immune-related adverse events. [S1]
- ⚠️ **PBS status:** dostarlimab is a PBS authority item for dMMR endometrial cancer only; for pMMR disease it is TGA-registered but not PBS-listed. Carboplatin and paclitaxel are on the general schedule. [S1]

## Treatment and dosing

- ⚠️ **Cycles 1 to 6, every 21 days, all on day 1:** dostarlimab 500 mg IV, paclitaxel 175 mg/m² IV and carboplatin AUC 5 IV. **From cycle 7:** dostarlimab 1,000 mg IV every 42 days, until progression, unacceptable toxicity or a maximum of 3 years. [S1]
- **Carboplatin is dosed by the Calvert formula, ideally with a measured GFR** when intent is curative or estimates may mislead: eGFR above 125 or at or below 45 mL/min/1.73 m², extremes of body size or muscle mass, amputation or paraplegia. Otherwise a BSA-adjusted eGFR is acceptable. [S1]
- **Recalculate the carboplatin dose only if kidney function shifts by more than 20% from baseline or the patient's condition changes.** [S1]
- **Early growth may be pseudoprogression** (rare, about 5%): if the patient seems to be benefiting, consider carrying on and repeating the scan 4 to 6 weeks later. Radiation recall is possible, so time the start carefully after a long radiotherapy course. [S1]
- **Hypersensitivity risk is high with both paclitaxel and carboplatin.** Carboplatin reactions become likelier with each cycle and premedication may not stop them; rechallenge after a reaction needs a desensitisation protocol with support on hand. [S1]

## Immune-related toxicity and monitoring

- ⚠️ **Immune-related adverse events can start early, worsen quickly and appear even after treatment stops; deaths have occurred.** The most dangerous include myocarditis, HLH-like syndrome, myositis, encephalitis, Guillain-Barré syndrome, myelitis, myasthenia gravis, pneumonitis and SJS/TEN. Monitor proactively, encourage early self-reporting, and interrupt treatment, consult specialists and give corticosteroids as required. [S1]
- **Baseline tests:** FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs, calcium, magnesium, cortisol, TFTs and glucose; consider ECG, troponin and urinalysis. **Each cycle:** FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs and glucose. TFTs (and consider cortisol) every 4-6 weeks on treatment and every 12 weeks afterwards as indicated; lipase and amylase if pancreatitis is suspected. [S1]
- **Screen everyone for hepatitis before a checkpoint inhibitor.** Patients with chronic hepatitis B or C, a detectable HIV viral load or AIDS need close monitoring and antiviral treatment if indicated. [S1]

## Dose changes

- **Dostarlimab is not dose-reduced.** No change is needed for older age or mild hepatic impairment; checkpoint inhibitors are unstudied in moderate to severe hepatic impairment. [S1]
- **Blood counts before each cycle:** delay for ANC 0.5 to under 1.0 or platelets 50 to under 75; for ANC under 0.5, platelets under 50 or febrile neutropenia, delay, and consider a 25% cut to both paclitaxel and carboplatin for later cycles. [S1]
- **Paclitaxel:** cut by 25% for mild and 50% for moderate hepatic dysfunction, and omit if severe. Grade 2 neuropathy at the next cycle: delay until grade 1 or better, then reduce paclitaxel by 25%, stopping it if the delay passes 3 weeks or neuropathy returns. Grade 3 or 4 neuropathy: omit paclitaxel. [S1]

## Evidence

- **The supporting trial is RUBY (phase III, 487 patients)** with primary stage III-IV or first recurrent endometrial cancer, untreated or relapsing at least 6 months after adjuvant or neoadjuvant therapy. Patients had carboplatin and paclitaxel with dostarlimab or placebo for six cycles, then dostarlimab 1000 mg or placebo 6-weekly, for a maximum of 3 years. [S1]
- ⚠️ **The benefit was concentrated in dMMR/MSI-H disease:** 24-month PFS 61.4% vs 15.7% (HR 0.28) and 36-month OS 78.0% vs 46.0% (HR 0.32). Overall, 24-month PFS was 36.1% vs 18.1% (HR 0.64) with an OS HR of 0.69. In pMMR disease the PFS HR was 0.76 and the OS HR 0.79, with a confidence interval that included no effect. [S1]
- **Serious treatment-related adverse events were commoner with dostarlimab (19.5% vs 12.2%), and so were stops for adverse events (19.1% vs 8.1%).** Nausea, alopecia and fatigue were the most frequent events in both arms. [S1]
