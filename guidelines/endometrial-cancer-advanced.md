# Advanced or recurrent dMMR endometrial cancer: carboplatin, paclitaxel and dostarlimab

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** first-line carboplatin, paclitaxel and dostarlimab, then dostarlimab maintenance, for adults with advanced, metastatic or recurrent mismatch repair deficient (dMMR) endometrial cancer: eligibility, PBS status, dosing, immune-related and chemotherapy toxicity, monitoring, dose changes and the RUBY trial. The durvalumab-based first-line regimen (eviQ ID 4592), lenvatinib with pembrolizumab (ID 4322), chemotherapy alone and hormonal therapy are separate eviQ protocols and are not covered. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`. Edition 2.0 adds, from S2 (SEOM-GEICO 2025), the wider pathway for advanced and recurrent disease (dMMR and pMMR first line, hormonal therapy, later lines, HER2 testing and follow-up); the other regimens' Australian doses are still not covered.

> ⚠️ **This guideline follows a single eviQ protocol (carboplatin, paclitaxel and dostarlimab).** It is regimen-level, not a full endometrial cancer pathway, and applies to dMMR disease only.

> ⚠️ International guideline (SEOM-GEICO, 2025) as S2. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ. S1 (eviQ, Australian) is a single regimen protocol; S2 adds the treatment pathway around it.

> ⚠️ **Australian funding differs from registration:** dostarlimab is TGA-registered for proficient MMR (pMMR) disease too, but the PBS funds it only for dMMR disease.

## Who is eligible

- **For dMMR endometrial cancer (mismatch repair deficient on immunohistochemistry) that is advanced, metastatic or recurrent**, and either untreated with systemic therapy or relapsing more than 6 months after the last neoadjuvant or adjuvant systemic treatment. [S1]
- **No earlier anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 drug for this indication, and ECOG performance status 0 or 1.** [S1]
- **Talk to an expert first** if the patient has significant autoimmune disease, an organ transplant, previous viral hepatitis or past lung irradiation. [S1]
- **Only specialists experienced with immunotherapy should start and supervise treatment**, and they must be confident in managing immune-related adverse events. [S1]
- ⚠️ **PBS status:** dostarlimab is a PBS authority item for dMMR endometrial cancer only; for pMMR disease it is TGA-registered but not PBS-listed. Carboplatin and paclitaxel are on the general schedule. [S1]

## Treatment and dosing

- ⚠️ **Cycles 1 to 6, every 21 days, all on day 1:** dostarlimab 500 mg IV, paclitaxel 175 mg/m² IV and carboplatin AUC 5 IV. **From cycle 7:** dostarlimab 1,000 mg IV every 42 days, until progression, unacceptable toxicity or a maximum of 3 years. [S1]
- **Carboplatin is dosed by the Calvert formula, ideally with a measured GFR** when intent is curative or estimates may mislead: eGFR above 125 or at or below 45 mL/min/1.73 m², extremes of body size or muscle mass, amputation or paraplegia. Otherwise a BSA-adjusted eGFR is acceptable. [S1]
- **Recalculate the carboplatin dose only if kidney function shifts by more than 20% from baseline or the patient's condition changes.** [S1]
- **Early growth may be pseudoprogression** (rare, about 5%): if the patient seems to be benefiting, consider carrying on and repeating the scan 4 to 6 weeks later. Radiation recall is possible, so time the start carefully after a long radiotherapy course. [S1]
- **Hypersensitivity risk is high with both paclitaxel and carboplatin.** Carboplatin reactions become likelier with each cycle and premedication may not stop them; rechallenge after a reaction needs a desensitisation protocol with support on hand. [S1]

## Immune-related toxicity and monitoring

- ⚠️ **Immune-related adverse events can start early, worsen quickly and appear even after treatment stops; deaths have occurred.** The most dangerous include myocarditis, HLH-like syndrome, myositis, encephalitis, Guillain-Barré syndrome, myelitis, myasthenia gravis, pneumonitis and SJS/TEN. Monitor proactively, encourage early self-reporting, and interrupt treatment, consult specialists and give corticosteroids as required. [S1]
- **Baseline tests:** FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs, calcium, magnesium, cortisol, TFTs and glucose; consider ECG, troponin and urinalysis. **Each cycle:** FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs and glucose. TFTs (and consider cortisol) every 4-6 weeks on treatment and every 12 weeks afterwards as indicated; lipase and amylase if pancreatitis is suspected. [S1]
- **Screen everyone for hepatitis before a checkpoint inhibitor.** Patients with chronic hepatitis B or C, a detectable HIV viral load or AIDS need close monitoring and antiviral treatment if indicated. [S1]

## Dose changes

- **Dostarlimab is not dose-reduced.** No change is needed for older age or mild hepatic impairment; checkpoint inhibitors are unstudied in moderate to severe hepatic impairment. [S1]
- **Blood counts before each cycle:** delay for ANC 0.5 to under 1.0 or platelets 50 to under 75; for ANC under 0.5, platelets under 50 or febrile neutropenia, delay, and consider a 25% cut to both paclitaxel and carboplatin for later cycles. [S1]
- **Paclitaxel:** cut by 25% for mild and 50% for moderate hepatic dysfunction, and omit if severe. Grade 2 neuropathy at the next cycle: delay until grade 1 or better, then reduce paclitaxel by 25%, stopping it if the delay passes 3 weeks or neuropathy returns. Grade 3 or 4 neuropathy: omit paclitaxel. [S1]

## The wider pathway for advanced and recurrent disease (S2)

- **Classify every endometrial cancer molecularly** for sharper risk grouping and treatment choice [III, A], and assess for hereditary syndromes, offering genetic counselling to those who test positive [III, A] (S2 summary table). [S2]
- **Stage III under FIGO 2023 (S2 Table 3)** is regional spread of any histology: IIIA1 ovary or fallopian tube (unless it meets IA3), IIIA2 uterine subserosa or through the serosa, IIIB1 parametria and/or vagina, IIIB2 pelvic peritoneum, IIIC1 pelvic nodes and IIIC2 para-aortic nodes up to the renal vessels, each nodal stage split into micro- and macrometastasis. [S2]
- **Stage IV under FIGO 2023:** IVA bladder or bowel mucosa, IVB peritoneal spread beyond the pelvis, IVC distant metastasis including nodes above the renal vessels. Molecular class (POLEmut or p53abn) changes the stage only in stages I and II, not III or IV, but should still be recorded. Macrometastases are over 2 mm; micrometastases 0.2–2 mm and/or over 200 cells. [S2]
- **Test every endometrial cancer for mismatch repair deficiency by immunohistochemistry** (Lynch syndrome screening, whatever the age or histology) [II, A]; MLH1 loss needs MLH1 promoter methylation (or BRAF) testing before germline analysis. Also assess ER at diagnosis [IV, A], because it predicts response to hormonal treatment in advanced disease. [S2]
- **Isolated pelvic recurrence or a single metastatic site:** consider surgery, radiotherapy or ablation [IV, A], followed by systemic treatment of uncertain benefit [IV, B]. Unresectable or metastatic disease is treated with chemotherapy, immunotherapy or hormonal therapy, and trial enrolment is strongly encouraged [V, B]. [S2]
- **The chemotherapy backbone is carboplatin AUC 5–6 with paclitaxel 175 mg/m² every three weeks** [I, A]: in GOG 209 it was non-inferior to the three-drug TAP regimen (PFS 12–14 months, OS 32 months) and less toxic. [S2]
- ⚠️ **dMMR first line (S2): chemotherapy with a checkpoint inhibitor, then checkpoint maintenance, is standard** [I, A]: pembrolizumab for 2 years (NRG-GY018, dMMR PFS HR 0.3) or dostarlimab for 3 years (RUBY, dMMR PFS HR 0.23 and OS HR 0.32). Atezolizumab and durvalumab have also shown first-line activity. The two trials differ in design (interval since adjuvant therapy, maintenance length, histologies included), which matters when choosing. [S2]
- ⚠️ **pMMR first line (S2): chemotherapy plus immunotherapy is also the standard** [I, B], though the gain is smaller than in dMMR. Adding a PARP inhibitor to chemotherapy and immunotherapy (RUBY part 2, DUO-E) is another option for pMMR [I, B]. Lenvatinib with pembrolizumab failed as first line (LEAP-001), but may be considered for pMMR relapse after adjuvant chemotherapy when more chemotherapy is contraindicated [III, C]. [S2]
- **Hormonal therapy suits low-grade, hormone receptor-positive disease that is not rapidly progressing or bulky** [II, A]: megestrol acetate 160 mg daily or medroxyprogesterone acetate 200 mg daily (preferred), alone or with tamoxifen, or tamoxifen, an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant [III, A]. Consider rebiopsy at recurrence to confirm receptor status [IV, B]; responses are about 10–25% in chemotherapy-naive patients. [S2]
- **Second line after platinum, if no prior immunotherapy:** dMMR: dostarlimab or pembrolizumab alone [III, A] (response rates 46% in GARNET and 57% in KEYNOTE-158), or pembrolizumab with lenvatinib [I, B]. pMMR: pembrolizumab with lenvatinib [I, A], which beat chemotherapy on PFS, OS and response whatever the MMR status, at the cost of frequent hypertension, hypothyroidism and diarrhoea. [S2]
- **After immunotherapy,** options are modest (weekly paclitaxel, docetaxel, oxaliplatin, liposomal doxorubicin, topotecan, ifosfamide), and platinum can be repeated after a late relapse. **Test HER2:** about 30% of serous carcinomas are HER2-positive; trastuzumab added to carboplatin-paclitaxel improved PFS and OS, and trastuzumab deruxtecan gave an 84% response rate in strongly HER2-expressing (+++) endometrial cancer (phase II). [S2]
- **Follow-up:** review every three to six months for two years, then every six to twelve months, with symptom review and a speculum and bimanual examination; teach the symptoms of recurrence. Serum markers and routine vaginal cytology are not recommended [I, A]. [S2]

## Evidence

- **The supporting trial is RUBY (phase III, 487 patients)** with primary stage III-IV or first recurrent endometrial cancer, untreated or relapsing at least 6 months after adjuvant or neoadjuvant therapy. Patients had carboplatin and paclitaxel with dostarlimab or placebo for six cycles, then dostarlimab 1000 mg or placebo 6-weekly, for a maximum of 3 years. [S1]
- ⚠️ **The benefit was concentrated in dMMR/MSI-H disease:** 24-month PFS 61.4% vs 15.7% (HR 0.28) and 36-month OS 78.0% vs 46.0% (HR 0.32). Overall, 24-month PFS was 36.1% vs 18.1% (HR 0.64) with an OS HR of 0.69. In pMMR disease the PFS HR was 0.76 and the OS HR 0.79, with a confidence interval that included no effect. [S1]
- **Serious treatment-related adverse events were commoner with dostarlimab (19.5% vs 12.2%), and so were stops for adverse events (19.1% vs 8.1%).** Nausea, alopecia and fatigue were the most frequent events in both arms. [S1]
