# Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP)

## Recognise

- **DFSP is a rare fibroblastic skin tumour that invades locally and recurs, but seldom spreads.** Diagnosis is often late, so tumours are large; it grows as irregular, finger-like projections well beyond what is visible. [S1]
- **Incidence is under 1 per 100,000 a year; most patients are 20 to 59.** Survival is excellent (5-year DFSP-specific survival 99.2%). [S1]
- **It usually starts as a slow, painless, firm plaque of 2 to 5 cm,** most often on the trunk. A sudden growth spurt may mean fibrosarcomatous change. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Think of DFSP when a solitary 'keloid' or hypertrophic scar has no history of injury or surgery;** a small punch biopsy before any big excision avoids mistreatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fibrosarcomatous DFSP (10–15% of cases) is more aggressive,** with metastasis risk of 15–20%: stage with CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis plus nodal ultrasound. [S1]

## Diagnose

- **Confirm on histology before extensive surgery.** (A) [S1]
- **Take a deep incisional biopsy, ideally reported by a sarcoma or dermatopathology expert;** keep formalin fixation to 72 h or less so molecular tests remain possible. Diffuse strong CD34 staining is typical. [S1]
- **Use CD34 in every suspected case, and re-biopsy if suspicion stays high after a non-diagnostic sample.** [S1]
- **Reports must state whether high mitotic rate or fibrosarcomatous change is present.** (A) [S1]
- **Molecular testing (FISH for COL1A1::PDGFB, present in more than 90%) should be done where possible** (B); about 8% are FISH-negative and may need RNA sequencing or expert review. [S1]
- **Contrast MRI can map extent before surgery (C),** especially for tumours over 3 cm, head and neck sites, lesions near bone or joints, or recurrences. Staging scans are not routine except for suspected metastasis, recurrence or FS-DFSP (B). [S1]
- **Ultrasound (7.5–10 MHz) or MRI can assess extent before surgery;** with suspected recurrence or distant disease, add lymph node ultrasound and CT or MRI. [S2]

## Staging and prognosis

- **There is no definitive DFSP staging system.** NCCN uses stage I local, II regional and III distant disease; the 2018 German guideline uses stage I primary tumour, II lymph node metastasis and III distant metastasis. Some apply the AJCC soft-tissue sarcoma system. [S2]
- **Deaths are rare but cluster in large or high-grade tumours:** in 7567 SEER patients, 89 (1.18%) died of DFSP, with higher disease-specific mortality at 10 cm or more (7.07%) and high histologic grade (10.08%). [S2]
- **Proposed risk factors for recurrence and metastasis:** fibrosarcomatous change or high grade, older age, female sex, tumour of 10 cm or more, margins under 3 cm or positive, short time to recurrence, multiple recurrences, recent rapid growth and pain. [S2]

## Surgery

- **Surgery aiming at microscopically clear (R0) margins is first-line for resectable DFSP.** (A) [S1]
- **Micrographically controlled surgery is preferred** (B): Mohs or any technique that examines the whole peripheral and deep margin, usually on paraffin sections. Go down to deep fascia; 1–1.3 cm lateral margins then seem enough. [S1]
- **In comparative studies recurrence was 1.7% after Mohs versus 3.7% after wide excision (not significant),** and micrographic surgery spares more healthy tissue. [S1]
- **Without micrographic surgery, excise with 2–3 cm lateral margins and the underlying fascia;** re-excise invaded margins until clear where feasible. (B) [S1]
- **Margin width matters in the review's data:** 1 cm left microscopic tumour in 70.7% of cases, 2 cm in 39.7%, 3 cm in 15.5% and 5 cm in 5.2%, and a meta-analysis of eight studies found margins of 3 cm or more lowered local relapse compared with narrower margins. [S2]
- **S2 calls wide excision with at least 3 cm margins the gold standard for localised DFSP,** accepting functional or cosmetic cost, and says skin flaps should be considered when needed at any age. A systematic review gave only a weak recommendation for Mohs over wide excision; slow Mohs on paraffin sections appears to clear more completely. [S2]
- **Reconstruct simply (direct closure, graft or healing by secondary intention), not with flaps,** so recurrence is seen early. (B) [S1]
- **Resect recurrences with micrographically controlled surgery wherever possible.** (B) [S1]

## Imatinib and other systemic therapy

- ⚠️ **Imatinib is decided by a multidisciplinary tumour board,** and given before surgery for primary or recurrent tumours that cannot be removed or only with unacceptable harm; operate if the tumour becomes resectable. (B) [S1]
- **Start imatinib at 400 mg/day, increasing to 600–800 mg/day by response;** 400 and 800 mg gave the same response (67.5% vs 67.1%). [S1]
- **Imatinib's European approval covers inoperable primary, locally advanced or recurrent, and metastatic DFSP.** In fibrosarcomatous disease, confirm the fusion in the FS component (FISH or RT-PCR) before starting. [S1]
- **No chemotherapy regimen is established.** Advanced DFSP or FS-DFSP should go to an expert-centre tumour board, with trials where available. [S1]
- **Imatinib response in advanced DFSP (systematic review, 152 patients):** complete 5.2%, partial 55.2%, stable 27.6%, progression 9.2%. The review judged 400 mg a day more appropriate than 800 mg because of adverse effects. [S2]
- **Imatinib can make advanced tumours operable:** in one series seven of fifteen (47%) had residual disease resected and stayed disease-free; in a French series of 27 locally advanced tumours given imatinib or pazopanib for a median 7 months, 24 (89%) went on to excision and 23 (85%) were disease-free at over 5 years' median follow-up. [S2]

## Radiotherapy

- **Radiotherapy never replaces adequate surgery and is not given after clear (R0) excision of non-transformed DFSP** (B). It may be added for close margins, recurrence or aggressive subtypes, and as salvage for positive margins. [S1]
- **Curative doses are usually 60 Gy for microscopic and 70 Gy for macroscopic residual disease,** in conventional 2 Gy daily fractions. [S1]
- **Radiotherapy detail (S2):** treat the tumour and any scars with 3–5 cm safety margins, 2 Gy five times a week to 60–66 Gy for microscopic or 66–70 Gy for macroscopic residual tumour; 50 Gy may suffice for palliation. It may be a primary option when wide excision would cause major cosmetic or functional harm. [S2]
- **Adjuvant radiotherapy to the tumour bed after excision is not recommended:** a systematic review of 12 retrospective studies showed only a weak trend to fewer local recurrences. [S2]

## Fibrosarcomatous DFSP

- **FS-DFSP goes to a sarcoma tumour board; standard treatment is complete excision with margins of at least 2 cm.** Micrographic surgery can be considered but is unproven here. [S1]
- **S2 states Mohs surgery is not indicated when there is fibrosarcomatous transformation.** [S2]

## Follow-up

- **Recurrences come late (median 55 months).** After complete excision with low-risk pathology, examine the site every 6 months for 3 years, then every 12 months to year 5 (C). With high-risk features, add MRI 6-monthly for 2 years, then 6–12-monthly to year 5 and at least yearly to year 10. Teach self-examination. [S1]
- **Routine chest X-ray in follow-up was not useful for typical DFSP** (Dutch study), only for fibrosarcomatous tumours with their higher lung-metastasis risk. Re-biopsy anything suspicious. [S2]
