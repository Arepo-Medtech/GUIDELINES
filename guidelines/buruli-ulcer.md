# Buruli ulcer (Mycobacterium ulcerans infection)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, antibiotic treatment, surgery, paradoxical reactions, wound care and prevention of *M. ulcerans* skin infection in adults and children in Australia. ## Recognise

- **Suspect Buruli ulcer in a slowly progressive, classically painless nodule, plaque, swelling or ulcer on exposed skin (usually a limb)** in someone exposed to an endemic area; early lesions are often mistaken for insect bites. The incubation period averages about five months (1–9 months), so cases peak in winter. [S1]
- **Endemic areas are expanding:** the Mornington and Bellarine Peninsulas, Melbourne's inner north-west and Geelong, far-north Queensland near Cairns, and more recently Bateman's Bay in NSW. Victoria recorded 362 new cases in 2023. [S1]

## Diagnose

- **Diagnose with IS2404 PCR on a swab taken around the undermined ulcer edge.** A negative PCR does not exclude it, especially in early lesions and children: biopsy non-ulcerated or PCR-negative suspicious lesions (centre of a nodule, margin of an ulcer) for PCR and to rule out other diagnoses such as malignancy. [S1]
- **Culture is less sensitive than PCR and can take up to 12 weeks.** Without PCR, a probable diagnosis rests on a compatible lesion, acid-fast bacilli on microscopy and endemic exposure. [S1]

## Antibiotics

- ⚠️ **First line for most patients: eight weeks of oral combination therapy containing rifampicin** (high-quality evidence). Injectable regimens are no longer recommended; always use two drugs, never monotherapy. [S1]
- **Doses:** rifampicin 10 mg/kg a day (maximum 600 mg), plus one of clarithromycin 7.5 mg/kg twice a day (maximum 500 mg 12-hourly), moxifloxacin 400 mg daily or ciprofloxacin 500 mg 12-hourly. Choose the partner drug on comorbidities, interactions and tolerability; clarithromycin is the preferred macrolide. [S1]
- **If rifampicin cannot be used, give clarithromycin plus a fluoroquinolone for eight weeks. In pregnancy, use rifampicin with clarithromycin.** Rifampicin has been PBS-listed for Buruli ulcer since November 2020. [S1]
- **Six weeks may suffice** for immunocompetent adults with a small ulcer (under 3 cm) or severe adverse effects, particularly alongside surgery. Avoid shortening when several risk factors for failure are present (weight over 90 kg, immunocompromise, rifampicin–clarithromycin, male sex); rifampicin with a fluoroquinolone may then be preferred. [S1]
- **About 20% have drug toxicity** (higher over 65 years, in women and with renal impairment). Check FBE, liver and renal function at baseline; do a baseline ECG because clarithromycin and fluoroquinolones prolong QTc, repeating it at two weeks if at risk; renally adjust clarithromycin and ciprofloxacin. [S1]
- **Rifampicin interacts widely:** check every medicine, and advise alternative contraception for users of the oral pill or progestogen implant (a levonorgestrel IUD stays effective). [S1]
- **Warn patients that ulcers usually get bigger on treatment and will not be healed when antibiotics stop.** Median healing in Australia is 4.5 months: about three months under 2 cm, four months at 2–4 cm, six months over 4 cm. [S1]

## Surgery

- **Aggressive surgery is no longer recommended,** but conservative debridement, narrow excision (usually after four weeks of antibiotics, allowing a shorter course for small lesions) or curettage still have a role, planned jointly with infectious diseases. Delay grafts or flaps until antibiotics are finished and the wound is healed or nearly so. [S1]

## Paradoxical reactions

- **About one in five patients develop a paradoxical reaction** (worsening induration, ulceration, pain, discharge or a new lesion after initial improvement). First exclude poor adherence; true failure in adherent patients is rare. Usually continue the same antibiotics at the same dose and duration. [S1]
- ⚠️ **For severe, destructive reactions, give prednisolone 0.5–1.0 mg/kg daily, with a four-to-eight-week taper;** the antibiotic course can be lengthened to 12 weeks overall. [S1]

## Children

- **Children get oral rifampicin with clarithromycin at the adult dose and duration.** Alternatives used in paediatric centres: azithromycin 10 mg/kg (maximum 500 mg) daily, or ciprofloxacin 15 mg/kg (maximum 500 mg) twice daily. Liquid rifampicin is not PBS-subsidised for this indication. [S1]
- **Paradoxical reactions occur in 39% of children;** a severe reaction warrants daily prednisolone at 0.5 mg/kg, capped at 40 mg, weaned slowly over a month or more. [S1]

## Prevention

- **Advise mosquito-bite avoidance** (repellent, covering clothing, screens), gloves and long sleeves when gardening, prompt washing and covering of cuts, and minimal contact with native possums and their droppings. [S1]
