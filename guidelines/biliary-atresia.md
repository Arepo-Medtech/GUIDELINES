# Biliary atresia

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** screening, diagnosis, Kasai portoenterostomy (KPE), post-operative medical care, nutrition (including MCT-enriched and hydrolysed formulas), liver transplant timing, follow-up, cholangitis and portal hypertension in infants and children with biliary atresia. First assessment of the jaundiced infant: see `jaundice-in-early-infancy`. Other cholestatic causes: see `progressive-familial-intrahepatic-cholestasis`. PBS access (protein hydrolysate formula with MCT) is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ⚠️ International guideline (Chinese Medical Association paediatric hepatobiliary surgery section with international experts, GRADE and Delphi, 2025). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

## Screen

- **A stool colour card can help pick up biliary atresia early** (1B); screening within the first 30 days allows timely diagnosis. [S1]
- **Heel-prick direct bilirubin screening is promising:** a first sample within 60 hours is positive above 3.4 µmol/L, and a repeat within 2 weeks positive above 17.1 µmol/L, prompting further tests. [S1]

## Diagnose

- ⚠️ **Suspect biliary atresia when direct bilirubin is 17.1 µmol/L or more and over 20% of total bilirubin.** A GGT above 100 U/L raises the possibility; above 300 U/L makes it highly likely. [S1]
- **Ultrasound after birth is the first imaging test** (1A); look for the triangular cord sign, hilar fibrous plaque, absent common bile duct and gallbladder abnormalities. Shear-wave elastography also helps. [S1]
- **Surgical exploration with cholangiography confirms the diagnosis** and excludes a choledochal cyst: an atrophic gallbladder without bile confirms it; if bile is present, cholangiography is needed. [S1]
- **Rule out the look-alikes first:** PFIC, citrin deficiency, bile acid synthesis defects, antitrypsin deficiency (AATD), Alagille syndrome, choledochal cyst and idiopathic infantile cholestasis. [S1]

## Kasai portoenterostomy

- **Treat in reference centres** linked in national and international networks, with registries for long-term follow-up. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Give vitamin K 0.2-0.3 mg/kg per dose IV before surgery** to cut bleeding risk; cholestasis impairs absorption of fat-soluble vitamins. [S1]
- **KPE is not advised** with severe fibrosis or cirrhosis on 2D shear-wave elastography, with serious associated malformations such as major heart defects, or with liver failure and severe bleeding or clotting problems. [S1]
- **Open or laparoscopic KPE are both acceptable,** chosen by centre experience. A single redo KPE suits children who drained well and then abruptly stopped. [S1]

## After surgery

- **Steroids after KPE improve jaundice clearance but not proven native-liver survival,** and remain contested; high doses (methylprednisolone above 4 mg/kg/day) have impaired growth up to 6 years of age. [S1]
- **Ursodeoxycholic acid improves liver biochemistry** (2D), though dosing and stopping rules are empirical. [S1]
- **Antibiotic prophylaxis is advised despite weak evidence:** usually an IV third-generation cephalosporin (with or without a nitroimidazole) for at least 2 weeks, then alternating oral cephalosporin and sulfonamide courses for 3-6 months. [S1]
- **Treat CMV co-infection with antivirals;** it improves native-liver survival (2C). [S1]

## Nutrition

- ⚠️ **Add MCT to feeds after KPE** to support growth (2B), and replace fat-soluble vitamins as needed (1B). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Feeds need 80-100 kcal/100 mL** (fortified breast milk, high-energy formula, added carbohydrate or MCT). Without breast milk, a **hydrolysed formula with MCT** is preferred after surgery; use parenteral nutrition if tube feeding fails. [S1]
- **Aim for 130-150% of age-based protein needs (about 2-5 g/kg/day).** In cholestasis, choose a formula with MCT above 30% of fat but keep at least 20% of energy as long-chain fat to avoid essential fatty acid deficiency. [S1]

## Liver transplant

- ⚠️ **Refer for transplant assessment promptly if total bilirubin is above 100 µmol/L three months after KPE.** Other triggers: failed drainage, progressive cirrhosis, intractable malnutrition, recurrent cholangitis, or complications of portal hypertension. [S1]
- **Best transplant timing:** PELD 15-25 in children 12 or younger; MELD above 20 at 12 years and over. [S1]

## Follow up and complications

- **Follow up for life:** monthly in the first year after KPE, 3-monthly in the second, 6-monthly in the third, then in the fifth and tenth years; cholangitis, portal hypertension and cancer can appear after 20 years. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat cholangitis aggressively** (fever, loss of bile-coloured stools, irritability): IV third-generation cephalosporin, with or without metronidazole, escalating to a carbapenem if fever lasts beyond 24 hours. [S1]
- **Portal hypertension:** endoscopy is advised in the fourth year after KPE; after resuscitation for variceal bleeding, start endoscopic treatment straight away; screen with echocardiography for pulmonary hypertension. [S1]
- **Vaccinate on the routine schedule** unless contraindicated; defer during severe coagulopathy or progressing liver failure, give live vaccines one year after surgery, and complete vaccination before transplant. [S1]
