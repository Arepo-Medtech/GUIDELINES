# Idiopathic multicentric Castleman disease (iMCD)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, diagnosis, severity assessment, first-line siltuximab, response assessment and refractory treatment of idiopathic multicentric Castleman disease in adults. HHV8-associated and POEMS-associated MCD and unicentric CD are named only as differentials.

## Recognise

- **Lymphadenopathy is present in every case,** often with B symptoms (fever, weight loss, night sweats) in 52%–72%. Anasarca (oedema, ascites, pleural effusions) is common, at 78%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **TAFRO is the aggressive end:** thrombocytopenia, anasarca, fever, marrow reticulin fibrosis, renal impairment and organomegaly, often with rapid multi-organ failure. Its nodes are usually small (<1.5 cm), ALP is raised and there is no polyclonal hypergammaglobulinaemia. [S1]
- **Markers of severity** include interstitial pneumonitis, severe renal dysfunction and haemoglobin ≤80 g/L; ECOG status decides urgency and whether the patient needs hospital, often high-dependency care. [S1]
- **Suspected iMCD: refer to haematology,** then review the diagnosis and plan at a multidisciplinary team meeting once work-up is done. [S1]

## Diagnose

- **Major criteria:** lymphadenopathy in at least two nodal stations plus characteristic histology (regressed germinal centres and/or sheet-like polytypic plasmacytosis), best judged on an excisional biopsy. [S1]
- **HHV8 LANA-1 immunohistochemistry must be negative,** and infection, immune disease and malignancy must be excluded before the diagnosis is made. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not use fine-needle aspiration to diagnose iMCD;** core biopsy is generally inferior to open excision. [S1]
- **Work-up:** CT or PET/CT for nodes, organomegaly, effusions and pneumonitis; FBC, creatinine/GFR, LFTs (albumin), immunoglobulins, ESR and CRP; test for HHV-8, EBV and uncontrolled infection. Bone marrow biopsy looks for malignancy, POEMS and TAFRO reticulin fibrosis. [S1]
- **Main mimics:** POEMS (needs polyneuropathy and a monoclonal plasma cell disorder), SLE, Sjögren disease, RA, IgG4-related disease, sarcoidosis, Still disease, VEXAS and ANCA vasculitis. Autoantibodies occur in 30% of iMCD, so serology alone does not separate them. [S1]

## Treat

- **Grade severity by the CDCN criteria:** meeting at least two of the five means severe disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Siltuximab is first line in both severe and non-severe iMCD** and is the only PBS-reimbursed drug for it. Standard dose: 11 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks. (Level II, Grade B) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe disease: siltuximab plus high-dose steroid** (such as methylprednisolone 500 mg IV once a day for 3 days). Weekly siltuximab for 4 weeks has been suggested; critically unwell TAFRO patients may also get cyclosporin A. [S1]
- **Asymptomatic with normal organ function: no treatment,** but monitor, initially 6-weekly then 3-monthly, with history, exam, FBC, electrolytes, renal and liver tests and CRP. Treat promptly once symptoms or organ dysfunction appear. [S1]
- **Symptomatic non-severe disease:** siltuximab 11 mg/kg every 3 weeks with lower-dose steroid (for example prednisolone 1 mg/kg) for only 4–6 weeks while siltuximab takes effect; minimal symptoms can start siltuximab alone. [S1]
- **Responders stay on siltuximab indefinitely** (at least PR, until progression), with steroids weaned. Keep 3-weekly dosing where possible: 6-weekly extension lacks consensus. [S1]
- ⚠️ **IL-6 blockade can suppress fever with infection:** warn patients to watch for infection symptoms without fever. Monitor blood pressure regularly. [S1]
- **Tocilizumab (8 mg/kg every 2 weeks) is an alternative if siltuximab is unavailable,** but it is not PBS-funded for iMCD. [S1]
- **Steroids alone are not recommended:** they had the lowest response rate (2.8%). [S1]

## Monitor response

- **Fever and thrombocytopenia settle first,** often within a month on siltuximab; haemoglobin, CRP and albumin improve within 3 months; nodes can take 6–12 months. Weigh symptoms and organ recovery above nodal response. [S1]
- **Review clinically and biochemically weekly until improving, then each 3-weekly cycle;** CT for nodal response at 6 and 12 months. [S1]

## Refractory disease

- **Poor response to steroids plus IL-6 blockade should prompt a diagnostic review.** Up to half of patients do not respond adequately to first-line therapy. [S1]
- **Second line is rituximab-based** (Level IV, Grade C); chemotherapy such as CHOP or etoposide is reserved for severe, rapidly progressive disease. After chemoimmunotherapy that controls disease, continue siltuximab maintenance. [S1]
- **Third-line options:** thalidomide, anakinra, sirolimus or cyclosporin (the last especially in TAFRO); Level IV, Grade D. Prefer immunomodulatory therapy over chemotherapy where possible: toxicity is lower and efficacy similar. [S1]
