## Background

- **About 1/3 of children get constipated,** often when solids are introduced, during toilet training, and on starting school. [S1]
- **Constipation symptoms tend to go unreported.** [S1]
- **Most children open their bowels at least every 2-3 days;** a breastfed baby may go as seldom as once a week. [S1]
- **Infant dyschezia is not constipation:** healthy babies under 6 months may strain and cry before passing a soft stool. Unless the stool is also hard, it settles on its own. [S1]
- **The withholding cycle:** a young child who ignores the urge builds up large, hard stools; if passing them hurts, fear follows, then more holding on and more hard stool. [S1]
- **Children with autism spectrum disorder or ADHD are at higher risk** of functional constipation. [S1]

## Diagnosis

- **Diagnose on clinical grounds.** An internal (rectal) examination and an X-ray are unnecessary. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Talk to a senior clinician before doing any internal examination.** [S1]
- **Routine investigations are not needed.** [S1]
- **Rome IV functional constipation — 2 or more of these, for at least 1 month (infants) or 2 months (older children):** 2 or fewer stools a week; retentive posturing or excessive deliberate holding (withholding, incomplete emptying); painful or hard stools; stools of large diameter; a large faecal mass in the rectum. [S1]
- **Extra criterion once toilet-trained:** soiling or incontinence at least 1 time a week after toileting skills have been learned. [S1]
- **Examine** the lower back and spine for occult spinal dysraphism or tethered cord, and the lower limbs and gait neurologically. [S1]

## ⚠️ Red flags and organic causes

- ⚠️ **Red flags:** an infant under 6 weeks (discuss with a senior doctor) · ribbon-like stools (anorectal malformation) · meconium not passed in the first 24 hours, as most babies do (think anorectal malformation or Hirschsprung disease) · weight loss or poor growth · persistent vomiting · an abdominal mass that does not fit with a faecal mass. [S1]
- **Organic causes to keep in mind.** Medical: coeliac disease, hypothyroidism, hypercalcaemia, slow-transit constipation. Surgical: Hirschsprung disease, meconium ileus, anal malformations, spinal cord abnormalities. [S1]

## Behaviour and diet

- **Sitting position:** a footstool so the knees sit above the hips; lean forward with elbows resting on knees. [S1]
- **Toilet sits:** three times a day, ideally after meals, each up to 5 minutes. Get the child to push the tummy out, praise them for sitting, and keep toileting a positive experience. [S1]
- **Hold off toilet training until the child passes soft stools without pain.** [S1]
- **Check access to toilets,** for example what stops the child using the toilets at school. [S1]
- **Too much cow's milk can crowd out dietary fibre** and worsen constipation in some children. [S1]
- ⚠️ **More fibre alone does not treat constipation.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fluids above normal daily maintenance are not needed.** [S1]

## Medicines: principles

- **Aim for one soft, easily passed stool every day,** adjusting the dose to get there; medicines are often needed. [S1]
- **Osmotic and lubricant laxatives usually go on for months to years.** Tell parents this is safe and will not make the bowel 'lazy'. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stopping laxatives too soon is a common reason constipation comes back;** treatment usually lasts several months. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid rectal treatment** (suppositories, enemas). [S1]
- **First-line choice by age:** under 1 month, Coloxyl drops · 1-12 months, an iso-osmotic laxative (macrogol 3350 with electrolytes) or lactulose · older children, an iso-osmotic laxative or a lubricant (paraffin oil). [S1]
- **Inpatient disimpaction may help** children who withhold stool, have pain on defecation, or have rectal bleeding or fissures. [S1]
- **For pain from an anal fissure, apply petroleum jelly.** [S1]

## Maintenance doses (appendix table — confirm on the page)

- ⚠️ **Lactulose (osmotic):** 3-5 mL/day at 1-12 mo; 5-10 mL/day at 1-5 yo; 10-40 mL/day at 5-14 yo. Give larger doses in two (bd). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Coloxyl drops (poloxamer), three times a day:** 0.3 mL under 6 mo; 0.5 mL at 6-18 mo; 0.8 mL from 18 mo-3 yo. With Coloxyl+Senna, the senna is a stimulant — avoid it unless stools are already soft, and only over 2 yo. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Macrogol 3350 + electrolytes sachets, daily:** ½ at 1-12 mo; 1 at 1-6 yo (up to 2/day); 1-2 at 6-12 yo (up to 2/day); 1-4 over 12 yo. PBS listed, no authority needed. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Macrogol 3350 powder (tin with a two-ended scoop, large 17 g, small 8.5 g), daily:** 1 small scoop at 2-6 yo; 1 large scoop at 6-12 yo; 1-2 large scoops over 12 yo. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Paraffin oil (lubricant), per day:** 10-15 mL at 1-6 yo; 15-20 mL at 6-12 yo; up to 40 mL over 12 yo. Orange oily leakage into underwear means the dose should come down. **Avoid with swallowing difficulty (aspiration risk), especially under 6 mo.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stimulant (sodium picosulfate drops or bisacodyl), at night:** 6 mo-4 yo 0.25 mg/kg (up to 5 mg = 10 drops); 4-10 yo 5-10 drops; over 10 yo 10 drops or 1-2 tablets. Handy when a child cannot manage large liquid volumes. **Not if impacted; may cramp; not for long-term use.** [S1]

## Disimpaction

- **Severe constipation: clear the impaction first, then start maintenance.** Oral outpatient treatment works and is preferred; move straight to maintenance once disimpaction is done. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A glycerine suppository or Microlax enema is a one-off option only,** and sedation deserves strong consideration. [S1]
- **On day 4, review whether disimpaction still needs to continue.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Outpatient macrogol 3350 + electrolytes (adult sachets) per day, over days 1-7:** ages 1-6 years → 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4 · ages 6-12 years → 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 6, 6 · ages 12+ → 8 on each of days 1-3 only. [S1]
- **Sachet equivalence:** one adult-strength macrogol 3350 with electrolytes sachet equals 2 Movicol Junior sachets. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Outpatient OsmoLax small scoops (8.5 g) per day, over days 1-7:** ages 2-6 years → 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 6 · ages 6-12 years → 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 9, 9. [S1]

## Inpatient disimpaction

- ⚠️ **Nasogastric washout:** macrogol/electrolyte solution (Colonlytely, Glycoprep), 1-3 L/day at 25 mL/kg/hr, capped at 400 mL/hr (or lower, depending on the pump). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Give usual maintenance oral fluids as well:** washout solutions add no net fluid, so dehydration is a risk. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fixed-dose sodium picosulphate:** 4-9 yo, 1 sachet then ½ sachet as the second dose (15 mg/day); over 9 yo, 1 sachet twice daily (20 mg/day). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Not with signs of obstruction or with renal impairment.** Think about stopping once the child passes clear fluid rectally. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Oral medicines given during a washout, or in the hour before it, may be washed through unabsorbed.** [S1]

## Follow-up and consultation

- **See the GP or paediatrician again within 4 weeks.** Complex or difficult cases, or faecal or urinary incontinence, may warrant a continence or encopresis service. [S1]
- **Consider the local paediatric team when** red flags or suspected organic disease are present; constipation has not resolved after 6 months of optimised outpatient care; or outpatient disimpaction fails and inpatient care is needed. [S1]
