# Small cell lung cancer (limited and extensive stage)

## Recognise and diagnose

- **SCLC is about 15% of lung cancers; most present at extensive stage.** Smoking is the main risk factor, and 10–20% have brain metastases at diagnosis. [S1]
- **Diagnose on WHO criteria, preferring histology to cytology.** PD-L1 testing is not routinely recommended; no predictive biomarker is validated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Rule out neurological paraneoplastic syndromes before immunotherapy**, because immunotherapy can worsen them. [S1]

## Stage

- **Stage with contrast CT of chest and abdomen plus brain MRI** (CT if MRI is impossible). PET-CT is recommended in limited stage to plan thoracic radiotherapy. [S1]
- **Limited stage** is disease confined to one hemithorax and regional nodes that can be treated with definitive chemoradiation (stage I–III); **extensive stage** is disease beyond that (stage IV). [S1]

## Limited stage

- **Stage I–IIA (node-negative, under 5% of cases): lobectomy with lymph-node dissection after mediastinal staging, then adjuvant cisplatin–etoposide.** PCI is not recommended in this subgroup. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stage IIB–IIIC: concurrent chemoradiation followed by PCI.** Cisplatin–etoposide for up to four cycles; carboplatin only when cisplatin is contraindicated. Avoid dose reductions, especially in the first two cycles. [S1]
- **Thoracic radiotherapy: 45 Gy in 30 twice-daily fractions of 150 cGy over 3 weeks**; 60–70 Gy once daily if twice-daily is not possible. Start with chemotherapy cycle 1 or 2. [S1]
- **PCI 25 Gy in 10 daily fractions** for responders to chemoradiation with PS 0–1. Share the decision when PS is 2, age is over 70, or there is pre-existing neurological disease; hippocampal-avoidance PCI is a reasonable alternative. [S1]
- **Sequential chemoradiation** is an option when poor PS, comorbidity or disease volume rules out concurrent treatment; concurrent treatment can be standard for fit older patients. [S1]

## Extensive stage

- ⚠️ **First line: platinum–etoposide plus atezolizumab or durvalumab** for untreated patients with ECOG PS 0–1 and controlled brain metastases, including older patients; carboplatin is preferred over cisplatin in the elderly. [S1]
- **The trials gave four cycles of chemo-immunotherapy, then maintenance immunotherapy until progression.** Median survival rose to 12.3 months (atezolizumab) and 12.9 months (durvalumab). PS 2 or worse was not studied. [S1]
- **Consolidative thoracic radiotherapy (30 Gy in 10 fractions)** is an option for PS 0–2 patients with residual thoracic disease who respond to first-line treatment. [S1]
- **PCI in extensive stage is controversial;** MRI surveillance is an alternative, and is preferred in older patients because of neurocognitive risk. [S1]

## Relapse

- **Consider a clinical trial for every relapse.** Relapse more than 3 months after platinum: rechallenge with platinum–etoposide. Otherwise topotecan (oral or IV), or CAV. [S1]
- **PD-1 inhibitor monotherapy is not recommended for relapsed SCLC.** [S1]

## Supportive care and follow-up

- **Keep chemotherapy dose intensity;** G-CSF prophylaxis is safe in limited stage and an option in extensive stage. [S1]
- **Follow-up CT:** limited stage every 3 months for 2 years, 6-monthly in year 3, then yearly; extensive stage every 2–3 months. Without PCI, brain MRI every 3 months in the first year, then 6-monthly. [S1]
