# Small cell lung cancer (limited and extensive stage)

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, staging, chemoradiation, prophylactic cranial irradiation, first-line chemo-immunotherapy, relapse and follow-up for adults with small cell lung cancer. Non-small cell lung cancer: see `non-small-cell-lung-cancer` (EGFR-mutant disease only). Lung neuroendocrine tumours (carcinoids): see `neuroendocrine-neoplasms`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise and diagnose

- **SCLC is about 15% of lung cancers; most present at extensive stage.** Smoking is the main risk factor, and 10–20% have brain metastases at diagnosis. [S1]
- **Diagnose on WHO criteria, preferring histology to cytology.** PD-L1 testing is not routinely recommended; no predictive biomarker is validated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Rule out neurological paraneoplastic syndromes before immunotherapy**, because immunotherapy can worsen them. [S1]

## Stage

- **Stage with contrast CT of chest and abdomen plus brain MRI** (CT if MRI is impossible). PET-CT is recommended in limited stage to plan thoracic radiotherapy. [S1]
- **Limited stage** is disease confined to one hemithorax and regional nodes that can be treated with definitive chemoradiation (stage I–III); **extensive stage** is disease beyond that (stage IV). [S1]

## Limited stage

- **Stage I–IIA (node-negative, under 5% of cases): lobectomy with lymph-node dissection after mediastinal staging, then adjuvant cisplatin–etoposide.** PCI is not recommended in this subgroup. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stage IIB–IIIC: concurrent chemoradiation followed by PCI.** Cisplatin–etoposide for up to four cycles; carboplatin only when cisplatin is contraindicated. Avoid dose reductions, especially in the first two cycles. [S1]
- **Thoracic radiotherapy: 45 Gy in 30 twice-daily fractions of 150 cGy over 3 weeks**; 60–70 Gy once daily if twice-daily is not possible. Start with chemotherapy cycle 1 or 2. [S1]
- **PCI 25 Gy in 10 daily fractions** for responders to chemoradiation with PS 0–1. Share the decision when PS is 2, age is over 70, or there is pre-existing neurological disease; hippocampal-avoidance PCI is a reasonable alternative. [S1]
- **Sequential chemoradiation** is an option when poor PS, comorbidity or disease volume rules out concurrent treatment; concurrent treatment can be standard for fit older patients. [S1]
- ⚠️ **After chemoradiation: consolidation durvalumab.** For stage I–III limited-stage SCLC that has not progressed after platinum-based chemoradiation, with ECOG 0 or 1, eviQ gives durvalumab 1,500 mg IV on day 1 of each 28-day cycle for up to 26 cycles (or until progression or unacceptable toxicity). Begin the first cycle no later than 42 days after chemoradiation ends. [S2]
- **ADRIATIC evidence:** consolidation durvalumab lengthened median overall survival to 55.9 from 33.4 months and median progression-free survival to 16.6 from 9.2 months, compared with placebo. The trial gave it for 24 months; PCI was allowed before the trial drug. [S2]
- **Toxicity was close to placebo:** grade 3 or worse treatment-related events 24.4% with durvalumab and 24.2% with placebo; stopping for toxicity 16.4% and 10.6%; grade 3–4 pneumonitis or radiation pneumonitis 3.1% and 2.6%. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Watch the lungs:** after chemoradiation, severe and even fatal radiation pneumonitis or immune pneumonitis can occur, so ask about breathlessness, cough and fever and look for new radiological change. Seek expert input first if there is grade 2 or worse pneumonitis, significant autoimmune disease, an organ transplant or past viral hepatitis. [S2]
- **Monitoring on checkpoint inhibitors (eviQ):** at baseline FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs, cortisol, thyroid function and glucose, with a hepatitis screen; before every cycle FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs and glucose; thyroid function 4–6-weekly while on treatment, then 12-weekly afterwards as indicated. Consider a baseline ECG and troponin. [S2]

## Extensive stage

- ⚠️ **First line: platinum–etoposide plus atezolizumab or durvalumab** for untreated patients with ECOG PS 0–1 and controlled brain metastases, including older patients; carboplatin is preferred over cisplatin in the elderly. [S1]
- **The trials gave four cycles of chemo-immunotherapy, then maintenance immunotherapy until progression.** Median survival rose to 12.3 months (atezolizumab) and 12.9 months (durvalumab). PS 2 or worse was not studied. [S1]
- **Consolidative thoracic radiotherapy (30 Gy in 10 fractions)** is an option for PS 0–2 patients with residual thoracic disease who respond to first-line treatment. [S1]
- **PCI in extensive stage is controversial;** MRI surveillance is an alternative, and is preferred in older patients because of neurocognitive risk. [S1]

## Relapse

- **Consider a clinical trial for every relapse.** Relapse more than 3 months after platinum: rechallenge with platinum–etoposide. Otherwise topotecan (oral or IV), or CAV. [S1]
- **PD-1 inhibitor monotherapy is not recommended for relapsed SCLC.** [S1]
- **Tarlatamab (a bispecific antibody) for extensive-stage SCLC progressing during or after platinum chemotherapy:** step-up dosing in cycle 1 (1 mg IV on day 1, then 10 mg on days 8 and 15), then 10 mg on days 1 and 15 of each 28-day cycle, continued until progression or unacceptable toxicity. eviQ records it as provisionally TGA-approved and not PBS-listed. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Cytokine release syndrome and neurotoxicity (ICANS) can be life-threatening.** Give tarlatamab only where both can be managed, with tocilizumab on hand (and more doses obtainable within 8 hours), and not during active infection or inflammatory disease. Premedicate with dexamethasone 8 mg IV before the first two doses. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Observe after the first two doses:** 16 hours of CRS/ICANS monitoring from the infusion start in a suitable health service, then staying within an hour of it, with a carer, until 24 hours; after the day 15 dose, 6–8 hours. Teach every patient the warning signs and to seek help at once. [S2]
- **DeLLphi-304 (tarlatamab versus chemotherapy, mostly topotecan, after first-line platinum):** median overall survival 13.6 v 8.3 months (HR 0.60), response 35% v 20%. CRS occurred in 56%, mostly grade 1–2 (grade 3 in 1%), and ICANS in 6%. [S2]

## Supportive care and follow-up

- **Keep chemotherapy dose intensity;** G-CSF prophylaxis is safe in limited stage and an option in extensive stage. [S1]
- **Follow-up CT:** limited stage every 3 months for 2 years, 6-monthly in year 3, then yearly; extensive stage every 2–3 months. Without PCI, brain MRI every 3 months in the first year, then 6-monthly. [S1]
