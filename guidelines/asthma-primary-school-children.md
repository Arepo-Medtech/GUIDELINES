# Asthma in primary school-aged children (6–11 years)

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment and stepped maintenance treatment of asthma in children aged 6–11 years. Acute exacerbations: `acute-asthma-children`. Adult and adolescent counterpart: `asthma` (12 years and over). Under 6: `preschool-asthma`.

> ✅ **PARAPHRASED, HASH-ANCHORED.** 22 of 22 claims paraphrased from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Asthma in primary school-aged children (6-11 years)*** (Last updated June 2023); **29 anchors re-checkable by machine; 6 doses.** The RCH text is not reproduced: its terms licence personal use only.

> ⚠️ **SABA alone only for mild, infrequent symptoms with no risk factors** — more than 3 MDI canisters a year with little preventer use is linked to severe exacerbations and death. **Low-dose ICS is first line.**

## Assess

- ⚠️ **SABA-only treatment is for mild, infrequent symptoms with no risk factors.** Frequent SABA alone (over 3 MDI canisters a year) with little preventer is associated with more severe exacerbations and death. [S1]
- **Diagnose on symptoms and treatment response** (clusters responding to SABA, or chronic symptoms responding to ICS) without red flags. **Red flags for another diagnosis:** productive or isolated cough, paraesthesia, chest pain, clubbing. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Exacerbation risk:** past intubation or ICU · 2 or more hospital presentations · SABA more than twice weekly or over 3 canisters a year · poor preventer adherence · poor technique · smoke/vaping exposure · obesity, sleep apnoea, confirmed food allergy, chronic rhinosinusitis. [S1]
- **Good control:** daytime symptoms and reliever use less than two times a week, no activity limitation, night waking less than two times a month, no systemic steroid, ED visit or admission. [S1]
- **Look for contributors:** allergic rhinitis, obesity, sleep apnoea, reflux, dysfunctional breathing, depression/anxiety, smoking and vaping, damp or crowded housing. [S1]
- **No investigations unless considering another diagnosis**; spirometry may help if diagnosis or severity is uncertain. **No allergy testing without a history of immediate reaction** to the allergen. [S1]

## Reliever

- **Every child gets salbutamol (100 microg MDI) with a spacer: 6–12 puffs**, spacer ± mask, carried at all times. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Count canisters at each review — more than 3 a year is high risk.** [S1]

## Stepped maintenance

- **Step 1 — SABA only**, if no exacerbation treatment (systemic steroid or hospital) in 12 months, infrequent symptoms and no contributing factors. **Most other children need a preventer.** [S1]
- **Step 2 — low-dose ICS via spacer: fluticasone 50 microg twice daily or ciclesonide 80 microg once daily.** Ciclesonide: once daily, fewer side effects, PBS subsidised (not all spacers are compatible). [S1]
- **Montelukast 5 mg once daily** is an alternative first-line preventer or ICS add-on — for steroid concerns or real difficulty with MDI + spacer. [S1]
- ⚠️ **1 in 6 children on montelukast get agitation, sleep disturbance or mood change — stop it and see if they resolve.** [S1]
- **Step 3 — ICS at moderate dose (ciclesonide 160 microg once daily or fluticasone 100 microg twice daily), OR montelukast plus low-dose ICS, OR ICS/LABA:** budesonide/formoterol twice daily — MDI 100/3 microg, 2 puffs; or DPI 200/6 microg, one inhalation. [S1]
- **Step 4 — montelukast added to moderate-dose ICS or to ICS/LABA, OR referral for expert advice.** [S1]

## Step up and down

- **Control:** good = all of daytime symptoms and reliever use ≤2 days a week, no activity limitation, no night symptoms; partial = one or two failing; poor = three or more. Pre-exercise reliever doses don't count. [S1]
- **Begin on Step 1; if needed, move up a step after 6 weeks — but always check adherence and technique first**, and consider other diagnoses. [S1]
- **About 3 months of good control → consider stepping down.** Keep the lowest effective treatment; **cease only after 6 months symptom-free**, reviewing 4–6 weeks after each step down. [S1]
- **Nearing adolescence (10–11 years) with poor control: consider ICS-formoterol MART** — budesonide/formoterol DPI 200/6 microg (preferred with adequate technique) or MDI 100/3 microg; may be off-label or private script. [S1]
- **Refer** if control is inadequate at Step 4 or the diagnosis is uncertain. [S1]

## Education, devices and exacerbations

- **Annual influenza vaccine; check adherence every visit; a written asthma action plan for home and childcare.** [S1]
- **Many children can use a DPI from about age 6** — MDIs have a large carbon footprint; good control and switching to DPI reduce it. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not increase the ICS dose during an exacerbation** in a child already on daily ICS. [S1]
