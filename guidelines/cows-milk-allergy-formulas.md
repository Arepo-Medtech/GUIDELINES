# Milk substitutes and specialised formula in cow's milk allergy

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** choosing and substituting specialised infant formula and plant-based milks for infants and young children with cow's milk allergy (CMA). Diagnosis and acute care: see `non-ige-food-allergy-children` (proctocolitis, enteropathy, FPIES) and `ige-mediated-food-allergy-children`. PBS access for each formula is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Before formula

- **Breastfeeding comes first** because of its benefits for mother and baby; this guidance is for when it is not possible. [S1]
- **A breastfeeding mother does not necessarily need to cut out cow's milk.** Discuss it with a medical specialist, and have a dietitian supervise any exclusion. [S1]
- **Eczema on its own does not justify a specialised infant formula.** [S1]

## Which formulas

- 🚩 **Do not use these for CMA:** partially hydrolysed or A2 formula, any standard formula (anti-reflux, lactose-free, organic, newborn or follow-on types included), or goat and other animal milks. Unlisted plant or vegan formulas lack hypoallergenicity and growth data. [S1]
- **Rice-protein formula is an option unless the child is allergic to rice,** but evidence for it in non-IgE food allergy is limited. Of the two in Australia, only Novalac Allergy has product-specific hypoallergenicity and growth studies; Alula Allergy is batch-tested for milk and soy contamination. [S1]
- **Use amino acid formula (AAF) or extensively hydrolysed formula (eHF) when growth is poor or there are several non-IgE food allergies.** [S1]

## Substituting when supply fails

- **If the usual formula is unavailable, swap within the same group** (one eHF for another eHF), after reviewing why it was first chosen and any change in history. [S1]
- **A thriving child over 6 months** whose earlier reaction to cow's milk formula was mild or moderate, and who was put on eHF before 6 months, can be switched to soy formula. [S1]
- **If AAF for over-12-month-olds is needed but unavailable, involve a paediatric allergy dietitian** to adapt a substitute recipe. With any formula change, go over the mixing instructions with the family, since scoop-to-water ratios differ a lot. [S1]

## Milks after 12 months

- **Over 12 months on soy formula:** calcium-fortified soy milk can take its place, provided growth is good and the child eats a broad range of family foods. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Allergic to both cow's milk and soy:** most plant milks without calcium fortification (such as rice or soy milk) have too little protein, fat and calcium for young children unless growth and nutrition are carefully checked. [S1]
- **Up to four years of age, 400-500 mL a day of calcium-fortified plant milk meets calcium needs;** drinking more can blunt appetite, intake and growth. [S1]
- **Every infant or child with CMA should see a paediatric allergy dietitian** for a nutrition assessment. [S1]
