# Hereditary angioedema in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition of hereditary angioedema (HAE, C1-esterase inhibitor deficiency), acute attack treatment with C1-INH concentrate or icatibant, airway, peripheral and abdominal attacks, disposition, short- and long-term prophylaxis and diagnostic testing in children. No adult counterpart exists in the corpus. Related: `anaphylaxis-children`, `urticaria-children`.

> ⚠️ **HAE angioedema does not respond to — and is not treated with — adrenaline, antihistamines or corticosteroids.** Airway attack = emergency: C1-INH concentrate 20 international units/kg IV or icatibant SC by weight, and senior/ICU/anaesthetic involvement.

---

## Key points

- **HAE angioedema is non-pitting swelling without urticaria, itching or redness.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **At any age, laryngeal oedema may threaten life.** [S1]
- **Each child with an HAE diagnosis needs their own ASCIA HAE management plan**, setting out when and how attacks are treated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Adrenaline, corticosteroids and antihistamines play no part in treating HAE angioedema.** Early specific treatment improves outcomes. [S1]

## Background

- HAE is autosomal dominant: **Type 1** is an absolute C1-INH deficiency (85% of cases), **Type 2** a functional one. [S1]
- **Presentations:** cutaneous swelling in over 90% — face, genitals and limbs (hands and feet above all) · upper airway swelling (tongue, soft palate, larynx), which can be life-threatening · abdominal pain with nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea, which can cause dehydration and collapse · a non-pruritic erythema marginatum rash in about one third. [S1]
- **An attack usually peaks by 24 hours and settles over 48–72 hours.** [S1]
- **Triggers:** injury (dental or other surgery included), psychological stress, infection, medications (ACE inhibitors and oestrogen-containing ones especially), menstruation, vigorous exercise, certain foods — or none. [S1]
- Symptoms typically start between 5 and 11 years (40% by age 5) and may worsen in adolescence. **A family history is present in 75%.** [S1]

## Assessment

- ⚠️ **Examine the airway:** tongue, soft palate, uvula; laryngeal oedema signs — stridor, hoarseness, drooling, altered voice. **Swelling behind the teeth progresses to airway obstruction more readily than swelling in front of them.** [S1]
- **Redness or itching suggests a diagnosis other than HAE.** Differentials: allergic reactions and anaphylaxis, idiopathic, drug-induced and (exceedingly rare in childhood) acquired angioedema. [S1]
- **Suspected acute attack with no prior HAE diagnosis: check C4.** [S1]

## Acute attack treatment

- Whether to treat an attack that does not threaten life is for the child and parent to decide. **Options: subcutaneous icatibant, or intravenous C1 esterase inhibitor concentrate.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **First line — C1-INH concentrate (Berinert®), any age: 20 international units/kg (round to the nearest 500 units), given as a slow IV injection at 4 mL/min.** Symptoms usually stabilise in 30 minutes; a second dose is seldom required but can follow 30 min to 2 hours after the first (<1% relapse). Do not shake the solution. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Icatibant, slow SC injection:** under 12 kg or under 2 years — seek Immunology advice; **10 mg (1 mL) for 12–25 kg · 15 mg (1.5 mL) for 26–40 kg · 20 mg (2 mL) for 41–50 kg · 30 mg (3 mL) over 50 kg**. A repeat dose is possible 6 hours after the first (<10% relapse); **max 3 doses/24 hours**. Injection-site reactions are usually mild. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Airway attack unresponsive to icatibant: give C1-INH concentrate as rescue.** [S1]
- **No response after 2 hours: reconsider the diagnosis** and look for other causes. [S1]
- **Second line, used only when neither of the above is available: FFP treated with solvent/detergent**, for severe abdominal and/or laryngeal attacks — it may paradoxically worsen angioedema. Consult immunology/haematology, but don't delay in an emergency with no alternative. Adverse effects: commonly nausea, pruritus, rash; rarely allergic reactions, bronchospasm, cardiorespiratory collapse. [S1]

## Special clinical features

- ⚠️ **An airway/laryngeal attack is a medical emergency: give icatibant or C1-INH concentrate at once.** If obstruction appears imminent → senior, ICU and anaesthetic involvement, ideally including people skilled in surgical airway and fibre-optic-guided intubation. [S1]
- **Peripheral swelling:** treat significant pain or disability, or angioedema of the face or genitals; mild swelling that doesn't affect function may not need treatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Abdominal attack with moderate–severe pain, vomiting and/or distension: give C1-INH concentrate**; consider fluid resuscitation if severe. Mild pain: simple analgesia including NSAIDs. [S1]

## Consultation, transfer and discharge

- **Consult the paediatric team:** a first swelling episode or no prior diagnosis · airway/laryngeal attack · medication not working within 2 hours · relapse after first-line treatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Transfer:** airway/laryngeal attack (usually needs PICU/HDU observation) · immediately life-threatening situation · care beyond the hospital's comfort level · inadequate response to acute treatment. [S1]
- **Discharge:** after successful emergency treatment, children improving from peripheral or abdominal attacks may go home even if peripheral swelling has not fully resolved. **Every child diagnosed with HAE needs an allergy/immunology consultation.** [S1]

## Prophylaxis and diagnosis

- **Short-term prophylaxis — first line, at any age: C1-INH concentrate (Berinert® IV) 20 units/kg rounded to the nearest 500 units, 1–6 hours before any medical, surgical or dental procedure.** [S1]
- **Second line, used only when first-line prophylaxis cannot be obtained: icatibant slow SC**, 2 years and over (under 2: seek Immunology advice), under 30 minutes before the procedure; keep an additional dose for emergency and **observe for over 12 hours** afterwards. Doses: seek Immunology advice under 12 kg; 10 mg (1 mL) for 12–25 kg; 15 mg (1.5 mL) for 26–40 kg; 20 mg (2 mL) for 41–50 kg; 30 mg (3 mL) over 50 kg. [S1]
- **Long-term prophylaxis** is prescribed by the child's immunologist: lanadelumab, garadacimab, oral tranexamic acid, or C1-INH concentrate given subcutaneously (Berinert® SC). [S1]
- **Non-acute screening, with an immunologist:** serum C4, C1-INH level and C1 functional assay; **two sets at least 3 months apart**, with at least 1 set after the age of 1 (results are hard to interpret under 1 year). Genetic testing is not usually needed. **Screen the family if confirmed.** [S1]
- **Type 1:** C1-INH function, C1-INH protein and C4 all low. **Type 2:** C1-INH function low, C1-INH protein normal or raised, C4 low. [S1]
