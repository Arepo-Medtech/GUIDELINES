# Peroxisome biogenesis disorders in the Zellweger spectrum (PBD-ZSD)

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** definition, laboratory and genetic diagnosis, and multisystem supportive management (feeding and nutrition, fat-soluble vitamins, liver, hearing, vision, seizures, bone, teeth, adrenal and kidney) of peroxisome biogenesis disorders in the Zellweger spectrum (PBD-ZSD). Rhizomelic chondrodysplasia punctata type 1 is excluded by the source. First-line metabolic samples in an unwell child: see `metabolic-disorders-children`. PBS access (arachidonic acid and DHA with carbohydrate) is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 33 claims; **69 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 1 doses.** **S1** Braverman NE (published online 23 December 2015 (issue March 2016); international): 20 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored · **S2** Klouwer FCC (published 1 December 2015; international): 13 claims, quoted

> 🌐 **Two sources.** S1 (US consensus treatment guidelines, 2016) is the main source. **S2** (Klouwer et al., Amsterdam, 2015) is a narrative clinical overview; it adds presentation by age, prognosis, citrate for kidney stones and when to treat adrenal insufficiency. Where they differ, both are shown.

> ⚠️ International guideline (US expert consensus treatment guidelines, 2016; more than 10 years old). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **The PBS product and the guideline disagree.** The only PBS listing for this condition is an **arachidonic acid and DHA** supplement. The guideline says DHA supplementation was once recommended but a **placebo-controlled trial showed no clinical benefit**; it recommends fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) instead.

## What it is

- **PBDs are autosomal recessive defects in building and running peroxisomes,** affecting an estimated 1 in 50,000 births in North America; PEX1 variants cause nearly 70% of PBD-ZSD. [S1]
- **Call it PBD-ZSD, graded severe, intermediate or mild,** rather than the old labels Zellweger syndrome, neonatal adrenoleukodystrophy and infantile Refsum disease, since patients sit on one continuum. [S1]
- **Severe disease presents in the newborn** with brain, kidney and skeletal malformations, a typical facial appearance, seizures and profound hypotonia; some milder patients show only peripheral neuropathy or cerebellar ataxia. [S1]
- **Why each defect matters (S2 Table 1):** VLCFA build-up damages brain, nerves and adrenals, and the DHA shortfall affects brain and vision; phytanic acid causes retinal, brain and nerve damage; DHCA and THCA are toxic to the liver and probably the brain; plasmalogen deficiency underlies growth and psychomotor delay, cataract and bone anomalies; glyoxylate turned to oxalate forms calcium oxalate kidney stones; raised pipecolic acid has no known clinical effect. [S2]
- **S2 groups patients by age at presentation: neonatal-infantile, childhood and adolescent-adult (late).** Heimler syndrome was recently recognised as a peroxisome biogenesis disorder and added to the (very) mild end of the spectrum. [S2]
- **Neonatal-infantile presentation:** hepatic dysfunction and profound hypotonia, causing prolonged jaundice and feeding difficulties; seizures are usual. Calcific stippling (chondrodysplasia punctata) may be present, especially in the knees and hips. [S2]
- **Childhood presentation:** usually delayed milestones, retinitis pigmentosa, cataract or glaucoma, sensorineural deafness (almost always present) and hepatic dysfunction with coagulopathy. Kidney oxalate stones and adrenal insufficiency may develop. Early-onset progressive leukodystrophy can take away acquired skills, but no rapid late-onset white matter disease after the age of five has been reported. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Adolescent-adult presentation** can be diagnosed in late childhood or adulthood: eye and hearing problems are the most consistent features, dysmorphism may be absent and intelligence can be normal. Primary adrenal insufficiency is common and probably under-diagnosed. Peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar ataxia and pyramidal signs are usual; the course is slowly progressive. [S2]

## Diagnose

- **Start with fasting plasma very-long-chain fatty acids (VLCFA);** repeat after an overnight fast if the result is equivocal. A ketogenic diet can give a false positive. [S1]
- **Show that several peroxisomal pathways are abnormal:** phytanic and pristanic acids, red-cell plasmalogens, pipecolic acid and the bile acid intermediates DHCA and THCA. Do not rely on VLCFA alone, since some PEX variants leave it near normal. [S1]
- **Test menu and expected findings (S1 Table 2):** in plasma and fibroblasts, raised VLCFA, pristanic and phytanic acids with poor oxidation of each in cells; low red-cell plasmalogens and poor plasmalogen synthesis in fibroblasts; in plasma or urine, build-up of the C27 bile acids DHCA and THCA and raised pipecolic acid; and in fibroblasts, catalase sitting in the cytosol instead of the peroxisome. [S1]
- **About 10-15% of suspected cases with raised VLCFA have a single-enzyme defect** (ACOX1 or D-bifunctional protein) instead; skin fibroblast studies help separate them. [S1]
- **Confirm with a PEX gene panel;** the genotype helps prognosis (two null alleles usually mean severe disease; PEX1 p.G843D usually milder) and identifies carriers for counselling. [S1]
- **S2's first-line tests** also include C26:0-lysoPC in dried blood spots and urine bile acids and oxalic acid. Mildly affected patients may have (near) normal blood and urine tests: if suspicion stays high, test fibroblasts, including culture at 40 °C. Confirm by mutation analysis in all patients; biochemical testing is still needed to confirm that a sequenced variant is pathogenic. [S2]
- **Newborn screening for X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy** (C26:0-lysoPC in dried blood spots) will also pick up ZSD at birth, but severity and progression are hard to predict for an individual child. [S2]
- **Offer genetic counselling to parents.** Carriers can have prenatal or preimplantation genetic diagnosis once the family's PEX variants are known; otherwise biochemical prenatal testing on chorionic villi or amniocytes is possible if the index patient's cells were clearly abnormal. [S2]

## Supportive care: severe disease, feeding and nutrition

- **Assess every organ system at diagnosis** and re-evaluate at least yearly to catch progression early. [S1]
- **In severe PBD-ZSD, care centres on seizures, feeding and breathing:** any standard antiepileptic can be used (avoid respiratory depressants if breathing is compromised); a gastrostomy may be needed; discuss escalation of respiratory support openly with the family. [S1]
- **Feeding:** selective eating often needs a behavioural feeding programme; a gastrostomy may be needed for calories, and elemental formulas may be tolerated better because bile acid deficiency causes malabsorption. [S1]
- **No specific diet is recommended.** Cutting dietary VLCFA has not been shown to help, and Lorenzo's oil may be harmful. Monitor plasma phytanic acid and restrict it in the diet only if levels become excessive. [S1]
- ⚠️ **DHA supplements showed no clinical benefit in a placebo-controlled trial,** although DHA synthesis is impaired; give supplements of vitamins A, D, E and K instead, because bile acid synthesis is defective. [S1]

## Supportive care: organ by organ

- ⚠️ **Liver:** give vitamin K 2.5-5 mg daily and monitor clotting and synthetic function; primary bile acid therapy (cholic acid) may reduce toxic intermediates, but evidence is limited. Refer overt liver dysfunction to gastroenterology. [S1]
- **Hearing:** test every year; fit hearing aids for significant loss, and consider cochlear implants when aids are not enough. [S1]
- **Vision:** regular eye reviews, early removal of any cataract, and glasses for refractive error; repeated ERGs add nothing, while OCT can track the retina. Link deaf-blind children with deaf-blind services. [S1]
- **Neurology:** EEG when seizures change; levetiracetam, phenobarbital, clonazepam, topiramate and lamotrigine are commonly used. Get a baseline brain MRI for leukodystrophy, and early physical, occupational and speech therapy for every child. [S1]
- **Bone and teeth:** DXA and vitamin D status if older than 1 year and non-weight-bearing or after fractures; bisphosphonates only with a metabolic bone specialist. Dental review every 6 months for enamel defects. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Adrenal:** from one year of age check ACTH and morning cortisol at least yearly; replace at standard doses if abnormal, and consider stress dosing in severe illness, fever or major surgery. [S1]
- ⚠️ **S2: give cortisone only for true adrenal insufficiency (an abnormal Synacthen test),** because it can cause severe side effects such as growth suppression and osteoporosis. [S2]
- **Kidney and vaccines:** screen older children (from about 4-6 years) for hyperoxaluria with urine oxalate and creatinine, and ultrasound for stones; give routine vaccines plus yearly influenza and RSV vaccination. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Kidney stones are common: 83% in a large Dutch cohort.** S2 screens urine oxalic acid every year, starts oral citrate for hyperoxaluria to prevent stones, and advises enough fluid. [S2]
- **Restrict dietary phytanic acid only when levels are extremely high,** not when moderately raised, since adequate calories matter more. Review eyes and hearing every year, and have a dentist follow the enamel hypoplasia present in nearly all patients. [S2]
- **Unproven treatments (S2):** there is no reason to expect benefit from Lorenzo's oil; evidence is insufficient that cholic acid helps, despite its FDA approval; plasmalogen precursors (batyl alcohol) have never been studied systematically. Liver or hepatocyte transplantation has been reported in single patients only, without long-term outcomes. [S2]
- **Prognosis:** severe neonatal-infantile disease usually means death within the first year. Childhood or adolescent presentations usually do better, but progressive liver disease or leukodystrophy carries a poor outlook. Milder patients can reach adulthood; later progression is mainly neuropathy and pyramidal signs, with stable cognition. [S2]
