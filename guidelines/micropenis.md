# Micropenis in infants and children: diagnosis, evaluation and testosterone treatment

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** boys from infancy to adolescence with a small but structurally normal penis: measuring stretched penile length, looking for an underlying endocrine cause, testosterone treatment and its timing, expectant management and the limited place of surgery. Pubertal delay and congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (CHH): see `disorders-of-puberty` and `constitutional-delay-of-growth-and-puberty`.

## Recognise

- **Micropenis is a structurally normal penis with a stretched penile length (SPL) 2.5 SD or more below the mean** for age and stage of sexual development. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat it as a sign, not just a finding:** in infancy or childhood it often points to a significant congenital or acquired endocrine disorder. [S1]
- **Congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (CHH) is the commonest known cause.** Syndromes include Prader-Willi, Kallmann and Klinefelter. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A full medical evaluation is essential**, because associated conditions can be life-threatening, e.g. hypoglycaemia from panhypopituitarism. [S1]
- **It usually reflects too little androgen during a critical window:** from 12 weeks' gestation through the postnatal 'mini-puberty' of the first six months. [S1]

## Measure

- **Diagnosis is clinical and depends on accurate SPL measurement.** Use a standard technique (SPLINT): supine, foreskin retracted if possible, rigid ruler pressed into the suprapubic fat, penis stretched to resistance, and at least two (preferably three) readings. [S1]
- **Penile size varies by ethnicity and region, so use the most relevant, current, population-specific nomogram**; local reference data are best. [S1]

## Treat

- **First steps: counselling, investigation of the endocrine cause, and hormone therapy** aiming for a length within the normal range for age. [S1]
- **Hormone therapy is the most accepted and effective treatment, especially in CHH,** though some boys, particularly with severe hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, do not reach a normal adult size. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Give testosterone early: infancy or early childhood, including mini-puberty, gives the best growth.** Earlier start means more growth; initial glans width is a strong predictor of response. [S1]
- **Monitor for premature virilisation and high serum testosterone, particularly with topical preparations.** [S1]
- **The evidence is thin:** there are no large, long-term placebo-controlled trials and no evidence-based guideline on testosterone for micropenis. Short-term results are favourable; long-term data are limited. [S1]
- **Expectant management may suit selected boys**, since many untreated patients normalise at puberty. It must be individualised, with structured clinical and psychosocial follow-up; the supporting studies had high drop-out. [S1]
- **Surgery is not first line.** Keep it for failed hormone therapy or anatomical abnormality; paediatric outcomes are poorly documented and the risks significant. [S1]
