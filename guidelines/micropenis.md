# Micropenis in infants and children: diagnosis, evaluation and testosterone treatment

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** boys from infancy to adolescence with a small but structurally normal penis: measuring stretched penile length, looking for an underlying endocrine cause, testosterone treatment and its timing, expectant management and the limited place of surgery. Pubertal delay and congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (CHH): see `disorders-of-puberty` and `constitutional-delay-of-growth-and-puberty`.

## Recognise

- **Micropenis is a structurally normal penis with a stretched penile length (SPL) 2.5 SD or more below the mean** for age and stage of sexual development. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat it as a sign, not just a finding:** in infancy or childhood it often points to a significant congenital or acquired endocrine disorder. [S1]
- **Congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (CHH) is the commonest known cause.** Syndromes include Prader-Willi, Kallmann and Klinefelter. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A full medical evaluation is essential**, because associated conditions can be life-threatening, e.g. hypoglycaemia from panhypopituitarism. [S1]
- **It usually reflects too little androgen during a critical window:** from 12 weeks' gestation through the postnatal 'mini-puberty' of the first six months. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Refer every infant with congenital micropenis for complete clinical and endocrine evaluation by a specialised multidisciplinary team** to exclude hormonal or syndromic causes (SIAMS/SIEDP R3.1: 1, ⊕⊕⊕⊕). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Time the hormone tests: in the first 2 days of life or in the minipuberty window (3 to 6 months),** measure testosterone, LH, FSH, inhibin B and AMH (R3.2: 1, ⊕⊕⊕○). This is the only window in which the physiological rise can be seen. [S2]

## Measure

- **Diagnosis is clinical and depends on accurate SPL measurement.** Use a standard technique (SPLINT): supine, foreskin retracted if possible, rigid ruler pressed into the suprapubic fat, penis stretched to resistance, and at least two (preferably three) readings. [S1]
- **Penile size varies by ethnicity and region, so use the most relevant, current, population-specific nomogram**; local reference data are best. [S1]
- **Practical cut-offs (S2, citing international guidelines): under 2 cm at birth and under 4 cm after 5 years of age.** Record girth at the base and coronal level, palpate the corpora and check for cryptorchidism (R3.3: 1, ⊕⊕⊕⊕). [S2]
- **Measured in the first 12 hours of life, stretched length may read about 10% short: measure again.** For karyotype, count more metaphases (e.g. 100) to find hidden mosaicism; FISH for SRY can exclude a DSD faster. [S2]

## Treat

- **First steps: counselling, investigation of the endocrine cause, and hormone therapy** aiming for a length within the normal range for age. [S1]
- **Hormone therapy is the most accepted and effective treatment, especially in CHH,** though some boys, particularly with severe hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, do not reach a normal adult size. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Give testosterone early: infancy or early childhood, including mini-puberty, gives the best growth.** Earlier start means more growth; initial glans width is a strong predictor of response. [S1]
- **Monitor for premature virilisation and high serum testosterone, particularly with topical preparations.** [S1]
- **The evidence is thin:** there are no large, long-term placebo-controlled trials and no evidence-based guideline on testosterone for micropenis. Short-term results are favourable; long-term data are limited. [S1]
- **Expectant management may suit selected boys**, since many untreated patients normalise at puberty. It must be individualised, with structured clinical and psychosocial follow-up; the supporting studies had high drop-out. [S1]
- **Surgery is not first line.** Keep it for failed hormone therapy or anatomical abnormality; paediatric outcomes are poorly documented and the risks significant. [S1]

## Treat: SIAMS/SIEDP guideline (S2)

- **Treat isolated idiopathic micropenis found early in life in a paediatric endocrinology centre** (R3.7: 1, ⊕⊕○○). With established androgen sensitivity, testosterone or DHT may be given in the first 6 months of life or later in childhood or adolescence (R3.8: 2, ⊕⊕○○). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Suggested regimen: testosterone enanthate 25 mg IM once a month for 3 months in prepubertal children, repeatable only once after 3–6 months; or transdermal DHT 5 mg daily for 6 weeks, repeatable 1–2 times.** No evidence supports hormone therapy after puberty. [S2]
- **The evidence behind it:** there is no consensus on age, dose, route or duration. Testosterone enanthate 25 mg IM monthly for 3 months more than doubled penile length in prepubertal boys (durability unknown), with the same result reported in minipuberty; 5% testosterone cream (10 mg daily on the penis for 30 days) and low-dose local DHT (0.1–0.3 mg/kg/day for 6 months) also increased length. [S2]
- **In a randomised study of 49 children and adolescents (mean age 9.7 years), transdermal DHT (5 mg daily for 5 weeks) outperformed testosterone enanthate (50 mg IM monthly for 3 months):** mean gain 2.82 versus 1.87 cm, both significant. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Testosterone enanthate and DHT gel are off label in children (at least in Italy), so obtain signed informed consent from parents;** DHT is not available everywhere, and whether very early treatment improves adult length is still unclear. [S2]
- **Minipuberty:** if androgen deficiency is found then, low-dose testosterone may be considered; gonadotropin therapy to recreate minipuberty has more limited reports. Both are off label. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Recommend against surgery before adulthood** (R3.9: 1, ⊕⊕○○). During transition, counsel with extreme caution about surgery because function is uncertain and complications are frequent. [S2]
- **Follow-up:** review at least once before puberty and regularly from expected puberty (R3.4: 1, ⊕⊕⊕⊕); offer psychosocial support to the family and the boy (R3.6: 2, ⊕⊕○○). [S2]
