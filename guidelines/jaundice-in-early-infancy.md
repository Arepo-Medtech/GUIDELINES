# Jaundice in early infancy

## Recognise it

- **Measure, don't eyeball:** when significant jaundice is suspected, check serum bilirubin and/or a transcutaneous bilirubinometer (TcB) reading. Visual assessment alone is unreliable. [S1]
- **Most jaundice is physiological and benign**, but ⚠️ severe unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia can cause acute bilirubin encephalopathy and kernicterus, with permanent neurological injury. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pathological features:** onset under 24 hours · bilirubin rising rapidly · infant looks unwell · jaundice that is prolonged · conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia · evidence of haemolysis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pale or acholic stools at any age: evaluate urgently** for cholestatic liver disease (biliary atresia included). [S1]
- **Risk factors for severe hyperbilirubinaemia:** prematurity · dehydration · poor feeding · haemolysis · excessive weight loss · bruising/cephalhaematoma. [S1]
- **In the history:** onset under 24 hours; **over 2 weeks is prolonged**; poor weight gain or weight loss over 10% in the first week of life. [S1]
- **In darker-skinned infants, jaundice can be harder to see.** [S1]

## Measure and investigate

- **TcB can screen well infants ≥35 weeks' gestation and ≥24 hours old.** Check SBR to confirm when TcB is within 50 micromol/L of the treatment threshold, ≥250 micromol/L, rapidly rising, or phototherapy is being considered or has started. TcB is less reliable after phototherapy. [S1]
- **SBR is the gold standard** for diagnosis and treatment decisions; interpret it by gestation, postnatal age and risk factors on a gestation-specific nomogram. [S1]
- **Request fractionated bilirubin** for an unwell infant, jaundice that is prolonged or suspected pathological, pale stools or dark urine. [S1]
- **Most well infants with unconjugated jaundice need no further investigation.** Physiological jaundice starts after 24 hours, peaks on day 3–5 and lasts 1–2 weeks. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Early onset (under 24 hours) is pathological:** every infant needs fractionated SBR, FBE and a direct antiglobulin test (DAT). [S1]
- **Prolonged jaundice (over 2 weeks):** fractionated SBR, FBE (with film and reticulocytes), blood group and DAT, TFTs; LFTs if conjugated bilirubin is over 10%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia (any age) — over 20 µmol/L or over 20% of total: refer to gastroenterology.** Biliary atresia is not excluded by a normal ultrasound. [S1]
- **Breast milk jaundice** occurs in healthy, thriving breast-fed infants: begins at 5–7 days, is at its peak at 2–3 weeks, and may persist as long as 12 weeks — a **diagnosis of exclusion**. [S1]

## Treat

- **Plot TcB/SBR on a gestation-specific nomogram and use local treatment thresholds.** Judge severity by gestation, postnatal age, bilirubin, clinical condition, hydration and risk factors. [S1]
- **Support hydration and feeding**, particularly for breast-fed infants and any with poor intake or excessive weight loss; consider involving the maternal and child health nurse and a lactation consultant. [S1]
- **Phototherapy follows local protocol** (intensity, biliblanket, monitoring), with close monitoring of weight, hydration and bilirubin and serial SBR until resolution. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Exchange transfusion only in, or with, a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit** — contact the paediatric retrieval service. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Breast milk jaundice: do NOT stop breastfeeding.** It can persist as long as 12 weeks; repeat bilirubin levels are only needed if the jaundice is getting worse. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Sepsis: treat immediately with IV antibiotics** as per the sepsis guideline. [S1]
- **Extrahepatic obstruction or neonatal hepatitis is uncommon, but early diagnosis improves outcome** — discuss with paediatric gastroenterology if suspected. [S1]

## Escalate and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team** if the child is unwell, the cause is unclear, or conjugated bilirubin is over 20% of total. [S1]
- **Consider transfer** if the level is rising despite adequate local treatment, or the child needs exchange transfusion. [S1]
- **Discharge when** SBR/TcB is below the treatment threshold with follow-up planned, causes needing treatment are excluded, and the infant is well and feeding well. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Sunlight exposure is not a treatment for jaundice.** [S1]
- **Arrange early MCH nurse and/or GP follow-up**, particularly when the infant is under 7 days old, is exclusively breastfed or feeds are still being established, or the level is borderline. **If borderline or still rising, repeat bilirubin in 24–48 hours.** [S1]
- **Tell parents to seek review** if jaundice persists 2–3 weeks without an established cause, they think it is worsening, or they have any other concern. [S1]
