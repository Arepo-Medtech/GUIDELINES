# The acutely swollen joint in children

> ⚠️ **Septic arthritis is an orthopaedic emergency** — think of it when a joint is severely painful and swollen in one place, especially if the child is febrile. A sudden inability to walk or bear weight is a red flag.

---

## Key points

- **First assessment exists to find serious causes and treat them.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flag: a child who suddenly cannot walk or bear weight.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Suspect septic arthritis — an orthopaedic emergency — whenever a child has severe, localised pain and swelling of a joint — above all with fever.** [S1]
- **Children discharged with an unclear diagnosis need early follow-up.** [S1]

## History and examination

- **Acute = onset under 2 weeks.** Note site and character (migratory, early morning). [S1]
- **Ask about** constant severe or night pain; systemic features (fatigue, weight loss, rash, night sweats, visual impairment, unexplained bruising, diarrhoea); **family history of haemophilia** or autoimmune/inflammatory disease. [S1]
- **ARF risk groups:** Indigenous Australians, Māori and Pacific Islanders; ARF or rheumatic heart disease in the child or the family. [S1]
- **Look, feel, move — start with the non-painful side.** Also check for pallor, lymphadenopathy, petechiae or purpura, hepatosplenomegaly and abdominal mass. [S1]

## Differential diagnosis

- ⚠️ **Septic arthritis / osteomyelitis:** severe localised joint or limb pain, systemic features, acute fever; commonly lower-limb joints. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Child abuse:** unexplained injury or bruising, particularly in a non-ambulatory child. [S1]
- **Reactive arthritis:** after pharyngitis, gastroenteritis or urethritis (Chlamydia), onset 7–14 days later; oligo- or monoarticular, usually lower limbs. [S1]
- **Haemophilia:** personal or family history, unexplained bruising or bleeding; most commonly the knee. [S1]
- **JIA / mixed connective tissue disease:** fever, rash, oligo- or polyarthritis, enthesitis, psoriasis; the child can normally weight bear. [S1]
- **Kawasaki disease:** fever over 5 days with rash, conjunctivitis, cervical lymphadenopathy, swollen hands and feet. [S1]
- **Acute rheumatic fever:** at-risk population, fever, **migratory polyarthritis or arthralgia of large joints**, murmur (mitral or aortic regurgitation); skin signs are rare. [S1]
- **HSP:** purpuric rash, usually lower limbs, with lower-limb joints affected. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Malignancy (leukaemia, bone or soft-tissue tumour):** fever, weight loss, lethargy, night sweats, **nocturnal pain**, pallor, bruising, hepatosplenomegaly or abdominal mass. [S1]

## Investigate and treat

- **No investigations may be needed** when red flags are absent and the working diagnosis is clear and/or review is planned within 7 days. [S1]
- **Treat according to the working diagnosis**, with pain management (consider immobilisation) and child-directed limitation of activity. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Septic arthritis: consult the orthopaedic team.** Infective causes go to the relevant sub-specialty team and follow local antibiotic guidelines. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Suspected ARF:** discuss with a paediatrician experienced in ARF, an ID physician or a paediatric cardiologist; **echocardiogram even without a murmur**; **avoid NSAIDs during the diagnostic workup** — they may mask migratory joint symptoms. [S1]

## Referral and discharge

- **Consult the paediatric team** for suspected child abuse or an unclear diagnosis. [S1]
- **Refer:** suspected septic arthritis → orthopaedics; **suspected haemarthrosis in known or suspected haemophilia → haematology**; joint pain over 4 weeks, multiple joints, or vasculitis other than HSP → rheumatology. [S1]
- **Discharge** when the cause is identified and managed — or, **if the diagnosis is unclear**, only when pain is controlled **and** high-risk diagnoses are excluded **and** follow-up is arranged. [S1]
