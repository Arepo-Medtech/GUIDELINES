# Metastatic colorectal cancer: systemic treatment by line and biomarker

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** biomarker testing, resectability, and first-line, maintenance, second-line, third-line and follow-up decisions for adults with metastatic colorectal cancer. Screening, prevention and early disease: see `colorectal-cancer-population-screening`, `colorectal-cancer-family-history-screening` and `colorectal-cancer-prevention-and-management`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Assess: molecular testing

- **Do not restrict HER2 amplification testing to RAS wild-type tumours**; test RAS-mutant disease too where resources and reimbursement allow, and if testing is not funded consider referral to a centre running trials. [S1]
- **Testing HER2 together with RAS, BRAF and dMMR/MSI in one pass saves tumour tissue.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **DPD testing (genotype or phenotype) may be considered before a fluoropyrimidine, depending on the population, and should be done after severe fluoropyrimidine toxicity.** The Asian panel softened ESMO's advice because DPD deficiency is rare in Asian patients (about 0.6% in one Japanese series). [S1]
- **UGT1A1 genotyping before irinotecan** is advised if UGT1A1 deficiency is suspected (low conjugated bilirubin) or if a dose above 180 mg/m² per administration is planned; the variants raise the risk of severe neutropenia. [S1]

## Resectable or potentially resectable disease

- **Do not deny resection or ablation just because first-line chemotherapy failed.** Reassess for resection once second-line treatment is working; intra-arterial chemotherapy can be a second-line route to response and liver resection. [S1]
- **Local treatment can be the primary or metastasis-directed treatment, or consolidation after systemic therapy.** [S1]
- **Intra-arterial options (TACE, TARE/SIRT, hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy)** may be used with non-curative intent. [S1]

## First-line treatment of unresectable disease

- ⚠️ **Left-sided, RAS wild-type: anti-EGFR antibody plus doublet chemotherapy.** In a Japanese phase III trial, panitumumab with mFOLFOX6 gave longer overall survival, higher response and more R0 resections than bevacizumab with mFOLFOX6. [S1]
- **Right-sided RAS wild-type tumours gained no survival benefit from panitumumab**, although R0 resection rates were similar with either antibody. [S1]
- **Do not currently combine an anti-EGFR antibody with FOLFOXIRI**: no phase III trial supports it, and a European trial found no gain over FOLFOX plus panitumumab. [S1]
- **FOLFOXIRI with bevacizumab** is an option only for selected patients in good performance status without comorbidities. [S1]
- **Frail or elderly patients not fit for chemotherapy with unresectable RAS wild-type disease** can be offered panitumumab alone. [S1]
- **If 5-FU or capecitabine cannot be tolerated because of cardiotoxicity or hand-foot syndrome**, the oral fluoropyrimidine S-1 may be substituted. [S1]
- ⚠️ **dMMR/MSI disease: first-line pembrolizumab is a standard of care.** Progression-free survival improved; the overall survival difference was not significant, probably because 60% crossed over. [S1]
- ⚠️ **FOLFOX6 (modified) with panitumumab, first line for RAS wild-type disease (eviQ):** every 14 days, panitumumab 6 mg/kg, oxaliplatin 85 mg/m², calcium folinate 50 mg flat bolus and fluorouracil 400 mg/m² bolus then 2,400 mg/m² by pump over 46 hours, until progression or unacceptable toxicity. Consider stopping oxaliplatin after 6 cycles, and do not exceed 12; maintenance can continue if stable or responding. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Test RAS in everyone before an EGFR antibody:** mutant or unknown RAS status can be harmed by one. BRAF mutation signals poorer prognosis and likely resistance, a right-sided primary may gain nothing, and panitumumab is not for disease that progressed on cetuximab. [S2]
- **PRIME evidence:** in tumours without RAS mutations, panitumumab with FOLFOX gave median overall survival of 26.0 versus 20.2 months with FOLFOX alone (HR 0.78). [S2]
- **EGFR antibody and FOLFOX safety:** acneiform rash usually peaks in weeks 2 to 4 (moisturiser, sunscreen, consider early doxycycline and 1% hydrocortisone); check magnesium and calcium before each cycle and for up to 8 weeks after stopping; expect cold-triggered oxaliplatin neuropathy within 48 hours of a dose. DPYD genotyping has been MBS-listed since 1 November 2025. [S2]
- ⚠️ **MSI-H or dMMR (validated test), ECOG 0 to 1: ipilimumab plus nivolumab (eviQ).** Nivolumab 240 mg with ipilimumab 1 mg/kg every 21 days for 4 cycles, then nivolumab 480 mg every 28 days until progression or unacceptable toxicity (the trial capped treatment at 2 years). [S2]
- **The phase 3 trial behind the eviQ protocol:** patients with no, one, or two or more earlier lines were enrolled. At a median 47 months, median PFS was not reached with the combination versus 39.3 months with nivolumab alone (HR 0.62); response 71% v 58%; grade 3–4 treatment-related events 22% v 14%. [S2]

## Maintenance

- **Maintenance means stepping down treatment intensity to reduce side effects** in patients not suitable for surgery or local therapy. First-line FOLFIRI can simply continue to progression if tolerated. On progression during maintenance, re-introduce the induction regimen that worked. [S1]

## Second line

- ⚠️ **BRAF V600E-mutated, previously treated: encorafenib with cetuximab is the best second-line option** (and the best third-line option if not used earlier); adding binimetinib is optional where available. [S1]
- **dMMR/MSI-H progressing after first-line chemotherapy:** ipilimumab with nivolumab is recommended; nivolumab or pembrolizumab alone are alternatives. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Encorafenib with cetuximab (eviQ):** BRAF V600E-mutant disease progressing after at least one line, ECOG 0 to 2, never given an EGFR antibody. Encorafenib 300 mg by mouth daily with cetuximab 400 mg/m² loading then 250 mg/m² weekly (500 mg/m² every 14 days is an alternative), continued until progression or unacceptable toxicity. In BEACON, median overall survival was 9.3 v 5.9 months (HR 0.61) and response 19.5% v 1.8%; adding binimetinib did not improve survival. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Cetuximab anaphylaxis risk is higher with red-meat allergy, tick bites or alpha-gal IgE;** consider alpha-gal testing in people from tick-endemic areas. On encorafenib, do not start if QTc exceeds 500 ms, and send any visual disturbance promptly to an ophthalmologist (uveitis, serous retinopathy), withholding treatment until reviewed. [S2]

## Third line and beyond

- ⚠️ **Trifluridine–tipiracil, with or without bevacizumab,** is recommended after fluoropyrimidines, oxaliplatin, irinotecan and biologics, or earlier after oxaliplatin and irinotecan failure, subject to local approvals. Adding bevacizumab improved overall and progression-free survival and response in a phase III trial. [S1]
- **RAS and BRAF wild-type, never given an EGFR antibody:** cetuximab or panitumumab as a single agent. [S1]
- **Fruquintinib is a further option after fluoropyrimidines, oxaliplatin, irinotecan and biologics**; two placebo-controlled trials showed a significant overall survival gain. [S1]
- **HER2-positive, RAS wild-type, refractory disease:** dual HER2 blockade works (trastuzumab with lapatinib), and tucatinib with trastuzumab is likely to become another option. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Trifluridine/tipiracil with bevacizumab (eviQ):** ECOG 0 to 1, once fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin and irinotecan regimens, a VEGF inhibitor and (for RAS wild-type tumours) an EGFR antibody have been used or are unsuitable. Trifluridine/tipiracil 35 mg/m² twice daily (maximum 80 mg a dose) on days 1–5 and 8–12, with bevacizumab 5 mg/kg on days 1 and 15, every 28 days. Median overall survival was 10.8 v 7.5 months with trifluridine/tipiracil alone (HR 0.61). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Fruquintinib (eviQ):** ECOG 0 to 1, once fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin and irinotecan regimens, a VEGF inhibitor and, where suitable, an EGFR antibody have been given. 5 mg by mouth once daily, 3 weeks on then 1 week off. Avoid with significant cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension or thromboembolism in the past 6 months; check blood pressure weekly for the first month then monthly, and LFTs every 2 weeks for 2 months. Median overall survival was 7.4 v 4.8 months with placebo (HR 0.66). [S2]

## Follow-up

- **During active treatment, image at least every 12 weeks** (usually CT or MRI) and measure tumour markers. Assess toxicity from systemic treatment and surgery, including in long-term survivors. [S1]
