# Gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, molecular testing, risk assessment, surgery, neoadjuvant and adjuvant imatinib, systemic therapy for advanced disease, response assessment and follow-up in adults with GIST. Paediatric, syndromic and SDH-deficient GIST are mentioned only where they change management. Other soft tissue sarcomas: see `soft-tissue-sarcoma`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise and diagnose

- **Typical presentations:** upper GI bleeding and anaemia; larger tumours cause pain or a mass. Small-bowel GISTs can stay silent until they bleed or rupture. [S1]
- **Investigate and diagnose any suspected GIST over 2 cm**, usually by EUS biopsy in the stomach, duodenum or rectum. Incidental lesions under 2 cm need no routine surveillance. [S1]
- **Diagnosis needs an experienced pathologist**, with CD117 and/or DOG1 immunohistochemistry and molecular testing where needed; about 1% of GISTs stain for neither. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Confirm the diagnosis and genotype before imatinib.** Some GISTs, notably PDGFRA exon 18 D842V, are insensitive to TKIs. [S1]
- **KIT/PDGFRA mutation analysis at minimum for:** resected moderate- or high-risk or ruptured tumours, biopsies before neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy, unresectable or metastatic disease, and suspected syndromic GIST. [S1]
- **For KIT/PDGFRA wild-type GIST not linked to NF1**, do SDHB immunohistochemistry, then test for BRAF/RAS and finally NTRK fusions, which are rare but targetable. [S1]

## Risk assessment and staging

- **Prognosis rests on mitotic rate, tumour size and site**; gastric GISTs do better than small-bowel or rectal ones, and rupture is adverse and must be recorded. TNM staging is not recommended. [S1]
- **Stage with contrast CT of abdomen and pelvis**; MRI is better for rectal GIST and an option in younger patients to limit radiation. [S1]

## Localised disease

- **Standard treatment is complete (R0, margin at least 1 mm) excision without dissecting clinically negative nodes**, by a trained cancer surgeon in or linked to a specialist centre. Avoid rupture: it carries a very high relapse risk. [S1]
- **Consider neoadjuvant imatinib when R0 surgery would need total gastrectomy or abdominoperineal resection**, provided the genotype is sensitive. Operate at maximal response, generally after 6-9 months; stop imatinib about a week before surgery. [S1]
- **Adjuvant imatinib for 3 years is standard for high-risk resected GIST** with a sensitive driver (e.g. KIT exon 11); 3 years beat 1 year for relapse-free and overall survival. [S1]
- ⚠️ **No adjuvant imatinib for PDGFRA D842V or NF1-related GIST.** An R1 margin alone is not an indication if the tumour is otherwise not high risk; SDH-deficient GIST has no agreed approach. [S1]
- **After tumour rupture, assume occult peritoneal disease** and give adjuvant imatinib for at least 3 years, probably for life. [S1]

## Advanced or metastatic disease

- **First line: imatinib 400 mg daily, continued indefinitely until progression.** For KIT exon 9 mutations, 800 mg daily improves PFS (and survival in long-term follow-up); not for PDGFRA D842V. [S1]
- **Support adherence:** explain drug and food interactions (especially grapefruit, a CYP3A4 inhibitor), and manage side-effects to keep dose intensity. [S1]
- **Second line: sunitinib**, 50 mg daily for 4 weeks in every 6, or 37.5 mg daily continuously. [S1]
- **Later lines:** regorafenib (160 mg daily, 3 weeks on/1 off) is the UK third-line standard. Ripretinib after 3 or more TKIs gave median PFS 6.3 v 1.0 months and median OS 15.1 v 6.6 months against placebo, but did not beat sunitinib in second line. [S1]
- **PDGFRA D842V: avapritinib is the most active drug** (300 mg daily; 79% partial and 9% complete responses in the phase I study). [S1]
- **Limited progression** (such as a 'nodule within a mass') may suit surgery, RFA or radiotherapy while continuing the TKI; surgery for generalised progression is not recommended. Offer trials to all with advanced disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Judge response on size and density together.** A tumour can grow briefly yet be responding if CT density falls; new enhancement inside a responding low-density lesion may signal progression. Have an experienced team confirm early progression. [S1]

## Follow-up

- **High risk on adjuvant therapy:** cross-sectional imaging every 3-6 months for the first 3 years, then 3-monthly for 2 years after stopping, 6-monthly for 3 years, then yearly for at least 5 years. High-risk patients not given adjuvant therapy follow the post-adjuvant schedule. [S1]
- **Intermediate risk:** 6-monthly scans for 5 years then yearly is reasonable, though perhaps unnecessary. **Low risk:** consider yearly CT or ultrasound for 5 years and watch for second cancers. **Very low risk:** no routine surveillance. [S1]
