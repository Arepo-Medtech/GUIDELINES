# Hirschsprung-associated enterocolitis (HAEC)

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, investigation, resuscitation, IV antibiotics, rectal washout and surgical referral for enterocolitis in children with Hirschsprung disease. > ⚠️ **Mild HAEC can mimic gastroenteritis or constipation.** Any child with Hirschsprung disease who is unwell needs prompt treatment and **discussion with the paediatric surgical team**.

---

## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **In Hirschsprung disease (HD), HAEC causes more significant morbidity and mortality than anything else.** Mild HAEC can look like constipation or simple gastroenteritis, yet it needs specialised treatment. [S1]
- **Every child with HD is at risk**, before and/or after pull-through surgery. [S1]
- **Higher risk:** under 2 years, undiagnosed HD, trisomy 21, long-segment HD, previous HAEC. [S1]
- **History:** fever, lethargy, anorexia, reduced intake, vomiting, stool pattern (explosive or malodorous diarrhoea, or constipation), rectal bleeding, recent viral illness, previous HAEC. **Most have reduced stool output**, though some have diarrhoea. [S1]
- **Establish the HD background:** extent (short segment, long segment, total colonic), current washout regimen, and the definitive procedure (Swenson, Soave or Duhamel). [S1]
- **Examine** for haemodynamic instability, fever, hydration, distension, tenderness and **peritonitis (may be subtle)**. Rectal examination for explosive gas/stool is for a **senior clinician or the surgical team** only. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Abdominal X-ray:** dilated loops, pneumatosis, air-fluid levels, rectosigmoid **cut-off sign** (proximal colonic gas with abrupt absence distally). [S1]
- **Bloods:** gas, UEC, blood culture, FBE (leucocytosis, left shift). **Stool:** MCS, plus PCR for rotavirus and *C. difficile*; a respiratory PCR can be considered. [S1]

## Treat

- ⚠️ **Resuscitate shock or hypovolaemia; nil by mouth; IV fluids; surgical review.** [S1]
- **IV antibiotics, once discussed with the surgeons:** cefazolin IV 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) every 8 hours **plus** metronidazole IV 7.5 mg/kg (max 500 mg) 3 times a day, **usually for 5 days**. [S1]
- **Antiemetic, ages 6 months–18 years:** IV ondansetron 0.15 mg/kg (max 8 mg) every 8 hours. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Rectal bowel washout by the surgical team only:** warm sodium chloride 0.9%, 20 mL/kg, as often as 3 times a day. [S1]

## Escalate, transfer, discharge

- **Discuss every child with HD and/or suspected HAEC** with the local paediatric team **and** the paediatric surgical team (local or referral hospital). [S1]
- **Transfer to a tertiary centre is common for HAEC.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not discharge until the paediatric surgical team has been consulted.** [S1]
