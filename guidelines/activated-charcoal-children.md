# Activated charcoal in poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** when activated charcoal may be considered, when it is contraindicated, how to give it safely and multi-dose charcoal in children. General decontamination principles are in `poisoning-initial-management-children`; the AMH counterpart (with adult and child doses) is `gastrointestinal-decontamination`. Agent pages that restrict charcoal include `benzodiazepine-poisoning-children`, `iron-poisoning-children` and `tricyclic-antidepressant-poisoning-children`.

> ⚠️ **Very rarely indicated in children, and never without a toxicologist.** **Aspiration can kill: confirm NG tube position on chest X-ray first**, and never give it with a reduced conscious state and an unprotected airway. **If indicated, give within 1–2 hours.** ⚠️ **This page gives no dose.**

---

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Charcoal has only a very small place in treating poisoned children — never give it unless a toxicologist has been consulted.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Aspirated charcoal causes significant morbidity and death. Confirm nasogastric tube position on chest X-ray before giving it.** [S1]
- **When indicated, give it without delay — typically in the first 1 to 2 hours after exposure.** [S1]
- **Reserve it for potentially severe poisonings in which supportive care plus antidotes alone would lead to a poor outcome** — ie benefit must outweigh risk. [S1]
- **Don't wait for drug levels** — they return too late to influence the early decision. [S1]

## When to consider it

- **Resuscitate first.** Charcoal is given early, but only after appropriate resuscitation; **intubation and ventilation may be needed beforehand.** [S1]
- **Think of it for an early presentation (eg 1–2 hours) following a potentially severe ingestion of:** calcium channel blockers (verapamil and diltiazem especially) · beta blockers · flecainide · amisulpride · carbamazepine · colchicine · methotrexate · quinine, chloroquine or hydroxychloroquine · paraquat/diquat. [S1]
- **Other drugs:** see the individual poisoning guidelines and discuss with a toxicologist. [S1]

## When it is contraindicated

- ⚠️ **Contraindicated with an altered or reduced conscious state, or imminent risk of seizures, and an unprotected airway.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Not helpful and contraindicated for:** cyanide · fluoride · hydrocarbons · acids, alkalis and corrosives · boric acid · ethanol, methanol and glycols · eucalyptus and essential oils · **metals — lithium, iron, potassium, lead**. [S1]

## Adverse effects and administration

- ⚠️ **Adverse effects: aspiration, progressive respiratory failure and death.** Beware oral use in a drowsy child or after substances that can rapidly depress the CNS or cause seizures. Also faecal discolouration and **bowel obstruction from bezoars.** [S1]
- **Giving it:** it is highly viscous — dilute with at least 0.25 parts water to 1 part charcoal — and **pass it down a 10–14 French large-bore NG tube.** [S1]

## Multi-dose charcoal

- **Multi-dose charcoal has a pharmacokinetic effect but no evident improvement in clinical outcome.** ⚠️ **Stop immediately if bowel sounds disappear.** [S1]
- **If repeating doses, monitor drug levels and the clinical state.** Some evidence backs repeat dosing when a **life-threatening** ingestion involves carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbitone, dapsone, theophylline or quinine. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Consider admission for every adolescent with an intentional overdose.** 24-hour advice: Victorian Poisons Information Centre 13 11 26. Transfers: PIPER 1300 137 650. Discharge criteria are on the individual poisoning pages. [S1]
