# Brief resolved unexplained event (BRUE) in infants

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** definition, history and examination, differential diagnosis, low- versus higher-risk stratification, targeted investigation and disposition for BRUE in infants under 12 months. Infant-only; there is no adult counterpart. Related: `seriously-unwell-neonate`, `gord-in-infants`, `pertussis`, `infantile-spasms`, `head-injury-children`.

> **Low-risk BRUE needs no investigations** and can be managed as an outpatient. ⚠️ **Consider inflicted injury** when the history changes or does not fit the infant's development, or there is bruising, subconjunctival haemorrhage or oral injury.

---

## Define it

- **A serious underlying diagnosis is absent in most infants who have a BRUE.** [S1]
- **BRUE** = an event in an infant **under 12 months** that is sudden, brief and now resolved, with one or more of: cyanosis or pallor · breathing that is absent, decreased or irregular · marked change in tone · altered responsiveness — **and no alternative diagnosis after history and examination.** [S1]
- **The commonest cause is thought to be exaggerated airway reflexes** from poor suck/swallow coordination or vagal stimulation — with feeding, reflux or upper respiratory infection. [S1]
- ⚠️ **An infant still needing acute treatment has not had a BRUE.** [S1]

## History and examination

- **Take the history from an adult who saw the event.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider inflicted injury** when there are multiple or changing versions of the history, or the circumstances do not fit the child's developmental stage. [S1]
- **Describe the event:** choking or gagging; breathing difficulty, pauses or apnoea; colour (cyanosis, pallor, plethora); distress; responsiveness; tone (stiff, floppy, normal); movements including eyes. [S1]
- **Circumstances:** awake or asleep, relation to feeding and vomiting, position, **sleeping arrangement, co-sleeping, temperature and bedding**, nearby objects that could be swallowed or suffocate, recent illness. [S1]
- **End of the event:** duration; how it stopped — self-resolved, repositioning, stimulation, or mouth-to-mouth/chest compressions; rapid or gradual recovery. [S1]
- **Other history:** previous or clustered events, baseline stridor/stertor/vomiting or GORD, **sudden death in the family**, sick contacts, medications or drugs in the home, social supports. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fully expose every infant.** **Suspect inflicted injury** with bruising, a full fontanelle, subconjunctival haemorrhage, epistaxis, oral injury, head circumference crossing centiles, or irritability/drowsiness. [S1]

## Differential diagnosis

- **Less serious, more common:** laryngospasm, choking, gagging; reflux; normal infant movement or periodic breathing; lower respiratory infection. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Less common but more serious:** seizure/infantile spasms; obstruction or abnormality of the airway; central or obstructive apnoea; head or other inflicted injury; pertussis or bacterial infection; congenital heart disease or arrhythmia; electrolyte or metabolic disorders, toxins, intussusception. [S1]

## Risk stratify

- **Stratify only once the infant has recovered and the examination is benign.** In a low-risk BRUE, recurrence is unlikely and a serious disorder very unlikely. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Low-risk BRUE = no concerning history or examination features AND all of:** age over 60 days · born at ≥32 weeks with corrected gestational age ≥45 weeks · trained healthcare staff did not give CPR · first event · lasted under 1 minute. **Anything else is higher-risk.** [S1]
- **4–8% of higher-risk BRUE** turn out to have a serious underlying diagnosis — **seizure or apnoea most often.** [S1]

## Investigate

- **Low-risk BRUE: no investigations.** In higher-risk BRUE yield is low: only 1–7% of tests help make the diagnosis. [S1]
- **Higher-risk, targeted:** **ECG with QT measurement** for dysmorphology or a family history of sudden death or arrhythmia; **nasopharyngeal sample** for pertussis exposure, respiratory symptoms or apnoea. [S1]
- **EEG/neurology** for abnormal movements, eye deviation, altered neurology or recurrence; **ENT** for stridor, epistaxis, obstructive symptoms or apnoea. [S1]

## Manage and disposition

- **Acknowledge the carer's distress**, educate, and reassure that these infants are **not at increased risk of death**; still offer **infant CPR training** resources. [S1]
- **Low-risk BRUE may be discharged** if parents feel reassured and capable; decide with the family whether observation helps. **Admission with cardiorespiratory monitoring reassures but uncommonly finds a diagnosis.** [S1]
- **On discharge, give clear advice about when to return** and a safety net for re-presentation. [S1]
- **Consult the local paediatric team** if the event is not low-risk or there is any concern for inflicted injury; **consider transfer** if a serious disorder is suspected. [S1]
- **Consider discharge** after a period of observation when suspicion of a serious disorder is low and the carer is reassured. [S1]
