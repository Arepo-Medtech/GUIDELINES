# Perioperative DMARD management in inflammatory arthritis

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis or axial spondyloarthritis having **elective** surgery: whether and when to withhold and restart csDMARDs, bDMARDs and tsDMARDs. Urgent surgery and glucocorticoid cover are outside it. Rheumatoid arthritis has its own guideline (`rheumatoid-arthritis`); the listed PBS rows are the no-guideline conditions this serves. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`. Edition 2.0 adds S2 (ACR/AAHKS 2022, elective hip and knee replacement), which also covers SLE drugs and glucocorticoid cover.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 25 claims; **42 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 6 doses.** **S1** Australian Living Guideline panel (ANZMUSC (published 22 May 2023; AU): 17 claims, quoted · **S2** American College of Rheumatology and American Association of Hip and Knee Surgeons (Goodman SM (published online 20 June 2022 (corrected 24 August 2022); international): 8 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ **Australia differs from the US guideline on biologics and JAK inhibitors.** The Australian panel advises **continuing** most bDMARDs through elective surgery, where ACR/AAHKS 2022 advises withholding them; for tsDMARDs Australia suggests stopping about **1 week** before, where ACR/AAHKS 2022 shortened this to **3 days**.

> 🇦🇺 **S1 is the Australian reference (Australian Living Guideline, 2023).** **S2** (ACR/AAHKS 2022) is the US guideline S1 compares itself with: it adds biologic timing, SLE drugs and glucocorticoid cover, for elective hip and knee replacement only. Where they differ, both are shown.

> ⚠️ International guideline (ACR/AAHKS, 2022) as S2. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

## Recommendations

- **Three conditional recommendations:** do not routinely stop csDMARDs; do not routinely stop bDMARDs, but consider a temporary pause when infection risk or its consequences are high; consider temporarily stopping tsDMARDs. [S1]
- **They apply to all inflammatory arthritis:** RA, PsA and axial SpA. [S1]
- **Individualise the decision.** Drugs within a class differ, and infection risk varies with the operation (e.g. urgent surgery, or opening the respiratory, alimentary or genitourinary tract). [S1]
- **What 'conditional' means here (GRADE):** most people in this situation would choose the recommended course, but many would not, so decide with the patient after a clear talk about benefits and harms, weighing the type and urgency of surgery, their infection risk factors, and what an infection or a flare would mean for them. [S1]

## Evidence behind them

- **Randomised evidence is thin:** three RCTs, all of methotrexate or leflunomide in RA before elective orthopaedic surgery. None covered PsA, axial SpA, bDMARDs or tsDMARDs. [S1]
- **Stopping csDMARDs may increase flares** without reducing postoperative infection (low certainty); the trial methotrexate doses were low (about 10 mg/week). [S1]
- **Eight observational studies agree:** stopping DMARDs may raise flare risk with no apparent fall in infection, including prosthetic joint infection. [S1]
- **Every ACR/AAHKS recommendation is conditional, resting on indirect or low-quality evidence;** no randomised trials appeared after the 2017 version. It covers only elective hip and knee replacement, though its principles may guide other operations. [S2]
- **Flares are not benign:** they are linked to long-term joint damage, disability and cardiovascular disease, and can derail rehabilitation or need extra glucocorticoids. [S1]

## Conventional synthetic DMARDs

- **Continue csDMARDs in most patients.** If withholding: methotrexate for one dosing cycle (surgery 1–2 weeks after the last dose), leflunomide about 7 days before surgery. Sulfasalazine and hydroxychloroquine are rarely withheld. [S1]
- **ACR/AAHKS also continues methotrexate, leflunomide, hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine and (now including) apremilast at the usual dose through hip or knee replacement.** People with severe or recurrent infections or a past prosthetic joint infection may choose to withhold them. [S2]
- **Leflunomide and methotrexate act for a long time**, leflunomide especially, which limits what a short pause achieves. [S1]

## Biologic DMARDs

- **Continue bDMARDs in most people;** withhold only where infection risk or its consequences are higher. Observational data do not show an important infection risk from continuing. [S1]
- **If withholding a bDMARD:** skip one dosing cycle and operate around when the next dose is due (monthly injection: surgery in the fifth week). For rituximab, operate at least 3 months after the last dose. [S1]
- ⚠️ **ACR/AAHKS: withhold all biologics, rituximab included, and schedule surgery just after the next dose would have been due,** when drug levels are low: for example week 3 for adalimumab given every 2 weeks, and month 7 for rituximab given every 6 months. Claims data found no difference between pausing about one dosing interval and pausing longer. [S2]
- **Operating within the dosing cycle is reasonable** when joint symptoms are severe (for example advanced osteonecrosis), and people whose disease has been hard to control may keep the biologic going rather than risk losing control. [S2]

## SLE drugs (ACR/AAHKS)

- **SLE that is not severe: stop mycophenolate, azathioprine, ciclosporin or tacrolimus 1 week before surgery, and withhold belimumab and rituximab.** Most can be watched closely afterwards and a flare treated; frequent flares or hard-to-control disease may justify continuing. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Severe (organ-threatening) SLE: continue mycophenolate, azathioprine, ciclosporin, tacrolimus, anifrolumab, voclosporin and belimumab through surgery, and time surgery for the last month of a rituximab cycle** (usually month 5 or 6 of a 6-monthly schedule). Involve the treating rheumatologist; a patient stable for over 6 months, or with serious past infections, might pause. [S2]

## Targeted synthetic DMARDs (JAK inhibitors)

- **Temporarily stop tsDMARDs, from about 1 week before surgery.** Reasons: no evidence, infection risk, a blunted acute-phase response and a possible thrombosis risk. [S1]
- ⚠️ **ACR/AAHKS 2022 cut the tsDMARD pause to 3 days**, after tofacitinib interruption caused a fast rise in disease activity, while allowing longer in high infection risk; it did not weigh thrombotic risk. [S1]

## Restarting and after surgery

- **Restart once sutures are out, the wound has healed and there is no sign of infection**, usually 1–2 weeks after surgery. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tocilizumab (IL-6) and possibly JAK inhibitors can suppress CRP**, so suspect infection even when inflammatory markers are normal. [S1]

## Glucocorticoids around surgery (ACR/AAHKS)

- **Keep the usual daily glucocorticoid dose; do not add supraphysiologic 'stress' doses on the day of surgery.** In one study of 432 people with RA, each extra 10 mg of glucocorticoid raised the short-term complication risk by 8.4%. [S2]
- **Exceptions:** people who had glucocorticoids for JIA during childhood development, or who take them for primary adrenal insufficiency or hypothalamic disease, may need supraphysiologic doses to stay haemodynamically stable. A high cumulative dose, even below 5 mg/day, may slow wound healing and add to infection risk. [S2]

## International comparison

- **ACR/AAHKS 2022 and BSR 2019 advise withholding bDMARDs**, balanced against flare risk. The US panel's consumers prioritised infection; UK patients in focus groups prioritised avoiding flares. [S1]
