# Micronutrient deficiency in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognising, testing for and replacing vitamin and trace-element deficiencies in children with highly restricted diets (e.g. autism, ARFID, multiple food allergies). Vitamin D and iron are deferred to `vitamin-d-deficiency-children` and `iron-deficiency-children` (adult iron: `iron-deficiency`). Related: `anaemia-children`. Adult thiamine replacement in alcohol use is in `alcohol-problems`.

> ⚠️ **Normal growth does not exclude micronutrient deficiency**, and some deficiencies cause permanent disability (e.g. blindness) or death. **Symptomatic children need urgent assessment and treatment.**

---

## Who is at risk

- ⚠️ **Consider micronutrient deficiency in every child with a diet highly restricted in variety**, e.g. from autism or cognitive impairment. **Normal growth does not exclude it.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Deficiency can cause permanent disability (e.g. blindness) or death**, and features may be subtle and non-specific. [S1]
- **This is not 'fussy eating'**: calories may be enough for growth while variety is not. Typical groups: autism and other neurodevelopmental conditions, ARFID, multiple food allergies, elective dietary restriction. [S1]
- **Red-flag diet history:** missing food groups, **fewer than 10 foods/food types regularly eaten**, little meal-to-meal variation; veganism. [S1]
- **Examine for** pallor and other skin changes, dry eyes, changes in the gums and oral mucosa, goitre, neuropathy, bony deformity, nail or hair changes; the child may have normal growth or be overweight. [S1]

## Specific features

- ⚠️ **Vitamin A:** xerophthalmia — dry conjunctiva, night blindness, corneal ulceration, blindness. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Thiamine (B1):** neuropathy (dry beriberi), cardiac failure (wet beriberi), **Wernicke encephalopathy** (ataxia, ophthalmoplegia, confusion, reduced consciousness). [S1]
- **Vitamin B12:** irritability, developmental delay or regression, involuntary movements, neuropathy; at risk — vegan/vegetarian diets and **exclusively breastfed infants of B12-deficient mothers.** [S1]
- **Vitamin C (scurvy):** fractures and joint pain, poor wound healing, bleeding gums, petechiae and purpura, corkscrew hair — in diets without fruit and vegetables. [S1]
- **Zinc:** acrodermatitis enteropathica — periacral and periorificial dermatitis, diarrhoea, alopecia. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Investigate at-risk children even without symptoms** — some complications are irreversible. **Prioritise symptomatic children** for urgent assessment and treatment, even empirical supplementation. **Refer to a dietitian.** [S1]
- **Take all first-line bloods in one collection** (about 10–15 mL whole blood), and consider opportunistic sampling under sedation or anaesthesia. [S1]
- **First-line:** FBE; iron studies; UEC, LFT, calcium, phosphate, magnesium; **vitamin A (foil)**; **thiamine (foil, on ice)**; folate; B12; **vitamin C (foil, dry ice)**; vitamin D; zinc. [S1]

## Replace

- ⚠️ **Vitamin A, level 0.35–0.7 micromol/L:** oral stat — under 6 months 50,000 IU · 6–12 months 100,000 IU · over 12 months 200,000 IU; repeat at 6 months if risk persists. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Vitamin A, level under 0.35 micromol/L or xerophthalmia: same age doses orally on days 1, 2 and 14.** Toxicity occurs with chronic daily intakes over 6,000 microg. [S1]
- **Thiamine, subclinical: 2 mg/kg oral once daily for 6 weeks** (rounded to the nearest 25 mg, max 100 mg). **Symptomatic: discuss parenteral treatment** with the paediatric team. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Folate-deficiency anaemia:** aged 1–12 months **0.5 mg/kg (max 5 mg) once daily for 4 months**; over 12 months **5 mg once a day for 4 months, followed by 5 mg every 1–7 days.** **Exclude B12 deficiency first.** [S1]
- **Folate prevention with ongoing risk:** 1 month to 12 years 2.5–10 mg orally once a day; 12–18 years 5–10 mg orally once a day. Supplementation can lower levels of phenytoin, carbamazepine and valproate. [S1]
- **Vitamin B12, subclinical: 100 microg oral once daily.** Symptomatic or unable to take oral: **discuss parenteral (IM hydroxocobalamin 1,000 microg)** with the paediatric team; high-dose oral may replace IM if there is no malabsorption. [S1]
- **Vitamin C: 100 mg three times daily (oral, IV or IM) for one week, then 100 mg oral once daily** for several weeks until recovered or levels normalise. [S1]
- **Zinc, subclinical: 1 mg/kg/day orally, split into 1–3 doses**, recheck at 1 month; **for acrodermatitis enteropathica, 3 mg/kg/day orally.** Excess zinc can cause copper deficiency. [S1]
- **Consider a multivitamin for every at-risk child**, and **maintenance dosing after replacement** until the dietary risk is resolved. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Involve the paediatric team** for symptomatic deficiency, unclear cause, failed oral supplementation, or when NG or gastrostomy feeding is being considered. **Discharge** once treated and risk factors addressed. [S1]
