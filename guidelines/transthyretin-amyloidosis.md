# Transthyretin (ATTR) amyloidosis: polyneuropathy and cardiomyopathy

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation

## Recognise

- **ATTRv-PN is a rare autosomal dominant multisystem disease from TTR gene variants,** causing a length-dependent sensorimotor and autonomic neuropathy, often with cardiomyopathy. Untreated, it brings major disability and early death. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Musculoskeletal red flags can precede systemic disease by years:** bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome, lumbar spinal stenosis, and biceps or other large tendon rupture. [S1]
- **TTR amyloid on a soft-tissue biopsy calls for neurological and cardiac assessment and TTR genetic testing.** If a variant is found, start treatment at the first minor sign of neuropathy; if wild-type, early cardiac signs (ECG, NT-proBNP, hs-troponin, echo) should prompt bone scintigraphy and/or cardiac MRI. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Exclude AL amyloidosis early with serum free light chains and immunofixation.** [S1]
- **Stage neuropathy by Coutinho (FAP) stage:** stage 1 walks unaided, stage 2 needs walking aids, stage 3 is wheelchair-bound or bedridden. [S1]

## Choose treatment (ATTRv-PN)

- **Treat symptomatic patients with a confirmed amyloidogenic variant as early as possible.** Asymptomatic carriers have no approved preventive therapy; carriers with biopsy-proven amyloid but no disease features need close monitoring and a multidisciplinary decision. [S1]
- ⚠️ **First line: a second-generation silencer, vutrisiran (siRNA) or eplontersen (ASO);** neither is superior. Vutrisiran is also studied and approved for ATTR-CM. [S1]
- **Patisiran (heavier burden: IV infusions with steroid premedication) and inotersen (more safety issues and monitoring) are second line.** Patients stable on patisiran who prefer to continue may stay on it. [S1]
- **Tafamidis (20 mg, or 61 mg with mixed phenotype) is first choice if the patient wants oral therapy,** and first line for very early FAP1 disease with p.(Val50Met), but it is a bridge: switch to a silencer at any sign of progression. It is not approved in stage 2. [S1]
- **Diflunisal** may work as well as tafamidis but carries gastrointestinal, cardiac and renal toxicity. **Acoramidis** is approved only for ATTR-CM. [S1]
- **Dosing burden:** vutrisiran is subcutaneous every three months; eplontersen monthly by autoinjector at home; patisiran IV every three weeks with premedication; inotersen weekly subcutaneous with monitoring for thrombocytopenia and kidney harm. [S1]

## Monitor and switch

- **Monitor regularly for side effects and response** using a sensible combination of tests, ideally by the same trained examiner each visit; progression is any new or worsening disease-specific sign or symptom. [S1]
- **If neuropathy progresses on a stabiliser, switch straight to a second-generation silencer;** if it progresses on a silencer, check serum TTR (prealbumin) to confirm suppression. Combining a silencer with a stabiliser is costly and unproven; reserve it for severe progression, decided by a multidisciplinary board. [S1]
- **Keep treating when ambulation is lost on a silencer;** stopping on progression is considered ethically questionable. [S1]
- ⚠️ **All gene silencers lower vitamin A: supplement it.** [S1]

## Cardiomyopathy and other organs

- ⚠️ **Cardiac involvement (ATTR-CM) causes most of the morbidity and mortality:** restrictive cardiomyopathy with HFpEF, arrhythmias (especially AF) and conduction disease that may need a pacemaker. Check the heart regularly even in neuropathy-led disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Three drugs are approved for ATTR-CM: tafamidis at 61 mg (not 20 mg), acoramidis and vutrisiran.** For predominantly cardiac disease (wild-type or variant), therapy includes any of these; in mixed neurological-cardiac disease, vutrisiran is an important option. [S1]
- **Eye involvement** (vitreous opacities, glaucoma) can progress despite systemic therapy; CNS involvement (leptomeningeal disease, cognitive decline, seizures, haemorrhage) is emerging as survival improves, because no approved drug reaches the brain. [S1]
- **Prescribing belongs to experienced specialists** in amyloidosis or neuromuscular centres, working with cardiologists, within specialised care pathways. [S1]
