# Transthyretin (ATTR) amyloidosis: polyneuropathy and cardiomyopathy

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis with polyneuropathy (ATTRv-PN), and the cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM, wild-type or variant) that often accompanies it: red flags, choosing and switching disease-modifying therapy (stabilisers and gene silencers), organ involvement and practical issues. AL amyloidosis is in `al-amyloidosis`; HCM and its phenocopies in `hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy`; heart failure care in `heart-failure`. PBS access is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise

- **ATTRv-PN is a rare autosomal dominant multisystem disease from TTR gene variants,** causing a length-dependent sensorimotor and autonomic neuropathy, often with cardiomyopathy. Untreated, it brings major disability and early death. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Musculoskeletal red flags can precede systemic disease by years:** bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome, lumbar spinal stenosis, and biceps or other large tendon rupture. [S1]
- **TTR amyloid on a soft-tissue biopsy calls for neurological and cardiac assessment and TTR genetic testing.** If a variant is found, start treatment at the first minor sign of neuropathy; if wild-type, early cardiac signs (ECG, NT-proBNP, hs-troponin, echo) should prompt bone scintigraphy and/or cardiac MRI. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Exclude AL amyloidosis early with serum free light chains and immunofixation.** [S1]
- **Stage neuropathy by Coutinho (FAP) stage:** stage 1 walks unaided, stage 2 needs walking aids, stage 3 is wheelchair-bound or bedridden. [S1]

## Suspect and diagnose the cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM)

- **Think of ATTR-CM in these settings:** heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (amyloid causes 6% to 30%, more over 75) and, even without LV hypertrophy, heart failure of unknown cause at any LVEF; aortic stenosis (up to 15%, and up to 30% with low-flow, low-gradient AS; screening severe AS over 75 is reasonable); and late-onset 'HCM' (26% of those over 80). [S2]
- **Test red flags:** a thick LV wall with low QRS voltage is highly suggestive, but only 25% to 40% meet low-voltage criteria and it appears late; poor R-wave progression is seen in more than half, and conduction disease is common. NT-proBNP out of proportion to the heart failure, and raised hs-troponin T, are red flags too. [S2]
- **Echo clues:** LV wall thickness typically 1.2 cm or more with a non-dilated LV, 'granular sparkling' myocardium, thickened AV valves, RV free wall and interatrial septum, pericardial effusion, diastolic dysfunction, biatrial enlargement, and reduced global longitudinal strain with relative apical sparing. [S2]
- ⚠️ **First step: exclude AL amyloidosis with three tests** (serum free light chains, serum immunofixation and urine immunofixation); all normal excludes AL with a negative predictive value above 99%. Do not use protein electrophoresis instead. If any test is abnormal, the non-invasive route is closed: biopsy the involved organ. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Second step: with AL excluded, bone scintigraphy grade 2 or 3 diagnoses ATTR-CM without biopsy;** then do TTR genetic testing. Use DPD, HMDP or PYP (not MDP). A positive planar scan is Perugini grade 2 or more and/or a heart-to-contralateral ratio of 1.5 or more at 1 h (above 1.3 at 3 h); SPECT is recommended. [S2]
- ⚠️ **A scan alone cannot separate ATTR from AL:** grade 2–3 uptake occurs in more than 10% to 30% of AL cardiomyopathy. Sensitivity for ATTR-CM is above 99% but specificity only 82–86%, rising to 100% when blood and urine tests for AL are negative. [S2]
- **When to biopsy:** a negative or equivocal scan (grade 0/1) with continuing suspicion, a monoclonal protein or abnormal kappa/lambda ratio, or no scintigraphy available. Endomyocardial biopsy is close to 100% accurate; a negative fat-pad biopsy does not exclude cardiac amyloid. Type any amyloid found by mass spectrometry or immunohistochemistry. [S2]
- **Genetic testing for every confirmed ATTR-CM, at any age, with genetic counselling:** in one study 5% of patients aged 70 or more had the hereditary form (10% of women). [S2]
- **Stage with the Gillmore (UK National Amyloidosis Centre) system:** stage I is NT-proBNP 3000 ng/L or less with eGFR 45 ml/min or more; stage III is NT-proBNP above 3000 with eGFR below 45; the rest are stage II. Median survival: 69.2, 46.7 and 24.1 months. [S2]

## Choose treatment (ATTRv-PN)

- **Treat symptomatic patients with a confirmed amyloidogenic variant as early as possible.** Asymptomatic carriers have no approved preventive therapy; carriers with biopsy-proven amyloid but no disease features need close monitoring and a multidisciplinary decision. [S1]
- ⚠️ **First line: a second-generation silencer, vutrisiran (siRNA) or eplontersen (ASO);** neither is superior. Vutrisiran is also studied and approved for ATTR-CM. [S1]
- **Patisiran (heavier burden: IV infusions with steroid premedication) and inotersen (more safety issues and monitoring) are second line.** Patients stable on patisiran who prefer to continue may stay on it. [S1]
- **Tafamidis (20 mg, or 61 mg with mixed phenotype) is first choice if the patient wants oral therapy,** and first line for very early FAP1 disease with p.(Val50Met), but it is a bridge: switch to a silencer at any sign of progression. It is not approved in stage 2. [S1]
- **Diflunisal** may work as well as tafamidis but carries gastrointestinal, cardiac and renal toxicity. **Acoramidis** is approved only for ATTR-CM. [S1]
- **Dosing burden:** vutrisiran is subcutaneous every three months; eplontersen monthly by autoinjector at home; patisiran IV every three weeks with premedication; inotersen weekly subcutaneous with monitoring for thrombocytopenia and kidney harm. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Approved ATTRv-PN therapies and doses in Germany (S1 Table 1):** tafamidis 20 mg orally once daily (61 mg once daily for ATTR-CM), stage 1; diflunisal 250 mg orally twice daily, stages 1–2 (monitor kidney function, GI tolerance and cardiovascular risk); inotersen 284 mg subcutaneously once weekly, stages 1–2 (platelets, UPCR and eGFR, vitamin A supplementation); patisiran 0.3 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks with premedication, stages 1–2 (infusion reactions, vitamin A); vutrisiran 25 mg subcutaneously every 3 months, stages 1–2 (vitamin A); eplontersen 45 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks by autoinjector, stages 1–2 (vitamin A). Coutinho stage 1 walks unaided, stage 2 walks with help, stage 3 is wheelchair-bound or bedridden; gene silencing started earlier may be continued in individual patients who reach stage 3. [S1]
- **Practical choice (S1 Table 2):** vutrisiran is often preferred in stage 1–2 PN for convenience (quarterly injection, no premedication), and eplontersen is a monthly home autoinjector option; inotersen and patisiran are not recommended now that second-generation silencers are available. Tafamidis and diflunisal are not recommended when large-fibre neuropathy is present; tafamidis is most relevant when the cardiac phenotype is prominent. Diflunisal carries NSAID risks: avoid or limit it in kidney disease, significant heart failure or high GI bleeding risk. Acoramidis (oral, twice daily) has no PN approval and is recommended for ATTRv-CM only. [S1]

## Monitor and switch

- **Monitor regularly for side effects and response** using a sensible combination of tests, ideally by the same trained examiner each visit; progression is any new or worsening disease-specific sign or symptom. [S1]
- **If neuropathy progresses on a stabiliser, switch straight to a second-generation silencer;** if it progresses on a silencer, check serum TTR (prealbumin) to confirm suppression. Combining a silencer with a stabiliser is costly and unproven; reserve it for severe progression, decided by a multidisciplinary board. [S1]
- **Keep treating when ambulation is lost on a silencer;** stopping on progression is considered ethically questionable. [S1]
- ⚠️ **All gene silencers lower vitamin A: supplement it.** [S1]

## Cardiomyopathy and other organs

- ⚠️ **Cardiac involvement (ATTR-CM) causes most of the morbidity and mortality:** restrictive cardiomyopathy with HFpEF, arrhythmias (especially AF) and conduction disease that may need a pacemaker. Check the heart regularly even in neuropathy-led disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Heart-failure drugs behave differently in amyloid (S2):** diuretics (a loop diuretic with a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist) are central but can drop preload and kidney function; beta-blockers and non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers are poorly tolerated, and profound hypotension on starting a beta-blocker is itself a clue; ACE inhibitors, ARBs and sacubitril/valsartan have no supporting evidence. If digoxin is needed for AF rate control, use 0.125 mg/day or less with level, renal and electrolyte monitoring. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Intracardiac thrombus is common, even in sinus rhythm:** consider anticoagulation with atrial mechanical dysfunction, do not rely on CHA2DS2-VASc, and do a TOE before cardioversion even when anticoagulated. Warfarin or a DOAC is generally safe. [S2]
- **Tafamidis eligibility (S2):** NYHA class I–III, preferably I–II (class IV is not eligible); age is no bar, but decide individually at 90 or over. Probable exclusions: 6-minute walk under 100 m, expected survival under two years, severe uncorrected aortic stenosis, or eGFR below 25 ml/min/1.73 m2. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Three drugs are approved for ATTR-CM: tafamidis at 61 mg (not 20 mg), acoramidis and vutrisiran.** For predominantly cardiac disease (wild-type or variant), therapy includes any of these; in mixed neurological-cardiac disease, vutrisiran is an important option. [S1]
- **Eye involvement** (vitreous opacities, glaucoma) can progress despite systemic therapy; CNS involvement (leptomeningeal disease, cognitive decline, seizures, haemorrhage) is emerging as survival improves, because no approved drug reaches the brain. [S1]
- **Prescribing belongs to experienced specialists** in amyloidosis or neuromuscular centres, working with cardiologists, within specialised care pathways. [S1]
