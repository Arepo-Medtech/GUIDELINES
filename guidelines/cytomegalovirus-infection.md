# Cytomegalovirus infection after solid organ transplantation

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** CMV risk stratification, diagnostic testing, prevention (prophylaxis or pre-emptive therapy), treatment and drug resistance in adult solid organ transplant recipients. Haematopoietic stem cell transplant CMV, congenital CMV and HIV-associated CMV retinitis are not covered.

> ✅ **PARAPHRASED, HASH-ANCHORED.** 24 claims paraphrased from **The Transplantation Society International CMV Consensus Group (Kotton, Kumar, Humar et al.), Transplantation (Wolters Kluwer) — *The Fourth International Consensus Guidelines on the Management of Cytomegalovirus in Solid Organ Transplantation*** (2025; origin: international). **37 anchors re-checkable by machine; 1 doses.** The source's words are not reproduced: its licence is *CC BY-NC-ND 4.0*.

> ⚠️ International guideline (Transplantation Society International CMV Consensus Group, 2025). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **Solid organ transplant only.** The PBS CMV rows also cover other settings (for example stem cell transplant); no Australian transplant CMV guideline was found, and ECIL-7 (stem cell transplant) is paywalled.

## Risk and diagnosis

- **Before transplant, test CMV IgG serology in both donor and recipient to stratify risk** (strong, high). Do not use CMV IgM or combined IgG/IgM tests (strong, low). [S1]
- **Equivocal serology:** in adults, treat an equivocal donor as positive, and place an equivocal recipient in the highest applicable risk group for post-transplant decisions (strong, low). [S1]
- **Measure CMV with a quantitative nucleic acid test calibrated to the WHO standard,** for diagnosis, pre-emptive surveillance and treatment monitoring (strong, high). Read changes on a log10 scale, and keep the same specimen type throughout monitoring (strong, high). [S1]
- **CMV retinitis is a clinical diagnosis made on eye examination;** blood CMV DNA rarely predicts it. [S1]

## Prevention

- **Every at-risk recipient needs a prevention plan: universal prophylaxis, or surveillance with pre-emptive therapy** (strong, high). Routine DNAemia monitoring is not needed while on correctly dosed prophylaxis (strong, moderate). [S1]
- **High-risk D+/R– kidney recipients:** prophylaxis (strong, high) or pre-emptive therapy (strong, moderate) are both acceptable; for prophylaxis use valganciclovir, ganciclovir or letermovir (strong, high). [S1]
- **Heart and lung recipients (D+/R– or R+): prophylaxis is suggested over pre-emptive therapy,** as it is linked to less CMV and fewer indirect effects on the graft and survival (weak, low). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Prophylaxis length for D+/R–:** kidney 6 months (strong, high); liver, heart and pancreas 3 to 6 months; lung 12 months (strong, moderate), which some experts extend. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Prophylaxis length for R+:** 3 months for routine kidney, pancreas, liver and heart recipients; 3 to 6 months with more potent immunosuppression (lymphocyte-depleting antibodies, desensitisation) or intestinal and composite grafts; lung 6–12 months (strong, moderate). [S1]
- **If valganciclovir or ganciclovir prophylaxis is not tolerated, switch to pre-emptive therapy or to letermovir prophylaxis** (weak, low). [S1]
- **Pre-emptive strategy:** test CMV at least weekly for 12–16 weeks after transplant (less often raises disease risk), with lower treatment thresholds for D+/R– than R+ (strong, moderate). [S1]
- **When DNAemia crosses the threshold in a well patient, treat with valganciclovir (strong, high) until it clears, for at least 2 weeks;** IV ganciclovir is less preferred unless absorption is a concern. [S1]
- **With D–/R–, letermovir prophylaxis or a pre-emptive approach, cover other herpesviruses** (varicella, herpes simplex) with aciclovir, famciclovir or valaciclovir (strong, high). Use leukoreduced or CMV-seronegative blood, especially for D–/R– (strong, moderate). [S1]
- **Low-dose valganciclovir prophylaxis is not recommended** (weak, low). [S1]
- **Rejection treated with lymphocyte-depleting antibodies should restart prophylaxis or pre-emptive therapy for 4–12 weeks** (weak, moderate). [S1]

## Treatment

- ⚠️ **First-line treatment with normal kidney function: oral valganciclovir 900 mg or IV ganciclovir 5 mg/kg, each 12-hourly** (strong, moderate). Oral for mild to moderate disease if the patient can take and keep it down; IV for severe or life-threatening disease, stepping down to oral once improving. [S1]
- **Oral ganciclovir, aciclovir and valaciclovir are not treatments for CMV disease** (strong, moderate); letermovir monotherapy lacks evidence and resistance develops easily. [S1]
- **Do not change drugs in the first 2 weeks if the patient is improving clinically,** even if DNAemia is flat or rising, as it often rises in week one. Suspect resistance after more than 4 weeks' cumulative antiviral exposure with failure despite at least 2 weeks of proper dosing. [S1]
- **Monitor weekly DNAemia and kidney function (Cockcroft–Gault creatinine clearance, not eGFR) during treatment.** Reduce doses only for worsening kidney function, not for leukopenia alone; for leukopenia, consider G-CSF or adjust other marrow-suppressing drugs. [S1]
- **Treat for at least 2 weeks and until the disease has resolved and DNAemia is below the laboratory's threshold** (strong, moderate). Secondary prophylaxis is not routine but may be used in high-risk situations, typically for 8–12 weeks. [S1]
- **Without concurrent rejection, consider reducing immunosuppression** in severe disease, poor response, high viral load or cytopenia (weak, very low). [S1]
- **If valganciclovir or ganciclovir is not tolerated, use maribavir or foscarnet second-line** (strong, low), chosen on viral load, nephrotoxicity risk and severity. [S1]

## Drug resistance

- **Genotype for resistance after at least 4 weeks of antiviral exposure with a refractory viral-load response after at least 2 weeks of optimal dosing** (strong, high), on a sample whose viral load exceeds 1000 IU/mL (3 log10), so the mutation read-out is reliable. [S1]
- **Maribavir is the main alternative for ganciclovir- or foscarnet-resistant CMV** (strong, high). Foscarnet is suggested first in a clinically unwell patient with a high viral load and suspected or proven ganciclovir resistance (weak, low). [S1]
