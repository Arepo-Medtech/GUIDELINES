# Central precocious puberty

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** children with suspected or confirmed central (gonadotropin-dependent) precocious puberty: who to watch, diagnostic tests and brain MRI, when to treat, GnRH agonist therapy, adverse effects, monitoring, stopping and long-term outcomes. ## Recognise

- **Precocious puberty means puberty starting before 8 years in girls or before 9 years in boys.** [S1]
- **Keep a closer eye on puberty in children with obesity, those born small for gestational age or growth-restricted, and those with a family history of early puberty** (all conditional). In obesity, bone age can run ahead without true puberty, so this must be told apart from CPP. [S1]

## Diagnose

- **Measure bone age (strong),** but treat it only as a support: even a bone age more than 2 SD ahead of chronological age does not prove CPP. [S1]
- **A GnRH stimulation test is recommended (strong).** In Korea, a stimulated peak LH above 5 IU/L defines CPP. Triptorelin 0.1 mg can replace LHRH as the stimulus, with the same cut-off, but LH peaks about 3 hours later. ⚠️ Obesity can blunt the LH response, so interpret with care. [S1]
- **Diagnosis rests on the whole picture:** Tanner stage II or more, faster growth velocity and advanced bone age, together with the stimulation test. [S1]
- **A random unstimulated LH on an ultrasensitive assay is a supporting test (conditional):** below 0.3 IU/L suggests prepuberty and above 0.83 IU/L suggests puberty (ICMA method). [S1]
- **Pelvic ultrasound can support the diagnosis in girls (conditional);** in a meta-analysis a uterus of 3.2 cm or longer separated CPP from isolated thelarche with 81.8% sensitivity and 82.0% specificity. **Breast ultrasound is not recommended (strong).** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Brain MRI:** recommended for every girl with CPP under 6 and for older girls with neurological signs (strong); considered selectively for girls starting puberty at 6–8 years without such signs (conditional); and recommended for boys with CPP (conditional). In girls, MRI abnormalities were found in 25% of those under 6 but 3% of those aged 6 to 8. [S1]

## Decide whether to treat

- **Watch for 3 to 6 months first to see how fast puberty is moving (strong):** most suspected cases need no treatment because the development is early or transient. [S1]
- **The treatment decision weighs age, speed of progression, Tanner stage, growth velocity, bone age and loss of predicted adult height.** Slowly progressive puberty typically shows no Tanner-stage change over 6 months or more of observation. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat rapidly progressive precocious puberty (strong).** In a meta-analysis, GnRH agonist therapy added 3.2 cm to adult height on average: about 5.1 cm when started before age 8, but 2.5 cm when started after 8. [S1]
- **Early puberty and slowly progressive puberty are not generally treated,** as untreated adult height is close to mid-parental height. Little height is gained if bone age is already well advanced, and girls treated after age 8 may gain none. [S1]
- **Treatment can be considered for girls at high risk of psychosocial distress from early menarche (conditional),** although long-term follow-up has not shown worse adult wellbeing than the general population. [S1]
- **S2 (Mexico) treats by age at assessment:** a GnRH agonist is recommended for CPP before age 7 in girls or 8 in boys (strong); after those ages, consider it only for a poor adult-height outlook or marked psychosocial impact that counselling alone does not settle, weighing the endocrinologist's judgement and the family's view. [S2]
- **Height evidence (S2):** in three trials of girls whose puberty began between 7 and 9, GnRH agonists did not change adult height; one high-bias trial in girls under 7 found 4.1 cm more with treatment. [S2]

## Treat

- ⚠️ **A GnRH agonist is the standard treatment (strong): leuprolide or triptorelin at the approved dose (strong).** Choose the dosing interval to suit the family, because 4-week and 3-month formulations worked equally well (strong). [S1]
- **Weight-based leuprolide dosing is no longer advised,** for lack of evidence. [S1]
- **Do not use GnRH antagonists or aromatase inhibitors for CPP (strong):** evidence of benefit is insufficient and neither is approved for it. [S1]
- **Growth hormone added to the agonist is optional (conditional),** for markedly slowed growth or a poor adult-height outlook. Discuss the daily injections and cost with the family first. [S1]
- **S2: use a depot GnRH agonist, at the lowest dose that suppresses puberty, monthly or quarterly;** heavier children may need more. Follow the maker's preparation instructions (for example, do not shake the vial). [S2]
- **Leuprorelin depot doses and suppression rates (S2 dose table):** monthly 3.75 mg succeeded in 75–78% and 7.5 mg in 97%; quarterly 11.25 mg in 67–86% (stimulated LH below 4 IU/L), 22.5 mg in 92% and 30 mg in 95–97% (the 30 mg product is not labelled for CPP). Success tends to rise with later doses. [S2]
- **Triptorelin depot doses and suppression rates (S2 dose table):** 3.75 mg every 28 days, 90–100% at 4 months; 11.25 mg every 84 days, 87.6% at 3 months, 92.8% at 6 and 89.1% at 12; 22.5 mg every 24 weeks, 93.2% at 6 months. [S2]
- **If suppression is inadequate (S2),** first confirm adherence and correct preparation and injection, then raise the dose or shorten the interval between injections. Medroxyprogesterone is not recommended for blockade, except as a fallback when no GnRH agonist is available. [S2]

## Adverse effects

- **Minor, passing effects do not require stopping (conditional):** headache and hot flushes usually settle. Local reactions such as sterile abscess are uncommon (four of 621, 0.6%, in one Korean study); if they persist, switch formulation. A withdrawal bleed after starting is expected, but bleeding lasting over 2 months needs another explanation. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Assess and treat at once for slipped capital femoral epiphysis, pseudotumour cerebri or anaphylaxis (strong).** SCFE risk is higher with obesity, male sex, a growth spurt or past radiotherapy, so investigate any hip or leg pain. [S1]
- **Suppression is not guaranteed (S2):** with leuprorelin, reported failure ranges from 33% (11.25 mg 3-monthly, judged at 3 months) to under 5% (7.5 mg monthly, or 22.5 mg and 30 mg 3-monthly), and failure becomes less common the longer treatment runs. [S2]

## Monitor and stop

- **Review response every 3 to 6 months (strong), sooner if failure is suspected (conditional).** Effect shows within 2 to 6 months: breast or testicular stage stops advancing and growth slows to prepubertal rates, though pubic hair may still progress with adrenal maturation. Check bone age every 6 months to a year. [S1]
- **Judge failure on the whole picture,** not a single hormone result: progressing Tanner stage and bone age, growth that fails to slow, and unsuppressed hormones together. Thresholds sometimes used (basal LH above 0.6 IU/L, oestradiol above 30 pg/mL, testosterone above 30 ng/dL, stimulated LH above 4 IU/L) are unreliable alone. [S1]
- **When to stop is individual (conditional).** Adult height benefits most when treatment runs to a bone age of 12–12.5 years (girls) or 13–14 years (boys); continuing in girls past a chronological age of 13 adds nothing. Menarche follows 12 to 18 months after stopping on average, sometimes up to 2 years. [S1]
- **S2 monitoring schedule:** a clinical and hormonal review (LH, sex steroids) 3 months after the first dose; then clinical review at least every 6 months (Tanner stage, breast diameter or testicular volume, growth velocity, adverse effects, psychosocial impact); bone age at least yearly. [S2]
- **S2 hormone checks in girls:** a basal LH below 0.3 IU/L with prepubertal oestradiol and no clinical progression means blockade is working; draw it in the week before the next dose. Do a stimulated LH if there are clinical signs of progression or basal LH is above 0.3 IU/L. **In boys, follow testicular volume;** routine LH stimulation is not recommended. [S2]
- **S2 on stopping:** stop when the child is psychosocially ready for puberty and predicted height is near family target height; do not continue beyond age 12 in girls or 14 in boys; follow until puberty is clinically seen to restart. [S2]
- **After stopping, follow the child until adult height is reached and gonadal function has returned** (menarche in girls). [S1]
- **Long-term outcomes are reassuring (strong):** GnRH agonist treatment does not impair fertility, raise PCOS risk, or harm metabolic, bone or mental health, so routine long-term surveillance for these is not recommended. [S1]

## How to read S1's grades

- **What S1's grades mean:** a strong 'do' applies to most patients, and a strong 'do not' means most patients should not receive it; a conditional grade means selective use after weighing benefit, harm, cost and the patient's preference, and a conditional 'do not' still allows limited, careful use. [S1]
