# Delayed puberty and pubertal induction in hypogonadism

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** children and adolescents with delayed or absent puberty: when to refer, separating constitutional delay (CDGP) from permanent hypogonadism, and inducing puberty with testosterone or gonadotropins (hCG ± FSH) in boys and 17β-oestradiol in girls, with monitoring. **Central precocious puberty is not covered** (no readable source; see Unresolved). Adult male hypogonadism: `androgen-deficiency-in-men`. GH deficiency with precocious puberty: `growth-hormone-therapy-children`. High prolactin as a cause of delayed puberty: `hyperprolactinaemia`.

## When to assess

- **Boys:** refer for expert assessment if puberty is delayed (puberty SD score below −2), there are no signs of puberty by age 14, or puberty is not progressing. [S1]
- **Girls:** refer if there are no signs of puberty by age 13 or the puberty SD score is below −2. Girls with a condition that puts them at risk of hypogonadism should be assessed from age 8; at-risk boys from age 9. [S1]
- **Treatment belongs with a multidisciplinary team** (paediatric and adult endocrinology, psychology, urology, gynaecology, genetics, nursing, as needed). [S1]

## Constitutional delay or hypogonadism?

- **CDGP is the commonest cause of delayed puberty in boys (about 63%)**, and in most cases (50–75%) a parent also had late puberty. In girls it accounts for about a third of cases. [S1]
- **CDGP is a diagnosis of exclusion:** rule out congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (CHH), primary gonadal failure, chronic illness and tumours. It is confirmed only when puberty then progresses on its own. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Clues to CHH rather than CDGP:** cryptorchidism (especially bilateral), micropenis, midline defects, reduced or absent smell, deafness, or kidney anomalies. [S1]
- **Assessment includes** history (including family timing of puberty), Tanner stage and testicular volume by orchidometer, growth velocity, gonadotropins and sex steroids, and bone age. Consider a karyotype or microarray in boys with high gonadotropins, atypical genitalia, malformations or features of a sex-chromosome disorder. [S1]
- **When CHH and CDGP cannot be told apart,** it may be necessary to wait beyond age 14 before starting induction. In girls, rule out spontaneous puberty and treatable causes first. [S1]

## Pubertal induction in boys

- **All boys with permanent hypogonadism should have pubertal induction,** started by age 12 if there is no pubertal development and the diagnosis carries a high risk of hypogonadism. [S1]
- **Induce with testosterone, starting low and increasing gradually to the adult dose,** for example testosterone enanthate 50 mg monthly, transdermal testosterone 10 mg every other day, or oral testosterone undecanoate 40 mg daily. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not use long-acting testosterone undecanoate injection to start puberty:** it can advance bone age too fast and cut final height. [S1]
- **Late-diagnosed young men with CHH** are often started on higher doses (testosterone enanthate 200–250 mg monthly, then every 2–3 weeks) for faster virilisation. [S1]
- **Monitor every 3–6 months:** pubertal and skeletal maturation and height velocity, plus local reactions, gynaecomastia, priapism, haematocrit, liver function and behaviour. [S1]
- **Watch for gynaecomastia:** it occurs in up to a third of boys on gonadotropins or testosterone, usually at too-high doses, and often reverses when the dose is adjusted. [S1]
- **S2 in CHH: build up to the full adult testosterone dose over about 18–24 months,** depending on the age at the start. A slow rise imitates natural puberty, gets the most height and leaves time for psychosexual development. [S2]
- **Set the injection interval by a trough testosterone level aimed at the low end of normal,** so the patient is not hypogonadal before the next dose. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Testes that grow on testosterone suggest reversal:** reassess the axis off testosterone. S2 puts spontaneous recovery at about 10–20%, including people with variants in known CHH genes; it may not last and nothing yet predicts it, so monitoring continues. [S2]

## Boys with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism: hCG and FSH

- ⚠️ **In boys with HH, discuss both options:** testosterone or gonadotropins. Neither is proven better for virilisation; the choice depends on preference, ease of use, availability and fertility plans. [S1]
- **Testosterone virilises but does not grow the testes or start sperm production;** gonadotropins can. [S1]
- **hCG can be used alone or with recombinant FSH,** but the combination works much better for testicular growth and sperm production. FSH pre-treatment before hCG may be better still. Gonadotropins need several injections a week. [S1]
- **Combined FSH/hCG for 6–24 months** grows the testes in almost all boys and produces sperm in 80–95% of those without undescended testes. Before switching to testosterone, do a semen analysis and consider sperm banking. [S1]
- **CHH can reverse in 5–20%,** so a short break (about 4 months) after gonadotropin induction and before testosterone may be considered to check for recovery. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Doses when testes are under 4 mL (S2):** GnRH by pump at 25 ng/kg per pulse every 2 h (titrated to trough testosterone), or combined gonadotropins given 2–3 times weekly depending on the product: hCG 1,000–1,500 IU plus FSH 75–150 IU per injection. [S2]
- **Testes over 4 mL and no history of cryptorchidism:** hCG on its own can start sperm production; add FSH if there are still no sperm after 3–6 months. In the most severe cases (testes under 4 mL), FSH is given alone first, before hCG, to expand the immature Sertoli and germ cells. [S2]
- **Poorer fertility outlook:** cryptorchidism (such men often need 18–24 months of treatment), prepubertal testes under 4 mL and low inhibin B. Around 75% develop sperm, though the count is seldom normal, and a low count does not rule out fathering a child. [S2]
- **After conception, switch back to testosterone** but keep fertility treatment going through the first trimester in case of miscarriage; bank sperm once fertility is achieved, since it is not guaranteed next time. [S2]

## Pubertal induction in girls

- **Start induction at about 11 when hypogonadism is confirmed and there are no signs of puberty, or at 13 if constitutional delay is suspected.** For CDGP, a short 3–6 month course of low-dose oestradiol may be used, with monitoring for spontaneous progress. [S1]
- **Use 17β-oestradiol** (oral or transdermal) rather than ethinyl oestradiol or conjugated oestrogens, and titrate the dose to pubertal progress. [S1]
- **Review at least every 3–6 months** (weight, height, blood pressure, Tanner stage). Add progesterone after breakthrough bleeding or after at least 2 years, then move to adult hormone replacement. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Oestradiol induction doses (S2, CHH):** transdermal is preferred, starting no earlier than age 10 at 0.05–0.07 μg/kg at night (0.08–0.12 μg/kg for an older girl for whom breast growth is the priority). Raise the dose slowly over 12–24 months, then add a cyclic progestogen (or add it after the first period). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Adult maintenance (S2):** oestradiol 1–2 mg by mouth, or a patch delivering 50 μg a day, or 0.06% gel at 1–2 pumps a day, with progesterone 200 mg on 14 days of each cycle against endometrial hyperplasia. This gives monthly bleeds but no ovulation; fertility needs gonadotropins or GnRH. [S2]
- **Women with CHH can be told their infertility is treatable and the outlook good:** a low AMH does not mean a poor ovarian reserve. Ovulation is induced with pulsatile GnRH (3–10 μg per pulse under the skin every 90 min) or FSH, usually 75–150 IU a day, then hCG or LH, aiming for one ovulation. [S2]

## Infancy and long-term care (CHH)

- **Infant boys with cryptorchidism, with or without micropenis:** measure gonadotropins, sex steroids and inhibin B at 4–8 weeks (mini-puberty). Do the same for any baby of a parent with CHH, with genetic testing if the parent's variant is known. Hypospadias argues against CHH. [S2]
- **Measure bone density at final height and again after 2 years of treatment;** sex steroids improve it but do not fully correct it. Adherence matters for metabolic health too: stopping for just 2 weeks raised fasting insulin in young men with CHH. [S2]
