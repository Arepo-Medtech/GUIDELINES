# Hairy cell leukaemia (HCL) in adults

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, treatment indications, first-line purine-analogue therapy, relapsed/refractory options and supportive care for classic hairy cell leukaemia in adults. HCL-like disorders (HCL variant, splenic diffuse red pulp lymphoma) are noted in Unresolved. Related: `chronic-lymphocytic-leukaemia` (other indolent B-cell disease).

## Recognise

- **HCL is rare (2-3% of leukaemias), mostly in men (5:1)**; median age at diagnosis is 63 in men and 59 in women. [S1]
- **It presents with marrow failure (infections, anaemia, bleeding), a large spleen, or hairy cells found on a routine count.** Lymph nodes and liver are rarely enlarged. About 20% have an opportunistic infection at diagnosis, and autoimmune disease affects about one in four. [S1]
- **Low monocytes are a clue** that sets HCL apart from look-alike disorders, but analysers often count hairy cells as monocytes and hide it. [S1]

## Diagnose

- **Diagnosis rests on four tests:** blood count with film review, trephine marrow biopsy (aspirates are often dry), multiparameter flow cytometry, and BRAF V600E testing. [S1]
- **Flow cytometry score:** one point each for CD11c, CD25, CD103 and CD123; HCL scores 3 or more in 98% of cases, look-alikes score below 3. [S1]
- **Test for BRAF V600E at diagnosis** (preferably on marrow aspirate): it is present in over 90% of HCL and usually absent in look-alikes. A karyotype is not recommended because it does not change treatment. [S1]

## When to treat

- **Treat only when a criterion is met:** Hb below 11 g/dL, platelets below 100 G/L or neutrophils below 1 G/L; symptomatic organomegaly; constitutional symptoms; or recurrent infections. Otherwise watch and wait. [S1]
- **Delay treatment until an active infection is controlled.** If that is not possible, bridge with low-dose BRAF inhibitor (not myelosuppressive), then switch to cladribine plus rituximab. [S1]

## First-line treatment

- ⚠️ **Fit patients: cladribine plus rituximab, given together** (rituximab from day 1 of cladribine, 8 weekly infusions). This beat purine analogue alone and the sequential schedule; response and CR were both 100%, with 97% reaching undetectable MRD. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Cladribine dose:** subcutaneous 0.1-0.14 mg/kg/day once daily for 5 days is preferred over IV 0.1 mg/kg/day by continuous infusion for 7 days. [S1]
- **A purine analogue alone remains an option and can give durable responses.** Pentostatin is 4 mg/m² IV every 2 weeks for one year; stop it and reconsider if there is no response at 6 months. [S1]
- **Unfit or very elderly patients:** purine analogue alone, or two or three days of subcutaneous cladribine instead of five. If unfit even for that, a BRAF inhibitor with or without rituximab, or interferon alfa. Rituximab alone is not recommended. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Kidney function:** cladribine is contraindicated at creatinine clearance of 50 mL/min or less, and pentostatin below 60 mL/min. Vemurafenib is an alternative if BRAF is mutated. [S1]
- **In pregnancy, delay treatment until after delivery;** if that is not possible, interferon alfa is preferred. [S1]
- 🇦🇺 **Australian protocol (eviQ ID 364): cladribine 0.14 mg/kg by IV infusion over 2 hours on days 1 to 5, as one course.** Most patients respond to that single course; if another is needed, wait until every blood-count toxicity has recovered. eviQ lists cladribine as PBS Authority. [S2]
- **eviQ accepts four published cladribine schedules as reasonable:** 0.1 mg/kg/day by continuous IV infusion for seven days; 0.14 mg/kg/day IV over 2 hours for five days; 0.15 mg/kg IV over 2 hours weekly for six weeks; or 0.085 mg/kg/day subcutaneously for seven days. It picked the 5-day IV schedule because it fits outpatient care in a short, fixed window. [S2]
- 🇦🇺 **Australian cladribine-rituximab protocol (eviQ ID 4383; HCL and HCL variant): rituximab 375 mg/m² IV on days 1, 8, 15, 22, 29, 36, 43 and 50, with cladribine 0.14 mg/kg IV on days 1 to 5, as one cycle.** eviQ flags it as resting on limited evidence, notes that the cladribine schedule and number of rituximab doses differ between studies, and lists rituximab on the PBS general schedule. [S2]
- **Rituximab carries a high risk of infusion reactions.** eviQ's default premedication, 60 minutes before each dose, is oral paracetamol 1,000 mg and loratadine 10 mg with IV hydrocortisone 100 mg; consider holding antihypertensives around the infusion because blood pressure can fall. [S2]
- **Kidney function (eviQ):** there are too few data to set a cladribine dose change for kidney impairment, so treat known or suspected impairment cautiously and monitor regularly for toxicity. [S2]

## Relapse

- **At relapse, repeat the full work-up** (examination, blood count, trephine, flow cytometry) and retest BRAF, IGHV status, VH4-34 use and TP53, which carry prognostic weight. [S1]
- **Choice by length of first response:** more than five years and fit, repeat cladribine plus rituximab; two to five years, choose between cladribine plus rituximab and vemurafenib plus rituximab; primary refractory or under two years, vemurafenib plus rituximab. [S1]
- **Vemurafenib plus rituximab** (off-label; in a single-centre trial, vemurafenib 960 mg twice daily for 8 weeks, rituximab 375 mg/m² for eight infusions over 18 weeks) gave response and CR of 87%. Watch for skin cancers, joint, eye, pancreatic and liver toxicity, and monitor the ECG for QT prolongation. [S1]
- **Other relapse options:** dabrafenib 150 mg twice daily with trametinib 2 mg daily; moxetumomab where still available. Beyond the third line, avoid further purine analogue, and use ibrutinib or venetoclax (with or without rituximab) after BRAF-inhibitor exposure. [S1]

## Supportive care and follow-up

- ⚠️ **After treatment, give Pneumocystis prophylaxis (cotrimoxazole) and herpes zoster prophylaxis (valaciclovir or aciclovir)** for at least six months and until CD4 exceeds 0.2 G/L. With purine analogues, start them two weeks after the analogue because of skin reactions. [S1]
- **Use G-CSF with broad-spectrum antibiotics for febrile neutropenia.** Vaccinate against influenza, SARS-CoV-2 and pneumococcus before treatment where possible, and consider irradiated blood products after purine analogues. [S1]
- **Second cancers, especially skin cancers, are common in HCL whatever the treatment:** arrange a full skin check with a dermatologist at least yearly. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Cladribine depletes CD4 and CD8 T cells deeply and for a long time, so expect more opportunistic infections during treatment and for six months afterwards** (eviQ). It recommends antimicrobial prophylaxis; Pneumocystis prophylaxis is left to the treating clinician (for example trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 160/800 mg, one tablet twice a day on two days each week, or one tablet on three days each week), and antiviral prophylaxis should be considered, above all with lymphopenia. [S2]
- **Tumour lysis (eviQ):** assess each patient's risk; allopurinol 100 mg by mouth once a day, from the first treatment day for 2 weeks, is recommended. [S2]
- **Blood tests (eviQ):** check FBC, EUC, eGFR and LFTs before starting, and the FBC every week during and after treatment; myelosuppression is severe, so watch counts closely for the first 4 to 8 weeks. Postpone further treatment while neutrophils are below 1.5 or platelets below 100 unless the leukaemia itself is the cause. [S2]
- **Also before and during treatment (eviQ):** use irradiated blood components; screen for hepatitis B (HBsAg, anti-HBc and anti-HBs) first; avoid live vaccines; and watch for new neurological symptoms, since progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy has been reported. [S2]
