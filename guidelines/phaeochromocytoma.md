# Phaeochromocytoma and paraganglioma

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with phaeochromocytoma and paraganglioma (PPGL): biochemical and imaging diagnosis, preoperative alpha-blockade and volume repletion, surgery and postoperative care, hypertensive crisis, pregnancy, genetic testing and long-term surveillance. Neuroendocrine neoplasms generally, including MEN syndromes, are in `neuroendocrine-neoplasms`; orthostatic hypotension in `orthostatic-hypotension-and-pots`. Japanese biochemical cut-offs (fractionated catecholamines) also differ from Western metanephrine-first practice.

## Recognise

- **About 83% of tumours are adrenal (phaeochromocytoma) and 17% extra-adrenal (paraganglioma);** metastatic disease is 10–15% overall and about three times more frequent in paraganglioma. Incidence is about 5 per million person-years and rising with incidental detection. [S1]
- **Catecholamine excess** causes headache, palpitations, sweating and wide blood pressure swings, and can present as chest pain, takotsubo-type cardiomyopathy, acute heart failure, pulmonary oedema or shock. [S1]
- **Who to test (ESH):** suggestive symptoms, whether spontaneous or triggered by drugs; cardiovascular events such as takotsubo cardiomyopathy with suggestive features; any adrenal incidentaloma of 10 HU or more, hypertensive or not; lean people (BMI under 25) with type 2 diabetes; carriers of a susceptibility-gene variant or those with syndromic features; and anyone with a personal or family history of PPGL. [S2]

## Diagnose

- **Screen as an outpatient** using any one of: free metanephrines in plasma above the reference limit; creatinine-corrected metanephrines in a random urine sample more than three times the limit; or blood catecholamines more than three times the limit. (1B) [S1]
- **If screening is positive, confirm with** a 24-hour urine collection (metanephrines more than three times, or catecholamines more than two times, the reference limit) or plasma free metanephrines; combine biochemistry with imaging because some tumours secrete little. (1B) [S1]
- **Sample carefully:** exercise, stress, posture, temperature, venepuncture, foods and drugs alter results. For plasma metanephrines, fast, lie supine 20–30 minutes and freeze within 30 minutes of separation, or false positives are common. Most antihypertensives do not interfere. [S1]
- **ESH (Western practice) tests first with free normetanephrine and metanephrine in plasma or urine, preferably by LC-MS/MS:** both exclude PPGL with a negative predictive value over 99% at about 94% specificity, and plasma is better in high-risk patients (incidentaloma or surveillance). Add plasma 3-methoxytyramine for dopamine-producing, SDHx or possibly metastatic tumours. [S2]
- **Draw plasma after at least 20 minutes lying down, fasting and without strenuous exercise** (24-hour urinary free metanephrines if that is not possible). A rise more than two-fold above the cut-off, or in two or more metabolites, justifies imaging; smaller single rises need weighing against pre-test risk, and a clonidine suppression test can help. [S2]
- ⚠️ **High-dose dexamethasone can trigger a hypertensive crisis:** use glucocorticoids cautiously when PPGL is suspected. [S1]
- **Image to locate, stage and look for metastases:** CT for primary tumours and lung or liver deposits; MRI for head and neck paraganglioma, adrenal cortical differentiation, and in children or when repeated scans are needed. 123I-MIBG is highly specific; 18F-FDG PET is better for metastases, especially with SDHx variants. [S1]
- **ESH imaging choices:** 68Ga-DOTA-somatostatin-analogue PET is first choice for head and neck paraganglioma, suspected SDH-related tumours and metastatic disease; 18F-FDOPA PET suits VHL or HIF2A tumours; 123I-MIBG is a fallback when PET is not available. Functional imaging is advised to exclude metastases in phaeochromocytomas over 5.0 cm and with SDHA/B/D variants. [S2]
- **Drugs and foods that can distort catecholamine results (S1 Table 3):** tricyclic antidepressants, levodopa, adrenergic agonists such as decongestant nasal sprays (naphazoline, tramazoline), many psychotropics (buspirone, for example), prochlorperazine, clonidine withdrawal, alcohol, amphetamines and metoclopramide; and caffeinated coffee, bananas, citrus, vanilla-flavoured foods, tyramine-rich cheese and red wine. [S1]
- **Imaging strengths and limits (S1 Table 4):** CT offers high spatial resolution and a typical pattern (unenhanced attenuation above 10 HU, dense arterial enhancement with late washout); MRI typically shows low T1 and high T2 signal without loss on opposed-phase imaging; neither is specific, so adrenal cancer and metastasis must be excluded. 123I-MIBG is specific and surveys the whole body but is less sensitive, especially in metastatic and SDHx-variant disease; 111In-octreotide is specific and still sensitive with SDHx variants, though low in resolution; 18F-FDG PET is sensitive but non-specific; 18F-FDOPA PET is specific and sensitive even in metastatic and SDHx disease but is not approved in Japan. [S1]

## Prepare for surgery

- ⚠️ **Block catecholamine effects adequately before surgery (and in non-operated functional tumours)** to control blood pressure and prevent cardiovascular events. Surgery can release catecholamines, causing crisis, arrhythmia, ischaemia, pulmonary oedema and organ failure. (1C) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Begin alpha-blockade 7 to 14 days or more ahead of the operation** and titrate to blood pressure and heart rate targets. The guideline's first choice is a selective alpha-1 blocker (1B), adding a calcium channel blocker or metyrosine if needed (2C); non-selective alpha-blockers give better intraoperative haemodynamics, without a difference in complications or deaths. [S1]
- **Doxazosin (long-acting):** begin 1–2 mg/day at bedtime (first-dose postural hypotension) and increase every 2–3 days up to 16 mg until the target is reached. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Phenoxybenzamine (non-selective, non-competitive; the PBS drug) and doxazosin (alpha1-selective) are the usual choices,** both given twice daily, started 7 to 14 days or more before the operation and increased gradually to target. In the only randomised trial, time outside the blood pressure target during surgery did not differ, but phenoxybenzamine gave more haemodynamic stability. Normotensive patients should also be blocked. [S2]
- **Calcium channel blockers can be added to an alpha-blocker,** but are not advised alone unless blood pressure is normal or only mildly raised, or alpha-blockade causes severe orthostatic hypotension. Metyrosine is usually combined with an alpha-blocker; it is hard to obtain and sedating. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Never start a beta-blocker before the alpha-blocker** (risk of hypertensive attack; 1A). Add one several days after adequate alpha-blockade for tachycardia, arrhythmia, heart failure or ischaemic heart disease. Avoid labetalol and carvedilol because their beta effect is too strong. [S1]
- **Proposed targets:** seated blood pressure under 130/80 mmHg, standing systolic at least 90 mmHg, heart rate 60–70 bpm seated and 70–80 bpm standing. [S1]
- **Restore blood volume:** a normal-salt diet (9 g/day) from day three of alpha-blockade (plasma volume takes 7–14 days to rise), then 1–2 L of saline from the evening before surgery. [S1]
- **Metyrosine** (catecholamine synthesis inhibitor) helps when alpha-blockade is not enough, e.g. catecholamine cardiomyopathy: start 0.5 g/day, usual 2–3 g/day, maximum 4 g/day. It causes drowsiness, extrapyramidal effects and diarrhoea; patients must drink at least 2 L/day to avoid crystalluria. [S1]

## Surgery and after

- **Surgical removal is first-line (1A):** laparoscopic or robot-assisted adrenalectomy for small phaeochromocytomas (1B), open surgery for large or invasive tumours (2C). Consider partial adrenalectomy in familial disease with high bilateral risk (2C). [S1]
- ⚠️ **After resection, watch for severe hypotension and hypoglycaemia:** manage in ICU with frequent blood pressure, heart rate and glucose checks; monitor glucose for 48 hours, especially in patients on diabetes drugs. Treat hypotension with fluids and vasopressors. [S1]
- **Head and neck paragangliomas:** block symptomatic patients before surgery; completely asymptomatic ones may be exempted (weak evidence). When radical resection is unsafe, options include wait-and-scan, stereotactic radiosurgery, conventional radiotherapy or somatostatin-receptor-targeted radionuclide therapy, decided by a multidisciplinary team. [S2]
- **Check metanephrines (and 3-methoxytyramine where raised before) two to six weeks after surgery;** normal values confirm complete resection and no imaging is needed. If they stay high, or the tumour was non-functional, image at 3 and 6 months. [S2]
- **ESH follow-up: everyone for the first ten years,** because recurrence runs at about 1 per 100 person-years and doubles in genetic disease: yearly examination and metanephrines for functional tumours, imaging every two years for non-functional ones. High-risk patients (large tumours, paraganglioma, childhood onset, multifocal disease, SDHB) need stricter yearly checks including 3-methoxytyramine. [S2]

## Hypertensive crisis

- ⚠️ **Triggers of hypertensive crisis (S1 Table 5).** Daily life: bending over, exercise, a blow to the abdomen, overeating, tyramine-rich food (aged cheese, red wine), alcohol, sneezing, passing urine or stool, stress, smoking and pregnancy. Procedures: abdominal palpation, enemas, tumour biopsy and iodinated contrast. Drugs: dopamine antagonists such as metoclopramide (contraindicated by mouth and by injection; domperidone needs similar caution), glucagon, a beta-blocker on its own, tricyclics, SNRIs, MAO inhibitors and dexamethasone above 2 mg. Other treatments: CVD chemotherapy, 131I-MIBG or external radiotherapy, arterial embolisation, ablation and contrast media. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hypertensive crisis (3–11% incidence, about 15% mortality) needs hospital admission:** give intravenous phentolamine, then a continuous infusion, adding calcium channel blockers or nitrates as needed; switch to oral doxazosin once stable. (1B) [S1]
- **Initial aim:** diastolic pressure at or under 110 mmHg, then about 160/100 mmHg within 2–6 hours. Phentolamine-induced tachycardia is treated with an oral beta-blocker. [S1]

## Pregnancy, genetics and follow-up

- ⚠️ **In pregnancy, untreated PPGL is highly lethal for mother and baby:** image with ultrasound or MRI; use a selective alpha-blocker (plus nifedipine or amlodipine if needed); if removing the tumour before delivery, operate in the second trimester, no later than 24 weeks. [S1]
- ⚠️ **20–40% of PPGL is hereditary:** take a detailed family history, tell every patient about genetic testing, and test after genetic counselling (the US and European guidelines advise testing everyone). SDHB variants carry a 35–75% metastatic risk. [S1]
- **Follow-up:** lifelong after surgery for hereditary PPGL; at least 10 years for sporadic tumours at high recurrence risk (noradrenaline-dominant, 5 cm or larger, or paraganglioma outside the head and neck). Metastasis occurs in 10–15% of phaeochromocytomas and 35–70% of paragangliomas. [S1]
- **ESH genetics:** up to 40% carry a germline variant, so consider testing every patient (shared decision), using a targeted next-generation sequencing panel; SDHB carries the highest metastatic risk (30–70%). Offer relatives cascade testing, from age 5 for VHL and SDHB and from 10 for SDHA, SDHC and SDHD. [S2]

## Metastatic PPGL

- **About 10–20% develop metastases.** Before treating, stage the disease and show progression (usually imaging over 3 months), unless there are many liver, lung or brain deposits. Rapid progression: chemotherapy, e.g. CVD (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, dacarbazine), with responses up to 70% in SDHx carriers against 30% in sporadic disease. [S2]
- **Moderately progressive metastatic disease: consider radionuclide therapy with 177Lu-DOTATATE or 131I-MIBG;** external beam radiotherapy is for painful bone lesions or threatened cord compression. Lifelong follow-up with yearly metanephrines and re-staging imaging, more often if the burden is high. [S2]
