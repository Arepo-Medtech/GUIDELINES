## What counts as invasive

- **iGAS carries significant morbidity and mortality**, and household contacts of a case are at increased risk of secondary iGAS. [S1]
- **iGAS = GAS isolated from a normally sterile site:** bacteraemia/septicaemia · streptococcal toxic shock syndrome · necrotising fasciitis · pneumonia and empyema · meningitis · osteomyelitis or septic arthritis · retropharyngeal abscess. [S1]
- **Cellulitis, scarlet fever and pharyngitis are not invasive disease.** [S1]

## Who is a household contact

- **Household contact** = over 24 hours spent in the case's house, from 7 days before symptom onset until 24 hours after effective antibiotics began. [S1]
- **Secondary cases usually occur within a month, mostly in the first 7 days.** [S1]

## Management

- **Inform household contacts about iGAS features and give chemoprophylaxis as soon as possible.** [S1]
- **The evidence for chemoprophylaxis is limited** — some experts and guidelines recommend it, but it has not been studied extensively. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Whether or not prophylaxis is given, educate every household contact** on their raised risk and the signs needing prompt review: fever · sore throat · severe headache · severe muscle aches · vomiting or diarrhoea · cellulitis. [S1]
- **Do not throat-swab asymptomatic close contacts** to assess carriage — it does not help. [S1]
- **Asymptomatic household contacts need not isolate or restrict activities.** [S1]
- **Consult the local paediatric or infectious diseases team** for non-household close contacts, or if adherence to chemoprophylaxis is a concern. [S1]
