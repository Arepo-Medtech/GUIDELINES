# HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** who should be offered oral PrEP, the checks before starting, daily and on-demand dosing, monitoring, stopping and restarting, for adults in Australian primary care. Post-exposure prophylaxis and HIV diagnosis: see `hiv`. HIV treatment: see `hiv-antiretroviral-therapy`. Hepatitis B in PrEP users: see also `hepatitis-b`.

> ✅ **PARAPHRASED, HASH-ANCHORED.** 22 claims paraphrased from **ASHM Health (funded by the Australian Government) — *ASHM National PrEP Guidelines 2025: Prevent HIV by Prescribing PrEP*** (produced July 2025 (web edition last updated August 2025); origin: AU). **33 anchors re-checkable by machine; 2 doses.** The source's words are not reproduced: its licence is *©ASHM 2025 (the PDF states copyright only, with no reuse terms; the ASHM Copyright & Disclaimer page returned 404 on 2026-09-23)*.

> ⚠️ **On-demand ('2-1-1') dosing is off-label in Australia.** The TGA has approved daily tenofovir disoproxil/emtricitabine only; ASHM endorses on-demand dosing for suitable people anyway.

> ⚠️ **Long-acting injectable cabotegravir PrEP is not PBS-listed and is unavailable in Australia** (since 12 June 2024), so oral PrEP is the only funded option.

## Who prescribes, and for whom

- **Any GP, other medical specialist or authorised nurse practitioner can prescribe PrEP under a PBS streamlined authority;** no specialist training is needed. [S1]
- **Do not talk people out of PrEP;** a prescriber who is uncomfortable should refer straight away to a colleague or service that provides it. Raise PrEP proactively when a sexual history shows HIV risk, even if the visit is about something else. [S1]
- **Offer PrEP to people with HIV risk in the past 3 months who expect similar risk over the next 3 months,** and also to people with no recent risk whose circumstances have changed so that they now foresee risk in the next 3 months. [S1]

## Before starting

- ⚠️ **Document a negative HIV test first, using a fourth-generation antigen/antibody venous blood test done within 7 days of the PrEP assessment;** the person should start PrEP within 7 days of that negative test. A rapid point-of-care test is not enough on its own. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not start PrEP in someone with symptoms or signs of acute HIV, or with an indeterminate HIV result, until HIV is excluded:** starting in undiagnosed acute infection has led to resistance, most often to emtricitabine. [S1]
- **A recent high-risk exposure beyond the 72-hour nPEP window:** start PrEP and watch for seroconversion with fourth-generation tests over the next 2–8 weeks, then return to routine monitoring. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Kidneys: check creatinine, eGFR, urine protein:creatinine ratio and blood pressure at baseline, and assess CKD risk factors.** Starting PrEP when the eGFR is well established below 60 mL/min/1.73m² is not recommended, as there are no data. [S1]
- **A new eGFR around 60 at baseline: repeat it within 7 days,** fasting or without a cooked-meat meal in the previous 4 hours. If it stays just above or below 60, talk to a PrEP specialist: PrEP may still be possible with close monitoring, and on-demand dosing may suit. [S1]
- **Hepatitis A, B and C serology at the start; vaccinate against hepatitis A and B where susceptible.** Refer newly found chronic hepatitis B or C for treatment assessment; neither diagnosis is a barrier to starting PrEP. [S1]

## The regimen

- **Use only co-formulated tenofovir disoproxil with emtricitabine,** the only combination licensed for PrEP in Australia. Never substitute or add any other antiretroviral, and never prescribe PrEP for someone not under your care (no expedited partner therapy). [S1]
- **Daily PrEP can be offered to anyone at risk.** It is the only schedule recommended for cisgender women, transgender women on oestrogen, transgender men who have vaginal sex, and people who inject drugs. [S1]
- ⚠️ **On-demand dosing: two tablets 2–24 hours before sex, then single tablets at 24 hours and at 48 hours, both timed from the first dose.** If sex goes on over several days, keep taking one tablet every 24 hours, and take one on each of the two days after the last sex. Not TGA-approved. [S1]
- **Offer on-demand PrEP to cisgender men (whatever the sex of their partners) and to trans and gender-diverse people who were male-assigned at birth and take no oestradiol-based hormones,** when they prefer it, their risky sex happens under twice weekly or unpredictably, and they can put sex off by 2 hours. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Not on-demand for cisgender women** (tissue levels are inadequate), not for protection from injecting, and with caution in adolescent MSM, where it has not been trialled and daily adherence has been poor. [S1]
- **The 'Ts and Ss' schedule (single doses on Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday) is not recommended,** because no trial has tested it. [S1]

## Monitoring

- **Review every 3 months: repeat the HIV test at each refill and prescribe no more than 90 days at a time.** The first script may be filled on the day the baseline HIV blood is taken, provided the laboratory reliably returns a fourth-generation result in 24–48 hours. On-demand users should still attend quarterly for HIV and STI testing. [S1]
- **Recheck eGFR 3 months after starting, then every 6 months;** monitor more closely in people over 40, with baseline eGFR under 90, with hypertension or diabetes, or on nephrotoxic drugs. [S1]
- **Ask about adherence at every visit.** If someone repeatedly takes fewer than four tablets a week on daily PrEP, enough to undermine its protection and their safety, stop prescribing it. [S1]

## Hepatitis B

- **Chronic hepatitis B no longer rules out on-demand PrEP;** daily PrEP should be offered and on-demand may be considered in some circumstances. Counsel strict adherence to avoid a hepatitis flare and HBV resistance. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stopping tenofovir/emtricitabine in chronic hepatitis B can cause a severe flare.** An experienced hepatitis B clinician should assess the person before PrEP stops, followed by close monitoring. [S1]

## Stopping and restarting

- ⚠️ **Stopping safely:** cisgender men can stop daily PrEP by taking a dose 24 and 48 hours after the last at-risk sex; on-demand users take one tablet a day for 2 days after the last sex. Everyone else on daily PrEP should continue for 28 days after the last exposure. [S1]
- **Before restarting after a break, repeat the HIV test** in case HIV was acquired while off PrEP. [S1]
