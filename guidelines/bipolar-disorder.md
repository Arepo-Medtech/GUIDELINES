## ⚠️ Read this before anything else

> **"Published studies about drug treatment have often only included patients with bipolar I disorder or
> have not subclassified subjects as bipolar I or II. EVIDENCE FOR EFFICACY OF DRUG TREATMENT FOR BIPOLAR
> DISORDER IS LIMITED; IT IS PARTICULARLY POOR FOR BIPOLAR II DISORDER."** [B1]
>
> ⚠️ **AMH then states that what follows applies to bipolar I.** **So does this guideline.** **Nothing
> here should be read as evidence-based guidance for bipolar II.**

**Rationale for drug use: treat mania, treat depression, prevent recurrence, and prevent complications —
including SUICIDE** [B1].

**Two things that can cause the presentation:** ⚠️ **CORTICOSTEROIDS, AS WELL AS ILLICIT STIMULANT DRUGS,
MAY INDUCE MANIA** [B1]. **Monitor and support medication compliance, as it can be poor** [B1].

## The ladder [B1]

| Phase | First line |
|---|---|
| **Prevention of manic or depressive episodes** | ⚠️ **LITHIUM — the drug of choice**, and **associated with a REDUCED RISK OF SUICIDE** |
| **Acute mania, mild-to-moderate** | **an antipsychotic, lithium OR valproate** |
| **Acute mania, severe** | ⚠️ **an ANTIPSYCHOTIC PLUS EITHER LITHIUM OR VALPROATE** |
| **Bipolar depression** | **lithium, valproate, lamotrigine, quetiapine or olanzapine as monotherapy** |
| **Maintenance** | **LITHIUM AND VALPROATE first line**; **quetiapine, lamotrigine (particularly where depressive episodes are a feature) and carbamazepine as alternatives** |

**Valproate and carbamazepine are alternatives when lithium is ineffective, ⚠️ CONSIDERED TOO DANGEROUS IN
UNRELIABLE PATIENTS, or poorly tolerated** — and are sometimes used with each other or with lithium [B1].

⚠️ **CHOICE OF AGENT IN WOMEN OF REPRODUCTIVE AGE REQUIRES CAREFUL CONSIDERATION** [B1]. See *The
reproductive-age problem* below — **it is the hardest decision in this guideline and no source retrieved
resolves it.**

### ⚠️ Antidepressants in bipolar disorder

> ⚠️ **AS ANTIDEPRESSANTS MAY PROVOKE MANIA OR A RAPID CYCLING PATTERN, ANTIDEPRESSANT MONOTHERAPY IS NOT
> RECOMMENDED** [B1].
>
> **When an acute depressive episode does not respond to maintenance lithium at optimal dose, an
> antidepressant (SSRIs may be preferred), valproate, lamotrigine or quetiapine may be ADDED** [B1].

⚠️ **This compendium's `adverse-effects-of-antidepressants` guideline carries the other half of the same
warning from AMH's SSRI page: all antidepressants may provoke a manic episode in people with bipolar
disorder, and some people without a bipolar history develop an antidepressant-induced manic episode —
which does not necessarily imply the diagnosis.**

### Rapid cycling [B1]
**Identify and treat contributing conditions — ⚠️ HYPOTHYROIDISM, drug or alcohol misuse.** ⚠️ **TAPER OR
STOP medicines that may contribute, SUCH AS ANTIDEPRESSANTS.** **Many people who experience rapid cycling
will require more than one drug** [B1].

### The acute manic episode in practice [B1][B2]
**In the initial phase, BEFORE THE ONSET OF ACTION of first-line drugs, an antipsychotic and/or a
benzodiazepine is usually needed** to relieve symptoms and reduce the risk of self-injury and risk to
others [B1]. ⚠️ **Lithium's onset of action may be delayed for 6–10 DAYS** [B2].

> ⚠️ **WHEN USED WITH BENZODIAZEPINES THE SEDATING EFFECTS OF ANTIPSYCHOTICS MAY BE POTENTIATED** —
> dosage adjustments may be needed, **PARTICULARLY AS SYMPTOMS BEGIN TO RESOLVE** [B1]. **The dose that
> was right in the acute phase becomes the wrong dose as the patient improves.**

**MOST PATIENTS REQUIRE HOSPITALISATION FOR ACUTE MANIA** [B2]. **ECT is an alternative in patients
unresponsive to drug treatment or with particularly severe symptoms**, and **maintenance ECT may be
considered for those who respond to it acutely** [B1].

**Psychological interventions — interpersonal and social rhythm therapy, cognitive behaviour therapy — may
help alongside drug treatment, and adjunctive psychological intervention is recommended in maintenance**
[B1].

## Lithium

⚠️ **All doses below are licensed and unquoted.**

| Indication | Dose [B2] |
|---|---|
| **Acute mania** | **initially 750–1000 mg daily in divided doses; increase by 250–500 mg daily if necessary, adjusting to serum concentration** |
| **Prophylaxis** | **250–1000 mg daily in divided doses, or as a single dose at night, for 2 weeks; then adjust to serum concentration** |
| **Elderly, renal impairment** | **reduced dose, monitor carefully** |

**Products: 250 mg scored tablets (Lithicarb) and 450 mg controlled-release (Quilonum SR)** [B2].

### ⚠️ Concentration monitoring — the numbers

> ⚠️ **TAKE BLOOD 12 HOURS AFTER THE LAST DOSE** [B2]. **A level drawn at the wrong time is not a level.**

| | [B2] |
|---|---|
| **After starting or a dose change** | **5–7 days, and after each change until stabilised** |
| **Then** | **every 3 months** |
| **Steady state** | **may not be reached for 4–7 days — longer in the elderly or renal impairment** |
| ⚠️ **More often** | **illness (e.g. gastroenteritis), manic or depressive phases, changes in weight, diet or temperature, pregnancy, concomitant medication such as DIURETICS** |

| Therapeutic range [B2] | |
|---|---|
| **Acute mania** | **usually 0.6–1 mmol/L; up to 1.2 mmol/L in some people** |
| **Prophylaxis** | **0.4–1 mmol/L (usually 0.6–0.8)** |
| ⚠️ **Toxicity common above** | **1.5 mmol/L — or 1.2 mmol/L IN THE ELDERLY** |

> ⚠️ **CLINICAL TOXICITY CAN SOMETIMES OCCUR AT LOWER CONCENTRATIONS**, particularly with organic
> neurological damage or other CNS illness such as epilepsy [B2]. **A level inside the range does not
> exclude toxicity.**

### Recognising toxicity [B2]

| | Features |
|---|---|
| **Mild-to-moderate** | **blurred vision, increasing diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, muscle weakness, drowsiness, apathy, ataxia,** ⚠️ **FLU-LIKE ILLNESS** |
| **Severe** | **increased muscle tone, hyperreflexia, myoclonic jerks, coarse tremor, dysarthria, disorientation, psychosis, seizures, coma** |

⚠️ **"Flu-like illness" is the one that gets missed** — and the situations that cause it (vomiting,
diarrhoea, dehydration) are themselves the situations that raise the lithium level.

### ⚠️ What tips a stable patient into toxicity [B2]

> ⚠️ **HYPONATRAEMIA INCREASES THE RISK.** Avoid lithium or use it cautiously where sodium may fall:
> **vomiting, diarrhoea, DIURETICS, dehydration, Addison's disease** [B2].
>
> ⚠️ **NSAIDs reduce renal clearance.** **Toxicity relates to serum concentration and the risk is
> UNACCEPTABLY HIGH in severe renal impairment and when renal function is unstable. Reduce the dose even
> in relatively mild renal dysfunction** [B2].
>
> ⚠️ **AVOID SODIUM BICARBONATE — in indigestion products such as Salvital and urinary alkalinisers such
> as Ural — IT MAKES LITHIUM LESS EFFECTIVE** [B2]. **Both are sold over the counter.**

**Consider interrupting lithium briefly around surgery**, as fasting and changes in fluid intake may alter
the concentration [B2].

### Counselling that prevents an admission [B2]

> **BE ALERT FOR TOXICITY — extreme thirst and frequent urination, nausea and vomiting — especially during
> illness, excessive sweating or low fluid intake.** ⚠️ **IF THESE OCCUR, STOP THE TABLETS AND SEEK
> MEDICAL ATTENTION IMMEDIATELY** [B2].

**Take with food.** **Do not break, crush or chew Quilonum SR, and avoid taking it with hot drinks.**
⚠️ **MAINTAIN A NORMAL DIET WITH REGULAR SALT AND FLUID INTAKE; drink more non-alcoholic fluid in hot
weather** [B2].

### The long-term harms [B2]

**Common: metallic taste, nausea, diarrhoea, epigastric discomfort, weight gain, fatigue, headache,
vertigo, tremor, acne, psoriasis, leucocytosis, nephrotoxicity, hypothyroidism (usually asymptomatic),
HYPERCALCAEMIA, HYPERPARATHYROIDISM, benign T wave changes on ECG** [B2].

> ⚠️ **NEPHROGENIC DIABETES INSIPIDUS with polydipsia and polyuria is FREQUENT, and may be reversible on
> stopping.** ⚠️ **IT GREATLY INCREASES THE RISK OF LITHIUM TOXICITY — reassess the need for lithium if it
> develops** [B2].

**Lithium is associated with reduced GFR, and there are reports of END-STAGE RENAL DISEASE.** **Long-term
treatment and episodes of acute toxicity are thought to increase nephrotoxicity risk** — though AMH notes
the contribution is complicated by background rates of diabetes and cardiovascular disease, **which are
higher in the population with bipolar disorder** [B2].

> ⚠️ **LONG-TERM TREATMENT OF MORE THAN 10 YEARS INCREASES THE RISK OF RENAL TUMOURS — cancers and
> oncocytomas** [B2].

**Treat HYPOTHYROIDISM before starting**; lithium often causes thyroid dysfunction, usually hypothyroidism.
**PSORIASIS may be exacerbated or precipitated** [B2].

| Baseline and ongoing monitoring [B2] | |
|---|---|
| **Baseline, then EVERY 3–6 MONTHS and AT LEAST ANNUALLY** | **weight · serum calcium including IONISED calcium · parathyroid hormone · renal function · thyroid function** |
| **More frequently** | **renal function if unstable** |
| **Clinically** | **monitor for signs and symptoms of thyroid dysfunction** |
| **Significant cardiac disease** | **consider ECG at baseline and follow-up** |

> ⚠️ **DO NOT STOP LITHIUM ABRUPTLY. WITHDRAW GRADUALLY OVER AT LEAST 4 WEEKS to avoid relapse** [B2].

## ⚠️ The reproductive-age problem

**This is the decision AMH flags and does not resolve, and neither does this guideline.**

| Drug | In pregnancy [AMH] |
|---|---|
| **Lithium** | **may increase the risk of CONGENITAL HEART DEFECTS**; neonatal neurotoxicity and hypothyroidism reported. **Avoid if possible, particularly first trimester** [B2] |
| ⚠️ **Valproate** | **AVOID IF POSSIBLE IN THOSE OF CHILD-BEARING POTENTIAL.** **Neural tube defects, orofacial clefts, cardiac, urogenital and limb defects.** ⚠️ **DELAY IN MOTOR AND COGNITIVE DEVELOPMENT IN UP TO 30–40% OF CHILDREN**, and some data suggest an association with autism [B3] |
| **Carbamazepine, lamotrigine** | named by AMH as **not suitable alternatives during pregnancy** [B2] |

> ⚠️ **AND YET:** **"continuing lithium may be the best option as poorly controlled bipolar disorder may
> compromise maternal health AND carbamazepine, valproate and lamotrigine are not suitable alternatives
> during pregnancy"** [B2].
>
> **Every option on the table is compromised. AMH's position is that the least bad one is usually
> lithium, and that the decision is made with, not instead of, specialist advice.**

**If valproate must be used where there is no effective alternative: lowest possible dose, and ⚠️ ENSURE
ADEQUATE CONTRACEPTION** [B3]. ⚠️ **Risk is DOSE-RELATED and appears to increase significantly ABOVE 650 mg
DAILY** [B3] — **below the 1–2 g daily maintenance range AMH gives for bipolar disorder** [B3].

### Lithium through pregnancy and delivery [B2]

| | [B2] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Clearance** | **INCREASES in the second half of pregnancy — dose adjustments may be needed; use the lowest dose possible** |
| **Monitoring** | **monthly, increasing towards term — e.g. EACH WEEK FROM WEEK 36 — then WITHIN 24 HOURS AFTER THE BIRTH** |
| **Around delivery** | **many recommend reducing the dose in the last days, or stopping for 1–2 days before planned delivery or onset of labour**, to avoid maternal and neonatal toxicity |
| ⚠️ **After the birth** | **GIVE THE PRECONCEPTION DOSE — clearance returns to normal IMMEDIATELY** |
| **Throughout** | **greater attention to hydration** |
| **Breastfeeding** | **contact a pregnancy medicines information service** |

⚠️ **The postpartum step is the dangerous one: clearance drops back the moment the baby is delivered, so a
pregnancy-adjusted dose becomes an overdose.**

## Valproate in bipolar disorder [B3]

| | Dose [B3] |
|---|---|
| **Bipolar disorder, adult** | **initially 600 mg daily in 2 doses; increase every 3 days by 200 mg daily according to response. Maintenance oral or IV 1–2 g (20–30 mg/kg) daily in 2 doses** |
| **Concentration** | ⚠️ **POOR correlation with efficacy** — useful to confirm toxicity or compliance. **Acute mania: usual range 40–100 mg/L (280–700 micromol/L), up to 150 mg/L in some; measure as a TROUGH; steady state after 3–5 days** |

**Common: nausea, vomiting, INCREASED APPETITE and WEIGHT GAIN, dose-related tremor, THINNING OR LOSS OF
SCALP HAIR (usually temporary), drowsiness, dizziness, memory impairment, ataxia, dose-related raised
aminotransferases, asymptomatic hyperammonaemia, dose-related thrombocytopenia, MENSTRUAL IRREGULARITIES,
POLYCYSTIC OVARIES, HYPERANDROGENISM IN FEMALES** [B3].

> ⚠️ **RARE BUT FATAL: HEPATIC FAILURE and PANCREATITIS — these usually occur IN THE FIRST 6 MONTHS and can
> be fatal** [B3]. Also hyperammonaemic encephalopathy, blood dyscrasias, extrapyramidal syndrome including
> parkinsonism, and reduced bone mineral density with fractures on long-term use.

**Should be safe in breastfeeding (one report of adverse effects) — consider monitoring the baby for
petechial rash** [B3].

**Carbamazepine note:** ⚠️ **its concentration correlates POORLY with effect on mood**, while the valproate
concentration needed appears similar to that used for seizure disorders. **Lamotrigine requires NO
concentration monitoring** [B1].

- Antipsychotic started immediately for acute mania with lithium/valproate/carbamazepine: olanzapine 10-15 mg orally daily (max 30 mg) or risperidone 2 mg daily titrated by 1 mg every few days (max 6 mg) first line; aripiprazole 10-15 mg daily (max 30), asenapine 5-10 mg SL twice daily, cariprazine 1.5 mg then 3 mg (max 6), paliperidone MR 3-6 mg (max 12), quetiapine IR 150 mg twice daily day 1 then 300 mg twice daily (max 800), ziprasidone 40 mg twice daily (max 80 mg twice daily) second line; haloperidol 2 mg daily (max 10) third line; stop once mania remits. [T1]
- Carbamazepine IR 100-200 mg orally twice daily for acute mania, increasing 100-200 mg/day every 2-3 days, target 20-50 micromol/L, max 1600 mg/day; continue at least 6-12 months. [T1]
- Agitation in acute mania: clonazepam 0.5-1 mg orally 4-hourly prn (max 4 mg/24 h), diazepam 5-10 mg 4-hourly (max 40 mg/24 h) or lorazepam 1-2 mg 4-hourly (max 6 mg/24 h), for up to 1 week; psychiatrist may use zuclopenthixol acetate 50-150 mg IM, repeat every 2-3 days, max 400 mg over 2 weeks in no more than 4 doses. [T1]
- Long-acting injectable maintenance after mania: aripiprazole monohydrate 400 mg IM every 4 weeks (300 mg if not tolerated) with 2 weeks oral overlap, or risperidone LAI 25 mg IM every 2 weeks (range 25-50 mg) with 3 weeks oral overlap. [T1]
- Maintenance lithium/valproate/carbamazepine for at least 6-12 months after acute mania, then assess need for prophylaxis; prophylaxis may be 3-5 years or indefinite. [T2]
- Second-line prophylaxis: lamotrigine 25 mg at night for 2 weeks, 50 mg for 2 weeks, 100 mg for 1 week, then 200 mg (max 400 mg/day), best for preventing depressive episodes; quetiapine titrated 50-100-200-300 mg over 4 days (usual min 300, max 800); aripiprazole 15 mg daily (10-30); asenapine 5-10 mg SL twice daily; paliperidone 3-6 mg at night (max 12); ziprasidone 40 mg twice daily (max 160/day). - Folic acid 5 mg orally once daily for at least 3 months before and after conception for people taking sodium valproate or carbamazepine who are planning or in pregnancy. [T3]
- Combination prophylaxis options after nonresponse: lithium plus sodium valproate, lithium plus lamotrigine, or lithium plus olanzapine. [T2]
