# Solid tumours with an NTRK gene fusion: larotrectinib

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** larotrectinib for adults with locally advanced or metastatic NTRK fusion-positive solid tumours: eligibility, the gap between TGA registration and PBS funding, dosing and dose levels, liver, drug-interaction and nervous-system precautions, monitoring and the evidence. Entrectinib and repotrectinib are not covered: no tumour-agnostic eviQ protocol was found, and on the PBS they are listed only under NSCLC (`non-small-cell-lung-cancer` covers EGFR-mutant disease only). PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ⚠️ **Registered tumour-agnostic, funded tumour-specific.** Larotrectinib has provisional TGA approval for any NTRK fusion-positive solid tumour, but PBS authority covers only five tumour groups (below).

> ⚠️ **Adult protocol only.** The PBS also lists larotrectinib when treatment began in childhood; paediatric dosing is not covered here.

## Who is eligible

- **For adults aged 18 or over whose locally advanced or metastatic solid tumour has an NTRK gene fusion**, shown by next-generation sequencing or FISH at an approved pathology provider, and has no known acquired resistance mutation. [S1]
- **The cancer must be metastatic, or resection would likely cause severe morbidity; it must have progressed after treatment or have no satisfactory alternative;** and the patient must not have had a TRK-active tyrosine kinase inhibitor. Trials excluded ECOG performance status 3 to 4, so use judgement there. [S1]
- ⚠️ **PBS authority is limited to:** glioma, glioneuronal tumour or glioblastoma; soft tissue sarcoma; NSCLC; secretory breast carcinoma; and mammary analogue secretory carcinoma of the salivary gland, even though TGA provisional approval covers NTRK-fusion solid tumours generally. [S1]

## Dosing

- ⚠️ **Larotrectinib 100 mg by mouth twice daily for adults, continued until progression or unacceptable toxicity.** It comes as capsules (25 mg or 100 mg) and as an oral liquid (20 mg/mL). [S1]
- **Take it twice daily roughly 12 hours apart, ideally at the same times each day, swallowed whole with water** without opening, crushing or chewing. A missed or vomited dose is not replaced; just take the next one on time. [S1]
- **Dose levels:** start at 100 mg twice daily; reduce first to 75 mg twice daily, then 50 mg twice daily, then 100 mg once daily. If even 100 mg once daily is not tolerated, consider stopping for good. [S1]
- **Moderate or severe hepatic impairment at baseline: cut the starting dose by 50%** (mild impairment needs no change). No kidney dose change is needed. [S1]

## Toxicity and dose changes

- ⚠️ **Transaminase rises:** grade 1 or 2, continue with more frequent LFTs. Grade 3 or 4 with bilirubin below 2 x ULN, withhold until recovery, then resume one level lower; stop permanently if it fails to resolve or grade 4 recurs. Grade 3 or more with bilirubin at or above 2 x ULN, withhold and consider stopping; if continuing, resume a level lower and stop for good on any recurrence. [S1]
- ⚠️ **CYP3A4 interactions:** with a strong inhibitor (for example azoles, macrolides, ritonavir, grapefruit juice), halve the larotrectinib dose and watch for toxicity; with a strong inducer (for example carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampicin, St John's wort), double it. Return to the earlier dose once the interacting drug has been stopped for 3 to 5 half-lives. [S1]
- **Other grade 3 or 4 reactions:** withhold until grade 1 or baseline; if that happens within 4 weeks restart one level lower, otherwise stop permanently. Grade 1 or 2 reactions may allow treatment to continue under close watch. [S1]
- **On-target neurological effects** (dizziness, unsteady gait, paraesthesia) usually start within the first 3 months; intolerable dizziness may need a dose change. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Liver toxicity also usually starts within 3 months.** Check FBC, EUC, eGFR and LFTs before starting, every 2 weeks during the first month, monthly for the next 6 months, then as indicated. [S1]
- **Expect either diarrhoea or constipation,** so patients may need antidiarrhoeals or laxatives. Emetic risk is minimal to low and routine antiemetics are not needed. [S1]
- **Pregnancy:** the drug may harm a fetus, so consider a pregnancy test before starting and make sure patients who could conceive use effective contraception during and after treatment. [S1]

## Evidence

- **The evidence base is limited:** eviQ's literature search found no strong evidence, and its reference panel relied mainly on pooled data from three early-phase (I/II) trials (LOXO-TRK-14001, SCOUT and NAVIGATE). [S1]
- **Pooled results (159 patients, 153 evaluable):** 79% responded, 16% completely; median response duration 35.2 months, progression-free survival 28.3 months and overall survival 44.4 months. Responses were seen in adults and children, across many tumour types and fusion genes; liver toxicity and dizziness were the most important side effects. [S1]
