# Invasive and chronic aspergillosis

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, treatment, prophylaxis and empiric therapy for invasive aspergillosis in adults with haematological malignancy, stem cell or solid organ transplant, plus chronic cavitary pulmonary aspergillosis. Invasive candidiasis: see `invasive-candidiasis`. Antifungal prophylaxis in children with cancer: see `antifungal-prophylaxis-paediatric-oncology`.

## Diagnosis

- **Get a chest CT whenever invasive pulmonary aspergillosis is suspected, even if the chest X-ray is normal** (strong; high). Contrast is not routine, but use it when a nodule or mass lies near a large vessel. [S1]
- **Do bronchoscopy with BAL when IPA is suspected** (strong; moderate), unless severe hypoxaemia, bleeding or refractory thrombocytopenia prevent it. Peripheral nodules yield little on BAL, so consider percutaneous or endobronchial biopsy. [S1]
- **Serum and BAL galactomannan is an accurate marker in haematological malignancy and HSCT** (strong; high). Do not use it for routine blood screening during mould-active therapy or prophylaxis (bronchoscopy samples are still fine), or to screen SOT recipients or people with chronic granulomatous disease. [S1]
- **Serum beta-D-glucan helps in high-risk patients (haematological malignancy, allogeneic HSCT) but is not specific for *Aspergillus*** (strong; moderate). [S1]

## Treating invasive pulmonary aspergillosis

- ⚠️ **Voriconazole is the primary treatment** (strong; high). **Start antifungals early when IPA is strongly suspected,** while the work-up continues (strong; high). [S1]
- **Alternatives: liposomal amphotericin B or isavuconazole** (both strong; moderate), or another lipid amphotericin formulation (weak; low). [S1]
- **Voriconazole plus an echinocandin may be considered in selected patients with documented IPA** (weak; moderate). An echinocandin alone is not recommended as primary therapy; keep it for when azoles and polyenes are contraindicated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat for at least 6–12 weeks,** depending on how deep and long the immunosuppression is, the site, and response (strong; low). If immunosuppression must continue after successful treatment, give secondary prophylaxis (strong; moderate). [S1]
- **Cut back or stop immunosuppressive drugs where feasible** as part of treatment (strong; low). [S1]
- **Therapeutic drug monitoring of the azole (itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole) once at steady state** during treatment, prolonged prophylaxis or when interactions are expected; also measure interacting drugs such as ciclosporin, tacrolimus and sirolimus. [S1]
- **Repeat the chest CT after at least 2 weeks of treatment** to judge response, sooner if the patient deteriorates (weak; low). [S1]
- **Aspergillosis does not absolutely rule out further chemotherapy or HSCT** (strong; moderate); the timing should be decided jointly by infectious diseases and haematology/oncology. [S1]
- **Salvage options: lipid amphotericin B, micafungin, caspofungin, posaconazole or itraconazole,** choosing a triazole in the light of prior antifungals, host factors, pharmacokinetics and possible resistance (strong; moderate). [S1]
- **CNS aspergillosis: voriconazole first,** with lipid amphotericin B only for those intolerant of or failing it (strong; moderate). [S1]

## First-line choice: the 2025 update (ATS)

- ⚠️ **For proven or probable invasive pulmonary aspergillosis, S2 accepts either a mould-active triazole alone or a triazole plus an echinocandin as first treatment** (conditional; low-quality evidence); the evidence could not separate the two. [S2]
- **Lean towards the combination in the critically ill, when triazole resistance is a worry, and when the diagnosis rests on a positive galactomannan** (serum or BAL). The data come only from haematological malignancy and stem cell transplant patients, so use in other groups is uncertain. [S2]
- **Posaconazole and isavuconazole are now often used first line instead of voriconazole** (similar efficacy, steadier drug levels, fewer side effects), and S2 extends its advice to them although the trials used voriconazole. Rezafungin, with its very long half-life, is not interchangeable with micafungin or anidulafungin. [S2]
- **Why combine:** voriconazole has resistance that may not extend to other azoles, very variable levels (often below target despite loading), and can miss mixed infections such as *Aspergillus* with Mucorales. All trial comparisons were voriconazole with or without micafungin or anidulafungin. [S2]
- **Azole resistance in clinical *A. fumigatus*:** about 3.2% in Europe and 1.4% in the United States, above 10% in some countries; international and European guidance already favours starting a combination where environmental resistance exceeds 10%. Susceptibility testing of the patient's own isolate may then allow a step down to the triazole alone. [S2]
- **Check the neutrophil count when choosing between one and two drugs.** How long to continue a combination, and when to step down to one drug, are not established. [S2]

## Prophylaxis and empiric therapy

- ⚠️ **In prolonged neutropenia at high risk of IA, give prophylaxis with posaconazole (strong; high), voriconazole (strong; moderate) or micafungin (weak; low).** Itraconazole works but is limited by absorption and tolerability. Do not combine triazoles with drugs that become toxic alongside them, such as vinca alkaloids. [S1]
- **Posaconazole prophylaxis for allogeneic HSCT recipients with GVHD at high risk of IA** (strong; high), continued for as long as the GVHD-related immunosuppression lasts (for example prednisone equivalent above 1 mg/kg/day for over 2 weeks). [S1]
- **After lung transplant, give 3 to 4 months of antifungal prophylaxis** with a systemic triazole (voriconazole or itraconazole) or inhaled amphotericin B (strong; moderate). [S1]
- **Empiric antifungals for high-risk patients with prolonged neutropenia and persistent fever despite broad-spectrum antibiotics:** a lipid amphotericin B or an echinocandin (caspofungin, micafungin) (strong; high), or voriconazole. Not when neutropenia is expected to last under 10 days, unless other findings point to invasive fungal infection. [S1]

## Chronic cavitary pulmonary aspergillosis

- **Treat chronic cavitary pulmonary aspergillosis with at least 6 months of antifungals when there are symptoms or declining lung function or imaging** (strong; low); asymptomatic, stable patients can be observed and reviewed every 3–6 months. [S1]
- **Oral itraconazole or voriconazole are the preferred agents** (strong; high); posaconazole is a third-line choice after adverse effects or failure (strong; moderate). [S1]
