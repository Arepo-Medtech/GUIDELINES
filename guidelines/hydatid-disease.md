# Hydatid disease (cystic echinococcosis)

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** stage- and size-based treatment of uncomplicated liver and small lung hydatid (*Echinococcus granulosus*) cysts: albendazole, PAIR, surgery, praziquantel cover for spillage, and follow-up. Complicated cysts, alveolar echinococcosis and multi-organ disease need specialist care and are outside the source's recommendations. `worm-infections` names hydatid disease but gives no treatment. PBS access for albendazole is in the table below.

## Australian context

- **In Australia hydatid disease is tied mainly to sheep farming, but dogs and dingoes also carry it.** The main cycle is dog–sheep–dog; a wild cycle runs between dingoes or wild dogs and wallabies, and a dog–feral pig cycle puts pig hunters at particular risk. Kangaroos, wallabies, cattle and camels are among the intermediate hosts. [S2]
- **People are infected by swallowing tapeworm eggs from dog faeces, hand to mouth; it does not spread between people.** Young children are infected more often because of closer contact with dogs and poorer hygiene, not greater susceptibility. [S2]
- **Victoria stopped notification in early 2001;** in the preceding decade there were about 16 notifications a year, mostly acquired overseas. Occasional local infections occur in visitors to rural areas with infected sheep or dingoes, and people who trap wild dogs are at risk. No school or childcare exclusion is needed. [S2]

## Recognise and confirm (Australian guidance)

- **Symptoms come from the cyst pressing, leaking or rupturing.** The liver is the commonest site, then brain, lungs and kidneys; heart, thyroid and bone are uncommon. A calcified cyst on X-ray can still be active. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Sudden rupture can cause fatal anaphylaxis.** [S2]
- **Imaging (X-ray, ultrasound or CT) suggests the diagnosis and serology confirms it:** 80–100% sensitive and 88–96% specific for liver disease, less for lung and other sites. Eosinophilia is unreliable. After a rupture, examine sputum, vomitus, faeces or urine for protoscoleces, brood capsules and cyst wall. [S2]

## Scope and staging

- **The guideline covers uncomplicated liver cysts and small (5 cm or less) lung cysts.** Complicated liver cysts usually go to open surgery where it can be done safely, and most lung hydatid is managed surgically. Recommendations are tied to cyst stage (CE1–CE5) and size, and to the facility tier available. [S1]
- **Inactive cysts (CE4/CE5) are usually watched with ultrasound or MRI,** avoiding surgery unless they cause complications. Multiple cysts, cysts at different stages and multi-organ disease need individualised management. [S1]

## Uncomplicated liver cysts

- ⚠️ **CE1 or CE3a under 5 cm: albendazole alone,** suitable at any level of care. [S1]
- **CE1 or CE3a of 5–10 cm: albendazole with PAIR (percutaneous puncture, aspiration, scolicide injection and re-aspiration);** over 10 cm: percutaneous treatment plus albendazole, PAIR preferred to catheterisation or surgery. Never use PAIR when the cyst communicates with the bile ducts; both need a tier 3 or 4 centre. [S1]
- **CE2 or CE3b of 5 cm or less: start with albendazole alone** (any tier). **Over 5 cm: surgery plus albendazole,** open (tiers 2–4) or laparoscopic (tiers 3–4). [S1]

## Albendazole

- ⚠️ **Albendazole 10–15 mg/kg a day in two doses (maximum 400 mg twice daily) with a fatty meal, given continuously without monthly breaks,** usually for 3–6 months depending on cyst stage and size. Use a good-quality product. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not give albendazole for cysts at risk of rupture or in the first trimester;** later in pregnancy benefit may justify it. Women of childbearing age need contraception on long courses. Use it cautiously in chronic liver disease, avoid it in bone-marrow depression, and monitor liver enzymes and blood counts. [S1]
- **Image at 3–6 months, then yearly for at least 5 years after the cyst becomes inactive.** No cyst change after 3 months counts as non-response (a repeat albendazole course can be considered); judge complete response no earlier than 12 months after treatment ends. [S1]

## Procedures and spillage

- **If cyst fluid spills (suspected or confirmed) during a percutaneous or surgical procedure, add praziquantel to albendazole.** Albendazole continues for at least 3 months, usually 6–12 months; praziquantel 40–50 mg/kg a day in two doses for 2 weeks (up to 4 weeks), taken with a fatty meal. [S1]
- **Praziquantel before a procedure** is used by some clinicians but is not yet recommended; evidence is lacking. [S1]
- **PAIR carries risk and is available only in some tier 3–4 centres;** the only trial comparing it with albendazole alone had 20 patients. [S1]

## Lung cysts

- ⚠️ **Active uncomplicated lung cysts of 5 cm or less: surgery (tier 4 only). Do not give albendazole before lung surgery;** give it afterwards only if spillage is suspected or has occurred. [S1]

## Australian treatment advice

- **The Victorian guidance describes surgery as often the treatment of choice,** sometimes with prolonged high-dose albendazole; percutaneous drainage with albendazole for liver cysts; praziquantel then albendazole after spillage; and CT-guided PAIR with anthelmintic cover for inoperable cysts. It points to *Therapeutic Guidelines: Antibiotic* and asks for specialist infectious diseases advice. S1's stage- and size-based choices are more recent and more specific. [S2]

## Household and prevention

- **The patient is not infectious;** encourage household contacts to keep good hygiene and report symptoms early. Dogs at the patient's home may need veterinary screening. [S2]
- **Break the cycle:** treat dogs with a tapeworm drug such as praziquantel every 6 weeks, alternating with a broad-spectrum wormer; keep dogs away from raw offal of sheep or kangaroos; burn or bury infected organs deeply; wash hands after gardening or handling dogs, and wash vegetables. [S2]
