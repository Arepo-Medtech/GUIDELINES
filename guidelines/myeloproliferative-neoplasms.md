# Myeloproliferative neoplasms — myelofibrosis (primary, pre-fibrotic and post-PV/ET)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** symptom assessment, pre-fibrotic primary myelofibrosis, molecular risk scoring, JAK-inhibitor choice and sequencing, recognising ruxolitinib failure (RR6), and transplant referral for primary and secondary (post-PV/ET) myelofibrosis in adults. Polycythaemia vera and essential thrombocythaemia outside myelofibrosis are not covered (see Unresolved). PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Assess symptoms and risk

- **Weight symptom burden equally with blood counts and clinical findings.** Measure it with the MPN-SAF TSS, which scores 10 core symptoms. [S1]
- **Score symptoms at every visit.** A fall of ≥ 50% in the MPN-SAF TSS counts as a clinically meaningful response. Symptom progression on its own, without haematological progression, is enough to escalate or change treatment. [S1]
- **Risk-score with molecular models:** MIPSS70+ v2.0 (MIPSSv2) for primary MF and MYSEC-PM for MF secondary to PV/ET. Observe or treat symptoms in low-risk disease; consider high- and very-high-risk patients early for allogeneic stem cell transplant. [S1]
- **Do NGS and conventional cytogenetics at baseline and during follow-up**, especially if transplant-eligible. FISH does not replace karyotyping for prognostic scoring. [S1]

## Pre-fibrotic primary myelofibrosis

- **Diagnosis combines marrow histology** (hypercellularity, atypical megakaryopoiesis, reticulin fibrosis grade 0–1) with a JAK2, CALR or MPL mutation or another clonal marker, plus minor criteria such as anaemia, leukocytosis, splenomegaly or a raised LDH. The clinician and an expert haematopathologist should discuss the case. [S1]
- **Pre-PMF has a worse prognosis than ET**, with more progression to overt MF, more secondary AML and shorter survival. [S1]
- **Treat by risk.** Observe low-risk, asymptomatic patients with low-dose aspirin. Use hydroxyurea or interferon-alpha for symptoms, high counts or thrombotic risk. Ropeginterferon-alpha is emerging as the preferred disease-modifying option in younger patients. [S1]
- **Thrombosis in PMF:** anticoagulation is the backbone for venous thrombosis, including at atypical sites. Antiplatelet therapy is mainly for selected arterial or microvascular events. Cytopenias, splenomegaly, portal hypertension and bleeding risk mean each case needs individual judgement. [S1]

## JAK inhibitors

- **JAK inhibitors are standard care for symptomatic MF.** Ruxolitinib stays first-line for most patients, with evidence for better symptom control and overall survival. [S1]
- **Choose by clinical profile:** ruxolitinib for classic symptomatic disease with splenomegaly, fedratinib after ruxolitinib failure, and momelotinib when anaemia is present. [S1]
- **Fedratinib is mainly second-line**, but it can be a first-line alternative when ruxolitinib is contraindicated or with comorbidities such as non-melanoma skin cancer, lymphoproliferative disorders or thrombocytopenia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Dose ruxolitinib by the approved platelet-based label.** Take care with marked baseline anaemia but avoid systematic underdosing. With clinically relevant anaemia, momelotinib may be the better first-line JAK inhibitor. [S1]
- **Momelotinib improves anaemia and reduces transfusion dependence**, unlike other JAK inhibitors. Its long-term efficacy and survival compared with ruxolitinib or fedratinib are still uncertain. [S1]
- **Add-on anaemia strategies (ESAs, luspatercept with a JAK inhibitor) rest mostly on early-phase or non-randomised data.** Randomised comparative evidence is lacking. [S1]
- **JAK inhibitors are not curative.** Their gains in symptoms, performance status and spleen size also make them a bridge to transplant in eligible patients. [S1]
- **Second line in Germany is fedratinib or momelotinib only.** A German regulatory statement deems all other combinations off-label, which excludes pacritinib. NCCN instead prefers pacritinib for higher-risk MF with platelets < 50 × 10^9/L when transplant is not feasible. [S1]

## Ruxolitinib failure and transplant

- ⚠️ **Check the RR6 score at 6 months on ruxolitinib.** Its inputs are a ruxolitinib dose < 20 mg bid, a palpable spleen reduction of ≤ 30% at 3 and 6 months, and red-cell transfusion need. RR6 high risk should prompt a switch of JAK inhibitor, a clinical trial or fast-tracked transplant evaluation. [S1]
- **Read RR6 in context:** it depends on spleen measurement and can misclassify slow responders, so interpret it alongside longitudinal clinical assessment. [S1]
- **Allogeneic transplant is the only curative option; refer early.** ONKOPEDIA follows the 2023 EBMT/ELN recommendations, considering biological age up to about 75 years. Transplant-eligible patients on a JAK inhibitor need an RR6 evaluation at 6 months, and a high-risk score means immediate transplant evaluation. [S1]
- **Give a JAK inhibitor before transplant** when the spleen is ≥ 5 cm or causing symptoms. [S1]
- **Splenic irradiation or splenectomy for marked splenomegaly is an individual decision.** Irradiation is less invasive and can bridge to transplant. Splenectomy reduces spleen burden more but carries considerable perioperative morbidity and mortality. [S1]
- **Offer clinical trial enrolment whenever possible.** [S1]
