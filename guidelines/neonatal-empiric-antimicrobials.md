# Neonatal empiric antimicrobial therapy

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Antibiotics go in within 1 hour of deciding to treat**; investigations should not hold them up unduly. [S1]
- **Early-onset sepsis (under 48 hours)** is usually GBS or *E. coli* — GBS more in term infants, *E. coli* in preterm. **Late-onset (over 48 hours)** pathogens differ between community and hospital acquisition. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider herpes simplex in all septic neonates, with a very low threshold for empiric aciclovir.** *Listeria* is rare but serious. [S1]
- **Neonatal sepsis mortality is up to 20% in high-income countries**, inversely related to gestational age. Unnecessary, broad or prolonged antibiotics also harm neonates and drive resistance. [S1]

## Investigations

- **Blood culture from a peripheral site and every central lumen** — minimum 0.5 mL, preferably 1 mL under 1.5 kg or 1.5 mL for 1.5–5 kg. Plus FBE and CRP or procalcitonin. [S1]
- **LP particularly in late-onset sepsis.** Meningitis is uncommon in suspected early-onset sepsis (1–2%): do an LP if suspicion is high (maternal chorioamnionitis or PPROM, neurological signs) or blood cultures are positive. [S1]

## Empiric treatment

- **Strongly consider removing the central line in sepsis.** Fulminant sepsis: urgent ID consultation on broadening. Start empirically, then tailor to microbiology. [S1]
- **Early-onset sepsis, meningitis not suspected or CSF normal:** **benzylpenicillin and gentamicin** (covers GBS, *E. coli*, *Listeria*). **Stop after 36 hours** if cultures show no growth and the baby is well. [S1]
- **Late-onset sepsis, meningitis not suspected or CSF normal:** **cefazolin and gentamicin.** Stop after 36 hours if cultures show no growth — earlier if another cause is found (e.g. enterovirus). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Meningitis suspected or CSF abnormal: benzylpenicillin and a 3rd-generation cephalosporin.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid ceftriaxone where possible under 41 weeks' gestation**, especially with jaundice or when calcium-containing solutions (TPN included) are running (cefotaxime is the other third-generation option). [S1]
- **Add aciclovir if herpes simplex is possible.** HSV risk factors: maternal HSV · recent exposure (incubation up to 12 days) · seizures or abnormal neurology · vesicular rash · hepatitis · corneal ulcer/keratitis. [S1]
- **Add benzylpenicillin if *Listeria* is suspected** (disseminated abscesses or granulomas, pustular rash, maternal listeriosis). [S1]
- **MRSA risk — use vancomycin instead of cefazolin, and add vancomycin for meningitis cover.** Risk factors: fulminant infection (e.g. inotropes) · colonisation known in the infant or mother · a child who is Pacific Islander or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander. [S1]
- **Suspected fungal infection: add liposomal amphotericin, continued for 72 hours pending blood culture.** Risk factors: fulminant infection · under 1500 g deteriorating on antibiotics · ≥7 days of antibiotics with deterioration · TPN or systemic steroids · thrombocytopenia. [S1]

## Source-directed treatment

- **UTI: gentamicin.** Uncomplicated (fever without vomiting or urological abnormality): **IV for 3 days, then oral for 4 days.** Bacteraemic: **IV for 7 days, then oral for 3 days.** Narrow to culture and request an early renal ultrasound. [S1]
- **Intra-abdominal infection (e.g. NEC): 7–10 days of amoxicillin plus gentamicin plus metronidazole.** At 72 hours: switch to amoxicillin-clavulanic acid if improving, or to piperacillin-tazobactam if not. [S1]
- **Cellulitis or post-operative wound infection: cefazolin, 5–7 days**, switching IV to oral at 3 days if improved. [S1]

## Prophylaxis

- **Fungal prophylaxis: nystatin for all infants under 1500 g**, stopping at ≥1500 g. [S1]
- **UTI prophylaxis: trimethoprim**, decided case by case with Nephrology or Urology; stop if nil by mouth. [S1]
- **Surgical prophylaxis: cefazolin within 1 hour before surgery**; post-operative doses usually not required (maximum 24 hours). Add **metronidazole** for intra-abdominal, pelvic, deep debridement, ischaemic limb, bowel-lumen entry or open fracture. [S1]
