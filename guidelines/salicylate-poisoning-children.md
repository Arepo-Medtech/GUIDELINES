# Salicylate poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk assessment, investigations, charcoal, fluid and electrolyte correction, urinary alkalinisation, haemodialysis criteria and disposition for acute salicylate poisoning in children. ## Key points

- ⚠️ **Early symptoms may be minimal; severe toxicity may only become apparent at 6–12 hours.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Serum concentration correlates poorly with toxicity; deterioration can occur as the serum level falls**, because CNS concentration is rising. [S1]
- **Classic acute features:** tinnitus, vomiting, hyperventilation, metabolic acidosis and respiratory alkalosis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hidden sources:** aspirin-containing compounds · **oil of wintergreen (1 teaspoon of 98% salicylate = 7000 mg)** · willow bark · Dencorub · some teething gels. [S1]

## Risk assessment

- **Under 150 mg/kg:** symptoms minimal. **150–300 mg/kg:** mild to moderate (tinnitus, vomiting, hyperventilation). ⚠️ **Over 300 mg/kg: severe (acidosis, seizures, hyperthermia).** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hyperventilation is not always present in children — its absence does not exclude significant toxicity.** Assess dehydration carefully. [S1]
- **History:** dose and time, immediate- or sustained-release product, co-ingestants, and whether accidental or intentional. [S1]

## Investigations

- **Blood gases indicate severity:** phase 1: respiratory alkalosis plus alkaluria → phase 2: respiratory alkalosis plus paradoxical aciduria (pH under 6) → **phase 3 metabolic acidosis and hypokalaemia.** [S1]
- **Salicylate level at presentation, then every 2–4 hours until falling if the child is symptomatic or the product enteric-coated.** Also U&E, creatinine and glucose. [S1]

## Who needs treatment

- ⚠️ **Treat (and admit/consult):** acute ingestion of 150 mg/kg or more · all symptomatic patients · unknown quantity. [S1]

## Resuscitation and decontamination

- ⚠️ **Intubation apnoea can worsen acidosis and cause cardiac arrest.** Get senior help, and consider sodium bicarbonate pre-loading. **If ventilated, keep pH 7.45–7.5** — which may mean matching the pre-intubation respiratory rate. [S1]
- **Activated charcoal 1 g/kg**, ideally within 1 hour, may be indicated after a massive overdose — **discuss with toxicology (13 11 26) first.** [S1]
- **Rehydrate to euvolaemia; correct electrolytes and hypoglycaemia.** ⚠️ **Correct the metabolic acidosis — this is critical for limiting CNS penetration.** [S1]

## Enhanced elimination

- **Urinary alkalinisation: slow IV bicarbonate bolus 2 mmol/kg, then infusion 1 mmol/kg/hr, keeping urine pH above 7.5.** Needs a catheter to monitor urine pH. For mild–moderate salicylism. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Alkalinisation is not possible with hypokalaemia and itself causes urinary potassium loss — replace potassium IV to keep a mid-range level.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider haemodialysis early in moderate–severe poisoning.** Indications include: alkalinisation not feasible (renal failure, pulmonary oedema) · **level over 7.2 mmol/L (100 mg/dL), or a level climbing past 4.4 mmol/L (60 mg/dL) even with alkalinisation** · renal failure, altered mental state, hyperthermia over 39 °C despite cooling, or refractory acidaemia or electrolyte imbalance. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Discharge after 6 hours' observation** if still asymptomatic with normal acid–base status and a level in the therapeutic range. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Refer to an ICU that can provide haemodialysis** if symptomatic after more than 300 mg/kg and/or meeting dialysis criteria. **PIPER: 1300 137 650.** [S1]
- **Intentional self-harm → local mental health services** (e.g. Orygen 1800 888 320); **recreational → YoDAA** (1800 458 685). [S1]
