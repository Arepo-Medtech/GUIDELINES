# Phenylketonuria (PAH deficiency) across the lifespan

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** newborn screening, diagnosis, when to treat, blood Phe targets by age, monitoring, diet and protein substitutes, sapropterin and pegvaliase, late-diagnosed PKU and maternal PKU. Tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) deficiencies and DNAJC12 deficiency are only excluded here, not managed. First-line samples for a suspected inborn error in an unwell child: see `metabolic-disorders-children`. PBS access for each product is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Screen and diagnose

- **Newborn screening for PKU should be a national requirement;** treat as soon as possible, ideally before 10 days of age. [S1]
- **Screen immigrant or refugee children younger than 6 years**, and test anyone unscreened with intellectual disability or neurological deficit, with or without behavioural or psychiatric problems. [S1]
- **Exclude BH4 defects and DNAJC12 deficiency in every hyperphenylalaninaemia**, and genotype the patient. [S1]

## When to treat and targets

- **Treat every child whose untreated blood Phe is 360 μmol/L or more.** Below 360 μmol/L no treatment is needed, but keep monitoring Phe until at least 6 years of age. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Target blood Phe:** 120–360 μmol/L up to age 12 years; 120–600 μmol/L from 12 to 18 years and in adults over 18; 120–360 μmol/L in pregnancy. [S1]
- **Lifelong follow-up in a specialist metabolic centre for everyone with PKU**, including adults off Phe-lowering treatment, with access to a metabolic physician, dietitian and (neuro)psychologist. [S1]

## Monitoring

- **Monitor with blood Phe** (dried blood spot and/or plasma); results should reach the family within 2 working days of the lab receiving the sample. Phe variation and the Phe/Tyr ratio are not for routine care. [S1]
- **Minimum blood Phe frequency (treated):** weekly in the first year, fortnightly from 1 to 12 years, monthly after 12; weekly before conception and twice weekly in pregnancy. [S1]
- **Up to age 12, if more than 50% of Phe levels are out of range over 6 months**, consider more frequent monitoring and re-education, psychology or social work, admission, or alternative therapy such as sapropterin. [S1]
- **Neuropsychological assessment around ages 5, 12 and 18 years, then every 5 to 10 years;** review quality of life and wellbeing at least yearly. First DXA bone density scan in early adolescence. [S1]

## Diet and protein substitutes

- **Prescribe a Phe-free or low-Phe protein substitute to anyone on a Phe-restricted diet** below the FAO/WHO/UNU 2007 safe natural-protein intake, spread evenly over at least 3 doses a day. Titrate Phe intake to keep blood Phe in range, reassess it yearly, and avoid Phe deficiency. [S1]
- **Glycomacropeptide (cGMP) substitutes can be used from 4 years of age**, counting their Phe in the daily allowance. No extra tyrosine is needed, and LNAA are not recommended routinely (never in children or pregnancy). [S1]
- **Encourage breastfeeding alongside a Phe-free infant amino acid formula.** Fruit and vegetables (not potatoes) with Phe up to 75 mg/100 g can be eaten freely; avoid aspartame. [S1]
- **Annual nutritional review for anyone on a Phe-restricted diet:** growth, plasma amino acids, B12, blood count, ferritin and vitamin D. [S1]

## Drug therapy

- **Consider sapropterin responsiveness in every PAH-deficient patient except those with 2 known null variants;** test with a single 20 mg/kg dose and a change in blood Phe. Prove long-term benefit in a trial (at least a 100% rise in natural protein, or 75% or more of Phe levels in range). [S1]
- **Pegvaliase may be offered over 16 years of age** when sapropterin cannot achieve control, with close safety follow-up; it is not recommended in pregnancy or before conception. [S1]
- **Late-diagnosed children and adolescents should always be treated;** in untreated adults, decide individually and try treatment for at least 6 months. [S1]

## Maternal PKU

- 🚩 **Maternal PKU is a high-risk pregnancy:** high Phe is teratogenic (growth restriction, microcephaly, intellectual disability, congenital heart defects). Reach target Phe before conception; if already pregnant, within 7 days. Prevent unplanned pregnancy. [S1]
- 🚩 **Unplanned pregnancy: the metabolic centre contacts the woman the same day and diet starts within 24 h.** [S1]
- **In pregnancy:** blood Phe at least twice weekly (weekly before conception); if Phe falls below 120 μmol/L, raise Phe intake by 50–100 mg/day at once; avoid weight loss. Give at least 400 μg folic acid daily before conception and for the first 12 weeks, and at least 200 mg/day DHA. [S1]
- **Fetal anomaly ultrasound at 18–22 weeks,** with extra growth scans if control is sub-optimal. Breastfeeding is supported whether or not the baby has PKU. [S1]
