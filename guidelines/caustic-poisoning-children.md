# Caustic (corrosive) poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** alkali and acid exposure by ingestion, inhalation, skin and eye in children: risk assessment, endoscopy, decontamination, irrigation and disposition. ⚠️ **Overlap:** this page covers the ground of the shorter, undated RCH `alkali-poisoning-children` page and is the dated source to prefer where they differ. ## Key points

- ⚠️ **Alkali ingestion is often asymptomatic early — that does not exclude serious injury**, and no mouth or pharyngeal ulcers does not rule out a gastro-oesophageal lesion. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Only an endoscopist should pass a nasogastric tube.** [S1]
- **Threatened airway: consider early intubation.** After inhalation, respiratory symptoms can be delayed. [S1]

## Background

- **Exposure** may be by inhalation, ingestion or topical (skin/eye). ⚠️ **Significant GI ulceration is likely when pH is over 11 or under 2**; liquids can travel further than powders. [S1]
- **Two injury patterns:** liquefactive necrosis penetrates tissue deeply over hours to days; coagulative necrosis limits burn depth by scar/eschar formation. [S1]

## Assessment

- **Assess:** every deliberate self-poisoning · every significant accidental ingestion · every symptomatic child · any child whose developmental age does not fit accidental poisoning (consider non-accidental poisoning). [S1]
- **Get the container or photos** and ask the **Victorian Poisons Information Centre (tel. 13 11 26)** how corrosive the product is. Consider co-ingestants such as paracetamol. [S1]
- **Symptoms can be delayed and/or minimal:** pain, nausea and vomiting, drooling, or refusal of food and drink · ⚠️ stridor, respiratory distress · splash burns of skin or eyes · systemic circulatory collapse and/or multi-organ failure. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In symptomatic children, endoscopy (within 12–24 hours) is the best way to assess GI injury, and is needed even when the chest X-ray and bloods are normal.** Bloods if systemic features; CXR; paracetamol level in all intentional overdoses. [S1]

## Ingestion or inhalation

- ⚠️ **Activated charcoal, GI decontamination and neutralisation are all contraindicated.** Severe poisoning may need standard resuscitation. [S1]
- **Asymptomatic: observe, then trial oral intake 4 hours** post-exposure — or earlier if suspicion is low or the ingestion likely benign, after discussion with Poisons Information. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Any symptoms: admit; oesophagoscopy within 24 hours** (PIPER, local paediatric surgery). **Keep nil by mouth and start an intravenous PPI.** [S1]
- **Analgesia:** if opiate or parenteral analgesia is needed, consult the local pain management service. [S1]

## Skin

- **Remove clothing and particulate matter; irrigate with copious low-pressure water for at least 10–15 minutes**, or until skin pH is normal (pH 6–7 in children). [S1]
- **Recheck the pH 15–20 minutes later; if still abnormal, irrigate again** — several hours may be needed. Then treat as a thermal burn. [S1]

## Eye

- ⚠️ **Alkali in the eye is an ophthalmic emergency.** Remove contact lenses, give topical anaesthetic if available, and **irrigate immediately with 1 L normal saline via a giving set for at least 10–15 minutes regardless of pH.** Sedation may be needed to do this promptly. [S1]
- **Aim for a conjunctival pH at the end of 7.5–8.0** (or that of an unaffected other eye), checked with indicator paper. **Test again 20 minutes after irrigating**; repeat irrigation may be necessary. [S1]
- **Record visual acuity if possible, check for corneal damage with fluorescein, and refer promptly to ophthalmology.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Consult the local paediatric team for all symptomatic children**; consider admitting every child or young person who has taken an intentional overdose. [S1]
- **Transfer** anyone needing care beyond the local hospital's comfort or needing intensive care. **PIPER: 1300 137 650.** [S1]
- **Discharge** once eating and drinking and still asymptomatic after 4 hours. Suspected non-accidental injury or mental health concerns may warrant admission on their own; after deliberate ingestion, a risk assessment must show low risk of further self-harm. [S1]
