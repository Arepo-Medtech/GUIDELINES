# Mantle cell lymphoma

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, staging, observation of indolent disease, limited-stage and advanced first-line treatment (younger fit, older/unfit, TP53/high-risk), relapsed/refractory disease (BTK inhibitors, CAR T-cells, pirtobrutinib, allograft) and follow-up including post-CAR-T care for adults with mantle cell lymphoma. ## Diagnosis

- **Base the diagnosis on an excised node where feasible** (V, A); use a core biopsy only when no node is easy to reach, never fine-needle aspiration, and have an expert haematopathologist review it. Leukaemic-only disease can be diagnosed from marrow or blood. [S1]
- **MCL needs a mature B-cell phenotype** (usually CD5-positive) plus proof of cyclin D1 (protein expression, CCND1 rearrangement, or both); conventional MCL expresses SOX11. [S1]
- **Check TP53 mutation status at diagnosis** (IV, A), preferably by sequencing, because immunostaining misses truncating mutations, which are up to 30% of the total. If only p53 staining is available, call it positive at 50% or more. [S1]
- **High-risk histology is blastoid or pleomorphic morphology, or Ki-67 of 30% or more.** Re-biopsy whenever new treatment is needed, repeating Ki-67 and TP53 testing. (IV, B) [S1]

## Staging and work-up

- **Stage with CT of neck, chest, abdomen and pelvis plus marrow aspirate and trephine** (V, A). If disease looks limited (stage I–II), add PET-CT and GI endoscopy with biopsies (V, B): PET-CT misses marrow and gut involvement too often. [S1]
- **Skip routine lumbar puncture without neurological symptoms** (CNS involvement under 1%). Screen for HIV and hepatitis B and C, and assess the heart before a BTK inhibitor or an anthracycline. [S1]

## Indolent and limited-stage disease

- **Asymptomatic, low-risk MCL can be watched:** review every 3 months at first, then every 3–6 months (V, B). In a UK cohort 38% were observed, with a median of about 21 months before treatment. Early targeted therapy belongs only in trials (II, C). [S1]
- **Stage I without risk factors: observe or give involved-site radiotherapy (24–36 Gy)** (II, B). For intermediate risk or tumour load, consider brief systemic therapy then radiotherapy (IV, B); stage II with high-risk features is treated as advanced disease (IV, B). [S1]

## Advanced disease: younger and fit

- **Fit younger patients (including fit people over 65): R-CHOP with ibrutinib alternating with R-DHAP or R-DHAOx, then ibrutinib for 2 years and rituximab maintenance for 3 years.** (I, A) [S1]
- **Autograft is now optional:** in TRIANGLE, adding ibrutinib without transplant matched ibrutinib with transplant at far lower toxicity, and ibrutinib after autograft adds much infection. Discuss autograft only for selected high-risk patients (II, C). [S1]
- **If no covalent BTK inhibitor is available first line:** rituximab with high-dose cytarabine induction, autograft, then 3 years of rituximab maintenance (I, B). Autograft can be left out after complete response with NGS-confirmed molecular remission (II, A). If ibrutinib specifically is unavailable, a second-generation BTK inhibitor is reasonable despite no phase III data. [S1]

## Advanced disease: older or unfit

- **Age 65–70 or over, or unfit for intensive therapy:** rituximab-chemotherapy (R-BAC, III, B; or BR, VR-CAP or R-CHOP, I, B), BR plus acalabrutinib (I, B), or rituximab with a continuous covalent BTK inhibitor (I, B; not EMA/FDA approved). [S1]
- **Give rituximab maintenance for 2 years or longer** after first-line BR, VR-CAP or R-CHOP. [S1]
- **VR-CAP spares the T-cell suppression of bendamustine,** keeping future CAR T-cell or T-cell-engager options open, so it may suit high-risk patients. [S1]
- **For very frail patients, low-dose palliative radiotherapy (2 Gy × 2)** can relieve symptoms at diagnosis or relapse. [S1]

## High-risk (TP53) disease

- ⚠️ **TP53-mutated MCL does badly with immunochemotherapy** (median PFS about 1 year). Consider a first-line anti-CD20 + BTK inhibitor + BCL2 inhibitor combination if available (III, B), and offer trials at every stage (IV, B). Fit patients up to 70 with p53 overexpression may get the ibrutinib-containing intensive schedule with or without autograft (II, C). [S1]

## Relapsed or refractory disease

- **BTK inhibitor-naive relapse after immunochemotherapy: a covalent BTK inhibitor (± venetoclax) at second line rather than later** (III, B), including CNS relapse (IV, B), where ibrutinib is preferred. [S1]
- **For BTK inhibitor toxicity, switch agents:** acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib cause less atrial fibrillation, hypertension and bleeding than ibrutinib, and switching usually fixes toxicity without losing control. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Relapse on continuous BTK inhibitor once meant median survival of about 8 months.** After BTK inhibitor-containing therapy, options are anti-CD19 CAR T-cells (III, A), pirtobrutinib (III, A) or immunochemotherapy (IV, B); rechallenge with a covalent BTK inhibitor only after prior intolerance or a fixed-duration course (V, C). [S1]
- **Brexucabtagene autoleucel** (ZUMA-2, 68 BTK inhibitor-exposed patients): 91% response, 68% complete, median PFS 25.8 months, but grade 3+ neurotoxicity in 31%, a real problem in older comorbid patients. Liso-cel gave similar responses (83%, CR 72%) with far less severe CRS (1%) and neurotoxicity (9%). [S1]
- **Pirtobrutinib** (non-covalent BTK inhibitor) responds less often (49%, complete 16%) but is well tolerated apart from infection and neutropenia; it suits patients unfit for or relapsing after CAR T-cells, or as a bridge when disease moves fast. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid bendamustine before CAR T-cell collection.** Consider allogeneic transplant for younger fit patients when CAR T-cells are unavailable or have failed (IV, B). [S1]

## Follow-up

- **Follow up for life, as MCL keeps relapsing** (V, D): history, examination, blood counts and chemistry with LDH every 3–4 months for 2 years, 6-monthly for 3 more years, then yearly. Do not use PET-CT for surveillance; check TSH yearly after neck radiotherapy. [S1]
- **After CD19 CAR T-cells:** antiviral prophylaxis (aciclovir 200–400 mg or valaciclovir 500 mg orally twice daily) and pneumocystis prophylaxis for at least 6 months and until CD4 exceeds 200 cells/ml. With IgG under 400 mg/dL and serious or recurrent infection, give immunoglobulin (IVIG 400–500 mg/kg every 3–4 weeks). Vaccinate against COVID-19 and influenza from 3 months; other inactivated vaccines wait until 6 months and live vaccines until beyond 1 year. [S1]
