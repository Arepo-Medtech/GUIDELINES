# Giant cell tumour of bone: denosumab

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** denosumab for adults and skeletally mature adolescents with giant cell tumour of bone (GCTB) that is recurrent, unresectable, or resectable only at the cost of severe morbidity: eligibility, loading and maintenance dosing, calcium and vitamin D, hypocalcaemia, jaw osteonecrosis, monitoring and the phase 2 evidence. Surgery and other local treatment are not covered. Denosumab for bone metastases: see `bone-metastases`. Soft tissue sarcoma: see `soft-tissue-sarcoma`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Who it is for

- **Sarcoma care is complex and multimodal, so a sarcoma multidisciplinary team should plan and review treatment,** and patients should be offered a clinical trial where one fits. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Denosumab is for GCTB in adults or skeletally mature adolescents when the tumour has recurred, cannot be resected, or could be resected only with severe morbidity.** [S1]
- **The evidence base is limited:** eviQ's panel relied chiefly on two phase II trials (Thomas 2010 and Chawla 2019). [S1]

## Dose and administration

- ⚠️ **Denosumab 120 mg subcutaneously on days 1, 8 and 15 of the first 28-day cycle (loading), then 120 mg on day 1 of each 28-day cycle,** continuing until unacceptable toxicity, progression, or the patient chooses to stop. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Every patient takes daily oral calcium (at least 500 mg) and vitamin D (at least 400 IU) for the whole course,** unless contraindicated. [S1]
- **Let the refrigerated syringe reach room temperature (about 30 minutes), inject slowly under the skin, and rotate sites** between upper outer thigh, abdomen (keeping 5 cm clear of the navel) and upper arm. [S1]
- **Denosumab is a PBS authority item for this use;** a biosimilar is available. [S1]
- **No dose change is needed for kidney function;** denosumab has not been studied in liver impairment. [S1]

## Safety

- ⚠️ **Dental review before starting and every 6 months on treatment,** and avoid dental procedures once denosumab has begun, to lower the risk of jaw osteonecrosis. [S1]
- **Jaw osteonecrosis occurs in roughly 2% of patients on denosumab** and may become more likely the longer it is used. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe symptomatic hypocalcaemia (corrected calcium below 1.75 mmol/L) has occurred, including deaths.** Look for confusion, tetany, seizures and QTc prolongation. Risk is higher with eGFR under 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, dialysis, or previous thyroid surgery. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never give denosumab together with a bisphosphonate.** Other calcium-lowering drugs (calcitonin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) add to hypocalcaemia: use them cautiously, watch calcium and keep calcium and vitamin D intake adequate. [S1]
- **Early side effects are headache, low calcium and phosphate, fatigue and joint or muscle pain; later ones are jaw osteonecrosis and, very rarely, atypical femoral fracture,** which may relate to how long treatment lasts. [S1]
- **Long-term use carries risks of jaw osteonecrosis, atypical femoral fracture and rebound hypercalcaemia once denosumab is stopped.** [S1]
- **Discuss fertility before starting; consider a pregnancy test, and use effective contraception during and after treatment.** [S1]

## Monitoring

- **Check calcium, magnesium and phosphate before starting and before every dose;** check vitamin D when clinically indicated. [S1]
- **Assess the patient before each dose;** any toxicity may mean a delay, dose change or omission and a medical review. [S1]

## Evidence

- **The main phase 2 study (2008 to 2016) enrolled 532 patients in 3 cohorts:** 267 with surgically unsalvageable GCTB, 253 whose planned surgery would cause severe morbidity, and 12 from an earlier denosumab study. All got 120 mg every 4 weeks, with loading doses on days 8 and 15 in the first two cohorts; safety was the primary end point. [S1]
- **After a median of 65.8 months in the unsalvageable cohort, the commonest grade 3 or higher treatment-related events were hypophosphataemia (5%), jaw osteonecrosis (3%), limb pain (2%) and anaemia (2%).** In that cohort the median time to progression or recurrence had not been reached, and 11% progressed in the first treatment phase. [S1]
