# Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy: diagnosis, imaging, drug treatment of outflow obstruction including mavacamten, septal reduction, atrial fibrillation, sudden-death prevention, genetic testing, pregnancy and exercise. Fabry disease, one HCM phenocopy, is in `fabry-disease`; heart failure with reduced ejection fraction is in `heart-failure`. PBS access is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ⚠️ International guideline (ESC 2023, AHA/ACC/multisociety 2024 and JCS/JHFS 2018, as compared in a 2025 systematic review). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **This is a systematic review of guidelines, not a guideline.** It reports what the three guidelines recommend and where they disagree; the AHA/ACC 2024 and ESC 2023 guidelines themselves could not be read (bot challenge).

> 🇦🇺 **Australian counterpart:** the CSANZ *Diagnosis and Management of Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy* position statement (Cardiac Society of Australia and New Zealand, 2016). It remains the Australian reference for diagnosis, family screening and genetic counselling, but it predates mavacamten and cardiac myosin inhibitors.

> ⚠️ **PBS position:** mavacamten is the only PBS-listed drug for obstructive HCM, and every listing is Authority Required (see the table below). Both international guidelines place a myosin inhibitor after a beta-blocker or non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker has failed to control symptoms of obstruction. **Echo monitoring of LVEF, CYP2C19 genotyping and dose titration for mavacamten are not covered by this source.**

## Diagnose

- **HCM is LV hypertrophy that abnormal loading or another cardiac, metabolic, storage, infiltrative or systemic disease does not explain;** the American and Japanese guidelines also mention identifying a pathogenic variant. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Adult threshold: end-diastolic wall thickness of 15 mm or more in any LV segment, or 13 mm or more when a relative has confirmed HCM.** Per AHA/ACC, 13–14 mm can be diagnostic with a pathogenic or likely pathogenic sarcomere variant; per ESC it needs other features weighed. [S1]
- **Start with transthoracic echocardiography, using provocation (such as Valsalva) to find the peak LVOT gradient.** If symptomatic with a resting gradient of 50 mmHg or less, do an exercise echocardiogram. [S1]
- **Cardiac MRI:** the ESC recommends it with late gadolinium enhancement at initial evaluation; the AHA/ACC reserves it for inconclusive echocardiography or a suspected alternative diagnosis (athlete's heart, storage or infiltrative disease). [S1]

## Treat outflow obstruction

- ⚠️ **First line for symptoms from LVOT obstruction: a non-vasodilating beta-blocker; if not tolerated, a non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker.** All three guidelines agree. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Mavacamten (a cardiac myosin inhibitor) or disopyramide is added when symptoms of obstruction persist despite a beta-blocker or calcium channel blocker.** It is a Class 1 recommendation in the AHA/ACC guideline and Class IIa in the ESC guideline; the older Japanese guideline predates myosin inhibitors. [S1]
- **Septal reduction therapy (myectomy or alcohol septal ablation) belongs in experienced centres with a multidisciplinary team.** ESC and JCS: gradient of 50 mmHg or more (rest or provoked) with NYHA class III–IV despite maximal drugs. AHA/ACC: symptoms from obstruction despite optimal therapy, a gradient around 50 mmHg or more with septal hypertrophy and systolic anterior motion, and enough septal thickness. [S1]
- **Choose myectomy when other lesions also need surgery; choose alcohol septal ablation when surgery is contraindicated or too risky.** [S1]

## Non-obstructive HCM

- **For angina-like chest pain or breathlessness without obstruction, use a beta-blocker or non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker.** ESC adds oral nitrates or ranolazine for angina; AHA/ACC and JCS support diuretics for exertional breathlessness, used cautiously if obstruction is present. [S1]

## Atrial fibrillation

- ⚠️ **Anticoagulate every patient with HCM and atrial fibrillation for life, whatever the CHA2DS2-VASc score.** AHA/ACC names direct oral anticoagulants first line over warfarin. [S1]

## Sudden cardiac death

- ⚠️ **Secondary prevention: an ICD after cardiac arrest from ventricular arrhythmia or documented sustained VT.** All guidelines agree. [S1]
- **ESC approach: the HCM Risk-SCD calculator** (age, maximum wall thickness, left atrial diameter, maximum LVOT gradient, family history of sudden death, non-sustained VT, unexplained syncope), repeated every 1–2 years. Consider an ICD at a 5-year risk of 6% or more; individually at 4–6%; and below 4% if LGE is 15% or more or LVEF is below 50%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **AHA/ACC approach: offer an ICD if one or more major risk factors is present:** HCM sudden death in a first-degree or close relative aged 50 or under; LV hypertrophy of 30 mm or more; recent syncope thought to be arrhythmic; an LV apical aneurysm with scar; or LVEF below 50%. It reassesses risk every 1–2 years and gives more weight to longer, faster, more frequent non-sustained VT. [S1]
- **Where they disagree:** ESC does not treat an apical aneurysm alone as a strong enough predictor to justify a primary-prevention ICD; AHA/ACC does. [S1]
- **A subcutaneous ICD is a reasonable alternative** to a transvenous device when no bradycardia pacing, resynchronisation or anti-tachycardia pacing is expected. [S1]

## Genetics, pregnancy and exercise

- **Offer genetic testing to the patient to enable cascade screening of relatives,** and in borderline or atypical cases or suspected phenocopies. Do not test phenotype-negative relatives unless a pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant has been found in the proband. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Mavacamten is contraindicated in pregnancy because it is teratogenic** (AHA/ACC). Offer genetic and reproductive counselling before conception, review medicines in advance, plan vaginal delivery for most women, and anticoagulate with low-molecular-weight heparin or a vitamin K antagonist. [S1]
- **Mild to moderate exercise benefits all able patients,** and genotype-positive, phenotype-negative people are not barred from high-intensity exercise or competitive sport. [S1]
- **High-intensity sport is where they differ:** ESC advises against it in high-risk patients and in obstruction with exercise-induced complex ventricular arrhythmias; AHA/ACC does not restrict most patients universally, asks for expert shared decision-making, and advises against an ICD placed only to allow competitive sport. [S1]

## Newer evidence

- **The guidelines mention only mavacamten among myosin inhibitors.** Phase 3 data for aficamten (shorter half-life) and longer-term and real-world mavacamten data were published after them, and myosin inhibitors are being tested in non-obstructive HCM. [S1]
