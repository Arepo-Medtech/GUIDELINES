# Advanced hepatocellular carcinoma: first-line atezolizumab with bevacizumab

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** first-line systemic treatment of unresectable or advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (BCLC B or C, Child-Pugh A) with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab: eligibility, pre-treatment variceal assessment, dosing, monitoring and stopping rules. Surveillance of people at high risk: see `hepatocellular-carcinoma-surveillance`. Other first-line and later-line regimens (durvalumab with tremelimumab, lenvatinib, sorafenib, ipilimumab with nivolumab) are separate eviQ protocols and are not covered here. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Who is eligible

- **Use as first systemic therapy for advanced or unresectable HCC that cannot be treated curatively or locoregionally**, when the disease is BCLC B or C, liver function is Child-Pugh class A, performance status is ECOG 0 or 1, and varices have been checked by endoscopy within the last 6 months. [S1]
- **Start only under specialists experienced with immunotherapy who can manage immune-related toxicity,** and decide it with a multidisciplinary team that includes a gastroenterologist and a medical oncologist. [S1]
- **Use clinical judgement and expert discussion if the patient has** significant autoimmune disease, an organ transplant, previous viral hepatitis or previous lung irradiation. [S1]
- **The pivotal trial excluded** autoimmune disease, untreated or partly treated oesophageal or gastric varices, and therapeutic anticoagulant or antiplatelet treatment (low-dose aspirin was allowed). [S1]

## Before starting: red flags

- ⚠️ **Fatal variceal bleeding risk.** Look for gastro-oesophageal varices and treat them as needed within 6 months before the first dose. Do not start if varices are untreated, bleeding now or recently, high-risk, or have not been checked in the past 6 months. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Caution with blood thinners:** current use of full-dose anticoagulants, thrombolytics or antiplatelet drugs, or use within 10 days before starting. [S1]
- **Screen every patient for hepatitis before a checkpoint inhibitor.** Patients with chronic hepatitis B or C, a detectable HIV viral load or AIDS need close monitoring and antiviral treatment where indicated. [S1]
- **Get blood pressure under control before bevacizumab** and keep monitoring it; start or adjust antihypertensives as needed. Do a baseline urine test for protein. [S1]
- **If steroids are needed before treatment, keep the dose as low as possible** (ideally no more than 10 mg prednisolone equivalent a day). [S1]

## Treatment and dosing

- ⚠️ **Regimen:** atezolizumab 1,200 mg IV plus bevacizumab 15 mg/kg IV, both on day 1 of a 21-day cycle, continued until progression or unacceptable toxicity. The first infusions run over 60 minutes (atezolizumab) and 90 minutes (bevacizumab), and later doses can be shortened to 30 minutes if tolerated. [S1]
- **Alternatives:** atezolizumab may be given subcutaneously (1,875 mg every 21 days). If bevacizumab is stopped, atezolizumab can continue alone, with an alternative IV schedule of 1680 mg every 28 days. [S1]
- **Do not dose-reduce the checkpoint inhibitor.** No adjustment is needed for older age or mild hepatic impairment; moderate to severe hepatic impairment has not been studied. [S1]
- **Early growth on scans may be pseudo-progression** (rare, about 5%). If the patient seems to be benefiting, consider carrying on and repeating the scan after 4 to 6 weeks before calling it progression. [S1]

## Monitoring

- **Bloods:** FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs, cortisol, thyroid function and glucose at baseline; FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs and glucose before every cycle; thyroid function every 4–6 weeks on treatment and 12-weekly afterwards (consider cortisol on the same schedule). [S1]
- **Check urine protein periodically (for example every 3 to 4 months)** or if there is oedema or unexplained low albumin; significant proteinuria (above 3 g/L) may need a treatment break. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Immune-related adverse events can start early, worsen fast, and appear after treatment stops; some are fatal.** Monitor actively, teach patients to report symptoms early, and manage with interruption or cessation, specialist input and corticosteroids. [S1]
- **Around surgery:** hold bevacizumab for at least 28 days before elective surgery and restart no sooner than 28 days afterwards, once the wound has healed. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stop bevacizumab permanently for:** grade 3 or worse haemorrhage; life-threatening venous thromboembolism, pulmonary embolism, stroke or arterial insufficiency; arterial thromboembolism; grade 4 or persistent grade 3 hypertension; nephrotic syndrome; GI perforation or fistula; or reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy. [S1]

## Evidence

- **Survival benefit over sorafenib:** in a phase III trial of 501 patients, median overall survival was 19.2 months with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab versus 13.4 months with sorafenib (HR 0.66). [S1]
- **Progression-free survival and response were also better:** median PFS 6.9 vs 4.3 months; confirmed response 30% vs 11%, with complete response in 8%. [S1]
- **Toxicity was similar and quality of life held up longer:** grade 3–4 treatment-related events 43% vs 46%; time to quality-of-life deterioration 11.2 vs 3.6 months. [S1]
