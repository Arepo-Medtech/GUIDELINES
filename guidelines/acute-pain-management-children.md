# Acute pain management in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment, non-drug measures, local anaesthesia and graded systemic analgesia (sucrose, paracetamol, ibuprofen, oxycodone, morphine, intranasal fentanyl, tramadol), opioid safety and naloxone for acute pain in children. Counterparts: `opioid-analgesic-stewardship-acute-pain` (ACSQHC standard, adults and children) and `chronic-pain` (adult, chronic).

## Principles

- **Assess pain regularly and after every intervention; combine non-drug and drug methods; use a graded drug approach.** [S1]
- **Undertreated pain has lasting effects** on the child's experience of pain and later medical encounters, and can harm healing and development. [S1]
- **Pain can make a child very quiet.** Reassess behaviour, physiology and pain after the predicted onset of each drug. [S1]
- **Pain tools:** FPS-R 3–18 years (self-rated); numeric rating scale over 7 years (self-rated); NIPS under 3 months (clinician); FLACC 2 months to 7 years (clinician, score 0–10); revised FLACC for all non-verbal children. [S1]
- **Constant pain: prescribe regularly, not PRN.** Give paracetamol and ibuprofen together where appropriate. **Use opioids early for moderate to severe pain.** [S1]

## Non-drug and local measures

- **Injuries:** splint or backslab suspected fractures immediately, ice (if age appropriate) and elevate, dress burns promptly, and consider a regional nerve block for limb or finger injuries. [S1]
- **Infants:** swaddling, feeding, skin-to-skin care and a dummy. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Topical anaesthetic eye drops are for examination only — they interfere with healing.** [S1]
- **Severe acute ear pain:** short-term topical 2% lignocaine or Auralgan®, 1–2 drops, **only onto an intact tympanic membrane**. [S1]

## Systemic analgesia

- **Sucrose (any concentration, e.g. 24%, 33%) orally per procedure:** preterm (32–40 weeks corrected) 0.2–0.5 mL; under 1 month 0.5–1 mL; 1–18 months 1–2 mL. Daily max: preterm 2.5 mL; under 3 months 5 mL; 3 months and over 10 mL. Give a quarter of the dose to the anterior tongue 2 minutes before the procedure. [S1]
- **Paracetamol** (dose on ideal body weight; oral preferred). **Oral:** 15 mg/kg (max 1 g) every 4–6 hours; daily max birth–1 month 60 mg/kg; over 1 month 90 mg/kg (max 4 g) for severe pain for up to 48 hours, then 60 mg/kg/day. **Rectal:** 15–20 mg/kg (max 1 g) every 6 hours, same daily limits; absorption delayed and erratic; avoid in immunocompromised children. **IV:** under 1 month 10 mg/kg every 6 hours (max 40 mg/kg/day); over 1 month 15 mg/kg every 6 hours (max 1 g a dose; 60 mg/kg/day, max 4 g). ⚠️ **mg/mL mix-ups have led to significant overdoses in young children.** [S1]
- **Ibuprofen orally, over 3 months:** 10 mg/kg every 6–8 hours (max 400 mg), with food; max 30 mg/kg/day (max 2.4 g). Caution in renal impairment, dehydration, bleeding or anticoagulation. **Asthma is not a contraindication.** [S1]
- **Oxycodone orally:** 1–12 months 0.05–0.1 mg/kg every 4 hours (max 0.6 mg/kg/day); over 12 months 0.1–0.2 mg/kg (adult 5–10 mg) every 4 hours (max 1.2 mg/kg/day). Short-term use; **do not prescribe for outpatients without a clear diagnosis.** [S1]
- **Morphine IV/subcut:** under 1 month 0.025 mg/kg; 1–12 months 0.05 mg/kg; over 12 months ≤0.2 mg/kg, capped at 5–10 mg. Cumulative max: under 1 month 0.1 mg/kg every 4–6 hours (0.6 mg/kg a day); 1–12 months 0.1 mg/kg every 2–4 hours (1.2 mg/kg a day); over 12 months 0.2 mg/kg every 2–4 hours (2.4 mg/kg a day). [S1]
- **Intranasal fentanyl, over 12 months:** first dose 1.5 microg/kg (max 100 microg), then 0.75 microg/kg after 10 minutes; total 3 microg/kg/day; split between nostrils by atomiser. **Not recommended under 12 months.** Consider other ongoing analgesia after the second dose. [S1]
- **Tramadol oral/IV, over 12 years:** 0.5–1 mg/kg every 6 hours (max 100 mg); max 4 mg/kg/day (max 400 mg); up to 2 mg/kg if no sleep-apnoea or respiratory-depression risk. ⚠️ **Avoid in epilepsy and in young people on SSRIs.** [S1]

## Ongoing pain and opioid safety

- **Never give opioids as the sole analgesic** — combine with regular simple analgesia. Consider causes of ongoing severe pain such as **compartment syndrome**. [S1]
- **Renal impairment:** prefer fentanyl over morphine, reduce oxycodone dose and frequency, and watch for opioid accumulation. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Opioid toxicity:** airway support, oxygen, assist ventilation; **naloxone 10 microg/kg (max 400 microg) IM or IV**, then **100 microg/kg (max 2 mg)** if no response. Repeat doses may be needed for long-acting opioids. [S1]
- **Prescribing:** involve the GP for courses over 1 week; keep courses short; **prescribe aperients with ongoing opioids**; check safe-prescribing databases before prescribing opioids. [S1]
- **Consult the paediatric team** if analgesia is inadequate despite a regular oral/IV regimen. **Discharge** when pain or distress is managed and anticipated pain has been planned for. [S1]
