# Cervical dystonia (spasmodic torticollis) — botulinum toxin therapy

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** botulinum toxin type A (BoNT-A) treatment of adult cervical dystonia (spasmodic torticollis): excluding mimics, classifying the pattern, when to start, goals, choosing muscles by movement direction, dilution, reinjection interval, dysphagia and other adverse effects, and non-response. ## Recognise and assess

- **Cervical dystonia is sustained or intermittent contraction of neck muscles that forces the head into abnormal positions.** BoNT-A is first-line treatment for focal and multifocal muscle overactivity. [S1]
- ⚠️ **First confirm isolated cervical dystonia and exclude mimics:** generalised, combined, paroxysmal or neurodegenerative dystonia, atlantoaxial subluxation, musculoskeletal disorders, cervical spine injury (traumatic, infectious or neoplastic), tics and functional dystonia. [S1]
- **Classify the pattern** with the classical terms (torticollis, laterocollis, anterocollis, retrocollis) and the COL-CAP system, which separates neck-driven (collis) from head-driven (caput) components. [S1]
- **Rate severity with TWSTRS** (severity, disability, pain); the modified Tsui scale is an alternative. Also ask about anxiety, depression, pain, sleep and fatigue. [S1]

## When to treat and what to aim for

- **Start BoNT-A once a clear dystonic pattern causes pain, abnormal posture, functional limitation or social impairment.** The decision rests on the dystonic driver and whether it is tonic, phasic or tremor-dominant, not just on how far the head deviates. [S1]
- **Record a baseline** (pain score, range of motion, dystonic activation, tremor) to guide muscle choice and measure later cycles. The panel favours early treatment to prevent maladaptive movement and secondary musculoskeletal problems. [S1]
- **Goals:** a more neutral, comfortable head posture, less pain, better daily function and less tremor. Set them individually and explain expected results. [S1]

## Evidence and choice of toxin

- **Evidence grade:** class I evidence shows all three BoNT-A products and BoNT-B improve cervical dystonia (level A for abobotulinumtoxinA, onabotulinumtoxinA and rimabotulinumtoxinB; level B for incobotulinumtoxinA). BoNT-A also reduces dystonia pain (level A). [S2]
- **BoNT-A and BoNT-B work equally well and for as long (level A), but BoNT-B causes more side effects (level B),** notably dry mouth and swallowing difficulty. [S2]

## Choosing muscles

- **Pick muscles by the direction of abnormal movement.** Rotation (torticaput/torticollis): contralateral sternocleidomastoid and semispinalis capitis; ipsilateral splenius capitis/cervicis, levator scapulae, inferior oblique of the head and trapezius. [S1]
- **Lateral tilt (laterocaput/laterocollis):** ipsilateral sternocleidomastoid, levator scapulae, middle and posterior scalenes, trapezius, splenius and semispinalis capitis. **Extension (retrocaput/retrocollis):** bilateral splenius, semispinalis, trapezius and inferior oblique of the head. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Flexion (anterocaput/anterocollis):** bilateral sternocleidomastoids, levator scapulae, anterior scalenes, longus colli/capitis. Take care with deep anterior neck muscles because of dysphagia and airway risk. [S1]
- **Ultrasound improves accuracy in the scalenes and deep neck flexors and extensors;** EMG helps find active motor units in tremor-dominant patterns. Guidance is not mandatory for every muscle. [S1]
- **EMG-assisted muscle selection (level A):** in treatment-naive patients, choosing muscles by examination plus polymyographic EMG and injecting under EMG guidance improves dystonia and pain more than examination alone; without polymyography 24–41% of dystonic muscles were missed. It can also help when benefit wanes (level C). [S2]

## Dose, dilution and interval

- **Individualise the total dose** by pattern complexity, number of muscles, body build and response to past cycles. Higher doses are often needed when both caput and collis components are active or tremor is prominent. [S1]
- **Working dilutions:** onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U/1 mL; incobotulinumtoxinA 100 U/1 mL; abobotulinumtoxinA 500 U/2.5 mL or 500 U/1 mL. [S1]
- **Reinject about every 12–16 weeks,** matching the expected duration of benefit. The panel accepts a flexible 8–12-week interval only for incobotulinumtoxinA. [S1]
- **Do not reinject earlier than 12 weeks without a compelling indication** (S1 summary of practice statements). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Reference average dose per muscle (S1 Table A7), units of onabotulinumtoxinA / incobotulinumtoxinA / abobotulinumtoxinA — rotators and tilters:** sternocleidomastoid 20–75 / 20–100 / 50–150; splenius capitis 20–75 / 20–100 / 40–150; splenius cervicis 15–80 / 15–80 / 25–200; levator scapulae 15–75 / 15–75 / 25–200; trapezius 25–100 / 25–100 / 50–200. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Reference average dose per muscle, continued (same units) — extensors and suboccipital muscles:** semispinalis capitis 15–80 / 15–100 / 25–200; semispinalis cervicis 15–80 / 15–80 / 25–150; longissimus capitis 15–50 / 15–75 / 25–100; obliquus capitis inferior 10–50 / 10–50 / 20–150; obliquus capitis superior 10–30 / 10–30 / 20–50; rectus capitis major and minor 10–50 / 10–50 / 20–80. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Reference average dose per muscle, continued (same units) — scalenes and anterior neck:** middle scalene 15–50 / 15–50 / 25–100; posterior scalene 15–50 / 15–50 / 20–100; anterior scalene 10–30 / 10–30 / 25–56; longus colli 10–50 / 10–50 / 20–80; submental complex 10–50 / 10–50 / 20–150. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Starting total dose per session:** abobotulinumtoxinA 500 IU (level A; an average 200–400 IU under EMG guidance also worked, with fewer side effects); incobotulinumtoxinA 120 IU (level B); rimabotulinumtoxinB 2,500 or 5,000 IU (level B) or 10,000 IU (level A). No optimal onabotulinumtoxinA starting dose can be recommended (level U); studies used 60 to 374 IU in total. [S2]
- **One tested split of 500 IU abobotulinumtoxinA:** 100–200 IU sternocleidomastoid, 250–350 IU splenius, 100–200 IU trapezius and 100–200 IU levator scapulae (RCT, 68 patients). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Switching products, dose conversion:** 1 IU onabotulinumtoxinA to 3 IU abobotulinumtoxinA (level A), though 1:2.5 may be equally safe and effective (level B); onabotulinumtoxinA to incobotulinumtoxinA 1:1 (level B). [S2]
- **Reinjecting sooner than 12 weeks:** only incobotulinumtoxinA has class I evidence that shorter intervals do not raise antibody risk; for the other products data are insufficient (level U). Experts advise leaving reinjection as long as clinically possible to limit antibody formation; product information gives a 10- to 12-week minimum. [S2]

## Adverse effects and follow-up

- ⚠️ **Dysphagia is the main adverse effect,** especially with deep or anterior neck injections or high doses there. Manage with diet changes, hydration and speech or swallow therapy; next time use lower doses or other targets, with US and EMG guidance. [S1]
- **Excess weakness shows as reduced neck support or exaggerated compensatory postures;** it is usually reversible and calls for a lower dose or better targeting. [S1]
- **Review within two to four weeks of injection** to pick up early complications and refine the plan. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Dysphagia rates and risk:** commoner after bilateral sternocleidomastoid injections; usually mild (severe in under 5%) and gone in 2–3 weeks. Reported rates range from 3.4% (current onabotulinumtoxinA) to 19.4% (abobotulinumtoxinA), with incobotulinumtoxinA 12.6% and rimabotulinumtoxinB 15.6%. Adding ultrasound guidance may lessen recurrent dysphagia (level U). [S2]

## Pregnancy, anticoagulants and neuromuscular disease

- ⚠️ **Avoid BoNT in pregnancy where possible, and during breastfeeding** (insufficient human data, level U). **Anticoagulants:** haematoma risk with coumarins seems low (level U). **Myasthenia gravis, ALS and Lambert–Eaton syndrome** are listed contraindications in product information, because BoNT can worsen or unmask them. [S2]

## When treatment stops working

- **True primary non-response is extraordinarily rare;** first re-examine the patient, the diagnosis and the injection strategy. [S1]
- **Secondary non-response, stepwise:** reassess and review goals; optimise muscle choice and technique with guidance; test for neutralising antibodies if suspected; switch BoNT-A formulation if resistance is shown; if still inadequate, consider a washout of about 8–12 months while considering functional neurosurgery. [S1]
- **Children:** focal cervical dystonia is uncommon, usually part of generalised or segmental dystonia, and no toxin is approved for childhood dystonia. [S1]
- **Secondary non-response is posture no longer improving after three or more failed cycles in a previous good responder;** it affects about 3–5%. Neutralising antibodies are one cause, commoner with rimabotulinumtoxinB (antibody failure in 44% in one short series), and switching from BoNT-A to BoNT-B helps only temporarily (level U). [S2]

## Other treatments

- **Adding physiotherapy to BoNT does not improve motor scores (level C),** but may improve disability and pain and prolong the effect (level U). [S2]
- **Oral drugs have a limited role:** trihexyphenidyl is poorly tolerated; diazepam or clonazepam mainly ease pain, anxiety and perhaps dystonic tremor; tetrabenazine is limited by depression and parkinsonism. **When BoNT is unsatisfactory,** pallidal deep brain stimulation appears a better choice than selective peripheral denervation, which is now rarely done. [S2]
