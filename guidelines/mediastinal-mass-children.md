# Mediastinal mass in children

> ⚠️ **A mediastinal mass on CXR needs urgent escalation to a tertiary paediatric hospital.** Never make the child lie flat; plan any sedation, anaesthesia or procedure for airway risk; no steroids without oncology.

---

## Why it is an emergency

- ⚠️ **Children with mediastinal masses can deteriorate acutely and rapidly become critically unwell.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **A mediastinal mass on CXR requires urgent escalation to a tertiary paediatric hospital** — involve retrieval services and oncology promptly. [S1]

## Assess

- **Ask about** noisy breathing (stridor or wheeze), **orthopnoea** (difficulty lying flat to sleep), bone pain, and collapse or dizziness. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Let the child adopt a position of comfort. Do not force them to lie flat.** [S1]
- **Examine for stridor or wheeze** (airway obstruction) and for **pericardial effusion or tamponade**: distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds, low blood pressure. [S1]
- **Look for SVC obstruction; test Pemberton's sign** by having the child lift both arms until they touch the sides of the face — positive if facial congestion, cyanosis and respiratory distress develop. [S1]
- **Examine for organomegaly or lymphadenopathy.** [S1]

## Investigate

- **Discuss with oncology before further bloods** to avoid repeat sampling; peripheral blood flow cytometry may be diagnostic. [S1]
- **Lateral CXR** to locate the mass; **CT chest only if the child can lie flat comfortably**; urgent echocardiogram and cardiology opinion if available. [S1]

## Manage

- ⚠️ **Arrange urgent retrieval to a tertiary paediatric hospital**, discussed with PICU and oncology. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Plan sedation, general anaesthesia and all invasive procedures carefully to minimise the risk of airway compromise.** [S1]
- **Start hydration as oncology advises.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not start corticosteroids without consulting oncology.** [S1]
- **Manage every child with a new or symptomatic mediastinal mass in a centre with paediatric intensive care**; discuss all children with a mediastinal mass with the local paediatric team. [S1]
