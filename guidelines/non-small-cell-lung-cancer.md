# Non-small cell lung cancer — EGFR-mutant disease, osimertinib

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** osimertinib for EGFR-mutant NSCLC in adults: first-line palliative treatment of locally advanced or metastatic disease, second-line treatment of T790M-positive disease, and consolidation after chemoradiation for unresectable stage III disease. It is authored from **one eviQ protocol page (ID 1996)**, because eviQ publishes regimens, not a treatment-sequence overview. ALK, ROS1, KRAS, MET, RET and BRAF-driven disease, chemo-immunotherapy for NSCLC without a driver, and resected early-stage NSCLC are not covered. Recognising anticancer-drug toxicity in general practice: see `anticancer-drugs-general-principles`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Who it is for

- **Osimertinib is first-line palliative therapy for locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC carrying an activating EGFR mutation.** It is also a second-line option once a T790M mutation is confirmed after progression on a first-generation EGFR TKI. [S1]
- **It also has a place after chemoradiation:** unresectable stage III NSCLC with an activating EGFR mutation that has not progressed after platinum-based chemoradiation. [S1]

## Dose and administration

- **Osimertinib 80 mg by mouth once daily**, continued until the disease progresses or toxicity becomes unacceptable. It is PBS authority-listed; 40 mg and 80 mg tablets exist. [S1]
- **If a strong CYP3A4 inducer** (such as carbamazepine, phenytoin or rifampicin) **must be used, consider raising the dose to 160 mg once daily** and watch for toxicity; drop back to 80 mg 3 weeks after the inducer is stopped. [S1]
- **No dose change is needed for kidney function** (use with caution when eGFR is below 15) **or for mild to moderate liver impairment;** osimertinib is not recommended in severe hepatic impairment. [S1]
- **Tablets are swallowed whole.** If swallowing is hard, disperse the tablet in 50 mL of plain water (no other liquid), drink it straight away and rinse the glass; the same method works through a nasogastric tube with 15 mL volumes, all given within 30 minutes. [S1]
- **A missed or vomited dose is not made up;** skip a forgotten dose if the next one is due in under 12 hours. [S1]

## Monitoring

- **Blood tests at baseline, then monthly:** calcium, magnesium, phosphate, FBC, EUC/eGFR and LFTs. [S1]
- **Check the heart before starting, especially with known cardiovascular disease:** ECG and relevant biochemistry, plus an echocardiogram in higher-risk patients. Repeat when clinically indicated or when a QT-prolonging drug is added. [S1]
- **Hepatitis B:** it is unknown whether routine HBsAg, anti-HBc and anti-HBs screening is needed with kinase inhibitors; decide case by case. [S1]

## Toxicity and red flags

- ⚠️ **QTc prolongation:** if QTc is 500 ms or more on at least 2 separate ECGs, withhold until it is under 481 ms (or back to baseline), then restart at 40 mg. Stop permanently if QTc prolongation comes with signs of serious arrhythmia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis:** fatal ILD has occurred in 0.3% of osimertinib-treated patients, and radiation pneumonitis may be more frequent. The product information advises stopping for good; the treating clinician may still consider reintroduction. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Keratitis:** refer promptly to ophthalmology for new or worsening eye inflammation, watering, photophobia, blurred vision, eye pain or a red eye. Contact lens wear may add risk. [S1]
- **Acneiform rash is common and tends to peak in weeks 2 to 4.** Advise moisturiser and sunscreen; early or prophylactic doxycycline (a tetracycline) with 1% hydrocortisone cream can be considered. [S1]
- **Diarrhoea:** loperamide is usually supplied. For grade 3 or 4 diarrhoea, hold osimertinib until it settles to grade 2 or less; if that takes no more than 3 weeks, restart at 80 mg or 40 mg, otherwise stop permanently. [S1]
- **Interactions:** osimertinib inhibits BCRP and P-gp, so levels of drugs such as rosuvastatin, methotrexate, digoxin or clopidogrel can rise (watch for myopathy with rosuvastatin). Other QT-prolonging drugs, such as antiarrhythmics, antipsychotics, azoles, clarithromycin, methadone or ondansetron, add to the risk of torsades. [S1]

## Evidence

- **FLAURA (first line):** 556 patients with exon 19 deletion or L858R disease were randomised to osimertinib or to gefitinib or erlotinib. Median overall survival was 38.6 versus 31.8 months (HR 0.80). [S1]
- **FLAURA toxicity:** grade 3 or worse adverse events in 42% on osimertinib and 47% on the older TKIs; rash or acne, diarrhoea and dry skin were the commonest. [S1]
- **AURA3 (second line, T790M-positive):** median progression-free survival 10.1 months with osimertinib against 4.4 months with platinum plus pemetrexed. [S1]
- **LAURA (after chemoradiation):** in 216 patients, median PFS was 39.1 months with osimertinib and 5.6 months with placebo (HR 0.16). The cost was more grade 3+ adverse events (35% vs 12%) and frequent radiation pneumonitis (48% vs 38%). [S1]
