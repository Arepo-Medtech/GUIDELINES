# Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia and small lymphocytic lymphoma

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** genomic work-up before treatment, first-line targeted therapy (continuous BTK inhibitor or fixed-duration venetoclax combinations), treatment of relapsed and double-refractory disease, cellular therapy and supportive care for adults with CLL or SLL in Australia and New Zealand. This is a companion update to the 2023 Australasian statement. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Work-up before treatment

- **Before first-line treatment, test the blood for IGHV mutation status and run NGS plus FISH or microarray.** (II, A) [S1]
- **Repeat the genomic testing before every later line of therapy**, looking for TP53 changes and for resistance mutations in the BTK and BCL2 pathways, which can steer the choice at relapse. (III, C) [S1]

## First-line treatment

- ⚠️ **Do not use chemoimmunotherapy routinely in first-line CLL, even with mutated IGHV.** (I, A) Its DNA-damage toxicity includes therapy-related myeloid neoplasms (6.3% after FCR); keep it for rare cases where no targeted drug can be obtained. [S1]
- **First-line options are a continuous covalent BTK inhibitor, or a fixed-duration venetoclax combination** (VenO, I + V, AV or AVO). (I, A) [S1]
- **Tailor the first-line choice** to comorbidity (cardiovascular and bleeding risk, kidney function), genomic risk such as TP53 and IGHV status, and whether the patient prefers a fixed course or ongoing therapy. (II, B) [S1]
- **For continuous BTK inhibition, choose acalabrutinib or zanubrutinib ahead of ibrutinib.** Both cause less atrial fibrillation (acalabrutinib also less hypertension), and their PFS in relapsed disease matched or beat ibrutinib. [S1]
- **Continuous BTK inhibitors never stop,** so harms accumulate: bleeding, hypertension, arrhythmia and serious (grade 3 or higher) infection at about 5–7% a year, plus ongoing pressure for resistant clones. [S1]
- **Adding six cycles of obinutuzumab to acalabrutinib improved 6-year PFS (78 vs 62%)** but raised neutropenia and serious infection, with no clear gain in TP53-altered disease; it suits younger patients who can better withstand infections. [S1]
- **VenO is a fixed course: 6 months of obinutuzumab with 12 months of venetoclax.** It gives deep remissions at any age or fitness (76% undetectable MRD at the end), can be repeated later, and stays reasonable in TP53-mutated or unmutated-IGHV disease, although PFS is shorter there. [S1]
- **VenO drawbacks:** intravenous infusions and infusion reactions, the logistics of tumour lysis prevention, and possibly more infection than all-oral fixed-duration combinations. Undetectable MRD at the end of treatment strongly predicts outcome. [S1]
- **Ibrutinib plus venetoclax runs for 15 cycles** (3 of ibrutinib alone, then 12 of both). ⚠️ In the GLOW trial of unfit patients, 4 of 106 (3.8%) died suddenly from cardiac causes, so it is less attractive for older or comorbid patients with cardiac risk factors. [S1]
- **In TP53 wild-type disease, acalabrutinib plus venetoclax (AV) beat chemoimmunotherapy on PFS and OS,** and the panel prefers AV over ibrutinib plus venetoclax because of its better safety. [S1]
- **A triplet (adding obinutuzumab) may be chosen for younger patients with unmutated IGHV,** provided the extra infection risk is clearly explained. [S1]
- **Venetoclax on its own is an acceptable first-line option** but is not preferred when other targeted drugs or combinations can be given, because supporting data are thin. [S1]

## Relapsed CLL

- **At relapse, choose by prior therapy:** separate mere exposure from intolerance and from true refractoriness, and weigh patient and disease factors. (I, A) [S1]
- **Standard second-line choices are venetoclax plus rituximab (VenR) or a covalent BTK inhibitor.** (II, B) After progression on a continuous BTK inhibitor, VenR is preferred (a non-covalent BTK inhibitor is an alternative); after fixed-duration venetoclax, a covalent BTK inhibitor or another venetoclax course are both reasonable. [S1]
- **Switching BTK inhibitor works for intolerance, not refractoriness:** acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib are tolerated after ibrutinib toxicity but do not work in genuinely ibrutinib-refractory disease. [S1]
- **Resistance mutations help pick a BTK inhibitor:** the BTK L528W and A428D variants make disease resistant to both covalent and non-covalent agents. [S1]
- **Re-treating after a fixed-duration course is a viable strategy** (II, B). In 46 relapsed patients, venetoclax re-treatment gave an 80% response rate and a median PFS of 25 months. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Double-refractory CLL** (progression on, or within 6 months of finishing, both a BTK inhibitor and venetoclax) carries a poor prognosis: prioritise a clinical trial, and outside a trial use a non-covalent BTK inhibitor. (I, A) Pirtobrutinib gave a 73% response rate but median PFS of only 19.6 months. [S1]
- **Allogeneic transplant is the only proven cure.** Consider it for young, fit patients once their second targeted regimen has started, especially with adverse genetics (II, B), and for young patients with Richter transformation. [S1]
- **CD19 CAR T-cell therapy** can give very durable remissions in relapsed CLL, but only in those reaching complete response; complete responses are uncommon and cytokine release and neurotoxicity are more frequent than in other B-cell cancers. [S1]

## Supportive care and survivorship

- **Plan supportive care around** immune suppression and infection, frequent cardiovascular disease, second cancers (in Australia notably skin cancer, and myeloid neoplasms after chemoimmunotherapy), osteoporosis and fragility fractures, frailty, and psychosocial harm, ideally within a person-centred survivorship model. [S1]
