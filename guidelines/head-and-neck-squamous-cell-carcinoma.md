# Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma: definitive chemoradiation and first-line recurrent/metastatic therapy

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** definitive cisplatin chemoradiation for locally advanced unresectable HNSCC, and first-line pembrolizumab-based treatment for recurrent or metastatic HNSCC, in adults. Nasopharyngeal carcinoma, salivary gland and thyroid cancers, induction and post-operative chemoradiation, and later lines are out of scope. Nutrition: see `head-and-neck-cancer-nutrition`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Before treatment

- **Plan and review treatment with a multidisciplinary team;** combined-modality treatment is common and the after-effects need joint management. Look for a clinical trial. [S1]
- **Nutrition risk is high:** a dietitian should see every patient first, and a speech pathologist should assess speech and swallowing before treatment starts. [S1]

## Locally advanced, unresectable disease

- **Definitive chemoradiation with three-weekly cisplatin** is for unresectable locally advanced HNSCC with performance status 0 to 2. Avoid it with grade 2 or worse neuropathy or significant hearing loss or tinnitus. [S1]
- **Regimen:** cisplatin 100 mg/m2 IV on day 1 of each 21-day cycle, 3 cycles given during radiotherapy. [S1]
- **Why cisplatin:** in the Intergroup trial, adding high-dose cisplatin to standard radiotherapy improved survival (projected 3-year survival 37% versus 23%), at the cost of more grade 3+ toxicity (89% versus 52%). [S1]
- **Aim for a cumulative cisplatin dose of at least 200 mg/m2;** efficacy seems to depend on total dose delivered. [S1]
- **Weekly versus three-weekly cisplatin is debated.** One prospective study found low-dose weekly treatment inferior; preliminary conCERT data suggest weekly 40 mg/m2 is non-inferior for two-year locoregional control (52.6% v 47.4%). [S1]
- **Other eviQ options** are weekly cisplatin chemoradiation and carboplatin with fluorouracil chemoradiation; the cetuximab chemoradiation protocol is marked superseded. [S1]

## Cisplatin and fluorouracil safety

- **Kidneys and hearing:** use measured GFR for the first cisplatin dose when eGFR is under 60 (especially above 50 mg/m2), give pre-hydration, and arrange audiometry if hearing symptoms appear. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fluorouracil:** DPYD genotyping has been MBS-funded since 1 November 2025. An antidote works well for overdose or overexposure if given within 96 hours. Monitor for cardiac toxicity, particularly with pre-existing heart disease. [S1]

## Recurrent or metastatic disease: first line

- **Who:** first-line treatment of recurrent or metastatic HNSCC arising more than 6 months after any platinum chemotherapy, with ECOG 0 or 1. [S1]
- **Regimen:** pembrolizumab 200 mg, cisplatin 100 mg/m2 and fluorouracil 4,000 mg/m2 (1000 mg/m2 a day by pump over 96 hours) every 21 days for 6 cycles, then pembrolizumab alone (or 400 mg every 42 days) until progression or intolerable toxicity; KEYNOTE-048 capped pembrolizumab at 35 cycles. [S1]
- **If full-dose cisplatin is unsuitable, substitute carboplatin.** For PD-L1 CPS of 1 or more, pembrolizumab on its own is a reasonable alternative. [S1]
- **KEYNOTE-048:** pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy beat cetuximab-based EXTREME for overall survival (median 13.0 v 10.7 months; HR 0.77), with benefit in CPS 1 or more (HR 0.65) and CPS 20 or more (HR 0.60). PFS was not improved overall. [S1]
- **Toxicity was similar to EXTREME** (grade 3+ in 85% v 83%); hypothyroidism was the commonest immune effect (16% with chemotherapy) and pneumonitis caused a death. [S1]

## Immunotherapy safety

- **Pseudo-progression happens in about 5%:** if the patient seems to be benefiting, consider continuing and repeating the scan 4-6 weeks later before calling progression. Radiation recall can occur after a long radiotherapy course. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Immune-related adverse events can start early, worsen fast, appear after stopping, and can kill** (e.g. myocarditis, myositis, encephalitis, pneumonitis). Monitor proactively, teach self-monitoring, and treat promptly with interruption, specialist input and corticosteroids. [S1]
- **Use expert judgement before immunotherapy** in significant autoimmune disease, organ transplant recipients, past viral hepatitis, or previous lung irradiation. [S1]
- **Monitoring:** screen for hepatitis in everyone before a checkpoint inhibitor; check thyroid function (and consider cortisol) every 4-6 weeks on treatment and every 12 weeks afterwards as indicated. [S1]
