# Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma: definitive chemoradiation and first-line recurrent/metastatic therapy

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** definitive cisplatin chemoradiation for locally advanced unresectable HNSCC, and first-line pembrolizumab-based treatment for recurrent or metastatic HNSCC, in adults. Nasopharyngeal carcinoma, salivary gland and thyroid cancers, induction and post-operative chemoradiation, and later lines are out of scope in S1; edition 2.0 adds S2's guidance on induction, perioperative immunotherapy, later lines and follow-up. Nutrition: see `head-and-neck-cancer-nutrition`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 30 claims; **52 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 9 doses.** **S1** eviQ Cancer Treatments Online (retrieved 23 September 2026 (protocol 3836 review due 30 June 2026, now overdue; 291 reviewed as required); AU): 19 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored · **S2** Spanish Society of Medical Oncology (SEOM) and Spanish Head and Neck Cancer Group (TTCC) (Arrazubi (published 17 March 2026; international): 11 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ **Cetuximab with radiotherapy is PBS-listed but eviQ has superseded it.** The PBS still lists cetuximab for SCC of the larynx, oropharynx or hypopharynx (table below), yet eviQ marks its cetuximab chemoradiation protocol superseded in favour of better alternatives. This guideline therefore gives no cetuximab regimen; discuss any use at the head and neck MDT.

> ⚠️ International guideline (S2: SEOM–TTCC, Spain, 2026). Its approval and funding statements describe Spain; Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **S1 is regimen protocols, not a narrative guideline; S2 (SEOM–TTCC 2025) is a graded guideline.** eviQ protocol 3836 was due for review on 30 June 2026 and had not been re-reviewed when retrieved.

## Before treatment

- **Plan and review treatment with a multidisciplinary team;** combined-modality treatment is common and the after-effects need joint management. Look for a clinical trial. [S1]
- **Nutrition risk is high:** a dietitian should see every patient first, and a speech pathologist should assess speech and swallowing before treatment starts. [S1]

## Locally advanced, unresectable disease

- **Definitive chemoradiation with three-weekly cisplatin** is for unresectable locally advanced HNSCC with performance status 0 to 2. Avoid it with grade 2 or worse neuropathy or significant hearing loss or tinnitus. [S1]
- **Regimen:** cisplatin 100 mg/m2 IV on day 1 of each 21-day cycle, 3 cycles given during radiotherapy. [S1]
- **Why cisplatin:** in the Intergroup trial, adding high-dose cisplatin to standard radiotherapy improved survival (projected 3-year survival 37% versus 23%), at the cost of more grade 3+ toxicity (89% versus 52%). [S1]
- **Aim for a cumulative cisplatin dose of at least 200 mg/m2;** efficacy seems to depend on total dose delivered. [S1]
- **Weekly versus three-weekly cisplatin is debated.** One prospective study found low-dose weekly treatment inferior; preliminary conCERT data suggest weekly 40 mg/m2 is non-inferior for two-year locoregional control (52.6% v 47.4%). [S1]
- **Other eviQ options** are weekly cisplatin chemoradiation and carboplatin with fluorouracil chemoradiation; the cetuximab chemoradiation protocol is marked superseded. [S1]
- **Radiotherapy (S2):** IMRT or VMAT, which cut xerostomia without losing control, at 70 Gy given as 35 fractions across 7 weeks, or 65–66 Gy as 30 fractions across 6 weeks [I, A]. [S2]
- **S2 on concurrent cisplatin:** 100 mg/m² on days 1, 22 and 43 for a total above 200 mg/m² [I, A]; weekly schedules have not been shown non-inferior [II, B]. Benefit falls in patients older than 70, so select carefully and support throughout. [S2]
- **Cisplatin-ineligible (S2):** cetuximab bioradiotherapy (400 mg/m² on day −8, then 250 mg/m² weekly with radiotherapy) is an alternative [I, A], although it is inferior to concurrent cisplatin. Adding immunotherapy to chemoradiation has not improved outcomes. [S2]
- **Induction chemotherapy (S2):** TPF (cisplatin 75 mg/m², docetaxel 75 mg/m² and fluorouracil 750 mg/m² a day by 96-hour infusion, given three-weekly) is the regimen of choice [I, A] for fit patients with good organ function; it lowers metastasis without a clear survival gain. Follow it with cisplatin chemoradiation where possible, as cetuximab is not non-inferior. [S2]
- **Consider salvage neck dissection** when the primary has responded completely but PET–CT still shows nodal disease 12 weeks after treatment [I, A]. [S2]
- **Resectable locally advanced disease with PD-L1 CPS 1 or more: perioperative pembrolizumab** (2 cycles before surgery and 15 in total afterwards) improved event-free survival over surgery then (chemo)radiotherapy in a phase III trial; FDA approval came in June 2025 and EMA approval in October 2025 [I, A]. Induction chemotherapy before surgery is not supported [I, A]. [S2]

## Cisplatin and fluorouracil safety

- **Kidneys and hearing:** use measured GFR for the first cisplatin dose when eGFR is under 60 (especially above 50 mg/m2), give pre-hydration, and arrange audiometry if hearing symptoms appear. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fluorouracil:** DPYD genotyping has been MBS-funded since 1 November 2025. An antidote works well for overdose or overexposure if given within 96 hours. Monitor for cardiac toxicity, particularly with pre-existing heart disease. [S1]

## Recurrent or metastatic disease: first line

- **Who:** first-line treatment of recurrent or metastatic HNSCC arising more than 6 months after any platinum chemotherapy, with ECOG 0 or 1. [S1]
- **Regimen:** pembrolizumab 200 mg, cisplatin 100 mg/m2 and fluorouracil 4,000 mg/m2 (1000 mg/m2 a day by pump over 96 hours) every 21 days for 6 cycles, then pembrolizumab alone (or 400 mg every 42 days) until progression or intolerable toxicity; KEYNOTE-048 capped pembrolizumab at 35 cycles. [S1]
- **If full-dose cisplatin is unsuitable, substitute carboplatin.** For PD-L1 CPS of 1 or more, pembrolizumab on its own is a reasonable alternative. [S1]
- **KEYNOTE-048:** pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy beat cetuximab-based EXTREME for overall survival (median 13.0 v 10.7 months; HR 0.77), with benefit in CPS 1 or more (HR 0.65) and CPS 20 or more (HR 0.60). PFS was not improved overall. [S1]
- **Toxicity was similar to EXTREME** (grade 3+ in 85% v 83%); hypothyroidism was the commonest immune effect (16% with chemotherapy) and pneumonitis caused a death. [S1]
- ⚠️ **S2 first-line choice by PD-L1 CPS (platinum-naïve or recurrence more than 6 months after platinum, good performance status):** CPS 20 or more, pembrolizumab alone is preferred when symptoms and disease burden are low, or pembrolizumab with platinum–fluorouracil when a response is needed; CPS 1–19, pembrolizumab with platinum–fluorouracil is preferred, with pembrolizumab alone if chemotherapy is unsuitable; CPS under 1, EXTREME (platinum–fluorouracil with cetuximab), or TPEX (cisplatin–docetaxel–cetuximab) if fluorouracil cannot be used [I, A]. [S2]
- **Performance status 2:** paclitaxel with cetuximab (ERBITAX), a single chemotherapy drug or cetuximab alone [III, C]; evidence for pembrolizumab here is limited, so use it case by case. Performance status 3 or worse: supportive care only. [S2]

## Platinum-refractory disease and later lines (S2, SEOM–TTCC 2025)

- ⚠️ **Progression or recurrence within 6 months of cisplatin: do not rechallenge with a platinum doublet.** For good performance status, nivolumab is preferred whatever the PD-L1 result [I, A]; pembrolizumab alone may be considered when TPS exceeds 50%. If anti-PD-1 therapy is contraindicated or unsuitable (high, fast-moving disease), use ERBITAX or single-agent chemotherapy. [S2]
- **Second line depends on first line:** after pembrolizumab alone, EXTREME or TPEX if platinum-fit (ERBITAX if PS 2 or platinum-unfit); after pembrolizumab with platinum–fluorouracil, ERBITAX; after EXTREME or TPEX, nivolumab [I, A] or a single agent (taxane or capecitabine). Beyond that, single-agent taxanes or antimetabolites by fitness; offer trials. [S2]

## Follow-up (S2, SEOM–TTCC 2025)

- **Follow up for at least 5 years:** 40–60% of locally advanced cancers recur, mostly within 2 years, and second primaries (lung, urinary tract, head and neck) arise at 2–4% a year. Examine with flexible nasopharyngolaryngoscopy, investigate persistent pain or new neck lumps, offer smoking and alcohol cessation support, and do not use serum tumour markers. [S2]

## Immunotherapy safety

- **Pseudo-progression happens in about 5%:** if the patient seems to be benefiting, consider continuing and repeating the scan 4-6 weeks later before calling progression. Radiation recall can occur after a long radiotherapy course. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Immune-related adverse events can start early, worsen fast, appear after stopping, and can kill** (e.g. myocarditis, myositis, encephalitis, pneumonitis). Monitor proactively, teach self-monitoring, and treat promptly with interruption, specialist input and corticosteroids. [S1]
- **Use expert judgement before immunotherapy** in significant autoimmune disease, organ transplant recipients, past viral hepatitis, or previous lung irradiation. [S1]
- **Monitoring:** screen for hepatitis in everyone before a checkpoint inhibitor; check thyroid function (and consider cortisol) every 4-6 weeks on treatment and every 12 weeks afterwards as indicated. [S1]
