# Chronic sialorrhoea (drooling) — botulinum toxin therapy

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** patient selection, swallowing and dental assessment, dosing, ultrasound-guided injection, goals, follow-up, side effects and alternatives for intraglandular botulinum toxin A (incobotulinumtoxinA) in chronic sialorrhoea of neurological cause in adults and children aged 2 years and over. Drooling in specific conditions: see `motor-neurone-disease`, `cerebral-palsy-children`, `parkinsons-disease` and `huntington-disease`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise

- **Chronic sialorrhoea is chronic excessive flow of saliva from the mouth,** usually from impaired swallowing or orofacial function rather than overproduction. Drooling is normal up to age 4 years. [S1]
- **Causes include Parkinson syndromes, dementia, Huntington disease, stroke, MS, motor neurone disease, brain tumour or injury and cerebral palsy,** plus head and neck cancer or surgery; drugs such as pyridostigmine and neuroleptics (especially clozapine, risperidone, haloperidol, aripiprazole) can cause it. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Harms:** skin maceration, and saliva entering the airway with aspiration pneumonia; also stigma and social withdrawal. Ask actively, because patients and carers may not realise treatment exists. [S1]

## Select patients

- **Candidates are adults and children aged 2 years or older (≥12 kg) with troublesome sialorrhoea;** the patient's own experience of severity and quality of life drives the decision. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Assess swallowing before treating:** dysphagia is the commonest cause, and BoNT/A can rarely worsen it. Investigate suspected aspiration, breathing trouble with eating, very long meals or repeated pneumonia (FEES or videofluoroscopy). [S1]
- **Treat caries or gingivitis first and keep up dental review,** since less saliva raises dental risk. In drug-induced sialorrhoea, reduce, stop or replace the drug first. [S1]
- **Anticoagulants:** pause if possible; otherwise observe for about 30 min after injection for floor-of-mouth haematoma. Avoid combining with head and neck irradiation or anticholinergics, and do not use in pregnancy or lactation. [S1]

## Treatment sequence in children and young people with cerebral palsy (NICE)

- **Before any drug, look at what drives the drooling:** positioning, current medicines, reflux and dental problems. [S2]
- ⚠️ **First line in NICE: an anticholinergic,** either glycopyrronium bromide (by mouth or enteral tube) or a transdermal hyoscine hydrobromide patch; trihexyphenidyl is an option for dyskinetic cerebral palsy with specialist input. Choose with the family, allowing for licensed age ranges (in the UK, oral glycopyrronium is unlicensed under 3 years and the hyoscine patch under 10). [S2]
- **Review every saliva-control drug regularly** for benefit, tolerability and side effects. [S2]
- **If anticholinergics are unsuitable, poorly tolerated or ineffective, refer to a specialist service** and consider ultrasound-guided botulinum toxin A injection of the salivary glands. [S2]
- **In the UK, incobotulinumtoxinA (Xeomin) is the only botulinum toxin A licensed for chronic neurological sialorrhoea** (adults, and ages 2 to 17 years at 12 kg or more); other preparations are off-label. NICE technology appraisal TA605 (2019) recommends it for people aged 18 and over. [S2]
- **Consider a surgical opinion** once a safe swallow is confirmed, if lifelong drug treatment may be needed, or if both anticholinergics and botulinum toxin A have failed or were not tolerated. [S2]

## Dose and injection

- ⚠️ **Adults: incobotulinumtoxinA 100 units in 2 mL saline 0.9%:** 30 units (0.6 mL) into each parotid and 20 units (0.4 mL) into each submandibular gland. Re-treat after 16 weeks. [S1]
- **Children 2–17 years: weight-based dosing, recalculated each session, about 2 U/kg in total.** [S1]
- **Injections go transcutaneously into the paired parotid and submandibular glands (four sites);** the sublingual glands are not injected. [S1]
- **Ultrasound guidance is mandatory in children and highly recommended in adults,** mainly because submandibular gland position and size vary. [S1]
- **If side effects occur, lower the total dose;** experts may start adults at 75 units instead of 100 units. [S1]

## Expect and follow up

- **Onset within a week, peak around 1 month, duration about 3 to 4 months.** Careful short-term anticholinergic use (usually off-label) can bridge the gap. [S1]
- **Do not stop or raise the dose after a disappointing first cycle:** effects build with repeated injections, so judge over two or three cycles. Review about 4 months after each injection. [S1]
- **Measure outcome** with the Drooling Severity and Frequency Scale, Teacher's Drooling Scale or Global Impression of Change. [S1]

## Side effects and alternatives

- **Side effects in adults include dry mouth, dysphagia, paraesthesia, speech disturbance, thickened saliva and taste change;** dysphagia occasionally occurs in children. In the adult trial, around 4% on 100 units reported dysphagia. [S1]
- **New or worse swallowing after injection:** consider disease progression and toxin spread (FEES helps); reduce the dose if salivary flow becomes too low for eating. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tell families that high-dose botulinum toxin A to the salivary glands can, rarely, impair swallowing:** return to hospital at once if breathing or swallowing becomes difficult. [S2]
- **Alternatives:** anticholinergics (atropine, scopolamine, glycopyrrolate) carry systemic side-effect risks; surgery and radiotherapy are options with their own risks. BoNT/A's local action suits the elderly with cognitive impairment, people with hallucinations, children with intellectual disability and those with cardiovascular risk. [S1]
