# Long-chain fatty acid oxidation disorders (LC-FAODs): diet, MCT and emergencies

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** long-term dietary management of long-chain FAODs (CPT-I, CPT-II, CACT, VLCAD, LCHAD/MTP): avoiding fasting, cornstarch, long-chain fat restriction, medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) supplements, triheptanoin, exercise, surgery, home and hospital management of decompensation, carnitine and drugs to avoid. Medium- and short-chain disorders (e.g. MCAD deficiency) are mentioned only where they differ. Hypoglycaemia in an unwell child: `hypoglycaemia-children`, `metabolic-disorders-children`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 16 claims quoted verbatim from **Peña-Quintana L, Correcher-Medina P, et al., Nutrients (MDPI) — *Nutritional Management of Patients with Fatty Acid Oxidation Disorders*** (vol. 16, article 2707, published 14 August 2024; origin: international); **32 fragments re-checked by machine; 4 doses.** Licence: *CC BY 4.0*.

> ⚠️ **Narrative review, not a society guideline** (Spanish metabolic clinicians, Nutrients 2024). No current open society guideline for LC-FAODs was found; the 2009 European workshop consensus is closed access. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **Australian funding differs.** The PBS `Long chain fatty acid oxidation disorders` row lists **medium-chain triglyceride** products only. **Triheptanoin**, which this review describes as FDA-approved and beneficial, is not on it.

## Recognise

- **Symptoms appear with fasting or metabolic stress:** cardiomyopathy, skeletal muscle and/or liver involvement, with neurological effects from hypoglycaemia; between crises patients are often well. [S1]

## Avoid fasting

- ⚠️ **Frequent meals:** every 3–4 h under 1 year; every 4–5 h over 1 year, never fasting more than 8 h. Never skip breakfast. [S1]
- **A midnight meal is advisable; uncooked cornstarch can be used from 8 months,** keeping glucose normal for 6–8 h. Start at 1.0–1.5 g/kg and increase gradually to 1.75–2 g/kg by 2 years. [S1]
- **With cardiomyopathy or feeding difficulty, consider continuous overnight tube feeding.** [S1]

## Fat restriction and MCT

- **Restrict long-chain triglycerides to 10% of total energy;** lower restriction risks essential fatty acid deficiency, higher allows toxic metabolites. Give linoleic acid 3–4% and linolenic acid 0.5–1% of calories. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Add MCT at 10–25% of total energy (at least 10%);** as pure MCT, 2–3 g/kg/day in the first year and 1–1.25 g/kg/day after 1 year. MCT bypasses the carnitine system and long-chain enzymes. Total MCT + LCT = 20–35% of total energy. [S1]
- **Triheptanoin** (odd-chain triglyceride) has been used in MCT non-responders and naïve patients, with fewer crises, hypoglycaemia, cardiomyopathy and rhabdomyolysis reported; it is FDA-approved from the neonatal period and can cause diarrhoea. [S1]
- **Breastfeeding is fine in mild to moderate disease,** topped up with MCT formula case by case; in symptomatic infants consider stopping it because of its fat content. [S1]
- **Before exercise** (for muscle pain or rhabdomyolysis), MCT 0.25–0.5 g/kg or carbohydrate 1 g/kg of ideal weight for height 20 min beforehand helps. Monitor essential fatty acids and fat-soluble vitamins. [S1]

## Surgery, sick days and emergencies

- ⚠️ **Any procedure needing fasting (including dental extraction) needs IV 10% glucose before, during and after until feeding is tolerated;** deaths have followed pre-operative fasting in unrecognised FAODs. [S1]
- **Sick day at home:** avoid prolonged fasting, increase carbohydrate quantity and frequency without delay, use the emergency preparation if food is not tolerated, and go to hospital for IV treatment if that fails. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hospital: with vomiting, lethargy or malaise, give 200 mg/kg glucose (2 mL/kg of 10% or 1 mL/kg of 20%) within minutes,** then a maintenance glucose drip for blood glucose 110–120 mg/dL (5–6 mmol/L). Delay can cause sudden death or brain damage. [S1]
- **Keep patients well hydrated in rhabdomyolysis to protect the kidneys;** correct acidosis with pH < 7.20 using IV bicarbonate. Carnitine is generally avoided in acute decompensation. [S1]
- **Give patients an emergency letter** for all doctors, especially emergency physicians. [S1]

## Drugs

- **Carnitine is controversial in LC-FAODs** (toxic long-chain acylcarnitines, linked to ventricular fibrillation and rhabdomyolysis) and may harm in CPT I, CPT II and CACT deficiency; it is essential only in primary carnitine uptake or transport disorders. Consider it cautiously only if levels are low. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Anaesthesia:** avoid volatile agents, lipid-rich agents such as propofol and etomidate, and muscle relaxants; midazolam, fentanyl, thiopental, sevoflurane and nitrous oxide may be used. Also avoid valproic acid, salicylates, pivalic acid, statins and adrenaline. [S1]
