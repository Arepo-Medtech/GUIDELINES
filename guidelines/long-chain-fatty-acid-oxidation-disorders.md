# Long-chain fatty acid oxidation disorders (LC-FAODs): diet, MCT and emergencies

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** long-term dietary management of long-chain FAODs (CPT-I, CPT-II, CACT, VLCAD, LCHAD/MTP): avoiding fasting, cornstarch, long-chain fat restriction, medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) supplements, triheptanoin, exercise, surgery, home and hospital management of decompensation, carnitine and drugs to avoid. Medium- and short-chain disorders (e.g. MCAD deficiency) are mentioned only where they differ. Hypoglycaemia in an unwell child: `hypoglycaemia-children`, `metabolic-disorders-children`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 30 claims; **52 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 8 doses.** **S1** Peña-Quintana L (vol. 16, article 2707, published 14 August 2024; international): 18 claims, quoted · **S2** Genetic Metabolic Dietitians International (GMDI) and Southeast Regional Genetics Network (SERN) (first edition February 2019, v1.4 updated November 2022; international): 12 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ **S1 is a narrative review, not a society guideline** (Spanish metabolic clinicians, Nutrients 2024); claims for CPT-I, CPT-II, CACT and LCHAD/MTP rest on it alone. **S2 is the GMDI/SERN evidence- and consensus-based nutrition guideline for VLCAD deficiency** (US, v1.4, 2022) and applies to VLCAD only. No open guideline covers all LC-FAODs; the 2009 European workshop consensus is closed access. The two sources disagree on cornstarch, fasting limits, how far to restrict long-chain fat and triheptanoin; both are shown. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **Australian funding differs.** The PBS `Long chain fatty acid oxidation disorders` row lists **medium-chain triglyceride** products only. **Triheptanoin**, which this review describes as FDA-approved and beneficial, is not on it.

## Recognise

- **Symptoms appear with fasting or metabolic stress:** cardiomyopathy, skeletal muscle and/or liver involvement, with neurological effects from hypoglycaemia; between crises patients are often well. [S1]

## Avoid fasting

- ⚠️ **Overnight fasting limits in a stable metabolic state (S1 Table 2):** newborns 3 h; under 6 months 4 h; 6–12 months 6–8 h; over 1 year 8–10 h. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Frequent meals:** every 3–4 h under 1 year; every 4–5 h over 1 year, never fasting more than 8 h. Never skip breakfast. [S1]
- **A midnight meal is advisable; uncooked cornstarch can be used from 8 months,** keeping glucose normal for 6–8 h. Start at 1.0–1.5 g/kg and increase gradually to 1.75–2 g/kg by 2 years. [S1]
- **With cardiomyopathy or feeding difficulty, consider continuous overnight tube feeding.** [S1]

## Fat restriction and MCT

- **Foods by fat content (S1 Table 3). Every day (under 1.5 g fat per 100 g):** rice, pasta, white bread and low-fat cereals; skimmed milk, yoghurt and 0% fresh cheese; white fish and most shellfish; skinless chicken or turkey breast, rabbit, low-fat ham; egg white; all fresh vegetables, and fresh fruit except avocado and olives; lentils, beans and peas; MCT oil; herbs and homemade vegetable stock. **Limited amounts (1.5–3 g/100 g):** quinoa, wholemeal bread, brown rice, semi-skimmed milk, oily or semi-fatty fish such as canned tuna and sea bass, lean pork or veal, serrano ham, chickpeas. **Not recommended, or on prescription only (over 3 g/100 g):** oatmeal, pastries and nut cereals; whole milk, cream, cheese and ice cream; salmon, sardines, trout and fried fish; fatty meats, sausages, pâté and offal; whole egg; avocado, olives, nuts and dried fruit; soya beans and tofu drinks; ordinary cooking oils, butter, lard, margarine and mayonnaise; chocolate and desserts made with whole milk, egg or cream. [S1]
- **Restrict long-chain triglycerides to 10% of total energy;** lower restriction risks essential fatty acid deficiency, higher allows toxic metabolites. Give linoleic acid 3–4% and linolenic acid 0.5–1% of calories. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Add MCT at 10–25% of total energy (at least 10%);** as pure MCT, 2–3 g/kg/day in the first year and 1–1.25 g/kg/day after 1 year. MCT bypasses the carnitine system and long-chain enzymes. Total MCT + LCT = 20–35% of total energy. [S1]
- **Triheptanoin** (odd-chain triglyceride) has been used in MCT non-responders and naïve patients, with fewer crises, hypoglycaemia, cardiomyopathy and rhabdomyolysis reported; it is FDA-approved from the neonatal period and can cause diarrhoea. [S1]
- **Breastfeeding is fine in mild to moderate disease,** topped up with MCT formula case by case; in symptomatic infants consider stopping it because of its fat content. [S1]
- **Before exercise** (for muscle pain or rhabdomyolysis), MCT 0.25–0.5 g/kg or carbohydrate 1 g/kg of ideal weight for height 20 min beforehand helps. Monitor essential fatty acids and fat-soluble vitamins. [S1]

## VLCAD deficiency: GMDI/SERN guideline (S2)

- **Newborn with suspected VLCAD:** begin fasting precautions while the diagnosis is confirmed. A well infant may keep breastfeeding (or take expressed milk) with no MCT, provided the feeding intervals are kept. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Set long-chain fat by severity, keeping total fat (long-chain plus MCT) within the dietary reference intake for age:** mild and symptom-free, no MCT substitution is needed; moderate, long-chain fat at 15 to 30% of estimated energy; severe, 10 to 15%. Going under 10% may add nothing even in severe disease. Then add MCT to make up the total fat. [S2]
- **Breastfeeding by severity:** mild and symptom-free, breast milk without MCT if supply and weight gain are adequate; moderate, breast milk with a possible low long-chain, high-MCT medical food; severe, that medical food as the main nutrition; symptomatic, some breast milk may still be possible. Breastfeeding stopped during a crisis can be partly resumed once the infant is well. [S2]
- **Fasting limits when well:** feeds every 3 to 4 hours for symptom-free newborns; an overnight fast of 8 to 10 hours at 9 to under 12 months; a maximum of 10 to 12 hours after 12 months. Use the shorter end in severe disease. [S2]
- **Uncooked cornstarch at bedtime is not indicated in VLCAD,** because hypoglycaemia is unlikely in a well person who avoids long fasts and meets energy needs; a complex-carbohydrate bedtime snack is preferred, with overnight tube feeds for severe disease if a snack is not enough. [S2]
- **Essential fatty acids:** infants on long-chain restriction with MCT should get a medical food with ARA and DHA. Adjust linoleic acid within 3 to 6% and alpha-linolenic acid within 0.5 to 1.2% of energy against plasma or red-cell profiles; if DHA stays low, supplement 60 mg/day (infants and toddlers) or 100 mg/day (older). [S2]
- **During illness:** with acute rhabdomyolysis, lean on carbohydrate (oral or IV) for energy. Prevent crises by spacing meals, keeping people active with extra energy around exercise, and considering a gastrostomy in severe disease with poor intake. Home glucose meters are discouraged, because low glucose is unlikely to be the presenting sign; give families an emergency letter. [S2]
- ⚠️ **In hospital, if intake is inadequate: at least 10% IV dextrose with electrolytes at 1.5 times maintenance or more.** Do not give L-carnitine in acute illness; avoid IV lipids, but supply essential fatty acids after 7 days. Consider a central line in severe disease with frequent admissions. [S2]
- **L-carnitine when well is probably unnecessary unless free carnitine is below 10 µmol/L;** then start at 10 to 25 mg/kg/day and titrate to levels. [S2]
- **Other treatments (S2):** at the guideline's writing triheptanoin was available only in trials or as an investigational drug, so no general recommendation was made; bezafibrate is experimental and belongs in trials; evidence for CoQ10 and other vitamins is insufficient. [S2]
- **Monitor creatine kinase and the plasma carnitine profile routinely;** add metabolic panel, acylcarnitines, essential fatty acids, fat-soluble vitamins and cardiac checks when circumstances call for it. Confirmatory results (acylcarnitines, genetics, enzyme) help judge severity. [S2]
- **Exercise:** encourage normal activity with energy support. For poor exercise tolerance, give MCT 20 minutes before: 0.1 to 0.3 g/kg of body weight, or 0.25 to 0.5 g/kg of lean mass. Counsel on fluids. [S2]

## Surgery, sick days and emergencies

- ⚠️ **Any procedure needing fasting (including dental extraction) needs IV 10% glucose before, during and after until feeding is tolerated;** deaths have followed pre-operative fasting in unrecognised FAODs. [S1]
- **Sick day at home:** avoid prolonged fasting, increase carbohydrate quantity and frequency without delay, use the emergency preparation if food is not tolerated, and go to hospital for IV treatment if that fails. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hospital: with vomiting, lethargy or malaise, give 200 mg/kg glucose (2 mL/kg of 10% or 1 mL/kg of 20%) within minutes,** then a maintenance glucose drip for blood glucose 110–120 mg/dL (5–6 mmol/L). Delay can cause sudden death or brain damage. [S1]
- **Keep patients well hydrated in rhabdomyolysis to protect the kidneys;** correct acidosis with pH < 7.20 using IV bicarbonate. Carnitine is generally avoided in acute decompensation. [S1]
- **Give patients an emergency letter** for all doctors, especially emergency physicians. [S1]

## Drugs

- **Carnitine is controversial in LC-FAODs** (toxic long-chain acylcarnitines, linked to ventricular fibrillation and rhabdomyolysis) and may harm in CPT I, CPT II and CACT deficiency; it is essential only in primary carnitine uptake or transport disorders. Consider it cautiously only if levels are low. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Anaesthesia:** avoid volatile agents, lipid-rich agents such as propofol and etomidate, and muscle relaxants; midazolam, fentanyl, thiopental, sevoflurane and nitrous oxide may be used. Also avoid valproic acid, salicylates, pivalic acid, statins and adrenaline. [S1]
