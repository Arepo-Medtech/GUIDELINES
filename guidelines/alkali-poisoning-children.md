# Alkali poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation

> ⚠️ **No charcoal.** **Normal mouth and pharynx do not exclude oesophageal burns.** Any symptom → surgical registrar and oesophagoscopy.

---

## Agents and risk

- **Alkalis include** drain and oven cleaners, automatic dishwashing liquids and powders, laundry detergents, ammonia and Portland cement. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Significant GI ulceration is likely once the pH is above 11.5.** [S1]
- **Get the container** to check contents and strength. Corrosive potential varies with concentration and preparation: **liquids are more likely than powders to burn the oesophagus.** [S1]
- **Check the product with the Poisons Information Centre** to establish whether it is weak, strong, irritant or corrosive. [S1]

## Assessment

- ⚠️ **Exposure can severely burn the GI tract, especially the oesophagus.** **No mouth or pharyngeal ulcers does not rule out gastro-oesophageal lesions.** [S1]
- **Symptoms may be minimal:** pain · nausea and vomiting · drooling, or refusal of food and drink · ⚠️ stridor or respiratory distress. [S1]

## Management and disposition

- ⚠️ **Activated charcoal is contraindicated.** [S1]
- **Asymptomatic: fluid dilution with water 10 mL/kg, max 250 mL.** [S1]
- **Discharge** once 4 hours have passed with no symptoms and the child can eat and drink. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Any symptoms: contact the surgical registrar and admit for oesophagoscopy.** [S1]
