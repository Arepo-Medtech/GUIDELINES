# Hereditary tyrosinaemia type 1 (HT-1): nitisinone and diet

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** confirming HT-1 after a positive newborn screen or a clinical presentation, starting and adjusting nitisinone (NTBC), the phenylalanine- and tyrosine-restricted diet with amino-acid medical foods (the PBS 'Tyrosinaemia' formula listings), acute liver failure and neurological crises, and lifelong surveillance for liver cancer, kidney, eye and neurodevelopmental problems. First steps for any suspected inborn error: `metabolic-disorders-children`. Other protein-restricted diets: `phenylketonuria`.

## Recognise and screen

- **Untreated HT-1 usually shows up before age 2, most often before 6 months,** as acute liver failure with kidney dysfunction. Painful neurological crises with high blood pressure and low sodium can occur at any age, and all affected children carry a high liver-cancer risk. [S1]
- **Screen newborns with blood succinylacetone (SA), not tyrosine.** Tyrosine is neither sensitive nor specific, and screening SA only after a raised tyrosine would miss an estimated 28% of cases. (Grade B and D) [S1]

## Confirm the diagnosis

- **After a positive screen, confirm promptly with a fresh blood or urine SA,** plus liver tests (PT, aPTT, INR, transaminases), AFP, plasma amino acids, electrolytes and glucose. (Grade D) [S1]
- **A raised SA is diagnostic; a raised AFP is not.** Normal plasma SA must be documented before a child is called negative. [S1]
- **Send FAH gene testing to confirm, but do not wait for it before treating.** Test siblings for SA straight away, and offer genetic counselling about the 25% recurrence risk. [S1]

## Nitisinone (NTBC)

- ⚠️ **Start nitisinone and the diet as soon as HT-1 is diagnosed.** (Grade B) [S1]
- **Starting dose 1.0 mg/kg/day, split into two doses in the first year of life;** after that, once-daily dosing can be considered because the half-life is about 54 hours. (Grade D) [S1]
- **Use the lowest dose that gives a blood level of 40–60 μmol/L and/or keeps SA normal.** Dosing by blood level is more reliable than by weight and may allow lower doses in older children. (Grade D) [S1]
- **If SA rises, check adherence first, then increase the dose.** A sudden return of SA signals missed nitisinone, not cancer. [S1]
- **Nitisinone has no known drug interactions,** so normal medicines and routine vaccination can be given. Check blood counts periodically (transient low platelets and white cells were seen in trials). [S1]

## Diet

- **Restrict both phenylalanine and tyrosine,** using a reduced intact-protein allowance plus amino-acid medical foods free of both, and low-protein foods, managed by a metabolic team with specialist dietitians. [S1]
- **Targets:** plasma tyrosine 200–600 μmol/L (Grade C); phenylalanine in the laboratory's normal range, adding intact protein or a phenylalanine supplement if it falls below 20 μmol/L. [S1]
- **Infants at diagnosis:** a phenylalanine- and tyrosine-free formula with no intact protein for up to 48 hours, with energy above 120 kcal/kg/day and 3.5 g/kg of protein from medical foods to prevent catabolism. [S1]
- **Watch vitamin and mineral intake:** the low-protein diet can cause deficiencies, so supply age-appropriate requirements. (Grade C) [S1]

## Acute presentations

- ⚠️ **Acute liver failure (clinically presenting child):** involve hepatology or intensive care; give IV 10% dextrose/normal saline at 1.5–2.0 times maintenance, consider fresh frozen plasma and vitamin K, and start nitisinone 1 mg/kg/day at once. [S1]
- **Expect urine SA to normalise within 24 hours and clinical improvement within 1 week.** Without improvement, or in severe liver failure, raise the dose to 2 mg/kg. Start transplant planning if there is no response by about 1 week (after 2–3 days with persistent severe coagulopathy or encephalopathy). [S1]
- **Neurological crisis** (pain, hypertonia, weakness, hypertension, low sodium): IV 10% dextrose/saline at 1.5 to 2 times maintenance with antihypertensives and analgesia, 2% saline boluses for recurrent hyponatraemia, and start or restart nitisinone. Crises have followed stopping nitisinone for more than 1 month. [S1]

## Lifelong monitoring

- ⚠️ **Liver cancer surveillance for life:** periodic AFP and liver imaging, many centres every 3–6 months, and imaging at once if AFP stops falling or rises. A normal AFP does not exclude HCC. Refer on for progressive disease, rising AFP or a focal lesion. (Grade C and D) [S1]
- **Early treatment sharply cuts HCC risk:** under 1% when started before age 1, 7% at 1–2 years, 21% at 2–7 years and 35% after 7 years. [S1]
- **Liver transplant is now reserved for** malignancy, decompensated liver disease that does not respond to nitisinone, or where nitisinone is unavailable. After transplant, screen the kidneys periodically. [S1]
- **Kidneys and bone:** at diagnosis, assess tubular function, kidney ultrasound and bone X-ray for rickets. The tubulopathy usually reverses within weeks on treatment; later kidney damage tracks how long SA stayed raised. [S1]
- **Eyes:** raised tyrosine can cause corneal crystals (photophobia, redness, pain); many centres keep tyrosine below 600 μmol/L. Examine with a slit lamp when symptomatic or at higher risk, and rule out herpes simplex keratitis. [S1]
- **Include neuropsychological assessment in routine follow-up;** low IQ, attention and executive-function problems have been reported on nitisinone. [S1]
- **Watch adherence:** brief lapses feel harmless but can cause corneal crystals, paralytic crises and higher cancer risk. [S1]
- **Pregnancy:** there was no consensus on nitisinone in pregnancy. Follow up babies exposed in pregnancy, and do not breastfeed on nitisinone. (Grade D) [S1]
