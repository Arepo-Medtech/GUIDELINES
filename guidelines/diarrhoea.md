## ⚠️ The priority is fluid, not the stool

> ⚠️ **ASSESSMENT AND CORRECTION OF DEHYDRATION AND ELECTROLYTE DISTURBANCE IS THE PRIORITY IN ALL
> CASES** [D1].
>
> **MOST ACUTE DIARRHOEA IS SELF-LIMITING AND DOES NOT REQUIRE DRUG TREATMENT.** **If necessary, rehydrate
> OVER 3–4 HOURS with oral rehydration solution and supplement for ongoing losses.** ⚠️ **VERY FEW
> PATIENTS WILL REQUIRE IV REHYDRATION** [D1].

**Also, before treating: EXCLUDE DIARRHOEA SECONDARY TO FAECAL IMPACTION** [D1]. **Overflow is not
diarrhoea and an antidiarrhoeal will make it worse.**

**ORAL REHYDRATION SALTS ARE FIRST LINE** [D1].

| Oral rehydration salts [D3] | |
|---|---|
| **Dose** | **follow the label for reconstitution; dose by severity of diarrhoea and the patient's weight or age** |
| **Tolerance** | **well tolerated; safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding** |
| ⚠️ **Children** | **CAN DEHYDRATE RAPIDLY — careful monitoring is essential.** ⚠️ **CONTINUED LARGE FLUID LOSS OR INABILITY TO TOLERATE FLUID REPLACEMENT REQUIRES URGENT MEDICAL ATTENTION** |
| **Practical** | **may be better tolerated FROZEN AS AN ICE-BLOCK** |

⚠️ **The only PBS-listed oral rehydration product AMH lists is a 4.9 g sachet, restricted to Aboriginal or
Torres Strait Islander patients or to rehydration in intestinal failure.** **Similar products are available
over the counter** [D3] — **the patient pays unless they fit one of those two restrictions.**

**After rehydration: normal food intake may start.** **BREASTFEEDING SHOULD BE CONTINUED — it may decrease
stool output and shorten the duration of diarrhoea — and DILUTION OF FORMULA FEEDS IS UNNECESSARY AND MAY
PROLONG SYMPTOMS** [D1].

## ⚠️ Children: not the same rules

> ⚠️ **ANTIDIARRHOEALS SHOULD NOT BE USED FOR ACUTE DIARRHOEA IN CHILDREN.** **They do not reduce fluid and
> electrolyte loss, MAY DELAY EXPULSION OF ORGANISMS, and may cause adverse effects** [D1].
>
> ⚠️ **LOPERAMIDE IS CONTRAINDICATED IN CHILDREN UNDER 12 YEARS** — though sometimes used under specialist
> supervision in younger children for conditions such as short bowel syndrome. **Diphenoxylate is not
> recommended under 12 years.** **ORAL REHYDRATION IS THE PREFERRED TREATMENT** [D2].

**Oral rotavirus vaccine prevents rotavirus gastroenteritis in infants and young children and is part of
the National Immunisation Program** [D1].

## Drug choice in adults [D1]

| | [D1] |
|---|---|
| **Oral rehydration salts** | **first line** |
| **Antidiarrhoeals** | **a SHORT COURSE may sometimes be warranted for symptom control of acute diarrhoea in ADULTS** |
| **Opioids** | **the most effective non-specific antidiarrhoeal agents** — ⚠️ **LOPERAMIDE IS PREFERRED** |
| **Bulk-forming laxatives** (psyllium, ispaghula) | **occasionally used, for their fluid absorption properties** |
| ⚠️ **Adsorbents** | **NOT RECOMMENDED — not effective, and may bind to drugs and reduce their absorption** |

### Special cases [D1]

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Diverticular disease** | **bulk-forming laxatives**; if diarrhoea persists, **loperamide as SHORT-TERM adjunctive treatment** |
| **Bile acid-related diarrhoea** | ⚠️ **COLESTYRAMINE IS THE TREATMENT OF CHOICE** |
| **Intractable** (intestinal tumours, short bowel syndrome) | **OCTREOTIDE**, to reduce intestinal motility and water secretion when refractory to antidiarrhoeals |
| **Infectious** | **may require an anti-infective** — AMH cross-refers to *Traveller's diarrhoea*, *Clostridioides difficile-associated disease* and *Giardiasis*, ⚠️ **none of which was retrieved** |

### Chronic diarrhoea [D1]

**Usually signifies underlying disease, most commonly IRRITABLE BOWEL SYNDROME OR FUNCTIONAL DIARRHOEA.**
**Investigate to establish the cause. Attend to diet and lifestyle.** ⚠️ **Drug treatment should be given
ONLY UNDER SPECIALIST SUPERVISION** [D1].

## Loperamide doses [D2]

⚠️ **Licensed and unquoted.**

| Indication | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Acute diarrhoea, adult and child >12 years** | **initially 4 mg, then 2 mg after each loose bowel action** |
| **Chronic diarrhoea or intestinal stoma** | **adjust to response; usual maintenance 4–8 mg daily in 1–3 doses** |
| **Usual maximum** | **16 mg daily** |
| **Chemo-/radiotherapy-induced (mild-to-moderate)** | **initially 4 mg, then 2 mg every 4 hours or after each loose motion (4 mg every 4 hours overnight); treat until diarrhoea-free for 12 hours.** **If it continues >24 hours, increase to 2 mg every 2 hours, max 16 mg daily.** ⚠️ **If it worsens or persists >36 hours, MANAGE IN HOSPITAL** |
| **Irinotecan- or sacituzumab govitecan-induced** | **4 mg at onset, then 2 mg every 2 hours.** ⚠️ **If severe or persisting >24 hours, MANAGE IN HOSPITAL** |

> ⚠️ **LOPERAMIDE MAY BE MISUSED.** **Serious cardiac adverse events — QT interval prolongation, torsades
> de pointes, ventricular arrhythmias, cardiac arrest — AND DEATH have been reported with high doses, for
> example 40 mg to 300 mg daily** [D2].
> **It is an over-the-counter drug with a cardiac ceiling.** ⚠️ **This compendium's `arrhythmias` guideline
> carries AMH's list of QT-prolonging drugs; loperamide is not on it, because at licensed doses it is not
> the problem — the dose is.**

### Contraindications and cautions [D2]

⚠️ **CONTRAINDICATED in intestinal obstruction.** ⚠️ **MAY PRECIPITATE TOXIC MEGACOLON IN SEVERE ULCERATIVE
COLITIS** — this compendium's `inflammatory-bowel-disease` guideline carries the same warning from two
further sources. **AVOID in severe hepatic impairment — may precipitate HEPATIC ENCEPHALOPATHY** [D2].

**Common adverse effects: abdominal pain and bloating, nausea, vomiting, constipation. Rare: paralytic
ileus, dizziness, rash** [D2].

**One or two doses are safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding; for chronic use in pregnancy, contact a
pregnancy medicines information service** [D2]. **Imodium Zapid contains ASPARTAME** [D2].

> **In adults with acute diarrhoea, use opioid antidiarrhoeals for SYMPTOMATIC TREATMENT ONLY.** **Some
> people with chronic diarrhoea may need them long term — and THAT SHOULD BE A SPECIALIST DECISION** [D2].

## Probiotics — what AMH actually says

> **LACTOBACILLUS GG has been shown to reduce the duration of diarrhoea in ROTAVIRUS-RELATED ENTERITIS.**
> ⚠️ **COST EFFECTIVENESS HAS NOT BEEN PROVEN** [D1].

⚠️ **That is a narrow claim, and it is the most positive thing AMH says about probiotics anywhere the
compendium has retrieved.** The `inflammatory-bowel-disease` guideline carries the other two:
**probiotics are NOT USEFUL in maintaining remission in Crohn's disease**, and **limited data suggest they
are NOT MORE EFFECTIVE THAN PLACEBO for inducing or maintaining remission in mild-to-moderate ulcerative
colitis**. **One organism, one syndrome, one outcome — not a general endorsement.**

## ⚠️ Diarrhoea after travel [S1]

**Between 43% and 79% of travellers become unwell while travelling or after returning home, and
TRAVELLERS' DIARRHOEA is among the most common presentations** [S1].

> ⚠️ **ASK ABOUT RECENT TRAVEL — THE PATIENT MAY NOT VOLUNTEER IT** [S1].

> ⚠️ **THE WARNING AMH DOES NOT CARRY:**
> **"Malaria symptoms can be many and include diarrhoea, for example, which could potentially be diagnosed
> as travellers' diarrhoea if malaria is not considered."** [S1]
> **Anyone returning from a malarious area with a fever should be considered to have malaria until proven
> otherwise** [S1]. **FEVER IN A RETURNED TRAVELLER IS MALARIA UNTIL PROVEN OTHERWISE** [S1].

| Incubation group [S1] | |
|---|---|
| **Short** | **travellers' diarrhoea / dysentery — bacterial or viral** |
| **Intermediate, 2–6 weeks** | ⚠️ **chronic diarrhoea — OFTEN PARASITIC** |

**Ask whether any travel companions were unwell and whether others had the same illness — this is important
with illnesses such as travellers' diarrhoea** [S1].

### Investigation [S1]

> **If acute and mild, investigations might not be necessary. If ongoing acute or chronic, or the patient
> is unwell, consider investigations.** ⚠️ **"Cultures might now be more important given the increasing
> amount of antibiotic resistance in returning travellers"** [S1].

**Consider: stool microscopy, culture and sensitivity; stool ova, cysts and parasites; and stool PCR** [S1].

⚠️ **AMH's own advice is that stool testing is not routine in self-limiting acute diarrhoea** — it says most
cases need no drug treatment at all [D1] — **while S1 raises the threshold for testing back up in returned
travellers specifically. The two are not in conflict; they address different populations.**

### Additional therapies in eTG

- Adult antiemetics for acute gastroenteritis: metoclopramide 10 mg orally 8-hourly as needed, ondansetron 4-8 mg orally 8- to 12-hourly as needed, or prochlorperazine 20 mg then 10 mg 2 hours later, then 5-10 mg 8-hourly as needed. [T1]
- Children 6 months or older with gastroenteritis vomiting: ondansetron 0.15 mg/kg (max 8 mg) orally, repeat after 8-12 hours if needed. [T1]
- Zinc 20 mg orally daily for 10-14 days only for malnourished children 6 months or older with gastroenteritis; probiotics may help children but insufficient evidence in adults. [T1]
- Haemodynamically compromised adults: 10 to 20 mL/kg lactated Ringer or sodium chloride 0.9% rapid IV, repeated as needed; SC sodium chloride 0.9% up to 60 mL/hour per site if IV not possible. [T2]
- Bile salt diarrhoea (post-cholecystectomy or ileal resection): colestyramine 4 to 8 g orally up to twice daily; avoid codeine preparations for chronic diarrhoea. [T4]
- During gastroenteritis consider temporarily withholding NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics, metformin, lithium, digoxin; warn that absorption of drugs such as combined oral contraceptives may fall. [T1]
