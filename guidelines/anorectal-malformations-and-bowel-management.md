# Anorectal malformations: bowel management after repair

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** assessment of continence potential and the stepwise bowel management programme (stimulant laxatives, rectal enemas, transanal irrigation, antegrade continence enemas, sigmoid resection, redo surgery) for children with an anorectal malformation (ARM) after primary repair. Neonatal diagnosis and colostomy are only outlined. Hirschsprung disease and its enterocolitis: see `hirschsprung-enterocolitis`. Functional constipation in children: see `constipation`. PBS access (bisacodyl; sorbitol-citrate micro-enemas) is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **OPEN AND QUOTED.** 16 claims quoted verbatim from **Bokova E, Svetanoff WJ, Lopez JJ, Levitt MA, Rentea RM (Comprehensive Colorectal Center, Children's Mercy Hospital, Kansas City), Children (MDPI) 10(5):846 — *State of the Art Bowel Management for Pediatric Colorectal Problems: Anorectal Malformations*** (published 8 May 2023; origin: international); **30 fragments re-checked by machine; 2 doses.** Licence: *CC BY 4.0*.

> ⚠️ International source (narrative state-of-the-art review from one US colorectal centre, 2023; not a society guideline). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **Avoid stool softeners in ARM.** Children with a repaired ARM lack normal anal sensation and detect formed stool better than soft stool: stimulant laxatives plus soluble fibre, or enemas, are the basis of treatment. **Bisacodyl** appears in the source only as a stimulant added to enema fluid; small-volume micro-enemas are not discussed.

## Why bowel management is needed

- **ARM occurs in 1 in every 4000-5000 newborns;** more than 75% have an associated anomaly, most often the VACTERL association. [S1]
- **After primary PSARP repair, up to 79% have constipation and up to 48% soil;** the target is 1-3 bowel movements a day with 1 or fewer accidents a week. [S1]
- **Most repaired ARMs lack a normally formed anal canal and anal sensation,** so soiling after repair is common; preserving the whole colon gives firmer stools that are easier to feel. [S1]

## Assess

- **Work up referred constipation or soiling** with the surgical history, a plain abdominal film for stool load, and examination under anaesthesia (sphincter, neoanus position, stricture, prolapse). [S1]
- **Image the spine and pelvis** (MRI; spinal ultrasound under 6 months) and do a contrast enema to define colonic anatomy. [S1]
- **Predict continence potential** from the ARM type, the sacrum and the spine (the ARM continence index). A sacral ratio under 0.4 means poor potential; 0.7 or more, a higher likelihood of continence. [S1]
- **Look for a megarectosigmoid,** which causes long-standing constipation, impactions and overflow incontinence. [S1]

## Bowel management programme

- **The goal: empty the colon every day** (confirmed on X-ray) with no stool between treatments, so the child can wear regular underwear. About 60% of ARM patients use rectal or antegrade enemas and about 35% laxatives. [S1]
- ⚠️ **With constipation and good continence potential, use stimulant laxatives (e.g. senna), titrated** at each visit by symptoms and X-ray, combined with water-soluble fibre. **Avoid stool softeners.** [S1]
- **Senna can cause perineal blistering** at high or erratic doses (60 mg/day); stepwise dosing kept it to 2.2%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Rectal enemas: give every 24 hours at the same time,** starting with 400 mL saline; tap water only from 3 years (younger children risk dehydration and hyponatraemia). Castile soap, glycerin or bisacodyl can be added; reduce the stimulant if soiling occurs between enemas. [S1]
- **If the child cannot hold a rectal enema, use transanal irrigation** (cone or balloon tip) **or antegrade flushes.** With regular irrigation, 84% of adherent ARM patients were clean at one year. [S1]
- **Consider an antegrade continence enema (ACE)** for children who will need enemas long term or cannot tolerate the rectal route; it can be made at a redo PSARP. [S1]
- **Sigmoid resection is rarely needed:** consistent laxatives or enemas almost always improve colonic function, and megarectosigmoid after PSARP can usually be managed conservatively. [S1]

## Redo surgery and outcome

- **Correct anatomical problems first:** a redo PSARP for mislocated anoplasty, stricture, fistula remnant or prolapse can be the key to continence; a skin-level stricture can be treated with a Heineke-Mikulicz-like anoplasty instead of dilations. [S1]
- **A dedicated bowel management programme works:** 70% of 222 children with faecal incontinence were clean at one year, with better quality-of-life scores. [S1]
