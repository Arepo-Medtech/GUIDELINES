# The limping or non-weight-bearing child

> ⚠️ **Acute inability to walk or bear weight is a red flag. Any child who limps, with swelling and reduced range of motion — above all with fever — may have septic arthritis: call orthopaedics ASAP.**

---

## Key points

- **Most limping children need no investigation**; watching the gait helps localise the problem. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flag: a child who suddenly cannot walk or bear weight.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Septic arthritis is an orthopaedic emergency** — think of it whenever a limping child has swelling and a reduced range of motion, particularly if febrile. [S1]
- **Common causes are minor trauma, transient synovitis and acute myositis**, but serious pathology must always be considered. **Pain can be referred** — examine the joints above and below (including the spine) and the abdomen. [S1]

## Causes at any age

- **At all ages consider:** infection (septic arthritis, osteomyelitis, discitis, bursitis, sepsis, epidural collection) · trauma including inflicted injury · malignancy (haematological, bone, soft tissue). [S1]
- **Also:** rheumatological causes including reactive arthritis, HSP and acute rheumatic fever · **appendicitis and ovarian or testicular torsion** · sickle-cell crisis or haemophilia · **vitamin C deficiency** with a restricted diet · functional limp. [S1]

## History and examination

- **History red flags to ask about:** symptoms over 7 days or repeated presentations · severe localised joint pain · pain that wakes the child · inability to walk or weight bear · fever, night sweats, rigors · weight loss, lethargy, anorexia (consider malignancy). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Absence of fever does not exclude infection.** Pallor, petechiae, purpura or ecchymosis should raise HSP, malignancy or inflicted injury. [S1]
- **Hip pain may localise to the knee.** Examine with Look, Feel, Move, including the joints above and below. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Markedly reduced range of motion points to significant pathology such as septic arthritis** — compare active and passive movement on both sides. [S1]
- **Also examine** neurology and neurovascular status of the limb, the heart where ARF is possible, and the abdomen, scrotum, back and hips. [S1]

## Investigations

- **No investigations are needed if all apply:** no red flags · walking with little or no discomfort on simple analgesia · there is a working diagnosis and/or a plan to review within 7 days of onset should the limp persist. [S1]
- **X-ray the area of suspicion.** A pelvis AP or frog-leg view helps identify SUFE, DDH (over 6 months), Perthes disease and pelvic avulsions. ⚠️ **Septic arthritis and early osteomyelitis are not ruled out by a normal X-ray**, and signs of inflicted injury may be subtle. [S1]
- **Hip ultrasound** looks for an effusion when septic arthritis is suspected, but **an effusion often cannot distinguish septic arthritis from transient synovitis.** [S1]
- **Bone scan or localised MRI** if the limp is ongoing at 7 days or there are features of osteomyelitis. [S1]
- **Raised inflammatory markers** (CRP, ESR, WCC, platelets) point to infection or inflammation, but **can be normal when infection is chronic or contained.** [S1]
- **Blood cultures before antibiotics if possible** when osteomyelitis, septic arthritis or septic bursitis is suspected. FBE and film if malignancy is a concern; vitamin C level with a restricted diet; if ARF is suspected, an ECG and streptococcal serology. [S1]

## Treatment

- **Simple analgesia (paracetamol and an NSAID) and child-directed limitation of activity.** ⚠️ **Needing stronger analgesia should prompt reassessment.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Septic arthritis: consult the local orthopaedic specialist ASAP**; refer to the right sub-specialty and follow local antibiotic guidelines. [S1]

## Escalate and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team** if symptoms last more than 7 days or the child cannot bear weight or permit movement after analgesia · systemically unwell · features of malignancy · concern for abuse · suspected inflammatory arthritis. **Suspected septic arthritis: consult orthopaedics.** [S1]
- **Discharge when** the cause has been found and managed, **or** the child walks comfortably (analgesia or not) **and** a high-risk diagnosis is unlikely **and** follow-up is arranged. [S1]
