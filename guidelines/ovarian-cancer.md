# Epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube and primary peritoneal cancer — olaparib maintenance

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** olaparib, a PARP inhibitor, as maintenance for adults with BRCA-mutated high-grade serous epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer: after first-line platinum chemotherapy, and after platinum-sensitive relapse. It covers eligibility, dosing, monitoring, toxicity and the SOLO1 and SOLO2 evidence. It is authored from **one eviQ protocol page (ID 3737)**, which also lists breast and prostate indications that are not covered here. **Not covered:** first-line carboplatin and paclitaxel, niraparib (eviQ ID 4166), olaparib with bevacizumab for HRD-positive disease (ID 4158), and chemotherapy for recurrent disease. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ⚠️ **This guideline covers one drug, not the whole disease.** It is written from the eviQ olaparib protocol and is limited to **BRCA-mutated** disease. Niraparib, the other PBS-listed PARP inhibitor for these conditions, is not covered.

---

## Who it is for

- **First-line maintenance:** olaparib is for BRCA-mutated (germline or somatic), FIGO stage III or IV, high-grade serous cancer of the ovary, fallopian tube or peritoneum that has responded, completely or partially, to first-line platinum chemotherapy. [S1]
- **First-line duration:** stop at 2 years if there is a complete response (no disease on imaging). If disease is still visible but stable at 2 years, the clinician may continue when further benefit is expected. [S1]
- **Maintenance after relapse:** for BRCA-mutated high-grade serous disease that relapsed but stayed platinum-sensitive and is responding after at least two platinum-containing regimens. No data support giving olaparib maintenance again after a later relapse. [S1]
- **Wait for blood counts to recover** from earlier chemotherapy before starting olaparib. [S1]

## Dose

- **Olaparib tablets 300 mg (2 × 150 mg) by mouth twice daily**, continuously until progression or unacceptable toxicity; taking it with food can reduce nausea. PBS authority; 100 mg and 150 mg tablets. [S1]
- **Metoclopramide 10 mg as needed** (no more than 30 mg in 24 hours, for up to 5 days) is supplied at the start. Nausea can be moderate in the first month or months but usually settles with antiemetics and reassurance, without dose changes. [S1]
- **Dose steps for blood toxicity go 300, then 250, then 200 mg twice daily, then stop.** For ANC under 1.0 or platelets under 75, delay until recovery; the first episode restarts at 300 mg and each later one a step lower. For grade 3 or 4 anaemia, delay until haemoglobin is above 9 g/dL. [S1]
- **Kidney:** with eGFR 31 to 50, reduce to 200 mg twice daily and monitor; it has not been studied below 30. **Liver:** no change for mild or moderate impairment; not recommended in severe impairment. [S1]

## Monitoring and red flags

- ⚠️ **MDS and AML:** PARP inhibitors may increase the risk of myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid leukaemia, so check a full blood count with film regularly. Do not give olaparib to anyone with a history of bone marrow dysplasia. [S1]
- **Bloods:** LFTs, FBC and EUC/eGFR at baseline, monthly through the first year, then as indicated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pneumonitis, occasionally fatal:** with new or worsening breathlessness, cough, fever or a radiological abnormality, withhold olaparib and investigate promptly; stop it if pneumonitis is confirmed. [S1]
- **Wound healing:** pause olaparib around major surgery and restart once healing is judged adequate. [S1]
- **Fatigue** is common early and usually eases. Exclude other causes and advise graded exercise; if it stays severe despite breaks, consider a short interruption or a lower dose. [S1]
- **Hepatitis B screening is not routinely needed** but can be considered in high-risk people. The safety of live vaccines during and after olaparib is not established. [S1]

## Evidence

- **SOLO1 (first-line maintenance, 391 BRCA-mutated patients):** after 5 years' follow-up, median PFS was 56.0 months with olaparib against 13.8 months with placebo (HR 0.33), and 48.3% against 20.5% were free of progression at 5 years. [S1]
- **SOLO1 at 7 years:** 67.0% were alive with olaparib against 46.5% with placebo (HR 0.55). The gain is clinically meaningful but missed the trial's prespecified significance threshold. [S1]
- **SOLO2 (platinum-sensitive relapse, 295 BRCA-mutated patients):** median PFS 19.1 against 5.5 months (HR 0.30). At final analysis, median overall survival was 51.7 against 38.8 months (HR 0.74, p=0.054). [S1]
- **Harms in SOLO2:** grade 3 to 4 anaemia in 21% on olaparib. Treatment-emergent events led to interruption in 50%, dose reduction in 28% and stopping in 17%, and 5 patients (3%) died of treatment-related MDS or AML. [S1]
