# Acute meningococcal disease in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, empiric and directed antibiotics, resuscitation, isolation, notification and disposition for invasive meningococcal disease in children. Prevention by vaccination is `meningococcal-disease`; general sepsis care is `sepsis-children`; meningitis more broadly is `meningitis-and-encephalitis-children`.

> ⚠️ **Give ceftriaxone or cefotaxime within 15 minutes of suspecting meningococcal disease** (benzylpenicillin if unavailable). Take blood cultures first if possible, but never delay the antibiotic.

> ⚠️ **Meningococcal disease is not ruled out by a rash that blanches** — early on it can be macular or maculopapular.

---

## Recognise it

- ⚠️ **Even when treated, meningococcal disease can kill rapidly.** Mortality is up to 10% despite prompt treatment; 10–20% are left with long-term morbidity such as deafness, amputations or neurological deficits. [S1]
- **It presents as sepsis with DIC and a fast-spreading petechial/purpuric rash that does not blanch** (higher mortality), **or as meningitis, with or without a rash**; less often as septic arthritis, pneumonia, pharyngitis, conjunctivitis or occult bacteraemia. [S1]
- **History:** over <12 hours, headache, nausea, vomiting, poor appetite, sore throat and coryza come on quickly; fever; myalgia or leg pain. **Infants may show only poor feeding, irritability, lethargy, mottling or a high-pitched moaning cry.** [S1]
- **Examination:** cool peripheries and/or pallor or mottling · altered conscious state · headache, neck stiffness, photophobia, bulging fontanelle · **non-blanching petechiae/purpura**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A blanching rash does not exclude it**; the rash can initially be macular or maculopapular and blanch. [S1]
- **Risk factors:** being Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander; immunodeficiency (asplenia, immunoglobulin or complement deficiency, sickle cell disease, HSCT, HIV); cigarette smoke; crowded housing; prolonged contact with a carrier. **Ask about vaccination status, including MenB.** [S1]

## Investigate

- ⚠️ **Investigations must not delay antibiotics.** Blood (IV or intraosseous) for culture before antibiotics if possible, plus PCR in a separate EDTA tube. [S1]
- **CSF only once stabilised and with no contraindication to LP:** cell count, biochemistry, Gram stain (Gram-negative diplococci), culture ± meningococcal PCR. [S1]

## Treat — initial

- **Until meningococcal infection is confirmed, treat and resuscitate as for sepsis.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **The moment meningococcal disease is suspected, give ceftriaxone/cefotaxime — within 15 minutes**; if neither is available, give benzylpenicillin. Empiric treatment is a third-generation cephalosporin. [S1]
- **Beyond 4 weeks of life: IV ceftriaxone once daily, 100 mg/kg (max 4 g).** [S1]
- **Alternatively IV cefotaxime, 50 mg/kg (max 2 g)** — 12-hourly in week 1 of life, 6–8-hourly in weeks 2–4, 6-hourly after week 4. [S1]
- **No IV or intraosseous access: give it IM** — volume or muscle size may mean two injections — then give the dose again when IV access is obtained. [S1]
- **No ceftriaxone/cefotaxime available? IV benzylpenicillin, 60 mg/kg (max 2.4 g)** — 12-hourly in week 1, 6-hourly in weeks 2–4, 4-hourly after week 4. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Children treated with benzylpenicillin also need contact-prophylaxis antibiotics** to eradicate carriage. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Anticipate rapid deterioration.** Carefully titrated fluid resuscitation with repeated assessment, and **early consideration of inotropes**. [S1]

## Ongoing management

- **Confirmed disease: 5 days of IV treatment.** Continue the third-generation cephalosporin until sensitivities are confirmed. [S1]
- **Once benzylpenicillin susceptibility is confirmed, de-escalate to it**: IV, 60 mg/kg (max 2.4 g) — 12-hourly in week 1, 6-hourly in weeks 2–4, 4-hourly after week 4. [S1]
- **Dexamethasone given for empiric meningitis can be stopped once N. meningitidis is confirmed** — no clearly demonstrated benefit. [S1]
- **Isolate with droplet precautions** per local infection-control guidelines. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Notify every presumed or confirmed case immediately** to the local state authority. [S1]
- **Give contact prophylaxis to at-risk contacts as soon as possible.** [S1]

## Vaccination after a case

- **Cases:** vaccinate only if the case has underlying risk factors. **Contacts:** for serogroups A, C, W or Y, non-immunised contacts may be offered vaccination as secondary prevention; **a single first dose of MenB is unlikely to protect a contact during the risk period.** [S1]

## Disposition

- ⚠️ **Manage every child with acute meningococcal disease in a facility that can provide intensive care**; if unavailable, stabilise and transfer. Consult the local paediatric team for all suspected cases. [S1]
- **Discharge:** IV treatment can be completed through HITH once haemodynamically stable and afebrile, and after contact prophylaxis has been considered. [S1]
