# Adult IDH-mutant glioma (astrocytoma and oligodendroglioma)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** testing, surgery, adjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy, IDH inhibitors (vorasidenib), follow-up, recurrence and survivorship for adults with IDH-mutant astrocytoma (WHO grade 2-4) or oligodendroglioma (grade 2-3). Children and adolescents, and IDH-wildtype glioblastoma, are out of scope. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Diagnose and grade by the latest WHO CNS classification**, combining morphology with molecular results into one integrated diagnosis. [S1]
- **Test every patient for IDH1 R132H by immunohistochemistry.** If that is negative and the patient is under 55, sequence for non-canonical IDH mutations. An NGS panel can check IDH1/2, 1p/19q and CDKN2A/B in one test for classification and grading. [S1]
- **Non-canonical IDH mutations** make up as many as 10% of cases overall but only 0.9% in patients aged 55 or older, which is why sequencing is aimed at younger patients. [S1]
- **MGMT promoter methylation testing is not routinely needed:** unlike in IDH-wildtype glioblastoma, its predictive value here is unclear. [S1]
- **MRI before any treatment is the standard.** Minimum sequences: axial T2, FLAIR, diffusion, and volumetric T1 before and after contrast. A T2-FLAIR mismatch is the most useful pointer to an IDH-mutant astrocytoma, but tissue confirmation is always required. [S1]

## Management principles

- **Discuss every patient at a dedicated neuro-oncology MDT**, offer clinical trials, and raise fertility preservation before any treatment starts. [S1]

## Surgery

- **Consider early maximal safe resection for everyone:** it is linked to longer PFS and OS and possibly slower transformation. Keep biopsy alone for tumours where resection would cause a significant neurological deficit. [S1]
- **Residual T2/FLAIR volume matters:** under 5 cm3 after surgery is associated with better survival than 5-25 cm3. Weigh location and the feasibility of further surgery as well. [S1]

## Grade 2 astrocytoma or oligodendroglioma

- **High-risk features (subtotal resection, older age, early progression): consider adjuvant radiotherapy plus chemotherapy.** With favourable features (complete resection, younger age) or toxicity concerns, both can be deferred. [S1]
- **Evidence for adding chemotherapy:** RTOG 9802 (high risk defined as age over 40 and less than gross total resection) added up to six 8-weekly cycles of PCV to RT, and median OS rose to 13.3 from 7.8 years. [S1]
- **Temozolomide (up to 12 four-weekly cycles) is an accepted alternative to PCV**, which is hard to tolerate. In E3F05, RT plus temozolomide raised 10-year OS to 70% from 47% with RT alone. [S1]
- **Radiotherapy dose for grade 2:** typically 50.4 Gy over 28 fractions, or 54 Gy over 30. IMRT or VMAT is preferred to spare healthy brain; proton therapy is unproven and kept for highly selected patients. [S1]
- **Vorasidenib (INDIGO):** 331 patients with grade 2 disease treated by surgery alone and suitable for watch-and-wait. Median PFS was 2.3 years on drug versus 0.9 on placebo. Excluded were patients with significant enhancement, uncontrolled seizures or brainstem tumours. [S1]
- **Vorasidenib toxicity:** 22.8% had grade 3 or worse adverse events, most often abnormal liver tests; 3.6% stopped permanently. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Vorasidenib's effect on survival is not known.** Its PFS is well short of that seen with first-line RT plus chemotherapy, and nobody yet knows whether RT and chemotherapy work as well after progression on it. [S1]
- **An IDH inhibitor is an option when immediate RT and chemotherapy are not needed.** The source notes TGA approval but no PBS funding at the time (see the banner above). [S1]

## Grade 3 oligodendroglioma

- **Offer radiotherapy then chemotherapy after surgery as routine.** Usual Australian doses: 59.4 Gy over 33 fractions, or 60 Gy over 30. PCV has the strongest evidence; temozolomide is reasonable if tolerability or access is a problem. [S1]
- **Australian practice follows the EORTC 26951 pattern**, RT first and then lower-intensity lomustine PCV, because the dose-intensive pre-RT schedule of RTOG 9402 caused two early neutropenic deaths for similar efficacy. [S1]
- **No IDH inhibitor for grade 3 disease:** these patients were excluded from INDIGO, and whether RT and chemotherapy can safely wait is unknown. [S1]

## Grade 3 or 4 astrocytoma

- **Grade 3: radiotherapy then sequential temozolomide.** In CATNON, 12 four-weekly adjuvant cycles improved median OS (6.7 v 3.8 years); concurrent temozolomide added nothing. [S1]
- **Grade 4: radiotherapy with or without concurrent temozolomide, then sequential temozolomide.** The Stupp glioblastoma regimen is what most Australian centres use. [S1]

## Follow-up and recurrence

- **MRI every 3-4 months at first**, then lengthen the interval once the disease course is clear, using the same acquisition protocol each time. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Read surveillance MRI cautiously.** Treatment effects are common in the first months after RT. Small T2/FLAIR change in a stable patient, or enhancement alone, does not prove progression; a new progressive neurological deficit is the trigger to investigate or treat. [S1]
- **At recurrence, get fresh tissue where possible** to confirm it and check for grade change, individualise treatment, and offer a trial because second-line options work poorly. [S1]

## Survivorship

- **Provide brain cancer care coordination and allied health referral**, and encourage early legal and financial planning (wills, medical and financial powers of attorney) while cognition is intact. [S1]
