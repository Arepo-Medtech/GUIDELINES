# Myasthenia gravis and Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome — immunoglobulin (IVIg) use

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** the national criteria for when intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) is funded in myasthenia gravis (crisis, pre-operative preparation and maintenance) and Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome: diagnosis requirements, qualifying and exclusion criteria, doses, and review and continuation rules. **S1 covers IVIg eligibility only; S2 (German Neurological Society guideline, 2023) adds pyridostigmine, corticosteroids, steroid-sparing drugs, thymectomy and the C5 and FcRn inhibitors.** It does not cover first-line MG treatment (pyridostigmine, corticosteroids, steroid-sparing immunosuppression, thymectomy) or the PBS complement C5 and FcRn inhibitors (ravulizumab, zilucoplan, rozanolixizumab) beyond noting that IVIg maintenance is not funded alongside them. ## Recognise and diagnose myasthenia gravis

- **MG is an autoimmune disorder of the neuromuscular junction** with antibodies to the acetylcholine receptor (AChR) or to MuSK; a minority of patients are seronegative. [S1]
- **Suspect it in fatigable, fluctuating weakness** of the eyelids, eye movements, bulbar, limb and breathing muscles: ptosis, diplopia, dysphagia and proximal weakness. Respiratory muscle failure is the danger (myasthenic crisis). [S1]
- **Reflexes and sensation are preserved.** Repetitive nerve stimulation shows a decrement at 2–3 Hz that recovers after brief exercise; antibody titres and single-fibre EMG help confirm. A neurologist must make the diagnosis for IVIg access. [S1]

## Treat myasthenia gravis (international guideline)

- **Symptomatic treatment is an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, mainly pyridostigmine,** but fewer than 20% of people with generalised MG stay stable long term on symptomatic treatment alone. [S2]
- **Choose disease-modifying treatment by age, thymus pathology, antibody type and disease activity.** For mild to moderate generalised MG with AChR or LRP4 antibodies, or seronegative disease, start oral corticosteroids and/or azathioprine (with or without thymectomy); mycophenolate, ciclosporin, tacrolimus or methotrexate are second choice when azathioprine fails, is not tolerated or is contraindicated. Keep steroids to the shortest period at a dose matched to severity. [S2]
- **Azathioprine takes several months to work.** Start with 25–50 mg daily in the first week and increase over 3–4 weeks with regular blood tests, or after a 3-day trial of 50 mg daily move straight to 2–3 mg/kg daily; aim for a lymphocyte count of 600–1000/μl. Avoid giving it for more than 10 years because of malignancy risk. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Highly active or refractory generalised MG:** with AChR antibodies, add a complement inhibitor (eculizumab, ravulizumab) or an FcRn modulator (efgartigimod), with or without thymectomy; rituximab may be first choice with AChR or LRP4 antibodies or seronegative disease. IVIG and plasma exchange or immunoadsorption are second choice. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Eculizumab and other complement inhibitors raise the risk of meningococcal infection 1000- to 2000-fold.** Before starting, vaccinate against meningococcal serogroups A, C, W135, Y and B; give eculizumab at least 2 weeks after vaccination, or add antibiotic cover (rifampicin 600 mg every 12 h, or ceftriaxone 2 g daily; avoid quinolones, which can worsen MG). Patients carry a card and take an antibiotic and seek care at once if symptoms appear; follow up for at least 3 months after stopping. [S2]
- **Eculizumab dose:** 900 mg IV weekly for the first four doses, then 1200 mg every 2 weeks from week 5. Treat as a response a fall in MG-ADL of at least three points and in QMG of at least 2. [S2]
- **Ravulizumab** is a longer-acting C5 inhibitor given every 8 weeks: weight-banded IV doses on day 1 and day 15 of 2400, 2700 or 3000 mg (for 40–60, 60–100 and over 100 kg), then 3000, 3300 or 3600 mg every 8 weeks, with the same meningococcal precautions. [S2]
- ⚠️ **MuSK-antibody MG behaves differently:** its IgG4 antibodies do not bind complement, so C5 inhibitors have no target; pyridostigmine often works poorly or is poorly tolerated; thymectomy is not indicated; and rituximab should be considered early as a first-choice option. [S2]
- **Look for thymoma in everyone with chest CT or MRI,** and remove a suspected thymoma as completely as possible. Without thymoma, thymectomy is advised for AChR-antibody-positive generalised MG aged 18–65, as early as possible: within 2 years and no later than 5 years from diagnosis. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Impending or established crisis: admit quickly to an intensive or monitored unit experienced in neuromuscular disease** and treat with IVIG or plasma exchange/immunoadsorption. These are equivalent; choose by comorbidity (plasma exchange is contraindicated in sepsis; IVIG in hypercoagulability, renal failure or immunoglobulin hypersensitivity) and local availability. [S2]

## When IVIg has a role in MG

- **IVIg is funded for three MG situations, with clear evidence of benefit (Category 1):** myasthenic crisis and pre-operative or pre-thymectomy preparation (each as an alternative to plasma exchange), and maintenance of moderate to severe MG after other treatments have failed or were not tolerated. [S1]
- **IVIg and plasma exchange work about equally.** IVIg is simpler to give and plasma exchange is not offered everywhere; weigh line insertion, sepsis and haemodynamic risks of plasma exchange against the inflammatory and thrombotic risks of IVIg. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In MuSK-antibody MG, plasma exchange is reported to work better than IVIg.** [S1]
- **IVIg does not bring remission or change the course of MG,** so treat it as a bridge while symptomatic drugs such as pyridostigmine are used and definitive immunotherapy takes effect. [S1]
- **Pure ocular MG is excluded,** and IVIg was generally ineffective for diplopia in the main trial. [S1]
- **Maintenance IVIg is not steroid-sparing,** in either the placebo-controlled trial or observational data. [S1]

## Qualifying criteria in MG

- ⚠️ **Crisis:** respiratory insufficiency needing intubation and ventilation, or impending crisis (persistent trouble speaking, chewing or swallowing, or breathlessness on minimal exertion), plus severe disability with an MG Composite (MGC) score of 4 or more. [S1]
- **Before surgery or thymectomy:** an operation is planned and the patient has advanced disease, bulbar symptoms or respiratory involvement. Without those features, preparation is generally unnecessary. [S1]
- **Maintenance:** moderate to severe MG (MGC of 4 or more), at least two other treatments ineffective, contraindicated, unavailable or not tolerated, and the patient is not on a PBS-funded C5 inhibitor or FcRn inhibitor. [S1]

## IVIg doses in MG

- **Crisis or pre-surgery: 1–2 g/kg split over 2 to 5 doses,** using the lowest dose that achieves the clinical goal. [S1]
- **1 g/kg is as effective as 2 g/kg,** so make 1 g/kg the usual dose for worsening MG and keep 2 g/kg for crisis, impending crisis or especially severe disease. [S1]
- **Maintenance: after an induction dose, give 0.4–1 g/kg every 4–6 weeks,** titrated to response, and no more than 1 g/kg in any 4 weeks (smaller, more frequent doses are allowed). [S1]
- **The German guideline doses differ slightly:** short-term IVIG 0.4 g/kg daily for five days, or 1 g/kg daily for 2 days (a total of 1 g/kg may be enough); maintenance, used only in individual cases, a pulse and then 0.4 g/kg every 4–8 weeks or more if needed. [S2]

## Review and continuation in MG

- **Give maintenance IVIg a 4-month trial (the induction dose and then 3 maintenance cycles);** stop if there is no benefit. A neurologist reviews within 4 months and yearly after that, and continuation needs documented effectiveness. [S1]
- **Measure response with the MG Composite:** at first review the score should have fallen by at least 3 points from qualifying (or a severe patient shows benefit after each infusion that wears off by cycle end), with at least two other treatments running concurrently where possible. Stable patients should have a planned weaning trial to detect remission. [S1]
- **The MGC is the practical clinical scale:** a 3-point or greater improvement is clinically meaningful. The longer Quantitative MG score, where above 11 predicts response to IVIg or plasma exchange, is mainly a research tool. [S1]

## Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS)

- **LEMS is a presynaptic neuromuscular junction disorder,** first linked to lung cancer but also seen without a tumour. Look for proximal weakness that strengthens after exercise, mild eye signs, reduced reflexes and autonomic features (dry mouth, constipation, erectile failure). Confirm with voltage-gated calcium channel antibodies, electrophysiology or both. [S1]
- ⚠️ **First-line LEMS treatment is 3,4-DAP (amifampridine) with pyridostigmine; IVIg is second line** and gives temporary benefit. In paraneoplastic LEMS, treating the cancer often helps, and immunosuppression (unlike IVIg) is generally avoided. [S1]
- **IVIg in LEMS qualifies when disease is severe** (MRC sum score 58 or lower, or major autonomic involvement) and other therapies have failed or fallen short. Dose: induction 2 g/kg over 2 to 5 doses, then 0.4–1 g/kg every 2–6 weeks. [S1]
- **LEMS immunotherapy (international guideline):** corticosteroids at a severity-matched dose for as short a time as possible, azathioprine to spare steroids (alternatives mycophenolate, ciclosporin, tacrolimus or rituximab), and avoid methotrexate, especially in paraneoplastic LEMS. Use IVIG, or plasma exchange if needed, for crisis-like deterioration. [S2]
