## Key points

- **The degree of clinical compromise, not the size of the pneumothorax, determines management.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe respiratory distress suggests tension pneumothorax — a medical emergency needing urgent needle thoracocentesis.** [S1]

## Background

- **Primary spontaneous pneumothorax (PSP)** occurs without underlying lung disease and with no provoking factor such as surgery, trauma or mechanical ventilation. [S1]
- **Secondary pneumothoraces** (e.g. cystic fibrosis) are harder to manage — seek respiratory or surgical advice. [S1]
- **PSP recurs in 30-40%**, usually ipsilaterally and inside 1 year (median 2 months). [S1]

## Recognise it

- **Acute pleuritic chest pain** radiating to the ipsilateral shoulder — ⚠️ **it can subside over 24 hours even though the pneumothorax persists** — with sudden shortness of breath. [S1]
- **A small pneumothorax may give a normal examination.** Look for tachycardia, tachypnoea, hyperresonance, reduced air entry and vocal fremitus on the affected side, asymmetric expansion and mediastinal shift; consider transillumination in young infants. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tension pneumothorax:** trachea deviated to the opposite side, tachycardia, hypotension, cyanosis — **urgent needle thoracocentesis.** [S1]

## Investigate

- **Chest X-ray confirms it: a standard erect film in inspiration.** No expiratory films are needed. [S1]
- **CXR signs:** a linear visceral pleural line with no lung markings beyond it · flattened or inverted hemidiaphragm · mediastinal shift away from the affected side · small effusions are common. [S1]
- **On a supine film** look for the deep sulcus sign and hyperlucency of the affected side. [S1]
- **Consider point-of-care ultrasound if available. CT is not routinely recommended.** [S1]

## Size

- **Size measurement is controversial and not age-specific**; clinical compromise matters more. [S1]
- **Over 12 years only — 'large' if** the apical lung-to-thoracic-cage distance is over 3 cm, **or** the lateral lung edge-to-chest wall distance at the hilum is over 2 cm (at least 50%). [S1]
- **Younger children:** no validated method exists; the Light method may be used. [S1]

## Escalate, transfer, discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team for:** a large pneumothorax · significant breathlessness, pain or hypoxia · age under 12 · secondary pneumothorax (CF, asthma, chronic neonatal lung disease) · thoracic surgery or trauma · positive-pressure ventilation · inhaled foreign body · tension · recurrence. [S1]
- **Transfer if** ongoing or underwater-seal drainage is needed or the child deteriorates during observation. [S1]
- **Discharge when** symptoms are minimal with no deterioration during observation, follow-up is arranged within 24-48 hours, and the family understands the recurrence risk and warning symptoms. [S1]
