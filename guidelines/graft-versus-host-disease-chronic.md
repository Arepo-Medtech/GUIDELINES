# Chronic graft-versus-host disease (cGVHD)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** chronic GVHD after allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplant: classification, risk factors, NIH diagnosis and global severity, first-line corticosteroids, steroid-refractory and steroid-dependent disease, second-line ruxolitinib and other agents, organ-specific supportive care and patient education. Acute GVHD: see `graft-versus-host-disease-acute`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise

- **GVHD is the leading cause of non-relapse illness, death and poor quality of life after allogeneic stem cell transplant.** It happens when donor immune cells treat the recipient's tissues as foreign. [S1]
- **The old day-100 line between acute and chronic GVHD no longer holds**, since acute GVHD can begin after day 100 with reduced-intensity conditioning or donor lymphocyte infusion. Chronic GVHD has no time limit, and an overlap syndrome shows features of both. [S1]
- **Roughly 40% of allogeneic transplant recipients get chronic GVHD.** Risk rises with HLA mismatch, a female (especially multiparous) donor for a male recipient, more intense conditioning, a peripheral blood graft (cord blood carries least risk), prior acute GVHD, older donor or recipient age, CMV serostatus, a donor seropositive for EBV and previous splenectomy; risk factors add up. [S1]

## Diagnose and grade

- **Think of chronic GVHD in anyone who has had an allogeneic transplant.** When it is suspected, consider an organ biopsy and liver tests, and look for other causes. [S1]
- **NIH consensus criteria:** the diagnosis needs one or more diagnostic features, or one or more distinctive features backed up by biopsy or another appropriate test. [S1]
- **Mouth and eyes:** lacy white lichen planus-like oral lesions are diagnostic; dry mouth, mucoceles and ulcers, and new dry, gritty or painful eyes or keratoconjunctivitis sicca, are distinctive and need confirming. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Lung:** bronchiolitis obliterans on biopsy, or bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome, is diagnostic; air trapping or bronchiectasis on chest CT is only distinctive. [S1]
- **NIH global severity:** mild means 1 or 2 organs, none scoring above 1, and a lung score of 0; moderate means 3 or more organs scoring up to 1, any non-lung organ scoring 2, or a lung score of 1; severe means any organ scoring 3, or a lung score of 2 or 3. [S1]

## First-line treatment

- **Treatment depends on which organs are involved, how severe it is, the prophylaxis used and earlier acute GVHD treatment**, and must be weighed against the graft-versus-tumour effect. Care works best in a multidisciplinary team of transplant, organ-specialist, nursing and allied health staff. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Mild disease can be treated topically. Moderate to severe disease: systemic prednisolone, usually 1 mg/kg/day, is first line** (tailored to organ, severity and patient). Continue or restart the original immunosuppressant, such as a calcineurin inhibitor; give antibacterial, antiviral and antifungal prophylaxis to all patients on steroids; and consider a trial. [S1]
- **There is no standard steroid taper.** Taper as early as clinically possible, to limit long-term harm and infection, but slowly and according to response. Allow at least one month before judging whether first-line treatment has worked. [S1]
- **Steroid-refractory:** progression despite 1-2 weeks of at least 1 mg/kg/day of prednisolone, or no improvement after 1-2 months on steroid (for example 0.5 mg/kg/day); outcomes are worse. **Steroid-dependent:** the dose cannot be taken below about 0.25 mg/kg/day despite two or more attempts at least 8 weeks apart. [S1]

## Second-line treatment

- ⚠️ **Ruxolitinib is PBS-funded second-line for moderate to severe chronic GVHD**, on the strength of the phase 3 REACH3 trial. Start at 10 mg twice daily; adjust for anaemia, thrombocytopenia or interactions (for example azole antifungals), and consider tapering once the patient has responded and is off steroids. [S1]
- **Other agents used second-line include** biologics (abatacept, alemtuzumab, etanercept, rituximab), kinase inhibitors (belumosudil, ibrutinib, imatinib), extracorporeal photopheresis, calcineurin or mTOR inhibitors, mycophenolate, hydroxychloroquine, interleukin-2 and pentostatin. [S1]

## Supportive care by organ

- **Skin:** emollients, SPF 50+ sunscreen and covering clothing. On intact body skin start with a mid-strength steroid such as triamcinolone 0.02%, stepping up to betamethasone dipropionate 0.05% if it fails; use hydrocortisone 0.5 - 1% on the face, armpits and groin. Topical tacrolimus helps dry, flaky skin. [S1]
- **Mouth:** keep up oral and dental hygiene; clobetasol or other topical steroids, topical tacrolimus or a dexamethasone rinse for ulcers or leukoplakia; water sips, ice or saliva substitutes for dryness; viscous lignocaine for pain; and candida prophylaxis with topical oral steroids. ⚠️ A dentist or oral surgeon should examine the mouth at least yearly to exclude oral cancer, and more often with longstanding oral GVHD or suspicious lesions. [S1]
- **Eyes:** artificial tears, topical steroid or ciclosporin drops, or autologous serum drops. **Gut:** culture the stool in diarrhoea; loperamide, codeine or octreotide for heavy diarrhoea; pancreatic enzymes if it persists; nutrition advice, fluids and small, frequent meals. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome: the FAM regimen** (inhaled fluticasone propionate 500 µg twice a day, montelukast 10 mg daily and azithromycin 250 mg on three days each week) alongside systemic steroids. [S1]
- **Vulvovaginal:** topical corticosteroid (clobetasol, betamethasone or triamcinolone) for dryness, with early gynaecology review for vaginal stenosis. Bring in organ specialists experienced in chronic GVHD, such as dermatology, ophthalmology and dentistry. [S1]

## Patient education

- **Early detection is everyone's job, and patients who report symptoms actively have fewer readmissions and better survival.** Teach about immunosuppressant side effects, monitoring and adherence, which symptoms to report straight away, skin and sun care, oral hygiene, sunglasses for dry eyes, avoiding infection, and regular exercise to preserve muscle while on steroids. [S1]
