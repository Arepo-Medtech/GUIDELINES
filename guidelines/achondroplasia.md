# Achondroplasia — vosoritide treatment in children

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** selecting, starting, monitoring, suspending and stopping vosoritide in children with achondroplasia, including babies under 24 months. The overall medical management of achondroplasia (spinal, airway, ENT and orthopaedic complications) is in the separate Australian achondroplasia management guideline, which is not covered here. PBS access is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## What vosoritide does

- **Vosoritide is a C-type natriuretic peptide analogue, given by daily subcutaneous injection,** that counters FGFR3 signalling and increases endochondral bone growth. [S1]
- **Phase 3 trials showed a gain of 1.57 cm/year in annualised growth velocity** in children aged 5 and over, sustained over 3 years. [S1]
- **Starting in infancy may reduce the risk of foramen magnum stenosis;** its effect on the foramen magnum under age 2 is still being studied. [S1]

## Before starting

- ⚠️ **Confirm the diagnosis genetically:** testing must show the FGFR3 Gly380Arg mutation. If genetic testing is negative, do not start vosoritide; refer for further diagnostic work-up. (E) [S1]
- **Growth plates must be open,** shown by knee X-rays or by growth velocity above 1.5 cm/year over at least 6 months; the X-ray route is more practical in infants under 6 months. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat severe complications first:** craniocervical junction or spinal cord stenosis or compression, spinal deformity and sleep-disordered breathing should be managed before starting. **Infants need a sleep study and a craniocervical junction MRI beforehand.** (R) [S1]
- **Optimise other conditions that affect growth (e.g. hypothyroidism).** Cardiac disease, conditions predisposing to hypotension and blood-pressure-lowering medicines raise the risk of symptomatic hypotension and may need closer monitoring at first doses. [S1]
- **Educate and train the family** on benefits, side effects, daily injection, refrigerated storage, reconstitution and site rotation. Safety is established only when at least 90% of prescribed doses are given. Obtain informed consent before prescribing. (E) [S1]

## Starting treatment

- **Dose by weight,** using the dosing calculator and the weight–dose band tables in the Product Information. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Give the first dose under supervision and observe for at least 2 hours** (1 hour can be considered from age 6 if stable). Check pulse and blood pressure before the dose, at 15 and 30 minutes, then every 30 minutes; symptomatic hypotension needs medical review. (E) [S1]
- **Hypotension is very common but usually asymptomatic and self-limiting.** [S1]
- **First follow-up at 2–4 weeks** to check adverse events, injection technique, tolerance and adherence; it can be by phone or video if safe. (R) [S1]

## Monitoring

- **Review growth, achondroplasia complications, efficacy and safety from 6 months, then at 6-monthly and annual visits,** measuring height with a calibrated stadiometer every time. (E) [S1]
- **Shared care with a local paediatrician or paediatric endocrinologist is reasonable for interim visits** once a child aged 2 or over is established, but annual visits stay in the specialist achondroplasia clinic. (R) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Watch for worsening spinal deformity and slipped upper femoral epiphysis,** which can come with accelerated growth. Report suspected serious adverse events to the TGA (Black Triangle Scheme). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Growth below 1.5 cm/year before late puberty needs urgent investigation.** Typical growth in achondroplasia is about 4 cm/year; poor response despite open growth plates may reflect scoliosis, knee varus or rarely growth hormone deficiency. [S1]

## Suspending and stopping

- **Suspend vosoritide when low blood pressure is a risk,** such as reduced fluid intake in severe intercurrent illness and around surgery (until out of recovery). (R) [S1]
- **Stop when X-ray shows growth plate closure, or when growth is below 1.5 cm/year in late puberty on two consecutive measures over at least 6 months.** [S1]

## Babies under 24 months

- ⚠️ **Under 28 days old, the second dose is also supervised in a specialist clinic, with 4 hours' observation after each of the first two doses.** (E) [S1]
- **Under 24 months, all monitoring visits happen in a specialist clinic, with a formal review 3 months after starting;** measure supine length in children who cannot stand. (R) [S1]
