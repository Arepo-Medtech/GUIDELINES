# Cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes (CAPS) — diagnosis, IL-1 blockade and monitoring

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognising the CAPS spectrum (FCAS, Muckle-Wells, NOMID/CINCA), genetic and clinical work-up, IL-1 blocking treatment (anakinra, canakinumab, rilonacept), and long-term monitoring including amyloidosis, infection, vaccination, transition and pregnancy. TRAPS, MKD and DIRA, covered by the same source, are summarised only where the advice is shared. PBS access (anakinra) is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise

- **CAPS is a spectrum of rare autosomal dominant diseases from gain-of-function NLRP3 mutations:** mild familial cold autoinflammatory syndrome (FCAS), moderate Muckle-Wells syndrome (MWS) and severe NOMID/CINCA. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Onset is usually neonatal or in early childhood,** with fever, abdominal pain, rash, joint and muscle symptoms, neurological features and raised inflammatory markers. Delayed treatment risks rapid organ damage and death. [S1]
- **Almost everyone has an urticaria-like neutrophilic rash.** Cold-triggered flares under 24 hours suggest FCAS; a negative ice-cube test separates it from cold urticaria. [S1]
- **Progressive sensorineural deafness** is common in MWS and NOMID/CINCA; chronic aseptic meningitis, raised intracranial pressure, cognitive impairment and bony overgrowth (distal femur, frontal bossing) point to NOMID/CINCA. [S1]
- **Differentials** include systemic JIA, adult-onset Still's disease, cancer, infection and autoimmune disease. [S1]

## Assess

- **Suggestive features should prompt genetic testing, usually by next-generation sequencing.** Severe cases are often de novo and may be somatic mosaics missed at standard depth; if testing is unrevealing, refer to an expert centre. [S1]
- **Every CAPS patient needs a baseline slit-lamp and retinal examination.** If neurological disease is suspected, add brain imaging and lumbar puncture (raised pressure, aseptic meningitis); dedicated MRI can show cochlear enhancement, atrophy and ventriculomegaly. [S1]
- **CRP, ESR and (where available) serum amyloid A track disease activity and damage risk.** [S1]

## Genetic testing in Australia (ASCIA)

- **ASCIA: consider genomic testing in selected patients with autoinflammatory disease:** a classical phenotype suggesting a monogenic disorder, or atypical features, severe disease, treatment resistance or family history, above all with early onset. [S2]
- **Expect a lower yield than in other inborn errors of immunity (7–20%)**, partly because of somatic mutations; treatment is often the same across genetic diagnoses, and isolated uncomplicated periodic fever in young children (e.g. PFAPA) is rarely monogenic. [S2]
- ⚠️ **There is no federal Medicare funding for IEI genomic testing in Australia;** the patient pays privately or the state Hospital and Health Service covers it. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Do not withhold treatment while genomic results are pending:** a genetic diagnosis is not a prerequisite for starting therapy, and treatment decisions should mostly rest on the phenotype. Decide on testing with an immunologist experienced in these disorders. [S2]
- **Know which genes the panel covers** (curated lists such as PanelApp Australia help). A negative result does not exclude a genetic cause, reanalysis raises the yield, and a variant of uncertain significance must not drive treatment on its own. [S2]
- **Give genetic counselling before and after testing,** ideally from an immunologist and a genetic counsellor with IEI expertise; Australian Genomics provides standard consent forms and information sheets. Cover at-risk relatives, reproductive decisions and cascade testing. [S2]

## Treat

- **Subcutaneous IL-1 blockade is standard care.** Anakinra (short-acting IL-1 receptor antagonist, blocks IL-1α and IL-1β), rilonacept (longer-acting trap) and canakinumab (long-acting anti-IL-1β antibody) are the options. [S1]
- **Treat to target: complete remission,** meaning no symptoms and normal inflammatory markers; a CRP under 5 or 10 mg/L indicates adequate control. [S1]
- **Most patients need continuous treatment,** with dose and interval adjusted to disease activity, inflammatory markers, weight gain and growth. IL-1 blockers control inflammation without steroids. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe disease often needs more than the label:** canakinumab more often than every 8 weeks, up to every 4 weeks in NOMID/CINCA. [S1]
- **Anakinra may have an edge in severe CAPS, particularly with neurological disease;** infants and preschool children may need it twice a day. [S1]
- **On-demand dosing is an option only for very mild, episodic FCAS** with normal markers between attacks. NSAIDs can be added for symptoms. [S1]
- **Care by a multidisciplinary team gives better control in CAPS.** [S1]

## Monitor

- **Follow neutrophil count, CRP and ESR** (SAA and S100 proteins where available). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Test for proteinuria every six months** to detect AA amyloidosis, especially with a family history of amyloidosis. [S1]
- **The most serious CAPS damage:** deafness, CNS disease, bone deformity, renal failure from amyloid and visual loss. [S1]
- **Watch for infection** (pneumococcal chest infections, staphylococcal skin infections). Give pneumococcal vaccines, including polysaccharide vaccine in CAPS, ideally before starting IL-1 blockade but also acceptable during it. [S1]
- **Plan transition early:** up to half of adolescents are not properly handed over to adult care. [S1]
- **Pregnancy:** discuss benefits and risks before conception; anakinra is the preferred IL-1 blocker. [S1]
