# Newly diagnosed multiple myeloma — transplant-eligible patients

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** induction, stem-cell mobilisation, high-dose melphalan with autologous transplant, consolidation, maintenance and follow-up for adults with newly diagnosed myeloma judged fit for transplant. Transplant-ineligible and relapsed/refractory myeloma: see `myeloma-transplant-ineligible` and `myeloma-relapsed-refractory`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 30 claims; **59 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 2 doses.** **S1** Medical and Scientific Advisory Group (MSAG) of Myeloma Australia (published 4 August 2025; AU): 20 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored · **S2** Pan-Pacific Multiple Myeloma Working Group (Chen W and others) (published 11 April 2025; international): 10 claims, quoted

> ⚠️ **Australia differs from the international standard here, because of PBS funding.** A CD38 antibody (daratumumab) added to induction is the global standard, but it is **not PBS-reimbursed upfront**. So RVD remains the Australian standard induction.

> ⚠️ International consensus (Pan-Pacific Multiple Myeloma Working Group, 2025) for **S2**. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ. S2's panel is from mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Japan, Taiwan and Russia, and it cites NCCN v1.2025.

## Who is eligible

- **Transplant eligibility is an individual judgement:** age, comorbidity, frailty, disability and the patient's wishes all count. Frailty and comorbidity indices can help, but none is validated for this decision. [S1]
- **Selected fit patients up to age 75 can be transplanted.** Trials mostly capped age at 65, yet data show transplant can be done safely in chosen older patients without a significant rise in transplant-related harm. [S1]

## Induction

- **Give 3 to 6 induction cycles before transplant, aiming for at least a partial response.** (Grade A, Level II) [S1]
- ⚠️ **RVD (lenalidomide, bortezomib, dexamethasone) is the recommended induction for most Australian patients**, because a CD38 antibody is not funded upfront. (Grade A, Level II) [S1]
- **Bortezomib by subcutaneous injection where possible.** A modified **weekly** schedule reduces neuropathy; keep **twice-weekly** dosing for when fast control matters, such as renal failure from cast nephropathy. (Grade B, Level III-3) [S1]
- **In acute severe renal impairment**, another bortezomib-based regimen (VD, VCD or VTD) can be used for induction instead. (Grade B, Level II) [S1]
- **With systemic AL amyloidosis**, where full-dose lenalidomide is poorly tolerated, use **daratumumab with cyclophosphamide, bortezomib and dexamethasone**, which the PBS funds for this. (Grade A, Level II) [S1]
- **Without a partial response to RVD, the best next step is uncertain.** Proceeding to transplant is reasonable; trials or salvage can be considered, but the PBS funds a new regimen only on proven progression. [S1]

## Stem cell mobilisation

- **Collect stem cells after 3–4 cycles of RVD, whether transplant is planned now or deferred.** (Grade A, Level II) [S1]
- **Three ways to mobilise:** G-CSF (10 mcg/kg) on its own; G-CSF plus plerixafor; or G-CSF plus cyclophosphamide (1.5–2 g/m²). When chemotherapy is part of it, choose the **intermediate** cyclophosphamide range over high doses (>2 g/m²): yields are much the same. (Grade B, Level III-3) [S1]
- **Use plerixafor when G-CSF alone or cyclophosphamide with G-CSF fails;** the PBS funds it only after failed chemo-mobilisation. (Grade B, Level II) [S1]

## High-dose melphalan

- ⚠️ **Melphalan 200 mg/m² for patients with normal kidney function.** Consider 140 mg/m² when eGFR is below 30 mL/min/1.73m². If a patient is judged unfit for 200 mg/m², reconsider whether transplant is appropriate at all. [S1]
- **Upfront transplant after induction is still the standard of care.** Deferral is reasonable in standard-risk disease, because overall survival was equal in both major trials. [S1]
- **Tandem (second) transplant may still suit eligible high-risk patients, particularly with del17p;** consider it in young high-risk patients, then give maintenance. [S1]

## After transplant

- **Consolidation has uncertain benefit.** Discuss it; offering it is reasonable if induction was well tolerated and there is no peripheral neuropathy. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Lenalidomide maintenance improves survival and is standard after transplant.** Continue until progression or unacceptable toxicity. [S1]
- **Warn patients about second cancers**, especially after 2–3 years from transplant, and keep up age-appropriate cancer screening. [S1]
- **In Australia, dual maintenance is not funded,** so lenalidomide stays the standard even in high-risk disease. [S1]
- **Assess response 3 months after transplant.** Bone marrow biopsy is needed to confirm complete response. MRD testing is possible but not funded in Australia. [S1]
- **Allogeneic transplant has a limited role outside trials;** if considered, it belongs in an experienced centre. [S1]

## International standard: CD38-antibody quadruplets

- ⚠️ **International consensus: a CD38 antibody added to VRd or KRd is suitable first-line treatment for transplant-eligible patients** (evidence A, strong; 92.9% of the panel agreed). NCCN v1.2025 now makes D-VRd its only preferred transplant-eligible regimen, with Isa-VRd as another option. In Australia a CD38 antibody is not PBS-funded upfront, so RVD stays the funded standard (S1). [S2]
- **PERSEUS (D-VRd v VRd, induction and consolidation around transplant):** complete response or better 87.9% v 70.1%, MRD negativity 75.2% v 47.5%, and 48-month PFS 84.3% v 67.7% (HR 0.42). Grade 3–4 adverse events were more frequent (91.5% v 85.6%), but fewer patients stopped for them (8.8% v 21.3%). [S2]
- **Isatuximab quadruplets:** in GMMG-HD7, adding isatuximab to VRd raised post-induction MRD negativity (50% v 36%) with no new safety signals but more neutropenia; in IsKia, Isa-KRd beat KRd on MRD negativity after consolidation (77% v 67%). [S2]
- **High-risk disease and stem-cell collection:** for high-risk patients on CD38-antibody quadruplet induction planned for tandem transplant, plan collection before the fourth cycle, with early plerixafor for poor mobilisers (evidence B, strong; 71.4% agreed). [S2]
- **Is a second transplant worth it after a quadruplet?** S2 calls the benefit debatable, because in IFM 2018-04 MRD negativity improved only slightly. S1 still suggests tandem transplant for young high-risk patients; both are shown. [S2]
- **Maintenance:** lenalidomide stays the cornerstone after induction and transplant. S2 adds that lenalidomide plus a CD38 antibody may be considered for high-risk cytogenetics or MRD negativity after quadruplet induction (evidence B, strong; 85.7% agreed), citing AURIGA's higher MRD negativity with D-R; NCCN v1.2025 moved KR and D-R to 'other recommended'. In Australia dual maintenance is not funded (S1). [S2]
- **Adverse events:** quadruplets raise grade 3 or higher infections (RR 1.34) and thrombocytopenia (RR 1.39), mostly mild overall, without more anaemia, neuropathy or pneumonia. Manage cytopenias with regular blood counts, dose changes and G-CSF (evidence A, strong). G-CSF prophylaxis fits a febrile-neutropenia risk of 20% or more, 10–20% with extra factors such as age over 65 or frailty, or regimens with neutropenia above 50% (lenalidomide combinations). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Before starting a quadruplet:** antiviral (aciclovir or valaciclovir) and antibacterial (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) prophylaxis, plus influenza and pneumococcal vaccination (evidence A, strong), with vaccination of close contacts too. Consider immunoglobulin replacement if IgG is below 400 mg/dL or there is specific antibody failure; itraconazole raises lenalidomide exposure. [S2]
- **Carfilzomib quadruplets (Isa-KRd):** baseline echocardiography and cardiac biomarkers (troponin, NT-proBNP), regular blood pressure checks, and repeat echo if heart failure is suspected. [S2]
- **CD38 antibodies and neuropathy:** infusion reactions are common but usually mild and at the first infusion (72.4% in GRIFFIN). If neuropathy threatens treatment, switching bortezomib from twice-weekly to once-weekly keeps Isa-VRd viable. [S2]
