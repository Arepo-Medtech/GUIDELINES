# Paediatric low- and high-grade glioma: molecular testing and targeted therapy

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** molecular testing and targeted-therapy choices for children and adolescents with low-grade glioma (LGG), high-grade glioma (HGG) and diffuse midline glioma (DMG), including BRAF V600E and BRAF-fusion testing and dabrafenib with trametinib. Surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy protocols are only touched on. Ependymoma and medulloblastoma, also in the source, are not summarised here. Adult IDH-mutant glioma: see `idh-mutant-glioma`; adult glioblastoma: see `glioblastoma`; NF1 tumour surveillance: see `neurofibromatosis-type-1`. PBS access (dabrafenib, trametinib) is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

In Australia the PBS lists **dabrafenib with trametinib** (authority required) for both paediatric low- and high-grade glioma; the source's caveats about access do not apply, but its safety warning does: **never give a type I BRAF inhibitor such as dabrafenib to a tumour with a KIAA1549-BRAF fusion.**

## Diagnosis and molecular testing

- **Molecular testing is now part of routine diagnosis and risk grouping** of paediatric brain tumours: the current WHO classification (CNS5) builds genetic and epigenetic features into the tumour definitions. [S1]
- **When the diagnosis is uncertain, use DNA methylation profiling;** it often confirms or revises the diagnosis where histology is inconclusive. [S1]

## Low-grade glioma

- **Surgery comes first:** the maximal resection that is safe is the mainstay of LGG treatment; molecular results guide prognosis and therapy, above all when disease progresses or recurs. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Test every LGG upfront for BRAF V600E and BRAF fusions.** BRAF is altered in up to 50% of paediatric LGG: the V600 point mutation in about 15%, fusions (usually with KIAA1549) in about 35%. [S1]
- **The two BRAF changes behave differently:** fusion-driven tumours tend to be indolent, while V600E tumours carry more risk of progressing or recurring. [S1]
- ⚠️ **For BRAF V600-mutant LGG that is inoperable, residual or refractory, consider dabrafenib plus trametinib, including first line.** In a randomised phase II trial the pair beat standard chemotherapy on response, progression-free survival and toxicity. Single-agent vemurafenib or dabrafenib is an option where access is limited. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not use a type I BRAF inhibitor (dabrafenib, vemurafenib) for a KIAA1549-BRAF fusion:** it can paradoxically switch on MAPK signalling and speed tumour growth. Tovorafenib, or a MEK inhibitor alone, are the alternatives. [S1]
- **In refractory LGG, also test IDH1, IDH2, FGFR and ALK;** in subependymal giant cell astrocytoma test TSC1 and TSC2 even without clinical tuberous sclerosis, since mTOR inhibition may work. [S1]
- **Other targeted options where drugs can be obtained:** alectinib for ALK-fusion LGG, mTOR inhibitors for TSC or PI3K-AKT-mTOR changes, and bevacizumab for progressive optic pathway glioma after standard chemotherapy has failed. [S1]
- **NF1:** 15-20% of people with NF1 develop gliomas, which respond well overall to MEK inhibitors; a phase III trial is comparing selumetinib with carboplatin/vincristine. Offer genetic testing and counselling for RASopathies as judged clinically. [S1]

## High-grade glioma

- **H3K27M is the key HGG biomarker,** signalling a poor outlook, especially in midline tumours. IDH mutations are uncommon in children but still worth testing in adolescents, as IDH-mutant tumours could be candidates for vorasidenib. [S1]
- **BRAF V600E is found in about 5-15% of paediatric HGG** and may make the tumour sensitive to BRAF plus MEK inhibition. [S1]
- **Also test MGMT promoter methylation** (less common than in adults; methylated tumours respond better to temozolomide), and in infant hemispheric HGG look for ALK, ROS1, MET and NTRK fusions. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider targeted therapy matched to the alteration:** dabrafenib with trametinib for BRAF V600E, larotrectinib or entrectinib for NTRK fusions, and agents for MET, ALK or FGFR changes. Because the outlook is so poor, this can be considered upfront when the drug is available (trial or compassionate access). [S1]
- **Combining several targeted drugs for multiple alterations has very little supporting evidence;** do it only with great caution, ideally in a trial. [S1]

## Diffuse midline glioma

- **Test DMG only when a biopsy is safe;** no targeted therapy is established, so wider panels mainly refine diagnosis and prognosis and open trial eligibility. H3K27M defines the entity, and loss of H3K27me3 on immunohistochemistry is a useful surrogate. [S1]
