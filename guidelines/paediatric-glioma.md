# Paediatric low- and high-grade glioma: molecular testing and targeted therapy

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** molecular testing and targeted-therapy choices for children and adolescents with low-grade glioma (LGG), high-grade glioma (HGG) and diffuse midline glioma (DMG), including BRAF V600E and BRAF-fusion testing and dabrafenib with trametinib. Surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy protocols are only touched on. Ependymoma and medulloblastoma, also in the source, are not summarised here. Adult IDH-mutant glioma: see `idh-mutant-glioma`; adult glioblastoma: see `glioblastoma`; NF1 tumour surveillance: see `neurofibromatosis-type-1`. PBS access (dabrafenib, trametinib) is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Diagnosis and molecular testing

- **Molecular testing is now part of routine diagnosis and risk grouping** of paediatric brain tumours: the current WHO classification (CNS5) builds genetic and epigenetic features into the tumour definitions. [S1]
- **When the diagnosis is uncertain, use DNA methylation profiling;** it often confirms or revises the diagnosis where histology is inconclusive. [S1]
- **Tissue-agnostic testing (S1 Table 2)** for a CNS tumour that is refractory or has relapsed, with no curative option left and the child still in good performance status: NTRK1, NTRK2 or NTRK3 fusions (RNA-NGS preferred; 100% panel agreement), BRAF V600E (100%), microsatellite instability (PCR preferred; 85%), tumour mutational burden by NGS (93%) and RET fusions (93%). DNA methylation profiling is advised for tumours with unusual histology, epidemiology, imaging or genomics (80%). [S1]

## Low-grade glioma

- **Paediatric LGG is the commonest childhood brain tumour (about one-third of paediatric CNS tumours).** Survival exceeds 90% at 20 years, but up to half of children eventually need further treatment for progression, so it is managed as a chronic disease. [S2]
- **Surgery comes first:** the maximal resection that is safe is the mainstay of LGG treatment; molecular results guide prognosis and therapy, above all when disease progresses or recurs. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Test every LGG upfront for BRAF V600E and BRAF fusions.** BRAF is altered in up to 50% of paediatric LGG: the V600 point mutation in about 15%, fusions (usually with KIAA1549) in about 35%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Use a fusion-sensitive test (RNA sequencing or targeted PCR) to look for BRAF fusions:** conventional DNA hotspot panels may miss them and FISH can be unreliable. [S2]
- **The two BRAF changes behave differently:** fusion-driven tumours tend to be indolent, while V600E tumours carry more risk of progressing or recurring. [S1]
- ⚠️ **For BRAF V600-mutant LGG that is inoperable, residual or refractory, consider dabrafenib plus trametinib, including first line.** In a randomised phase II trial the pair beat standard chemotherapy on response, progression-free survival and toxicity. Single-agent vemurafenib or dabrafenib is an option where access is limited. [S1]
- **Standard chemotherapy is carboplatin with vincristine, or vinblastine alone,** with 5-year progression-free survival of 40 to 50% for either. Radiotherapy is now generally kept for refractory disease after medical and surgical options have failed, because of late neurocognitive and vascular harm. [S2]
- **The dabrafenib-trametinib trial result (phase II, BRAF V600E):** response 47% against 11% with vincristine-carboplatin, and median progression-free survival 20.1 against 7.4 months; FDA approval as first-line therapy followed in March 2023. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Do not use a type I BRAF inhibitor (dabrafenib, vemurafenib) for a KIAA1549-BRAF fusion:** it can paradoxically switch on MAPK signalling and speed tumour growth. Tovorafenib, or a MEK inhibitor alone, are the alternatives. [S1]
- **Tovorafenib, a CNS-penetrant type II RAF inhibitor, works where type I inhibitors cannot:** in FIREFLY-1 the response rate was 53% and median progression-free survival 13.9 months, leading to FDA accelerated approval in April 2024 for relapsed or refractory RAF-altered pLGG. Trials of it as frontline therapy are ongoing. [S2]
- **MEK inhibitors:** selumetinib gave response rates of 36–40% in recurrent or progressive pLGG, but the phase 3 upfront trials against standard chemotherapy (NCT04166409, NCT03871257) closed early because of poor accrual. Trametinib alone also gives durable control in refractory disease, though toxicity leads some to stop. [S2]
- **NTRK fusions (about 2% of paediatric gliomas) respond to TRK inhibitors:** larotrectinib and entrectinib gave response rates of 46% and 54%, so fusions are worth looking for despite their rarity. [S2]
- **In refractory LGG, also test IDH1, IDH2, FGFR and ALK;** in subependymal giant cell astrocytoma test TSC1 and TSC2 even without clinical tuberous sclerosis, since mTOR inhibition may work. [S1]
- **Other targeted options where drugs can be obtained:** alectinib for ALK-fusion LGG, mTOR inhibitors for TSC or PI3K-AKT-mTOR changes, and bevacizumab for progressive optic pathway glioma after standard chemotherapy has failed. [S1]
- **NF1:** 15-20% of people with NF1 develop gliomas, which respond well overall to MEK inhibitors; a phase III trial is comparing selumetinib with carboplatin/vincristine. Offer genetic testing and counselling for RASopathies as judged clinically. [S1]
- **More from S1's graded LGG table (Table 3):** in refractory or metastatic LGG, also test for CDKN2A mutation or deletion (87% agreement) and NF1 mutation or deletion (80%), both rated as prognostic markers on limited evidence; and for a tumour carrying the KIAA1549-BRAF fusion, tovorafenib is an option to consider (85%). [S1]
- **Molecular classes of pLGG (S2 Table 1):** BRAF fusion (KIAA1549::BRAF), mostly pilocytic astrocytoma of the posterior fossa or optic pathway, indolent, MEK-inhibitor sensitive but resistant to type I BRAF inhibitors, and needing a fusion-sensitive assay; BRAF V600E, supratentorial, with higher progression risk that is worse with CDKN2A loss (IHC is reliable; assess CDKN2A); NF1-driven, optic pathway or brainstem, usually indolent (germline testing important); MYB/MYBL1-altered, excellent prognosis and not MAPK-targetable; IDH-mutant, mostly supratentorial and hemispheric. [S2]
- **Other targeted options and their status (S2 Table 2):** trametinib alone as a second-line option for BRAF-fusion tumours that need treatment (not yet approved first line); repotrectinib after TRK-inhibitor resistance (response 50% in pretreated patients; FDA accelerated approval from 12 years, June 2024); vorasidenib for IDH1/2-mutant tumours after resection or with residual disease (FDA approval from 12 years, August 2024; paediatric data limited). Selumetinib is approved only for NF1 plexiform neurofibroma, so its use in NF1-associated pLGG is off-label. [S2]

## Low-grade glioma: how long to treat, and toxicity

- **The best length of targeted therapy is not known,** and tumours often regrow when it stops. Canadian consensus suggests about 36 months for BRAF V600E pLGG with a gradual taper, usually the MEK inhibitor first and then the BRAF inhibitor, with close MRI surveillance while reducing. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Manage toxicity proactively:** fever is common with dabrafenib-trametinib, skin toxicity with MEK and RAF inhibitors, and tovorafenib often causes low-grade rash and slows growth, which mostly recovers when treatment is interrupted. Long-term growth and cardiovascular effects are still being studied. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Adverse effects by drug (S2 Table 2):** dabrafenib-trametinib, pyrexia (68%), headache, rash, weight gain; trametinib alone, acneiform rash, GI symptoms, raised CPK and rebound on stopping; tovorafenib, rash (93%), hair colour change, fatigue, haemorrhage and slower growth; selumetinib, GI symptoms, raised CPK, rash and paronychia, with cardiac and eye monitoring required; larotrectinib, raised transaminases, anaemia and fatigue; entrectinib, weight gain (35%), pathological fractures, anaemia, dizziness or ataxia and neurocognitive effects; erdafitinib, slipped capital femoral epiphysis, accelerated linear growth and hyperphosphataemia; everolimus, stomatitis, infections and hyperlipidaemia. [S2]

## Low-grade glioma: cancer predisposition

- **Consider germline testing when LGG is diagnosed before age two, is multifocal, comes with a personal or family cancer history or café-au-lait macules, or has an atypical profile such as hypermutation.** Confirm with blood or saliva: tumour-only sequencing misses about 8–10% of germline variants. The result changes care, for example avoiding radiotherapy in Li-Fraumeni syndrome. [S2]
- ⚠️ **NF1 optic pathway glioma: treat for clinical decline, mostly falling vision, not for scan changes alone.** Follow with serial eye assessments, keeping MRI for symptoms or when vision cannot be tested reliably. Avoid radiotherapy whenever possible (moyamoya and second cancers). [S2]
- **Tuberous sclerosis (SEGA): mTOR inhibitors (everolimus, sirolimus) have largely replaced surgery as first-line treatment,** except for acute hydrocephalus or mass effect. Tumours commonly regrow when they stop, so treatment is often prolonged. [S2]

## High-grade glioma

- **H3K27M is the key HGG biomarker,** signalling a poor outlook, especially in midline tumours. IDH mutations are uncommon in children but still worth testing in adolescents, as IDH-mutant tumours could be candidates for vorasidenib. [S1]
- **BRAF V600E is found in about 5-15% of paediatric HGG** and may make the tumour sensitive to BRAF plus MEK inhibition. [S1]
- **Also test MGMT promoter methylation** (less common than in adults; methylated tumours respond better to temozolomide), and in infant hemispheric HGG look for ALK, ROS1, MET and NTRK fusions. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider targeted therapy matched to the alteration:** dabrafenib with trametinib for BRAF V600E, larotrectinib or entrectinib for NTRK fusions, and agents for MET, ALK or FGFR changes. Because the outlook is so poor, this can be considered upfront when the drug is available (trial or compassionate access). [S1]
- **Combining several targeted drugs for multiple alterations has very little supporting evidence;** do it only with great caution, ideally in a trial. [S1]

## Diffuse midline glioma

- **Test DMG only when a biopsy is safe;** no targeted therapy is established, so wider panels mainly refine diagnosis and prognosis and open trial eligibility. H3K27M defines the entity, and loss of H3K27me3 on immunohistochemistry is a useful surrogate. [S1]
