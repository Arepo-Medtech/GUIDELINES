# Glycogen storage disease type I (GSD Ia and Ib)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** glycogen storage disease type I (Ia and Ib): diagnosis, preventing hypoglycaemia (frequent feeds, overnight tube feeds, uncooked cornstarch and modified cornstarch), treating hypoglycaemia, liver adenoma surveillance, kidney protection, anaemia and GSD Ib neutropenia, surgery, contraception and pregnancy. Other GSD types (III, VI, IX) are not covered. Hypoglycaemia in an unwell child: `hypoglycaemia-children`, `metabolic-disorders-children`.

## Recognise and diagnose

- **Low glucose, lactic acidosis, raised cholesterol and triglycerides, and high uric acid together fit GSD I;** neutropenia points to type Ib, though it can occur in Ia and may be absent in Ib in early childhood. [S1]
- **Confirm by sequencing G6PC (Ia) and SLC37A4 (Ib);** liver biopsy is not needed when GSD is suspected, now that gene testing is available. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Undiagnosed or undertreated children can drop below 40 mg/dl after only 3–4 hours of fasting;** prolonged hypoglycaemia risks developmental delay, seizures, brain damage and death. [S1]

## Prevent hypoglycaemia

- ⚠️ **Avoiding fasting is the first-line treatment:** small, frequent feeds rich in complex carbohydrate spread over 24 hours, aiming for blood glucose of at least 70 mg/dl without big swings. [S1]
- **Diet mix:** 60–70% of calories from carbohydrate, 10–15% from protein, and fat below 30% after age 2; sucrose, fructose and lactose are usually limited, so a complete multivitamin with minerals, plus calcium and vitamin D, is essential. [S1]
- **Infants:** sucrose-, fructose- and lactose-free (or soy) formula on demand every 2–3 hours; once sleeping longer, either wake to feed or use overnight gastric feeds. Parents should be trained to pass an NG tube, or a gastrostomy placed, so glucose can always be given. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In GSD Ib with neutropenia, a gastrostomy carries infection risk** and should only be placed if G-CSF is being given. [S1]
- **Overnight tube feeds usually deliver glucose at 8–10 mg glucose/kg/min in infancy and 4–8 in older children;** feed straight after stopping them, and use pump alarms and other safety measures because pump failure has caused deaths. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Raw uncooked cornstarch is usually trialled between 6 months and 1 year;** a guide is 1.6 g/kg every 3–4 hours in young children and 1.7–2.5 g/kg every 4–5 hours (sometimes 6) in older children and adults. Increase from a small starting dose for tolerance. [S1]
- **Modified cornstarch (Glycosade) can be used overnight,** but only with close monitoring of metabolic control; any change of cornstarch brand or dose should be supervised by the metabolic team with frequent glucose checks. Do not mix cornstarch with hot water, lemonade or high-dose vitamin C. [S1]
- **Adolescents and adults:** avoid fasting beyond 5–6 hours with cornstarch and/or overnight feeds, and keep checking glucose and lactate even when feeling well. Overtreatment is harmful too, causing insulin resistance. [S1]

## Treat hypoglycaemia and illness

- **Treat glucose below 60 mg/dl with fast-acting glucose, then a snack or cornstarch to hold it.** Patients may feel nothing until levels are very low. Everyone should wear a medical alert bracelet. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Surgery:** admit 24 h beforehand for IV D10; **never use lactated Ringer's** (worsens lactic acidosis); do not stop IV glucose abruptly, only once the patient is eating and holding glucose. [S1]

## Organ surveillance

- **Liver:** image the abdomen at baseline and every 12–24 months (ultrasound in children), moving to CT/MRI with contrast every 6–12 months once adenomas appear. A rapid rise in adenoma size or number raises concern for cancer; alpha-fetoprotein does not predict it. [S1]
- **Kidney:** at routine visits check renal ultrasound, urinalysis, urine albumin, citrate and calcium, and electrolytes with eGFR. Microalbuminuria that persists (>30 μg albumin /mg creatinine) or overt proteinuria calls for ACE-inhibitor or ARB treatment; consider it for hyperfiltration too (eGFR >140). Give citrate for low urinary citrate. [S1]
- **Blood:** investigate anaemia (diet, adenomas, enterocolitis, menorrhagia, occult loss). In GSD Ib, treat neutropenia with the lowest effective G-CSF dose, beginning with 0.5–1.0 µg/kg/day by subcutaneous injection, especially with recurrent fever, infections or enterocolitis. [S1]
- **Heart and general care:** screen blood pressure from infancy, keep lipids normal, and screen for pulmonary hypertension by echocardiography from age 10 years, every 3 years. Avoid drugs that cause hypoglycaemia, and contact or competitive sports. [S1]
- **Contraception and pregnancy:** avoid oestrogen contraceptives (adenoma risk) and, in GSD Ib, an IUD; plan pregnancies, stop ACE inhibitors, allopurinol and lipid-lowering drugs, and have a high-risk obstetric team manage them. [S1]
