# Nontuberculous mycobacterial infection — focus on *Mycobacterium avium* complex

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis and drug treatment of *M. avium* complex (MAC) pulmonary disease, cervical lymphadenitis in children, and disseminated MAC and MAC prophylaxis in people with HIV, with the general NTM treatment principles that apply to MAC. Other NTM (*M. abscessus*, *M. kansasii*, *M. marinum*, rapid growers) are mentioned only where they change a principle; *M. ulcerans* (Buruli ulcer) is not covered. Tuberculosis: see `tuberculosis-bcg`. Related: `bronchiectasis`, `cystic-fibrosis`, `hiv-antiretroviral-therapy`.

## Recognise

- **MAC is the NTM that matters most:** it accounts for about 80% of NTM notifications in the Northern Territory, and lung disease makes up as much as 90% of NTM disease overall. [S1]
- **A single positive culture is not a diagnosis:** NTM live in the environment and can be contaminants. There is no evidence of spread between people or from animals to people. [S1]
- **Impaired cell-mediated immunity raises the risk** — genetic defects, acquired immunosuppression, oral steroids of 15 mg or more for 14 days or longer, TNF-alpha inhibitors and transplant immunosuppression. [S1]
- **Two common lung patterns.** Without prior lung disease it typically affects non-smoking women over 50 (often slender with scoliosis, pectus excavatum or mitral prolapse), showing mid-zone nodules with cylindrical bronchiectasis best seen on HRCT, and bacilli are shed only intermittently. [S1]
- **With existing lung disease** (COPD, bronchiectasis, pneumoconiosis, cystic fibrosis, old TB), symptoms blur into the underlying illness, and imaging can look like TB with upper-lobe infiltrates and thin-walled cavities. [S1]

## Diagnose pulmonary disease

- ⚠️ **All three criteria — clinical, radiological and microbiological — must be met** before calling it NTM lung disease, because respiratory isolates may just be colonisation or contamination. [S1]
- **Microbiology criterion:** one NTM species cultured from 2 or more sputum samples taken a week or more apart over no more than 12 months, or from 1 or more bronchial washes or lavages (or compatible biopsy or pleural fluid findings). [S1]
- **Confirmation can take months and the decision to treat is a judgement** of individual risk and benefit; people who do not yet meet the criteria should be followed up until the diagnosis is made or excluded. [S1]

## Before treating

- **Treatment decisions belong with experienced clinicians,** and treatment should be started by a specialist (such as infectious diseases) and co-managed with the TB/NTM unit. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Never treat active disease with one drug** — resistance follows; single-drug use is reserved for defined prophylaxis. Test MAC for clarithromycin susceptibility before starting. [S1]
- **Baseline work-up:** weight; blood count, liver and kidney function, glucose or HbA1c and uric acid; HIV, HTLV1 (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander patients) and hepatitis screens; visual acuity and colour vision if using ethambutol; ECG for QTc with a macrolide; contraception and drug-interaction review. [S1]

## Treat MAC lung disease

- **Mild to moderate nodular/bronchiectatic MAC — three times a week:** clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily or azithromycin 500 mg, **plus** ethambutol 25 mg/kg, **plus** rifampicin 600 mg (450 mg if under 50 kg). [S1]
- **Severe nodular/bronchiectatic or fibrocavitary MAC — daily:** clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily (250 mg if under 50 kg or over 70 years) or azithromycin 250 mg daily, **plus** ethambutol 15 mg/kg daily, **plus** rifampicin 600 mg or rifabutin 300 mg daily. [S1]
- **Severe disease or a poor response:** consider adding an aminoglycoside (amikacin, or streptomycin 25 mg/kg IM 3 times a week) and adjuvant interferon; resection of localised disease or a solitary nodule is another option. [S1]
- **Duration: 12 months after sputum cultures turn negative.** Most responders improve in the first 3 to 6 months. [S1]
- **Treatment failure** means no clinical, radiological or microbiological response by 6 months, or sputum still growing the same organism after 12 months. Not everyone responds and relapse is common; induced sputum or bronchoscopy should not be used to prove culture conversion. [S1]
- **Review monthly** (symptoms, side effects, adherence, weight, sputum, blood count and liver tests, and visual acuity with colour vision on ethambutol), with a chest X-ray every 3 months and follow-up at 6 and 18 months after treatment ends. [S1]

## Children: MAC lymphadenitis

- **Cervical NTM lymphadenitis mostly affects children under 5:** a firm, non-tender, slowly enlarging node on one side, usually MAC. Excision without antibiotics is the treatment of choice. In the NT, TB lymphadenitis is more common than NTM in Aboriginal children. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not incise and drain** (sinus tracts, poor cosmetic result). Send biopsy or aspirate in saline, never formalin, or culture is lost. If excision would endanger the facial nerve, a clarithromycin-based multidrug regimen can be considered with an infectious diseases physician or paediatrician. [S1]

## People with HIV: disseminated MAC

- **Disseminated MAC is the main NTM threat in HIV, with risk climbing once CD4 falls below 50/µL.** Symptoms are non-specific (weight loss, fever, sweats at night, diarrhoea, abdominal pain); it is confirmed by culture of blood, bone marrow or tissue. Starting ART can unmask it as localised lymphadenitis. Management belongs with HIV and infectious diseases specialists. [S1]
- **Treatment:** clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily (the preferred macrolide) or azithromycin 600 mg daily, **plus** ethambutol 15 mg/kg daily, with or without rifabutin 300 mg daily. The right duration is not settled; at least 12 months of therapy and six months of immune reconstitution are reasonable markers. [S1]
- **Add amikacin or streptomycin** when immunosuppression is advanced (CD4 50 cells/mm³ or less), the mycobacterial load is high, or effective ART is absent. In people not yet on ART, wait until 2 weeks of MAC treatment are done before starting it. [S1]
- **Primary prophylaxis once CD4 is under 50/µL:** azithromycin 1.2 g once a week or clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily, stopped 3 months after CD4 rises above 100/µL. [S1]

## Prevent hospital-acquired NTM

- **Keep tap water away from invasive devices and specimens:** protect IV and peritoneal catheters from tap water, do not wash fibre-optic endoscopes in it, and do not let patients rinse their mouth with it before giving sputum. NTM disease is notifiable in the NT (since 1999). [S1]
