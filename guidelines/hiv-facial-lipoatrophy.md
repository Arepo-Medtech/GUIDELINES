# Facial lipoatrophy (mainly HIV-associated): recognition and correction

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognising and grading acquired facial lipoatrophy, above all the form caused by older antiretroviral drugs; prevention by drug choice; and correction with fat transfer or injectable fillers, including poly-L-lactic acid (the PBS item). Choosing and switching antiretroviral therapy: see `hiv-antiretroviral-therapy`. HIV diagnosis and first care: see `hiv`. Facial lipoatrophy from lupus panniculitis or morphea is mentioned only in passing. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise

- **Facial lipoatrophy is loss of fat that flattens or hollows the normally convex contours of the face.** [S1]
- **Fat is lost mostly from the cheeks and temples and from around the ears, eyes and mouth.** [S1]
- **Grade severity clinically on a 5-point scale:** the first degree is subtle flattening of one or more regions with no visible bone or muscle, and the fifth is deep hollowing with strongly prominent facial bones and muscles. [S1]
- **CT, MRI and ultrasound are not used to measure severity,** because of cost and the absence of agreed standards for reading the images. [S1]
- **Unlike the slow, symmetrical fat loss of ageing, pathological lipoatrophy progresses quickly, can be asymmetrical, and often causes body-image distress and social withdrawal.** [S1]
- **HIV-associated facial lipoatrophy was first reported in 1998, in people on combination antiretroviral therapy (HAART).** [S1]
- **Tell it apart from late-stage AIDS wasting:** lipodystrophy comes with its typical fat pattern, metabolic abnormalities and long ART exposure, whereas cachexia is mainly loss of muscle. [S1]
- ⚠️ **The stigma is real and can undermine treatment:** in one survey 84% of affected patients thought others could identify them as HIV-positive from their face; isolation can lead to depression and to refusing ART. [S1]

## Prevent and manage the cause

- **Changing antiretrovirals once lipoatrophy has developed seldom brings visible improvement.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Prevention is by drug choice:** avoid the thymidine analogues stavudine (d4T) and zidovudine (AZT), which cause the most fat loss, especially when starting treatment; tenofovir and abacavir cause much less. [S1]
- **Address the metabolic side too:** advise a healthy diet, no smoking and exercise, and add a statin or fibrate if lipids stay well above target, checking interactions (especially with protease inhibitors). [S1]

## Correct the facial fat loss

- **Restoring facial volume matters because it cuts stigma and improves quality of life,** but data on the safety and effectiveness of each method are sparse. [S1]
- **Autologous fat transfer is less reliable in HIV:** abnormal fat metabolism can make the graft vanish quickly or overgrow, results are unpredictable, and many patients lack enough donor fat. [S1]
- **Poly-L-lactic acid and calcium hydroxyapatite are the fillers the US FDA specifically approved for HIV-associated lipoatrophy (in 2004 and 2006);** other fillers are off-label for deep volume restoration. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Poly-L-lactic acid is a collagen stimulator, not a volume filler:** it provokes a fibroblast response that thickens the skin, lasts up to 2 years as it is reabsorbed, and needs a course of several injection sessions for moderate wasting. [S1]
- **Calcium hydroxyapatite gives an immediate result lasting up to 18 months; hyaluronic acid has given good results in HIV lipoatrophy lasting at least 6 months** with high satisfaction and no serious adverse events. [S1]
- **Cost is a major barrier:** fillers are absorbed, so repeated sessions are needed to keep the result, and only a few countries fund them for antiretroviral-related lipodystrophy. [S1]
