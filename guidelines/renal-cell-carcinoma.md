# Renal cell carcinoma — advanced clear cell disease: first-line ipilimumab and nivolumab

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** first-line ipilimumab plus nivolumab, followed by nivolumab maintenance, for adults with advanced or metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma at intermediate or poor IMDC risk: eligibility, dosing, response assessment, immune-related adverse events, monitoring and the CheckMate 214 evidence, including the favourable-risk comparison with sunitinib, from **one eviQ protocol page (ID 3561, S1)**, because eviQ publishes regimens, not a treatment-sequence overview. **S2 (SITC 2026)** adds the choice between the four first-line immunotherapy combinations, treatment duration, local therapy, later lines, adjuvant pembrolizumab eligibility and special populations, without doses. **Not covered:** dosing and toxicity of the VEGF tyrosine kinase inhibitors (sunitinib, pazopanib, axitinib, cabozantinib, lenvatinib), everolimus and belzutifan. Recognising immune-related toxicity in general practice: see `anticancer-drugs-general-principles`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 29 claims; **51 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 2 doses.** **S1** eviQ Cancer Treatments Online (ID 3561 v.10; first approved 28 March 2019, last reviewed 30 June 2025, review due 30 June 2029; AU): 19 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored · **S2** Society for Immunotherapy of Cancer (SITC) expert panel (published 23 March 2026; international): 10 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ **S1 (eviQ 3561) covers one regimen, not the whole disease; S2 (SITC 2026) adds the first-line choice, later lines and adjuvant therapy, but no doses.** The PBS items listed below for these conditions are mostly tyrosine kinase inhibitors, which have their own eviQ protocols; eviQ lists ipilimumab and nivolumab as PBS authority items for this indication.

> ⚠️ **Intermediate or poor risk only.** In CheckMate 214, favourable-risk patients did better on sunitinib for progression-free survival and response.

> ⚠️ **Favourable risk is where the sources differ:** S1 restricts this regimen to intermediate or poor risk; S2, with 6-year CheckMate 214 data, would offer any immunotherapy combination whatever the IMDC risk group. Both are shown.

## Who it is for

- **Ipilimumab with nivolumab is a first-line option for advanced unresectable or metastatic clear cell RCC of intermediate or poor risk**, in patients with ECOG 0 to 1. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Discuss with an expert and use clinical judgement** if there is significant autoimmune disease, an organ transplant, past viral hepatitis, previous lung radiotherapy, abnormal baseline LFTs or brain metastases. Radiation necrosis has been reported after ipilimumab combined with stereotactic treatment. [S1]
- **Only specialists experienced with immunotherapy should start and supervise treatment,** and only if they are confident they can manage immune-related adverse events. [S1]
- **Special populations (SITC):** with significant autoimmune disease or a solid organ transplant, hold off checkpoint inhibitors at first and ask a specialist. HIV and older age are not contraindications; a geriatric assessment can help judge whether treatment will be tolerated. [S2]

## Choosing first-line therapy (SITC)

- **An immunotherapy-based first line is recommended for anyone fit for systemic therapy.** Four combinations have improved overall survival: nivolumab with ipilimumab, pembrolizumab with axitinib, nivolumab with cabozantinib, and pembrolizumab with lenvatinib. No trial compares them head to head, so any of them may be chosen. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Sarcomatoid features: ipilimumab with nivolumab is the preferred choice.** In CheckMate 214 these patients had not reached median overall survival at the 42-month analysis, against 14.2 months on sunitinib (HR 0.45). [S2]
- **Favourable risk, with longer follow-up:** by the 6-year analysis, overall survival in favourable-risk patients had shifted towards the combination (median 79 against 68 months, HR 0.87). SITC judges IMDC risk nearly useless for predicting immunotherapy response, so any approved combination may be offered whatever the risk group. [S2]
- **Small, slow-growing, symptom-free metastatic disease may be watched** or treated with metastasis-directed therapy. Whether to remove or irradiate the kidney primary, now or later, is still under study and is an MDT decision. [S2]

## Regimen

- **Induction: nivolumab 3 mg/kg IV, then ipilimumab 1 mg/kg IV 30 minutes later, on day 1 of a 21-day cycle for 4 cycles.** Then continue with nivolumab alone until progression or unacceptable toxicity. [S1]
- **Nivolumab maintenance uses flat dosing every 2 or 4 weeks** (weight-based dosing is superseded). Start 3 weeks after the last induction dose for 2-weekly dosing, or 6 weeks after for 4-weekly dosing. [S1]
- **How long (SITC):** the best evidence supports 2 years of checkpoint inhibitor treatment; carrying on past 2 years can be tailored to the individual. [S2]
- **Assess response at 12 weeks,** when induction ends. About 5% of patients have pseudoprogression (early growth or new lesions before later shrinkage); if the patient is well, carrying on and rescanning 4 to 6 weeks later can be considered. [S1]
- **Doses are never reduced.** If either drug is withheld, withhold both; whether to restart the combination or nivolumab alone is a matter for clinical judgement or expert advice. [S1]
- **Progression on scans but clinically well (SITC):** immunotherapy may continue with close monitoring if the patient is stable and tolerating it. Reported rates of pseudoprogression or atypical response vary from 1.8% to 14%. [S2]
- **Oligometastatic or oligoprogressive disease:** consider local treatment, with stereotactic radiotherapy for oligoprogression; brain metastases should get local therapy wherever feasible. None of this requires stopping immunotherapy. [S2]

## Later lines and adjuvant therapy (SITC)

- **After checkpoint inhibitor failure:** a TKI-based regimen, or belzutifan once a TKI has been used; if the first line paired immunotherapy with a TKI, switch to a different TKI. Moving to another anti-PD-(L)1 drug has no proven role. [S2]
- **Adjuvant pembrolizumab after nephrectomy** should be considered for pT2 with grade 4 or sarcomatoid disease, pT3 or higher, node-positive disease, or M1 with no evidence of disease. Image within 3 months beforehand, start within 3–4 months of surgery and treat for 1 year. Non-clear cell RCC has no standard adjuvant treatment. [S2]

## Immune-related adverse events

- ⚠️ **Immune-related adverse events can begin early, escalate fast and appear even after treatment stops.** They are more frequent and earlier with the combination than with nivolumab alone, and deaths have occurred. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Recognise the high-mortality irAEs:** myocarditis, HLH-like syndrome, myositis, encephalitis, Guillain-Barré syndrome, myelitis, myasthenia gravis, pneumonitis, and Stevens-Johnson syndrome or TEN. Triple M syndrome (myocarditis, myositis and myasthenia gravis together) is rare but can kill. [S1]
- **The common irAEs** are thyroid dysfunction, enterocolitis, joint and muscle pain, and rash or itch. Less common are hypophysitis, type 1 diabetes, inflammatory arthritis, dry eye, kidney toxicity and vitiligo. Endocrine damage may be permanent and need long-term replacement. [S1]
- **Symptoms should settle quickly once immunosuppression starts;** if they do not, reconsider the diagnosis and get specialist advice. [S1]

## Monitoring

- **Bloods:** at baseline, FBC, EUC/eGFR, LFTs, cortisol, thyroid function and glucose. Before each cycle, FBC, EUC/eGFR, LFTs and glucose. Cortisol and TFTs every 4 to 6 weeks on treatment and every 12 weeks after it ends; lipase and amylase if pancreatitis is suspected. [S1]
- **Other baseline checks:** consider ECG and troponin (their value is unproven; refer high-risk people to cardiology) and urinalysis where kidney risk factors exist. Screen everyone for hepatitis; chronic hepatitis B or C or detectable HIV needs close monitoring and antiviral treatment where indicated. [S1]
- **Vaccines:** live vaccines can generally be given if the person is only mildly immunocompromised, and non-live vaccines are safe but may work less well. Immunisation during checkpoint inhibitor therapy carries a theoretical risk of immune-related events. [S1]
- **Fertility effects are unknown,** although hypogonadism from orchitis or hypophysitis may affect it. Consider a pregnancy test and advise effective contraception during and after treatment. [S1]

## Evidence

- **CheckMate 214** enrolled 1096 previously untreated patients with advanced clear-cell RCC. Nivolumab plus ipilimumab (4 induction doses, then nivolumab 3 mg/kg every 2 weeks) was compared with sunitinib 50 mg daily for 4 weeks of every 6, and was superior on all three co-primary endpoints (overall survival, response and PFS) in intermediate- or poor-risk patients. [S1]
- **Responses were deeper and lasted longer with the combination:** complete responses in 11.6% against 3.1% with sunitinib, and 63% against 50.3% of responses still ongoing (whole trial population). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Favourable-risk disease did better on sunitinib** in an exploratory analysis: median PFS 28.9 against 12.4 months (HR 1.6) and response 51.6% against 29.6%, although complete responses were commoner with the combination (12.8% against 6.5%). eviQ says longer follow-up is needed before firm conclusions. [S1]
- **Toxicity:** grade 3 or 4 treatment-related events in 48% with the combination against 64% with sunitinib. Severe immune toxicity clusters in or soon after the four-cycle induction, while sunitinib toxicity builds up over time, especially vascular events. [S1]
