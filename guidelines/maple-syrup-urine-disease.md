# Maple syrup urine disease (MSUD): crisis, sick days and lifelong diet

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** suspecting and confirming MSUD after newborn screening or a clinical presentation, hospital management of a metabolic crisis (fluids, BCAA-free amino acids with isoleucine and valine, dialysis, cerebral oedema), sick-day management at home, plasma BCAA targets and monitoring, and the BCAA-restricted diet from infancy. First steps for any suspected inborn error in an unwell child: `metabolic-disorders-children`. Other protein-restricted diets: `phenylketonuria`, `organic-acidaemias`.

> The PBS `Maple syrup urine disease` row covers the **leucine-, isoleucine- and valine-free amino acid formulas** and the **isoleucine and valine supplements** this guideline relies on.

## Recognise and diagnose

- **Classic MSUD presents in the first weeks of life** after feeds begin: poor feeding, vomiting, drowsiness, then lethargy and falling consciousness; coma and cerebral oedema follow. Variant forms present later, triggered by illness, fasting or surgery. [S1]
- **Suspect MSUD** with an abnormal newborn screen, or with refractory hypoglycaemia, seizures, hypertonia, 'boxing and bicycling' movements or a maple-syrup odour. [S1]
- **Positive screen (leucine + isoleucine > 250 nmol/L, or ratio > 3): confirm with plasma amino acids and alloisoleucine; if BCAAs are clearly high and/or alloisoleucine > 2 nmol/ml, treat immediately.** Never give a protein challenge to make the diagnosis. [S1]
- **Molecular genetic testing is the preferred confirmation, but do not delay treatment waiting for it.** [S1]

## Acute metabolic crisis (hospital)

- ⚠️ **Aim to lower leucine by 500 to 1000 µmoles/L/day;** symptomatic patients should preferably go to PICU. [S1]
- **Stop natural protein for 24 to 48 h (up to 72 h in adults);** give 1.25–1.5 times maintenance IV fluid as 10–12.5% dextrose in normal saline, with Intralipid 1–2 g/kg/day for extra calories. [S1]
- **Check glucose every 4 to 6 h, keeping it between 100 and 160 mg/dl;** insulin infusion if hyperglycaemic. Keep sodium between 138 and 145 mEq/L and avoid osmolality falling more than 5 mOsm/kg H2O per day. [S1]
- **Give BCAA-free amino acids (2–3.5 g/ kg /day if IV is available) with isoleucine and valine supplements;** once oral feeding is possible, run BCAA-free formula continuously by NG tube. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider dialysis for leucine above 1100 µmole/L and/or rapidly progressing neurological signs;** short-course continuous haemodialysis beats peritoneal dialysis. [S1]
- **Treat triggers** (infection, trauma, dehydration) with a low threshold for empiric antibiotics. Thiamine-responsive patients take 50–200 mg daily, IV if oral is not tolerated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Cerebral oedema needs ICU care;** options include mannitol, hypertonic (3%) saline and furosemide, holding sodium at 138 to 145 mEq/L. [S1]

## Sick days at home

- **Start the sick-day protocol at the first symptoms;** home care only suits fever under 38.5 °C, tolerated oral or NG feeds, no frequent vomiting or severe diarrhoea, and no neurological signs. Otherwise admit. [S1]
- **At home:** cut leucine by 50–100%, reduce or stop natural protein, raise BCAA-free formula to 120% of usual, give extra isoleucine and valine (20–40 mg/kg/day each), frequent carbohydrate drinks, and reassess every two hours. [S1]

## Targets, monitoring and diet

- ⚠️ **Plasma targets:** leucine 75–200 µmol/L up to 5 years and 75–300 µmol/L above 5 years; valine and isoleucine 200 to 400 µmol/L. The diet is lifelong. [S1]
- **When stable, check BCAAs every 1 to 2 weeks until 5 years, every 2 to 4 weeks until 12 years, then monthly;** give iron, zinc, calcium, selenium, vitamin A and D supplements. [S1]
- **New infant:** admit to start treatment; stop breast milk and formula briefly, give 3 g/kg/day BCAA-free protein equivalent in a high-energy feed, then reintroduce leucine from formula or expressed breast milk once plasma leucine is < 800 µmol/L. Breastfeeding on demand is possible after a measured BCAA-free feed. [S1]
- **Non-classic forms need less:** intermediate MSUD needs leucine restriction ± BCAA-free amino acids and an emergency regimen; intermittent MSUD may need only moderate protein restriction; thiamine-responsive patients also take thiamine. [S1]
- **Liver transplant** allows a normal diet but is reserved for patients uncontrolled on diet with multiple attacks. [S1]
