# Acromegaly — treatment goals, medical therapy and monitoring

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** biochemical, imaging and clinical treatment goals after surgery, and the choice, sequence and monitoring of somatostatin receptor ligands, pegvisomant, cabergoline and combinations for adults with acromegaly. Diagnosis (OGTT GH suppression testing) and acromegaly complications are outside this source's scope. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`. Edition 2.0 adds diagnosis (IGF-I, OGTT, pituitary MRI, earlier detection) and remission and follow-up criteria from S2.

> ⚠️ International guideline (Acromegaly Consensus Group, 2025). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

## Diagnose (S2)

- **Diagnosis: in someone with typical features, an IGF-I above 1.3 times the upper limit of normal for age confirms acromegaly.** A fasting GH is not needed for the diagnosis. If results are equivocal, repeat IGF-I on the same validated assay; an OGTT may help. (MQ / SR / DR) [S2]
- **Reserve the OGTT for when baseline hormones leave the diagnosis unclear.** Give 75 g glucose after fasting and measure GH at 30, 60, 90 and 120 min. BMI-based nadir cut-offs can be considered: below 0.4 µg/L if BMI is under 25, below 0.2 µg/L if BMI is 25 or more (assay dependent). Stopping oral oestrogen 4 weeks before may avoid a falsely high nadir. (SR / DR) [S2]
- **Know the assay:** reference ranges differ between IGF-I immunoassays, and a high BMI lowers IGF-I for age. Use the same well-validated IGF-I assay for every assessment, and interpret new rises allowing for pregnancy, oestrogen, starvation and metabolic change. (MQ / SR) [S2]
- ⚠️ **Measure IGF-I in every newly found pituitary mass,** and consider it for people with classic features (acral enlargement, facial and bite changes), especially with unexplained sleep apnoea or ventricular hypertrophy. Diagnosis is typically delayed 5–10 years or more, which raises comorbidity and mortality and lets the adenoma invade. (SR / DR / HQ) [S2]
- **Pituitary MRI at diagnosis for everyone:** gadolinium-enhanced, high resolution (1.5T or 3T), T1 and T2 sequences, 2–3 mm slices, reporting invasion by modified Knosp grade. Refer an equivocal microadenoma to an experienced neuroradiologist. (SR) [S2]
- **Consider referral to a multidisciplinary pituitary centre** when biochemistry, pathology or imaging is equivocal at diagnosis, or when the response to standard treatment is insufficient. (S2 Table 2) [S2]
- **The OGTT is safe with impaired glucose tolerance or type 2 diabetes;** in uncontrolled diabetes, read both random and post-OGTT GH with caution. (S2 Table 2) [S2]

## Goals of treatment

- **Surgery (transsphenoidal adenoma resection) is the preferred first-line treatment**, controlling the disease in roughly 50% of patients in referral centres; about half of those who then need medical therapy reach IGF1 control. [S1]
- **The biochemical target of medical therapy is a normal IGF1 for age**, using the reference range for the specific assay (and a sex-specific range in puberty). (SR) [S1]
- **After surgery, remission is shown by a GH nadir below 1 ng/ml on OGTT**, or below 0.4 ng/ml with modern ultrasensitive assays. Check basal and OGTT GH and IGF1 at 3 months after surgery. (DR / SR) [S1]
- **Even when controlled, keep checking GH/IGF1 for 5 years or more after surgery**, because residual tumour or relapse can occur. In year one, IGF1 every 3–6 months, then every 6–12 months. [S1]
- **Manage comorbidities regardless of biochemical control:** hypertension, diabetes, osteoarthrosis and sleep apnoea often persist in a sizeable share of controlled patients. [S1]
- **S2 defines surgical success as a normal IGF-I 12 weeks after surgery,** with early random GH (days 1–14) against the pre-operative level as a guide to how much adenoma was removed. If SRL was given before surgery, repeat at 3–6 months to confirm remission. In clinically controlled patients, an IGF-I in the mid to upper half of the age range may avoid inducing GH deficiency. (SR / DR / HQ) [S2]

## First-line medical therapy

- **Somatostatin receptor ligands (SRLs: octreotide, lanreotide) are the first-line medical option**, mostly as adjuvant treatment for active disease after surgery; primary SRL therapy suits selected patients who decline or cannot have surgery. (SR) [S1]
- **Cabergoline alone is only for mild disease** (IGF1 below 2.0–2.5 × ULN) or mixed GH–prolactin adenomas; watch for valvulopathy and impulse-control disorders at very high long-term doses. (DR) [S1]
- **Pegvisomant alone can be first line** when glucose metabolism is badly impaired and the tumour mass is not a concern, especially where poor SRL response is expected (T2-hyperintense adenoma, very high IGF1). [S1]
- **Check IGF1 after the first three monthly SRL injections**, ideally just before the next dose, then titrate dose or interval to IGF1. For oral octreotide capsules, check IGF1 after 2–4 weeks to titrate. (SR / DR) [S1]
- **Surgery, not medical therapy, when there is visual loss with optic chiasm compression.** (SR) [S1]

## Second- and third-line therapy

- **Switch drug class when control is not reached at maximum tolerated doses, or with adverse events;** if one drug fails, a two-drug combination of different classes is suggested. (SR) [S1]
- **When a single SRL is not enough, low-dose SRL plus weekly pegvisomant is both highly effective and more cost-effective** than higher doses or more frequent dosing of either drug (S2). Pasireotide may shrink residual adenoma more than octreotide or lanreotide. (MQ / DR) [S2]
- **Higher-dose SRL regimens (off label) can help SRL-sensitive patients:** lanreotide 180 mg every 28 days or 120 mg every 21 days, or octreotide LAR 60 mg every 28 days or 30 mg every 21 days. [S1]
- **Pegvisomant monotherapy may be the preferred second line with pre-existing hyperglycaemia or diabetes;** SRL plus pegvisomant suits partial SRL responders, and controls IGF1 in 80–96%. (DR) [S1]
- **Pasireotide LAR is an option for inadequately controlled disease**, especially with a relevant or growing adenoma and low hyperglycaemia risk; over half of patients on it develop hyperglycaemia or diabetes, so check fasting glucose and HbA1c routinely on any SRL. (SR) [S1]
- **If medical therapy fails, consider stereotactic radiosurgery or repeat surgery;** reserve temozolomide and other chemotherapy for aggressive or malignant tumours, under neuro-oncology. [S1]

## Monitoring on treatment

- **Pegvisomant: test liver function before starting and during titration, and stop it if transaminases exceed five times the upper limit of normal.** Rotate injection sites. Diabetes (especially on insulin) and obesity raise the dose needed. (SR) [S1]
- **GH levels are not informative on a GH receptor antagonist** (pegvisomant): follow IGF1 instead. (SR) [S1]
- **Pegvisomant and cabergoline: check IGF-I every 1–3 months after starting or changing the dose, then every 6–12 months;** do not measure GH on either. Injectable SRL: IGF-I 3 months after a start or dose change, then every 6–12 months once controlled. (SR) [S2]
- **After radiotherapy, medical therapy bridges until the radiation works;** once IGF-I is stable in range, pause treatment at least once a year to test for radiation-induced remission. (SR / DR) [S2]
- **MRI 6 months after starting or switching medical therapy;** otherwise routine MRI is not needed on an SRL with controlled hormones, but image promptly for visual or eye-movement symptoms. Rapid growth or a rising largest diameter means resistance and a change of strategy. (SR) [S1]
- **S2's MRI schedule:** MRI 3–6 months after surgery as the baseline; then only for biochemical or clinical progression or when a change of modality is considered (such as before repeat surgery or radiotherapy). Regular MRI is not needed for everyone. (SR) [S2]
- **The SAGIT and ACRODAT scoring tools may help track disease severity and progression over time;** a prospective study of ACRODAT's clinical benefit as a monitoring tool is under way. (S2 Table 2) [S2]
- **No routine gallbladder ultrasound or ECG is needed on SRLs,** but take care with other QT-prolonging drugs. (DR) [S1]
- **Cancer screening:** there was no consensus on colonoscopy for everyone at diagnosis; UK gastroenterology guidance suggests regular screening from age 40. Screen for other cancers, including thyroid, as for the general population. (MQ) [S2]
