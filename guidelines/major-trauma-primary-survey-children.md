# Major trauma in children: the primary survey

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** the ED primary survey of a child with major trauma: pre-arrival preparation, catastrophic haemorrhage, airway with c-spine control, breathing, circulation and fluids, disability and exposure. ## Before and on arrival

- **Before arrival:** activate the trauma team, brief the team on the anticipated injuries and priorities, **estimate the child's weight** and prepare age- and weight-appropriate doses and equipment. [S1]
- **Take a 'hands-off' ambulance handover** unless a life threat needs immediate action, then perform the primary survey. A dedicated staff member should support the family. [S1]
- **Priorities, assessed and managed in parallel:** catastrophic haemorrhage · airway with c-spine control · breathing · circulation · disability · exposure/environment. [S1]

## Catastrophic haemorrhage

- ⚠️ **Controlling exsanguinating external haemorrhage comes first.** Apply direct pressure with a gauze pad; **use a tourniquet for life-threatening limb haemorrhage.** [S1]
- **Leave bandages that control significant bleeding in place** until IV access is secured and the child has stabilised. Don't use layers of gauze that absorb and hide blood loss. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Suspected pelvic fracture with haemodynamic compromise → pelvic binder**; activate the massive transfusion protocol if appropriate. [S1]

## Airway and cervical spine

- **Assess the airway, anterior neck and conscious level (AVPU)**; look for facial fractures and blood, vomit or loose teeth in the airway. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Burns — look for airway involvement:** singed facial or nasal hair, facial burns, hoarse voice, harsh cough, soot in the mouth, nose or saliva, swelling of the head or neck. [S1]
- **Use an age-appropriate neutral head position** — under 8 years, a thoracic elevation device or a towel under the shoulder blades. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Every spontaneously breathing patient gets high-flow oxygen** (typically by non-rebreather mask at 10–15 L), whatever the need for airway support. [S1]
- **Airway support:** jaw thrust (no head-tilt or chin lift), gentle suction, an oropharyngeal airway if tolerated or a nasopharyngeal airway if base-of-skull fracture is unlikely, and endotracheal intubation. [S1]
- **Protect the cervical spine: manual in-line stabilisation, maintained until a soft foam collar is on.** [S1]

## Breathing

- **Breathing life threats to find and treat:** tension or open pneumothorax, flail chest, massive haemothorax, tracheo-bronchial injury. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tension pneumothorax: decompress the chest — finger or instrument, thoracostomy, or intercostal catheter.** In an emergency, needle decompression is an option, though it is unlikely to succeed. [S1]
- **Open pneumothorax:** close it immediately and insert a chest drain. **Massive haemothorax:** blood transfusion plus a chest drain. **Flail chest:** analgesia; positive pressure ventilation may be considered. [S1]
- **Ask for a chest X-ray done portably.** In intubated children, **insert an orogastric tube** so gastric dilatation doesn't impair ventilation. [S1]

## Circulation

- **Hunt for bleeding:** external (don't remove penetrating foreign bodies), intra-thoracic, intra-abdominal, intra-pelvic, long bones (particularly femoral), retroperitoneal, and scalp or intracranial in infants. [S1]
- **In paediatric trauma, eFAST has only a limited role.** Actively exclude obstructive shock (tension pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade) and neurogenic shock (spinal injury above T6). [S1]
- **Two IV cannulae, the largest practicable, ideally one per cubital fossa**; send cross-match, FBE, VBG, LFTs, UEC, lipase and coagulation screen. **If IV access isn't rapid, go IO** into a non-injured leg or the humerus. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Inadequate circulation: sodium chloride 0.9% bolus, 10 mL/kg.** Ongoing visible or suspected occult bleeding: **packed red cells 10 mL/kg** if available. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Haemodynamic compromise from suspected haemorrhage → tranexamic acid 15 mg/kg.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Life-threatening abdominal, pelvic or retroperitoneal bleeding may need surgery or interventional radiology — consult early.** Uncontrollable bleeding may need theatre before the secondary survey is finished; a chest drain in massive haemothorax may help ventilation but only theatre stops the bleeding. [S1]

## Disability and exposure

- **Any impairment on AVPU → formal GCS.** Check pupils, movement in all four limbs, and **blood glucose on arrival.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Prevent secondary brain injury:** keep oxygenation adequate, correct hypotension and hypoglycaemia, nurse head-up 30 degrees. Consider urgent CT brain and neurosurgical discussion. [S1]
- **Expose to find life-threatening injuries, but prevent hypothermia** — limit exposure and warm all ongoing fluids. [S1]

## Transfer

- ⚠️ **Every child with major trauma needs transfer to a major trauma service** for definitive care, in line with local guidelines for pre-hospital and inter-hospital transfer. [S1]
