# Malaria in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** suspicion, diagnosis, severity assessment and species-specific treatment (artemether-lumefantrine, chloroquine, IV artesunate, primaquine) for malaria in children. There is no adult or other-source malaria treatment guideline in this compendium; `diarrhoea` carries the returned-traveller differential, `febrile-child` the general febrile work-up.

> ⚠️ **Any fever after travel to an endemic area: think malaria. One negative film does not exclude it. In an unwell child, assume *P. falciparum*.**

## Recognise it

- **Suspect malaria in any febrile child who has travelled to an endemic region.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Young children, especially under 5, can deteriorate rapidly** and are more likely than adults to get severe and cerebral malaria. [S1]
- **P. falciparum typically incubates for 14 days (range 7–30).** *P. ovale* and *P. vivax* form liver hypnozoites that can relapse months to years later. [S1]
- **Early symptoms are non-specific** — headache, myalgia, chills, sweating, abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhoea. **Ask about prophylaxis** (drug, duration, adherence). [S1]

## Diagnose

- **Send both thick and thin films and a malaria antigen test** — 1 mL of EDTA blood is enough for both. The antigen test detects *P. falciparum* with over 90% sensitivity but is insensitive for other species and low parasitaemia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **One negative film or antigen test does not rule malaria out:** repeat thick and thin films with fever spikes until positive or 3 negative films. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severity markers:** haemolytic anaemia, hypoglycaemia, metabolic acidosis, raised lactate, haemoglobinuria, **parasitaemia over 2%**, renal impairment, pulmonary oedema, coagulopathy. [S1]
- **Uncomplicated malaria** = confirmed parasitological diagnosis without signs of severity (organ dysfunction). [S1]

## Uncomplicated malaria

- ⚠️ **If the child is unwell, presume *P. falciparum* is there** — mixed infection is relatively common. [S1]
- **Artemether-lumefantrine for P. falciparum or an unidentified species** (each tablet 20/120 mg): 1 tablet for 5–14 kg · 2 for 15–24 kg · 3 for 25–34 kg · 4 (adult dose) over 35 kg — **give orally at hours 0, 8, 24, 36, 48 and 60, taken with breastmilk, full-fat milk or fatty food.** [S1]
- **Outpatient care may suit** if adherence to antimalarials can be assured. [S1]
- **P. vivax, P. ovale, P. malariae, P. knowlesi (unless chloroquine-resistant P. vivax is suspected): oral chloroquine base: 10 mg/kg (max 620 mg) at hour 0, then 5 mg/kg (max 310 mg) at 6, 24 and 48 hours**, followed by primaquine for *P. vivax*, *P. ovale* or unknown species. Round to the nearest ¼ tablet. [S1]
- **Chloroquine is accessed through the Special Access Scheme**; artemether-lumefantrine is an alternative. [S1]
- **P. vivax with known chloroquine resistance:** artemether-lumefantrine as for *P. falciparum*, **and** primaquine. [S1]

## Severe malaria

- ⚠️ **Severe malaria (any species) or unable to tolerate oral: artesunate 2.4 mg/kg IV/IM bolus over 1–2 minutes on admission, repeated at 12 and 24 hours, then daily until oral is tolerated** — then complete a full oral course. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Strict fluid balance — avoid fluid overload** (cerebral and pulmonary oedema). Artesunate is via the Special Access Scheme and complex to give: contact pharmacy. [S1]
- **Once oral is tolerated, change to artemether-lumefantrine**; discuss alternatives with Infectious Diseases if unavailable. [S1]

## Primaquine (radical cure)

- ⚠️ **Primaquine is mandatory for P. ovale, P. vivax or an unknown species, to prevent relapse — and G6PD screening must come first.** If G6PD-deficient, consult Infectious Diseases. [S1]
- **Primaquine base (children over 6 months), 7.5 mg tablets: 0.5 mg/kg (max 30 mg) by mouth once a day with food, for 14 days** for *P. vivax*, *P. ovale* or unknown species; for *P. vivax* or unknown species it may be divided 12-hourly if it causes nausea. [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team for every suspected case** and consider Infectious Diseases. ⚠️ **Consider transfer for any feature of severe malaria.** [S1]
- **Discharge** when afebrile for 24 hours, tolerating oral medicines, and parasite counts are falling. [S1]
