# Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, bleeding-score risk stratification, treatment by bleeding severity, and family education for immune thrombocytopenia in children. Head injury in a child with ITP: see also `head-injury-children`.

> ✅ **PARAPHRASED, HASH-ANCHORED.** 24 of 24 claims paraphrased from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Immune thrombocytopenic purpura*** (Last updated December 2025); **32 anchors re-checkable by machine; 5 doses.** The RCH text is not reproduced: its terms licence personal use only.

> ⚠️ **Treat the bleeding, not the platelet count.** Most children need no treatment; aim to halt active bleeding rather than bring the count back to normal.

> ⚠️ **Any head injury or sign of intracranial haemorrhage: emergency department review and consult Haematology.**

---

## Recognise and assess

- **ITP is a diagnosis of exclusion**: isolated thrombocytopenia in a well child with no concerning features and an otherwise normal FBE and film. There is no confirmatory test, and other causes of petechiae and purpura must be excluded. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Decide on treatment from clinical features, not the platelet count; most children need none.** The risk of intracranial haemorrhage is very low (under 1%). [S1]
- **Newly diagnosed ITP** is most common at 2–6 years, often follows a viral infection, starts suddenly, is typically self-limiting and usually recovers within 3–6 months. **Persistent** = 3–12 months; **chronic** = over 12 months, seen more often in adolescents (20–30%). [S1]
- **Typical story:** sudden petechial rash and easy bruising in an often well-looking child; epistaxis, gum, GI bleeding, haematuria or menorrhagia; a preceding viral infection or recent live vaccine (e.g. MMR). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Intracranial haemorrhage is rare (under 1%)**: headache, nausea, vomiting, lethargy, irritability, reduced consciousness or neurological symptoms. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags for another diagnosis:** limp, bone pain, weight loss, anorexia, fever, jaundice, sweats or infection · rash, arthritis, myalgia, dry eyes, mouth ulcers, recurrent infections · family or personal bleeding history · recent NSAIDs, anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs. [S1]
- **Differentials:** leukaemia · bacterial or viral infection · inflicted injury · marrow disorders such as aplastic anaemia · SLE · drug-induced thrombocytopenia · TTP or HUS · localised SVC-distribution petechiae after vomiting or Valsalva. [S1]

## Grade the bleeding (modified Buchanan score)

- **0** none · **1 minor**: 100 or fewer petechiae and/or 5 or fewer small bruises (3 cm or less), no mucosal bleeding · **2 mild**: over 100 petechiae and/or over 5 bruises over 3 cm. [S1]
- **3a (moderate, low risk)**: nares with blood in them, painless oral purpura, petechiae on the palate or in the mouth, buccal purpura only along the molars, epistaxis 5 minutes or less · **3b moderate, high risk**: epistaxis over 5 minutes, haematuria, haematochezia, painful oral purpura, significant menorrhagia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **4 (severe)**: suspected internal haemorrhage (brain, lung, muscle, joint) or mucosal bleeding · **5 (life-threatening)**: bleeding anywhere that threatens life, or documented ICH. [S1]

## Investigate

- **FBE and blood film are the only initial tests needed**; the film must be reviewed to exclude another diagnosis, and other tests are targeted to the differential or complications. [S1]

## Treat

- **Treatment aims to halt active bleeding — a normal platelet count is not the target.** Weigh active bleeding, future bleeding risk (e.g. impending surgery) and psychosocial factors. [S1]
- **Low risk (grades 0–3a): outpatient, no medical treatment** unless there are significant safety or psychosocial concerns; **review with a repeat FBE after 1 week**, and educate the family. [S1]
- **Moderate (3b): often needs admission.** A haematologist must review or discuss the film before treatment starts; raise the platelet count enough to stop bleeding, not to normal. [S1]
- **First line: 4–7 days of prednisolone by mouth, 2–4 mg/kg (max 100 mg).** [S1]
- **Second line** (poor response, or a rapid rise needed, e.g. before surgery): **IVIg 0.8–1 g/kg**, discussed with Haematology. [S1]
- **For epistaxis, tranexamic acid by mouth 25 mg/kg (max 1.5 g)**, possibly with ENT; heavy menstrual bleeding is also listed for additional treatment. ⚠️ **Never give tranexamic acid if there is haematuria.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe (4)** — suspected internal haemorrhage or mucosal bleeding needing immediate intervention: **urgent Haematology consultation; IVIg 0.8–1 g/kg together with IV pulse methylprednisolone for 3 days, 30 mg/kg (max 1 g) each day.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Also in severe bleeding: transfuse platelets 20 mL/kg** (continuously if required) · **tranexamic acid IV, 15 mg/kg (max 1–1.5 g)** · thrombopoietin receptor agonists (romiplostim or eltrombopag) if life-threatening · urgent surgery or referral depending on the bleeding site. [S1]
- **Haematology consultation for:** life-threatening or severe bleeding · age under 1 or over 14 · any film abnormality · any concern for another diagnosis · chronic ITP · signs of ICH or a head injury. [S1]

## Family education and disposition

- **Give written information and a letter documenting the diagnosis.** Restrict activities to limit head injury: **no contact sports** (football, rugby, soccer, hockey, martial arts); limit horse-riding, skiing, scooters, skateboards, bikes, climbing equipment and trampolines. **Any head injury: present to ED.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid antiplatelet drugs, NSAIDs, anticoagulants and intramuscular injections.** Significant bleeding or signs of ICH need emergency department review. [S1]
- **Involve the paediatric team** for diagnostic uncertainty, red flags, follow-up arrangements, or concern about the family's ability to follow the plan. Ongoing care: Haematology, General Paediatrics or GP depending on context. [S1]
- **Consider transfer** for severe or life-threatening haemorrhage or care beyond the local service. **Discharge when the family understands** the condition, restrictions and when to go to ED, with a **specific follow-up plan including when to repeat the FBE.** [S1]
