# Chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia (CMML) in adults

## Recognise and classify (counts ×10⁹/L)

- **CMML is the commonest myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm (about 1/100,000 a year)** and ranges from MDS-like disease with monocytosis to highly proliferative disease with high white counts, splenomegaly and extramedullary involvement. [S1]
- **Classify by WHO 2016 blast counts:** CMML-0 (<2% blood, <5% marrow), CMML-1 (2–4% blood and/or 5–9% marrow), CMML-2 (5–19% blood, 10–19% marrow, or Auer rods). The grade carries prognostic weight. (Level D) [S1]
- **Also split dysplastic from proliferative CMML at a white count of 13:** they behave and are managed differently (cytopenias versus organomegaly, leucocytosis and constitutional symptoms). [S1]
- **Examine for organomegaly; measure a suspected enlarged spleen** (e.g. ultrasound). Serous effusions and skin infiltration signal worse prognosis. [S1]

## Risk assessment

- **Survival varies from over 50 months to under a year,** so every patient needs risk stratification with a CMML-specific model that uses mutations (GFM, CPSS-mol or Mayo Molecular); without mutation data, use CPSS or the MD Anderson score. (Level D) [S1]
- **Adverse markers:** complex karyotype and chromosome 7 abnormalities; ASXL1 mutation (consistently shorter survival); RUNX1, NRAS and SETBP1 mutations; more mutations overall. [S1]
- **Weigh the patient as well as the disease:** age (median about 77), performance status, comorbidity, frailty, nutrition, cognition and quality of life. Scores were built in patients around 70 or older, so use caution in younger people. [S1]

## Monitoring

- **Monitor with full blood count and differential, spleen size and extramedullary disease, and a standard symptom score (e.g. MPN-SAF).** Repeat the marrow with cytogenetics if these change. [S1]

## Treatment (counts ×10⁹/L)

- **First decide whether the patient could have an allogeneic transplant.** [S1]
- **Watch and wait** when there is no marrow blast excess and no (or only mild, asymptomatic) cytopenia or major myeloproliferation. Treatment usually starts for Hb under 10 g/dL, platelets under 30 or bleeding, symptomatic splenomegaly, other extramedullary disease or constitutional symptoms. (Level D) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Allogeneic transplant is the only cure.** Recommended under age 70 for higher-risk disease with a donor and no major contraindication; consider it in selected lower-risk patients with severe cytopenias or poor-risk mutations (ASXL1, RUNX1, SETBP1, NRAS). (Level B) [S1]
- **Before transplant, CMML-2 is usually debulked.** Intensive chemotherapy suits fit patients with high disease burden; a hypomethylating agent suits unfit patients or bridges while a donor is found. [S1]
- **Intensive chemotherapy is otherwise not recommended** (chemoresistant disease, short remissions), except as a bridge to transplant in CMML-2 or when disease borders on AML (Auer rods, NPM1 mutation). (Level D) [S1]
- **Azacitidine for transplant-ineligible dysplastic CMML with more than 10% marrow blasts** (Level B). Consider it, ideally in a trial, for blasts above 5% with significant cytopenias or myeloproliferation (Level D). [S1]
- **Hypomethylating agents give about 50% overall and 25% complete response, with median survival around 20 months;** there is no basis for choosing azacitidine over decitabine. Grade 3–4 blood toxicity occurs in 15% to 55%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **After hypomethylating-agent failure the outlook is poor** (median survival about 7 months; half transform to AML), and switching azacitidine to decitabine does not seem to help. [S1]
- **Hydroxyurea for proliferative CMML without major cytopenias or blast excess** (Level A): 1 g/day beat etoposide 150 mg/week (median survival 20 vs 9 months). Start on symptoms and comorbidity, not a set count; taper or stop if transfusion needs rise. [S1]

## Supportive care

- **ESAs for symptomatic anaemia in lower-risk disease with low serum EPO** (Level B); erythroid response was 64%, lasting a median 7 months. [S1]
- **G-CSF:** no data support its safe use for azacitidine-induced neutropenia, though it may be tried in febrile patients not responding to antibiotics. Drug-induced severe neutropenia is managed by tapering or pausing the drug. [S1]
- **Transfuse red cells on symptoms, not a fixed number;** Hb below 80 g/L is usually symptomatic. Rising transfusion needs should prompt a search for hypersplenism, bleeding, haemolysis or progression. (Level D) [S1]
- **Iron chelation may be considered** in transfusion-dependent lower-risk patients with ferritin above 1000 ng/mL after about 25 units, if life expectancy is not otherwise under 3 years. (Level D) [S1]
- **Isolated severe thrombocytopenia without anaemia or blast excess suggests an immune component:** a short steroid course may help (Level D). Thrombopoietin agonists are not recommended outside trials. [S1]
- **Offer clinical trials at every stage.** [S1]
