# IgA vasculitis (Henoch-Schönlein purpura) in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, investigation, symptomatic treatment, renal follow-up and referral criteria for IgA vasculitis (Henoch-Schönlein purpura, HSP) in children. Its commonest abdominal complication is covered in `intussusception`; for isolated thrombocytopenic purpura see `immune-thrombocytopenia-children`.

## Recognise

- ⚠️ **Urinalysis and blood pressure are mandatory when HSP is suspected.** The illness usually settles by itself; **close follow-up is critical** because renal involvement is usually asymptomatic. [S1]
- **HSP is the commonest childhood vasculitis**, most often at 2–8 years. **It is a clinical diagnosis:** rash together with at least one of — abdominal pain (50%) · arthritis/arthralgia (50–75%) · nephritis (25–50%). [S1]
- **Features can take days to weeks to appear**, in any order. Purpura occurs in everyone but presents first in only 75%; a recent URTI (viral or group A strep) precedes 50% of cases. [S1]
- **Rash:** palpable purpura, petechiae and ecchymoses, usually symmetrical, on gravity/pressure-dependent areas (buttocks and legs in walking children); **painful non-pitting subcutaneous oedema**. **Joints:** usually large lower-limb joints, without significant effusion or warmth. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Abdomen: intussusception is the commonest complication**; others are pancreatitis, protein-losing enteropathy, GI haemorrhage, and bowel ischaemia, necrosis or perforation. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Testicular pain: exclude testicular torsion.** Rare: respiratory distress or diffuse alveolar haemorrhage; mental-state change or focal signs — **consider intracranial haemorrhage.** [S1]

## Investigate

- **Urinalysis is usually the only test needed in a classic presentation.** With hypertension, macroscopic haematuria or significant proteinuria add **urine microscopy, UPCR, UEC and albumin.** [S1]
- **If the diagnosis is unclear** (e.g. ITP, leukaemia, meningococcal infection) or to find complications: FBE · UEC · albumin · cultures of blood and urine · abdominal imaging · ASOT and anti-DNase B; and, where renal involvement is significant and the diagnosis unclear, ANA, dsDNA, ANCA, C3, C4. [S1]

## Treat

- **Subcutaneous oedema: bed rest and elevation.** **Mild pain:** regular paracetamol plus a short NSAID course — **ibuprofen TDS, 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg), or naproxen BD, 10 mg/kg (max 500 mg)** — unless contraindicated (e.g. GI bleeding, renal impairment). [S1]
- **Moderate–severe abdominal or joint pain: consider steroids** (may shorten symptoms). **Steroids do not change long-term renal outcome.** [S1]
- **Prednisolone orally, 1–2 mg/kg/day (max 60 mg/day)**, or **methylprednisolone IV, 0.8–1.6 mg/kg/day (max 1 g/day)**, while symptoms persist, then wean appropriately. [S1]

## Follow-up

- ⚠️ **If initial urinalysis is normal or shows only microscopic haematuria, check BP and urinalysis:** every week in month one · fortnightly over weeks 5–12 · then once each at 6 and 12 months. A flare or new renal signs means going back to weekly reviews. [S1]
- **Normal urinalysis at 12 months, with no significant renal involvement → follow-up can stop.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Whenever hypertension, proteinuria or macroscopic haematuria develops → paediatric review** and the extended investigations. [S1]

## Admission and referral

- ⚠️ **Consider admission for:** severe debilitating pain · serious abdominal complications · severe renal involvement · neurological or pulmonary involvement · if prednisolone is being considered. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Refer to a renal specialist for:** abnormal renal function · hypertension · 5 days of macroscopic haematuria · nephrotic or acute nephritic syndrome · persistent proteinuria — **UPCR over 250 mg/mmol for 4 weeks, over 100 for 3 months, or over 50 for 6 months.** [S1]

## Prognosis (discharge advice)

- **Without significant renal disease, a first episode generally clears within 4 weeks**; rash is often last to go. Joint pain usually settles within 72 hours, uncomplicated abdominal pain within 24–48 hours. [S1]
- **HSP recurs at least once in 25–35%, within 4 months**, usually more mildly. **90% of renal complications appear within 2 months of onset, 97% within 6 months.** [S1]
