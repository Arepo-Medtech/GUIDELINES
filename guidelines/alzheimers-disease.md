**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation

## ⚠️ Start here, because it changes the conversation

> ⚠️ **"NONE OF THE AVAILABLE DRUGS PREVENTS ALZHEIMER'S DISEASE OR MODIFIES ITS PATHOLOGY"** [Z1].
>
> **Anticholinesterases and memantine show AT BEST MODEST EFFICACY in improving cognition and/or reducing
> the rate of cognitive and functional decline.** ⚠️ **"Their clinical usefulness and effect on quality of
> life REMAINS UNCERTAIN"** [Z1].

**This is AMH's framing, in its own order, and it is the most important content on the page.** A
prescribing decision made without it is a different decision.

---

## What the drugs actually deliver [Z2]

> **Deterioration of cognition is DELAYED BY 6 MONTHS IN 25–50% of patients, and BY 1 YEAR IN 12–20% of
> patients with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease** [Z2].
>
> ⚠️ **THERE ARE NO RELIABLE PREDICTORS OF RESPONSE** [Z2].

| Therefore [Z1][Z2] | |
|---|---|
| **When any benefit appears** | **generally within 3–6 months of starting** |
| **When to assess** | ⚠️ **AFTER 3 MONTHS at full or highest tolerated dose** |
| ⚠️ **When to stop** | **significant adverse effects · poor compliance · OR lack of stabilisation or improvement** |
| **How long it might continue** | **optimal duration unclear; open-label studies suggest some may benefit for 3 YEARS OR MORE** |

> ⚠️ **ANTICHOLINESTERASES DO NOT REDUCE THE RISK OF DEVELOPING ALZHEIMER'S DISEASE OR DEMENTIA IN PATIENTS
> WITH MILD COGNITIVE IMPAIRMENT** [Z2]. **They are not a preventive.**

## Doses

⚠️ **Licensed and unquoted.**

| Drug | Dose [AMH] |
|---|---|
| **Donepezil** | **initially 5 mg once daily for A MINIMUM OF 4 WEEKS**, then **10 mg once daily according to response** [Z3] |
| **Memantine** | **initially 5 mg in the morning; increase by 5 mg daily EACH WEEK if tolerated, to 20 mg once daily** [Z4] |
| **Memantine, renal** | ⚠️ **reduce if CrCl <30 mL/min; CrCl 5–29 mL/min → maintenance 10 mg once daily** [Z4] |

**Donepezil 10 mg daily appears to provide MARGINALLY MORE BENEFIT than 5 mg, BUT INCREASES THE RISK OF
ADVERSE EFFECTS** [Z3]. **Usually given in the evening — consider the morning if more convenient, or if
INSOMNIA OR VIVID DREAMS occur with night-time dosing** [Z3].

> ⚠️ **TWO DOSING RULES THAT PREVENT HARM** [Z2]:
> **If adverse effects occur, OMIT ONE OR MORE DOSES, and reduce to the previous well-tolerated dose if
> they persist.**
> ⚠️ **IF TREATMENT IS INTERRUPTED FOR SEVERAL DAYS, RESTART AT THE INITIAL DOSAGE — to minimise the risk
> of SEVERE VOMITING.** **A patient who misses a week in hospital must not resume at their old dose.**

**PBS:** **donepezil for mild-to-moderately severe disease; memantine for moderately severe disease** [Z3][Z4].
**Memantine treatment should be STARTED BY A SPECIALIST** [Z4].

### Choosing between the anticholinesterases [Z2]

**There are FEW comparative trials.** **Donepezil, rivastigmine and galantamine seem to have SIMILAR
EFFICACY — but AT FULL DOSE ORAL RIVASTIGMINE MAY HAVE MORE GI ADVERSE EFFECTS** [Z2].

> **LIMITED DATA SHOW THAT SOME PATIENTS UNRESPONSIVE TO ONE ANTICHOLINESTERASE IMPROVE WHEN SWITCHED TO
> ANOTHER** [Z2]. **Non-response to one is not non-response to the class.**

### ⚠️ Combination: the trial that did not support it [Z1]

> **Specialists may use combination treatment in some patients, although evidence of benefit is LIMITED
> AND CONFLICTING.** **A 2012 study in patients with moderate-to-severe disease living in the community on
> a stable dose of donepezil showed that ADDING MEMANTINE DID NOT IMPROVE cognitive and functional
> outcomes over 12 months compared with donepezil alone** [Z1].

## ⚠️ Safety [Z2][Z4]

**Anticholinesterases are CONTRAINDICATED in GI or ureteric obstruction and ACTIVE PEPTIC ULCER.**
**Risk of aggravation in: a history of peptic ulcer disease · SEIZURES · HEART BLOCK · BRADYARRHYTHMIAS
including sick sinus syndrome · PARKINSON'S DISEASE · ASTHMA and obstructive pulmonary disease** [Z2].

> ⚠️ **DRUGS WITH ANTICHOLINERGIC EFFECTS SHOULD BE AVOIDED** — they **contribute to impaired cognition**
> AND **may reduce the efficacy of anticholinesterases** [Z1]. **AMH states this twice, on two pages** —
> **avoid the combination** [Z2]. **Two drugs pulling in opposite directions on the same receptor.**

> ⚠️ **PROLONGED QT INTERVAL AND TORSADES DE POINTES HAVE BEEN REPORTED** with anticholinesterases; the
> risk rises with other QT-prolonging drugs, and drugs causing bradycardia increase the risk of
> bradycardia and hypotension [Z2]. **This compendium's `arrhythmias` guideline carries AMH's list of
> QT-prolonging drugs.**

**Common: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, anorexia, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, headache, insomnia, VIVID
DREAMS, depression, fatigue, drowsiness, dizziness, tremor, WEIGHT LOSS, muscle cramps, URINARY
INCONTINENCE, increased sweating, hypertension, fainting** [Z2].
**Infrequent or rare: bradycardia, heart block, SEIZURE, agitation, hallucination, confusion, GI
haemorrhage, prolonged QT, torsades, INCREASED LIBIDO and INAPPROPRIATE SEXUAL BEHAVIOUR — most published
cases of the last with donepezil** [Z2].

⚠️ **Memantine is CONTRAINDICATED in a history of SEIZURES** [Z4], and **seizure is itself a rare
memantine adverse effect** [Z4].

## ⚠️ Neuropsychiatric symptoms: almost everything fails

> **IDENTIFY AND TREAT POSSIBLE CAUSES FIRST — PAIN, INFECTION, or environmental factors such as boredom
> and noise** [Z1].
>
> ⚠️ **"Few randomised controlled trials have addressed the optimal drug management of non-cognitive
> symptoms in Alzheimer's disease. ALWAYS CONSIDER NON-DRUG INTERVENTIONS FIRST AS DRUG TREATMENT IS NOT
> PARTICULARLY EFFECTIVE"** [Z1].
>
> **REGULARLY REVIEW the need to continue — SOME BEHAVIOURAL DISTURBANCES ARE TRANSIENT** [Z1].

| Drug class | AMH's position [Z1] |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Antipsychotics** | **MODEST efficacy for agitation or psychosis** — **but SUBSTANTIAL ADVERSE EFFECTS INCLUDING INCREASED RISK OF STROKE, AND INCREASED MORTALITY** |
| **Carbamazepine** | **may be beneficial for agitation or aggression in some patients** |
| ⚠️ **Valproate** | **evidence suggests it is NOT effective** |
| **Anticholinesterases / memantine** | **small improvement reported in some trials is OF UNCERTAIN CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE** |
| ⚠️ **Antidepressants** | **MIRTAZAPINE AND SERTRALINE SEEM INEFFECTIVE in people with dementia and depression**; convincing evidence for others is **LACKING** |

## What else does not work [Z1]

> **EVIDENCE IS POOR for ANTI-AMYLOID THERAPIES, antioxidants, estrogens and GINKGO BILOBA.**
> ⚠️ **THERE IS EVIDENCE THAT ANTI-INFLAMMATORY DRUGS ARE NOT BENEFICIAL** [Z1].

> ⚠️ **THE ASPIRIN REVERSAL.** **Epidemiological studies showed aspirin users have a LOWER risk of
> Alzheimer's disease than non-users — but in a randomised trial LOW-DOSE ASPIRIN DID NOT BENEFIT, AND
> INCREASED BLEEDING RISK IN, patients with Alzheimer's disease** [Z1]. **A clean example of an
> observational signal not surviving a trial.**

**Dementia Australia can assist with non-drug treatments, support services and education — 1800 100 500,
dementia.org.au** [Z1].

### Additional therapies in eTG

- Galantamine modified-release 8 mg orally each morning for 4 weeks, then 16 mg each morning if tolerated; may increase to 24 mg daily if deteriorating after initial response at 16 mg. [T1]
- Rivastigmine patch 4.6 mg/24 h daily for 4 weeks, then 9.5 mg/24 h, then 13.3 mg/24 h after at least 4 more weeks if needed; or oral rivastigmine 1.5 mg twice daily increasing by 1.5 mg twice daily every 2 weeks to a maximum of 6 mg twice daily. [T1]
- Before starting an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, perform an ECG, record weight and assess falls risk. [T1]
- When stopping an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor or memantine, halve the dose every 4 weeks until the lowest dose has been used for 4 weeks, then stop; if a severe withdrawal reaction occurs within a week, resume the previous minimum effective dose. [T1]
- Do not use acetylcholinesterase inhibitors or memantine in frontotemporal dementia; avoid vitamin E (increased mortality). [T1]
