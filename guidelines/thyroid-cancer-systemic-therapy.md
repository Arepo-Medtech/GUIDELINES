# Radioactive iodine-refractory differentiated thyroid cancer: local and systemic therapy

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognising radioactive iodine-refractory (RAI-refractory) differentiated thyroid cancer, referral, molecular testing, re-operation, radiotherapy and ablation, and choice of multikinase inhibitor, genotype-directed drug, immunotherapy or chemotherapy. Initial thyroid cancer surgery, RAI dosing, TSH suppression, medullary and anaplastic thyroid cancer are out of scope. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise RAI-refractory disease

- **RAI-refractory DTC affects under 5% of patients but has a 10-year survival of only 10%**, so early recognition matters. [S1]
- **The five defining scenarios:** metastases progressing despite RAI uptake; no uptake on the post-therapy scan despite known structural disease; uptake in some foci but not others; progression after a cumulative RAI activity above 22.2 GBq (600 mCi); and no uptake on a diagnostic radioiodine scan. [S1]
- **Raised thyroglobulin with a negative whole-body scan is a warning:** about a quarter of such patients are scan-negative, because refractory disease does not take up 131I. FDG uptake rises as radioiodine avidity falls. [S1]
- **FDG-PET is a complementary, case-by-case test**, most useful when structural imaging and clinical suspicion disagree (for example a rising thyroglobulin with no visible disease). [S1]
- **Histology that predicts poor RAI uptake:** poorly differentiated carcinoma (necrosis and/or at least 3 mitoses per 2 mm2) and high-grade DTC (necrosis and/or at least 5 mitoses per 2 mm2). [S1]
- **True refractoriness must be proven by giving RAI**, which is also needed to qualify for systemic treatment; other features only raise the suspicion. [S1]

## Refer and test

- **Refer for local or systemic therapy when there is structural progression despite RAI (highest priority)**; err on the side of referring early, and fast-track patients with heavy disease burden or risk of complications. [S1]
- **Test tumour tissue early when refractory disease is suspected** (for example FDG-PET positive), rather than waiting until it is proven. [S1]
- **BRAF mutations, RET fusions and NTRK1-3 fusions must be tested** to establish eligibility for targeted drugs; BRAF V600E immunohistochemistry is an acceptable screen only if rigorously validated and fast. [S1]

## Surgery and local therapy

- ⚠️ **Re-operation carries real morbidity:** transient hypoparathyroidism 7.1%, permanent 2.7%, and recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy 1.6%. [S1]
- **Small-volume neck disease in an operated field can be watched** if nodes are not enlarging and cytology is not aggressive. Re-operate for oligometastatic, rapidly progressive or symptomatic disease, new neck metastases, or disease threatening major structures. [S1]
- **External beam radiotherapy** does not improve survival or distant spread but does improve locoregional control; use it in selected cases. [S1]
- **Offer SRS/SRT for limited brain metastases** after neurosurgical review, and SABR for oligometastases; phase II data favour SABR in selected patients with up to 5 metastases. [S1]
- **Treat metastatic disease actively**, especially in younger or fit patients, because it usually progresses slowly. [S1]
- **Unresectable DTC (under 10% of advanced cases):** neoadjuvant TKI treatment can shrink tumour and reduce surgical morbidity. [S1]

## Systemic therapy

- ⚠️ **Multikinase inhibitors (lenvatinib, sorafenib, cabozantinib) are indicated for RAI-refractory DTC but are toxic.** In SELECT about 76% had grade 3+ treatment-related events on lenvatinib and 14.2% stopped for adverse events (2.3% on placebo); hypertension, diarrhoea and fatigue were commonest. [S1]
- **With an actionable alteration, consider a targeted drug.** RET- and NTRK-fusion inhibitors can be used before lenvatinib; BRAF inhibitors should not routinely precede lenvatinib because they are less effective and the evidence is weaker. [S1]
- **Immune checkpoint inhibitors only when nothing else is available** and the patient is eligible (e.g. mismatch-repair deficient); pembrolizumab gave a response rate of about 7% in KEYNOTE-158. [S1]
- **Cytotoxic chemotherapy is a last resort:** doxorubicin-based regimens give responses of around 20%. [S1]
- **RAI resensitisation (for example BRAF/MEK inhibition) only within a clinical trial**; adding selumetinib to adjuvant RAI did not improve complete remission in a phase 3 trial. [S1]
