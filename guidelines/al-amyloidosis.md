# Systemic light chain (AL) amyloidosis

## Recognise

- **AL amyloidosis comes from a small clonal plasma-cell (or B-cell) population making unstable light chains that deposit in organs, mainly the heart and kidneys.** Early diagnosis matters because organ damage becomes irreversible. [S1]
- **It is rare (5.1–12.8 per million a year), with median age at diagnosis 64;** MGUS and myeloma carry a significant risk of progressing to AL. One-year mortality is 12%–30%, reflecting late diagnosis, and heart involvement drives prognosis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Diagnosis is typically late:** 50% of patients see five or more doctors, and time from symptoms to diagnosis runs from about six months to 2.7 years. Early symptoms (fatigue, dizziness, neurological symptoms, abdominal pain) are vague. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Suspect AL amyloidosis in HFpEF, nephrotic-range proteinuria without diabetes, peripheral neuropathy, unexplained hepatomegaly or diarrhoea, especially when several organs are involved.** Macroglossia, periorbital purpura, submandibular swelling and the shoulder pad sign are specific but rare. [S1]
- **In people with MGUS, monitor ALP, proteinuria and NT-proBNP regularly** to catch early organ involvement. [S1]

## Diagnose

- **Four steps:** clinical suspicion; find a monoclonal light chain; tissue diagnosis; then type the amyloid. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Look for the monoclonal light chain with three tests: serum immunofixation, urine immunofixation and the serum kappa/lambda ratio.** Electrophoresis is often negative because the clone is small (under 10% of marrow cells), so it is usually left out. [S1]
- **Confirm amyloid on biopsy with Congo red and apple-green birefringence under polarised light.** Start with fat pad, bone marrow or salivary gland; together fat and marrow give 89% sensitivity (endomyocardial biopsy 93.5%). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Negative fat and marrow biopsies do not exclude AL:** about a 15% chance of disease remains, so biopsy an affected organ when suspicion is high. [S1]
- **Type the amyloid protein** with immunological methods or mass spectrometry once Congo red is positive. With no monoclonal protein and suspected cardiac amyloid, do bone-tracer scintigraphy for ATTR and DNA testing to separate hereditary from wild-type. [S1]

## Stage

- **Staging rests on cardiac biomarkers (troponin T and NT-proBNP or BNP).** The Mayo 2004 model and its European modification are most used; the European version better predicts early death, Mayo 2012 better predicts late survival. A renal staging system (eGFR and proteinuria) predicts dialysis risk. [S1]
- **Use cardiac biomarkers at every stage:** diagnosis, prognosis, follow-up and transplant assessment. [S1]

## Treat

- **The goal is a rapid, deep haematological remission.** Organs can keep recovering for up to two years after light chains fall, so if at least a very good partial response is reached, do not change treatment early just because organs have not yet responded. [S1]
- ⚠️ **First line: daratumumab plus bortezomib, cyclophosphamide and dexamethasone** (the ANDROMEDA regimen), which improved the speed and depth of response, overall survival and survival free of major organ deterioration. If daratumumab is unavailable, use bortezomib-cyclophosphamide-dexamethasone. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Autologous transplant suits only a minority (around 20%):** younger patients with limited cardiac involvement, selected by EHA-ISA risk-adapted criteria. Not for advanced cardiac disease (especially Stage IIIb), refractory pleural effusions or orthostatic hypotension, active GI bleeding, or factor X deficiency with bleeding. Conditioning melphalan is 200 mg/m² or 140 mg/m² by age, heart, kidneys and condition. [S1]
- **Lenalidomide and the newer proteasome inhibitors (carfilzomib, ixazomib) have a limited role,** alone or combined, because of cardiotoxicity risk and little evidence of efficacy. Fibril-directed antibodies (birtamimab, anselamimab) failed their phase 3 trials. [S1]
- **Relapse:** consider daratumumab rechallenge, or a daratumumab-based regimen if not used before; bortezomib re-exposure (ideally in a different combination) if the earlier response was durable and there is no neuropathy; venetoclax (investigational) for t(11;14); and clinical trials. [S1]

## Monitor

- **Measure haematological response as dFLC (involved minus uninvolved free light chain) at least every three months** during and after treatment; reaching dFLC below 10 mg/L predicts better survival and organ response. [S1]
- **Rising dFLC does not automatically mean second-line therapy;** individualise. After a prior very good partial response (dFLC under 40 mg/L), consider salvage before overt organ progression. High-risk dFLC progression means dFLC over 20 mg/L (over 20% of baseline) and a more than 50% rise from the nadir. [S1]

## Localised AL amyloidosis

- ⚠️ **Do not mistake localised AL (about 10% of cases) for systemic disease:** there is no circulating monoclonal protein or organ involvement, it rarely becomes systemic, and chemotherapy is not indicated. Treat for symptoms with surgery first, radiotherapy if unresectable, or watch and wait. [S1]
