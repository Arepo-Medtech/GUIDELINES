# Candidaemia and invasive candidiasis in adults

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** candidaemia (with and without neutropenia), chronic disseminated candidiasis, suspected invasive candidiasis and prophylaxis in the ICU, intra-abdominal candidiasis and *Candida* in respiratory samples, in adults. Oral, oesophageal and vulvovaginal candidiasis: see `candidiasis`. Aspergillosis and mould prophylaxis: see `invasive-aspergillosis`. Neonatal candidiasis is in the source but not here.

## Candidaemia without neutropenia

- ⚠️ **Start with an echinocandin** (strong; high): caspofungin 70 mg once, then 50 mg a day; micafungin 100 mg a day; or anidulafungin 200 mg once, then 100 mg a day. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fluconazole (IV or by mouth), first dose 800 mg (12 mg/kg) and 400 mg (6 mg/kg) a day after that, is an acceptable first choice for selected patients** who are not critically ill and unlikely to carry a fluconazole-resistant species (strong; high). [S1]
- **Test every bloodstream (and other clinically important) *Candida* isolate for azole susceptibility;** add echinocandin testing after prior echinocandin treatment or for *C. glabrata* or *C. parapsilosis*. [S1]
- **Moving from the echinocandin to fluconazole, typically by day 5–7, is advised** once the patient is stable, the isolate is fluconazole-susceptible (for example *C. albicans*) and repeat blood cultures are negative (strong; moderate). [S1]
- ⚠️ ***C. glabrata* step-down only if the isolate is susceptible:** fluconazole 800 mg (12 mg/kg) a day, or voriconazole 200–300 mg (3–4 mg/kg) twice a day. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Lipid amphotericin B 3–5 mg/kg a day** is a reasonable alternative for intolerance, poor availability or resistance, and is the choice when azole- and echinocandin-resistant *Candida* is suspected. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Every non-neutropenic patient with candidaemia needs a dilated eye examination, ideally by an ophthalmologist, in the first week** (strong; low). [S1]
- **Repeat blood cultures daily or on alternate days until clearance is shown,** then treat for 2 weeks after the first negative culture and resolution of symptoms, if there are no metastatic complications (strong; moderate). [S1]
- **Remove a central venous catheter as early as possible when it is the presumed source and removal is safe,** deciding case by case (strong; moderate). [S1]

## Candidaemia with neutropenia

- **An echinocandin is again the first choice** (strong; moderate). Lipid amphotericin B works but is less attractive because of toxicity; fluconazole is for those not critically ill and never previously given an azole; voriconazole is an option when mould cover is also wanted. [S1]
- ***C. krusei*: use an echinocandin, lipid amphotericin B or voriconazole** (strong; low). [S1]
- **In neutropenia the eye findings stay minimal until the count recovers,** so do the dilated fundus examination in the first week after neutrophil recovery. Sources other than the catheter (such as the gut) predominate, so catheter removal is an individual decision. [S1]

## Chronic disseminated (hepatosplenic) candidiasis

- **Give lipid amphotericin B or an echinocandin for several weeks, then oral fluconazole 400 mg (6 mg/kg) a day** if the isolate is unlikely to be resistant. Continue until the lesions have resolved on repeat imaging, usually months; stopping early risks relapse. [S1]
- **Do not delay chemotherapy or stem cell transplant because of chronic disseminated candidiasis;** keep antifungals going through the high-risk period. [S1]

## Intensive care

- **Consider empiric antifungals in critically ill patients with risk factors and no other cause of fever,** and start them as soon as possible if there are signs of septic shock (strong; moderate). An echinocandin is preferred; fluconazole is an alternative when there has been no recent azole and no colonisation with azole-resistant *Candida*. [S1]
- **Empiric therapy lasts 2 weeks if the patient improves;** if there is no response by 4–5 days and no evidence of invasive candidiasis (or a negative high-NPV non-culture test), consider stopping. [S1]
- **Fluconazole prophylaxis may be used for high-risk patients in adult ICUs where invasive candidiasis exceeds 5%** (weak; moderate). [S1]
- **Intra-abdominal candidiasis needs source control (drainage, debridement)** plus the same antifungals as for candidaemia; duration depends on source control and response. [S1]
- ***Candida* grown from respiratory secretions is usually colonisation and seldom needs antifungals** (strong; moderate). [S1]
