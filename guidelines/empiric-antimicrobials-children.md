# Empiric antimicrobial therapy in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** initial empiric antimicrobial choice, dose and duration by infection site in children. This is the dosing table the other RCH condition guidelines defer to. Adult/general counterparts: `drug-choice-for-selected-infections`, `anti-infectives-general-principles`.

## How to use this table

- **These are for choosing initial empiric treatment.** **Not for neonates or immunocompromised patients** unless specified. **Always ask about previous antibiotic hypersensitivity.** [S1]
- **Once sensitivities are known, switch to narrow-spectrum agents.** Doses may need adjusting for neonates and for renal or hepatic impairment. [S1]
- **Durations are a guide**, and generally mean the **minimum total IV plus oral** course; stepping down from IV to oral is appropriate in many cases. [S1]

## Doses referred to throughout

- **Ceftriaxone:** standard 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV once daily; **for severe infection, including meningitis, orbital cellulitis and brain abscess, 100 mg/kg (max 4 g) IV once daily.** [S1]
- **Cefotaxime:** 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 12-hourly in week 1 of life, 6–8-hourly in weeks 2–4, 6-hourly after week 4. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Where possible avoid ceftriaxone in neonates under 41 weeks' gestation**, particularly when jaundiced or on calcium-containing solutions (TPN included). [S1]
- **Gentamicin, IV once daily:** 5 mg/kg from birth to 1 month; 7.5 mg/kg (max 320 mg) under 10 years; 6 mg/kg (max 560 mg) at 10 years and over. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Gentamicin monitoring:** with normal renal function, when over 3 doses are needed, check the trough before the third dose and every 3 days after that, **target under 1 mg/L**. In renal failure, check levels before every dose and discuss with a specialist. [S1]
- **Vancomycin trough targets:** cellulitis 10–15 mg/L; severe infection (meningitis, osteomyelitis, pneumonia, endocarditis, bacteraemia) or a known high MIC 15–20 mg/L. [S1]
- **Penicillin allergy or MRSA risk:** replace cefazolin or flucloxacillin with one of: IV vancomycin 15 mg/kg (up to 750 mg) 6-hourly, **or** clindamycin 10 mg/kg (max 450 mg) oral tds, **or** trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 4/20 mg/kg (max 320/1600 mg) oral bd. **MRSA risk:** high-prevalence areas such as the NT and remote northern Queensland, prior MRSA, a Pacific Islander or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander child. [S1]

## Central nervous system and eye

- **Meningitis over 2 months:** high-dose 3rd-generation cephalosporin. Duration by organism: 10 days for *S. pneumoniae*, 5–7 days for *N. meningitidis*, 7–10 days for Hib. **Consider adding IV dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg (max 10 mg) 6-hourly for 4 days.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **If *S. pneumoniae* is suspected, also give IV vancomycin 15 mg/kg (up to 750 mg) 6-hourly** (e.g. Gram-positive cocci on CSF). Stop it once cephalosporin sensitivity is confirmed or another cause is found. [S1]
- **Meningitis under 2 months:** IV benzylpenicillin 60 mg/kg (up to 2.4 g) — 12-hourly in week 1, 6-hourly in weeks 2–4, 4-hourly after week 4 — **plus** high-dose 3rd-generation cephalosporin. Duration: 3 weeks if Gram-negative; 2–3 weeks for GBS/Listeria. [S1]
- **Encephalitis — aciclovir IV 8-hourly:** 500 mg/m² for ages 3 months–12 years; 10 mg/kg over 12 years; at least 3 weeks. [S1]
- **Meningococcal contact prophylaxis:** ciprofloxacin 250 mg (5–12 years) or 500 mg (12 years and over) orally, single dose. If tablets cannot be taken: rifampicin orally bd for 2 days, 5 mg/kg under 1 month or 10 mg/kg from 1 month (max 600 mg). [S1]
- **Hib contact prophylaxis:** rifampicin 20 mg/kg (max 600 mg) orally daily for 4 days. [S1]
- **Orbital (postseptal) cellulitis:** high-dose 3rd-generation cephalosporin IV, usually 3–4 days, then amoxicillin/clavulanate 22.5 mg/kg (max 875 mg amoxicillin) orally bd; **10 days minimum in total.** [S1]
- **Mild periorbital (preseptal) cellulitis:** cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) orally tds for 7–10 days. Swelling that is bilateral, painless or non-tender in a well child points more to allergy. [S1]

## Respiratory, ear and throat

- **Otitis media:** over 6 months, consider withholding antibiotics for 48 hours. If treating: amoxicillin 30 mg/kg (max 1 g) orally bd for 5 days. [S1]
- **Pneumonia, mild (outpatient):** amoxicillin 30 mg/kg (max 1 g) orally tds for 3–5 days. **Moderate (inpatient):** the same for 5 days; if oral intake is not tolerated, consider benzylpenicillin IV 6-hourly at 60 mg/kg (max 2.4 g). [S1]
- **Severe pneumonia** (at least 2 of: altered mental state, marked tachycardia, severe hypoxaemia or cyanosis, severe respiratory distress; or empyema needing ICU): 3rd-generation cephalosporin, 10 days minimum; consider oseltamivir and IV azithromycin daily at 10 mg/kg (max 500 mg). [S1]
- **Tonsillitis — treat only features of GAS in a child 4 years or older AND a high-risk group or suppurative complications:** phenoxymethylpenicillin 250 mg bd (under 20 kg) or 500 mg bd (20 kg and over) orally for 10 days, **or** benzathine benzylpenicillin 450 mg IM (under 20 kg) / 900 mg IM (over 20 kg) once. [S1]
- **High-risk groups for GAS:** Indigenous Australians · Māori and Pacific Islander · rheumatic fever or rheumatic heart disease in the child or family · immunosuppressed. [S1]
- **Pertussis:** azithromycin 10 mg/kg (max 500 mg) daily from birth to 6 months; 10 mg/kg on day 1 then 5 mg/kg (max 250 mg) daily from 6 months; 5 days. [S1]
- **Influenza — oseltamivir orally bd for 5 days:** 3 mg/kg (birth–12 months); 30 mg (over 12 months and under 15 kg); 45 mg (15–23 kg); 60 mg (23–40 kg); 75 mg (over 40 kg). [S1]
- **Epiglottitis:** ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg (max 1 g) IV daily for 5 days. [S1]

## Urinary tract

- **UTI over 3 months and not sick:** cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) orally tds, **or** trimethoprim 4 mg/kg (max 150 mg) orally bd, **or** trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 0.5 mL/kg (max 20 mL) orally bd; 5 days. [S1]
- **UTI under 3 months, sick, or acute pyelonephritis:** gentamicin (as above) for 5–7 days in UTI or 7–10 days in pyelonephritis; consider early switch to cefalexin 25 mg/kg (max 1 g) qid under 12 months or 45 mg/kg (max 1.5 g) tds from 12 months. [S1]
- **UTI prophylaxis is not routinely recommended.** [S1]

## Skin, soft tissue and bone

- **Cellulitis, mild/moderate (outpatient):** cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) orally tds for 5–10 days; where it is rapidly progressive, consider also giving IV clindamycin 15 mg/kg (max 600 mg) 8-hourly. **Moderate/severe (inpatient):** cefazolin 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 8-hourly, or, for hospital-in-the-home, ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV daily. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Necrotising fasciitis:** IV vancomycin 15 mg/kg (up to 750 mg) 6-hourly **and** IV meropenem 20 mg/kg (up to 1 g) 8-hourly **and** IV clindamycin 15 mg/kg (up to 600 mg) 8-hourly; consider IVIg. [S1]
- **Bites — established infection:** amoxicillin/clavulanate 22.5 mg/kg (max 875 mg amoxicillin) orally bd for 5 days. **Prophylaxis generally not required, except:** presentation after 8 hours · puncture wound that can't be debrided · hands, feet or face · deep tissue · open fracture · immunocompromised · cat bites. Check tetanus. [S1]
- **Impetigo (5 days):** mupirocin 2% ointment tds when localised; or oral cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) tds. [S1]
- **Uncomplicated osteomyelitis:** cefazolin 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 8-hourly; switch to cefalexin 45 mg/kg (max 1.5 g) orally tds once afebrile, clinically improving and inflammatory markers falling; **3 weeks** for uncomplicated cases. **Septic arthritis: as for osteomyelitis — always consider surgical drainage.** [S1]
- **Scabies:** permethrin 5% cream from the neck down, left on at least 8 hours (usually overnight); may need repeating after 7 days; treat the whole family. Consider ivermectin 200 micrograms/kg orally with fatty food, repeated in 7 days, as an alternative. [S1]
- **Dental abscess:** amoxicillin 25 mg/kg (max 500 mg) orally tds or IV benzylpenicillin 60 mg/kg (up to 2.4 g) 6-hourly, 7 days — **dental/surgical management is required.** [S1]

## Gastrointestinal

- **Antibiotics are generally unnecessary for diarrhoea**, except *Salmonella* in an infant under 3 months or an immunocompromised child (3rd-generation cephalosporin, 5–7 days). [S1]
- ***C. difficile*:** metronidazole 7.5 mg/kg (max 400 mg) orally tds for 7–10 days. **Giardiasis:** metronidazole 30 mg/kg (max 2 g) orally daily for 3 days. [S1]
- **Appendicitis:** uncomplicated, including non-perforated gangrenous — **stop antibiotics immediately after appendicectomy**; complicated — **4 days total** after adequate source control. [S1]

## Sepsis

- **Sepsis over 2 months, CSF unknown (meningitis not excluded):** high-dose 3rd-generation cephalosporin; add IV vancomycin 15 mg/kg (up to 750 mg) 6-hourly if a central line is in situ or MRSA is suspected. [S1]
- **Sepsis over 2 months with meningitis excluded:** cefazolin 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 8-hourly **and** gentamicin. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Toxic shock syndrome:** high-dose 3rd-generation cephalosporin **and** vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV 6-hourly **and** IV clindamycin 15 mg/kg (up to 600 mg) 8-hourly **and** IVIg 2 g/kg. Prophylaxis for GAS contacts: cefalexin 25 mg/kg (max 1 g) orally bd for 10 days. [S1]
