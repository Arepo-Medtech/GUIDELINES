# Endemic mycoses — histoplasmosis and sporotrichosis

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis and antifungal treatment of histoplasmosis (disseminated, including in advanced HIV, and less severe disease) and sporotrichosis (cutaneous, lymphocutaneous, disseminated and pulmonary). The source also covers blastomycosis, coccidioidomycosis, emergomycosis, paracoccidioidomycosis and talaromycosis, which are not summarised here. Other invasive fungal disease: `invasive-aspergillosis`, `invasive-candidiasis`, `cryptococcal-meningitis`. PBS access for itraconazole is in the table below.

## Histoplasmosis — diagnose

- **Get tissue whenever possible:** histopathology with fungal stains (such as Grocott methenamine silver) plus fungal culture. Blood cultures pick up only about half of cases in advanced HIV. [S1]
- **Histoplasma antigen in urine or serum** gives a rapid probable diagnosis and can be followed over time. Serology helps most in chronic pulmonary histoplasmosis and may fail in severe immunosuppression. [S1]
- **Exposure history matters:** the fungus lives in soil contaminated by bat and bird droppings; it is highly endemic in the Mississippi, Ohio and St Lawrence basins and common in Latin America, but occurs worldwide, including Africa and Europe. [S1]

## Histoplasmosis — treat

- ⚠️ **Moderate-to-severe disease in advanced HIV: induction with liposomal amphotericin B,** which improved survival over the deoxycholate form in a randomised trial (3 mg/kg daily). Other amphotericin formulations are acceptable if the liposomal form is unavailable. [S1]
- ⚠️ **After induction for disseminated disease, give itraconazole 200 mg twice daily, usually for at least a year.** Itraconazole alone is an alternative induction option for less severe infection. [S1]
- **Prefer itraconazole to fluconazole** for milder disease: fluconazole works less often and resistance can emerge on it. Voriconazole is not routinely recommended. [S1]
- **In people without immunosuppression, treat for at least 6 months,** adjusting for severity and site. [S1]
- **Histoplasmosis on a TNF-alpha inhibitor: stop the TNF blocker during antifungal treatment.** Immunosuppression may be restarted after a clinical response, about 12 months of antifungal therapy and a negative Histoplasma antigen. [S1]

## Sporotrichosis — diagnose

- **Diagnose by culture and histopathology of skin or tissue aspirates or biopsies;** culture is the most sensitive test but can be negative, and histopathology is often negative. Imaging is rarely needed unless spread to bone or deeper tissue is suspected. [S1]
- **Species matters:** *S. schenckii* usually causes chronic subcutaneous disease, whereas zoonotic *S. brasiliensis* (spreading from Brazil) often causes more severe infection. [S1]

## Sporotrichosis — treat

- ⚠️ **Cutaneous and lymphocutaneous disease: itraconazole 200 mg orally once daily, continued for 2–4 weeks after the lesions resolve** (typically 3–6 months in total). Response rates with itraconazole are 80–100%. [S1]
- **Alternatives: terbinafine 500 mg orally twice daily, or itraconazole increased to 200 mg twice daily.** Do not use voriconazole or isavuconazole, which appear ineffective. Potassium iodide works but is poorly tolerated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Disseminated or severe pulmonary sporotrichosis: liposomal amphotericin B 3–5 mg/kg daily (or deoxycholate amphotericin 0·7–1·0 mg/kg daily), then step down to itraconazole 200 mg twice daily for at least 12 months** once the patient is improving. [S1]
