# Secondary hyperparathyroidism and mineral–bone disorder in CKD

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults and children with CKD G3a–G5D: monitoring calcium, phosphate, PTH and vitamin D; lowering phosphate; when to use calcitriol or vitamin D analogues; PTH targets and PTH-lowering treatment (calcimimetics such as cinacalcet) on dialysis; parathyroidectomy. General CKD detection and management in primary care: see `chronic-kidney-disease`. High phosphate in children: `hyperphosphataemia-children`.

> ⚠️ S1 is an international guideline (KDIGO, 2017). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

It covers what a GP checks and when to involve a nephrologist; the treatment targets and drug choices on dialysis rest on KDIGO (S1). S2 defers most CKD-MBD management to nephrologists.

## Monitor

- **Start checking calcium, phosphate, PTH and alkaline phosphatase from CKD G3a in adults (1C),** and from G2 in children (2D). How often depends on how abnormal the results are and how fast CKD is progressing. [S1]
- **Consider measuring 25(OH) vitamin D,** and treat deficiency the same way as in the general population. (2C) [S1]
- **Act on trends, not one result,** and look at calcium and phosphate separately rather than as a calcium-phosphate product. (1C, 2D) [S1]
- **PTH or bone-specific alkaline phosphatase can indicate bone turnover** when markedly high or low (2B). Test bone density if the result would change treatment (2B). [S1]
- 🇦🇺 **Australian primary care (S2):** calcium, phosphate, PTH and vitamin D metabolism usually start to change once GFR is 60 or below, and the changes are linked to fracture and cardiovascular death, possibly through vascular calcification. Managing CKD-MBD is complex and usually runs through a nephrologist. [S2]
- 🇦🇺 **When a GP tests (S2):** CKD-MBD usually does not need testing or treatment in stages 1–3. In the action plans for eGFR 30–59 with microalbuminuria (or 30–44 without) and for eGFR below 30, consider calcium and phosphate, and PTH every 6–12 months once eGFR is below 45. [S2]
- 🇦🇺 **Bone density (S2):** in stages 3–5 with CKD-MBD or osteoporosis risk factors, BMD testing is suggested when the result would change treatment; low DXA BMD predicts fracture, and no one anti-resorptive is favoured. [S2]

## Phosphate and calcium

- **Base treatment on serial phosphate, calcium and PTH taken together.** Bring high phosphate down toward normal, and avoid hypercalcaemia in adults (keep calcium in the age-appropriate range in children). (2C) [S1]
- **Start phosphate-lowering only for phosphate that is rising or stays high.** In adults, limit calcium-based binders (2B); avoid long-term aluminium binders (1C). [S1]
- **Limit dietary phosphate,** taking its source (animal, plant, additives) into account (2D). On dialysis, increase dialytic phosphate removal for persistent hyperphosphataemia (2C), and use a dialysate calcium of 1.25–1.50 mmol/L (2C). [S1]
- 🇦🇺 **S2:** bring phosphate toward the normal range (a kidney dietitian can help cut dietary phosphate) and avoid hypercalcaemia; do not overload with calcium supplements, because CKD brings vascular calcification. [S2]

## PTH before dialysis (CKD G3a–G5)

- **The ideal PTH before dialysis is unknown.** If intact PTH keeps rising or stays above the assay's upper limit, look for correctable causes such as raised serum phosphate, low calcium, a diet high in phosphate, or low vitamin D. (2C) [S1]
- **A modest PTH rise may be an appropriate adaptation** as kidney function falls, so do not start treatment on a single high value. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not routinely give calcitriol or vitamin D analogues to adults before dialysis (2C).** Reserve them for severe hyperparathyroidism that keeps worsening in stage G4–G5. In children they may be used to keep calcium in the normal range. [S1]
- **Why:** the PRIMO and OPERA trials of paricalcitol showed no heart benefit but more hypercalcaemia. If calcitriol or an analogue is started, begin with a low dose, titrate to PTH, and avoid hypercalcaemia. [S1]
- 🇦🇺 **Vitamin D (S2):** in early CKD, vitamin D can be checked and topped up if low; in more advanced CKD with hypocalcaemia, calcitriol is preferred because the kidney can no longer hydroxylate 25-hydroxyvitamin D. S2 also says the optimal PTH in CKD is not known. [S2]
- ⚠️🇦🇺 **Denosumab and other CKD-MBD treatments in stages 3–5: talk to a nephrologist before starting** (S2). Serial calcium, phosphate and PTH guide treatment, and the risk of hypocalcaemia after denosumab climbs as CKD advances, so watch calcium closely. [S2]

## PTH on dialysis (CKD G5D)

- **Target:** keep intact PTH at roughly 2 to 9 times the assay's upper normal limit (2C). A marked change in either direction within that range should prompt starting or adjusting treatment. [S1]
- ⚠️ **To lower PTH on dialysis, use a calcimimetic (e.g. cinacalcet), calcitriol or a vitamin D analogue, or a calcimimetic plus calcitriol/analogue.** (2B) KDIGO lists them alphabetically, not in order of preference. [S1]
- **Cinacalcet evidence (EVOLVE):** the trial missed its primary endpoint of death or major vascular events, though secondary analyses suggested benefit, especially in older patients. Its higher cost weighed against making it first-line. [S1]
- **Choose the agent by the current calcium and phosphate and other treatments;** dialysate calcium also affects PTH. [S1]
- **Children:** evidence for cinacalcet is limited to small series; use PTH-lowering drugs cautiously to avoid hypocalcaemia in a growing skeleton. [S1]
- **Refer for parathyroidectomy** when severe hyperparathyroidism does not respond to medical treatment, at any stage from G3a to G5D. (2B) [S1]
