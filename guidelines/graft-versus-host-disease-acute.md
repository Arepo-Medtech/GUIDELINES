# Acute graft-versus-host disease (aGVHD): prophylaxis, grading and treatment

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation

## Recognise

- **Acute GVHD develops in 20 to 50% of allogeneic transplant recipients; about 15% have severe (grade III-IV) disease.** Comorbidities before transplant raise both the rate and the severity. [S1]
- **HLA mismatch is the strongest risk factor.** Others include a multiparous female donor for a male recipient, conditioning intensity, total body irradiation, a peripheral blood graft (cord blood carries least risk), the type and status of the underlying disease, and an older recipient. [S1]
- **Skin, gut and liver are the classic targets.** Skin is usually hit first and most often: a widespread maculopapular rash with a liking for the palms, soles, ears, neck, backs of the limbs and cheeks. [S1]
- **Liver GVHD is staged on bilirubin alone** and is easily confused with other liver problems: veno-occlusive disease, sepsis, viral infection, drug toxicity, iron overload or cholestasis from TPN. **Lower GI GVHD** is a secretory diarrhoea that can progress to frank blood, severe pain or ileus at its worst. [S1]

## Work-up and grading

- **If acute GVHD is suspected, obtain an organ biopsy and liver tests**, and exclude other causes such as drug toxicity, infection or sinusoidal obstruction syndrome. [S1]
- **Tests by organ:** a skin punch biopsy for rash; for diarrhoea, stool microbiology (including C. difficile and cryptosporidium) and flexible sigmoidoscopy with biopsy looking for CMV; for jaundice or abnormal liver tests, hepatitis A to E and CMV testing and a liver Doppler ultrasound to exclude SOS, with a hepatologist involved before any liver biopsy. [S1]
- **Stage and grade with the MAGIC criteria** (Glucksberg is still acceptable). Each organ is staged 0 to 4 (skin by the share of body surface with rash, liver by bilirubin, gut by nausea, vomiting, anorexia and stool volume) and the stages combine into one overall grade. [S1]
- **MAGIC stage thresholds:** rash under 25%, 25 - 50% and over 50% of body surface, with stage 4 being erythroderma plus bullae or desquamation over 5%; bilirubin 34 - 50, 51 - 102, 103 - 255 and above 255 micromol/L; diarrhoea 500 - 999, 1000 - 1500 and over 1500 mL/day, with stage 4 being severe pain, ileus or grossly bloody stool. [S1]
- **Overall grade:** grade I is skin stage 1 - 2 alone; grade II is skin stage 3 and/or stage 1 in liver, lower or upper gut; grade III is liver and/or lower gut stage 2 - 3; grade IV is stage 4 in skin, liver or lower gut. [S1]

## First-line treatment

- **Treat on the clinical picture.** A biopsy before treatment is recommended, but do not hold treatment while waiting for histology. [S1]
- **Grade I: supportive care and topical steroid,** with triamcinolone 0.1% on the body and hydrocortisone 0.5% on the face, used two or three times a day when needed. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Grade II-IV:** keep or restart the original immunosuppressant at therapeutic levels, use topical steroid as for grade I, consider a trial and consider anti-infective prophylaxis that covers moulds. Systemic treatment is 2 mg/kg/day of methylprednisolone (or 2 - 2.5 mg/kg/day of prednisone), in one or two doses, for at least 7 days; for grade II confined to skin or upper gut, methylprednisolone 0.5 - 1 mg/kg/day may do. [S1]
- **No standard taper exists.** Do not reduce during the first 7 days; parenteral steroid can be switched to oral until the GVHD settles, and the taper should be slow and response-led. [S1]
- **Gut GVHD:** a non-absorbable oral steroid (budesonide 9 mg daily, or beclomethasone 1.3 - 2 mg four times daily) can be added to the systemic steroid. [S1]

## Steroid-refractory disease

- ⚠️ **Steroid-resistant GVHD:** progression after 3 days of high-dose steroid, no response by day 7, or failure during the taper; it needs second-line therapy. High-dose steroids work in only 40–50%, the day-28 response best predicts 2-year transplant-related mortality, and refractory disease often kills through infection and organ failure. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Ruxolitinib is PBS-funded for grade II to IV acute GVHD after steroids, when the disease is steroid-refractory, steroid-dependent or the patient cannot tolerate steroids** (phase 3 REACH2 trial). The recommended dose is 5 - 10 mg twice daily; adjust for anaemia, thrombocytopenia or interactions such as azole antifungals, and consider tapering after response once off steroids. [S1]
- **Beyond that, there is no clear best option and responses are poor:** needing second-line therapy goes with a 1-year survival of 20-30%. Enrol in trials where possible. [S1]

## Supportive care

- **Gut:** watch fluid balance and electrolytes closely, and move immunosuppressants to IV to get round poor absorption. Once infection is excluded, give loperamide 4-8 mg stat, reassess at 24 hours, then 2 mg every 2 hours up to 16 mg in 24 hours; for persisting severe diarrhoea, octreotide 100 to 150 mcg subcut every 8 hours for up to 7 days (up to 500 mcg every 8 hours if needed), stopped within 24 hours of the diarrhoea settling to avoid ileus. A dietitian may be needed, as some patients need parenteral nutrition. [S1]
- **Skin:** emollients, antihistamines for itch and gentle hypoallergenic soap. **Liver:** stop hepatotoxic drugs, azoles included, and consider ursodeoxycholic acid, 12 mg/kg each day split into several doses. [S1]

## Prophylaxis

- **Prevention is critical, because severe GVHD that fails first-line treatment has a very poor outlook.** No regimen is agreed as standard; the commonest is a calcineurin inhibitor (ciclosporin or tacrolimus) plus methotrexate. Mycophenolate with a calcineurin inhibitor is used mainly after reduced-intensity conditioning, where acute GVHD risk is lower. [S1]
- **Matched unrelated donors, and matched related donors at high GVHD risk: add ATG to standard prophylaxis.** In phase 3 trials ATG cut both acute and chronic GVHD without more relapse or non-relapse deaths, but it raises infection risk including EBV-driven PTLD, so monitor EBV viral load weekly. **Haploidentical transplants:** post-transplant cyclophosphamide 50 mg/kg on days +3 and +4, then tacrolimus with mycophenolate. [S1]
- **Methotrexate:** 15 mg/m² IV on day +1, then 10 mg/m² on days +3, +6 and +11; lower doses (commonly 5 mg/m²) after reduced-intensity conditioning. Reduce it for major fluid overload, renal or hepatic impairment, or very severe mucositis. [S1]
- **Ciclosporin:** usually started IV on day -1 at 3 mg/kg/day, then given orally every 12 hours at two to three times the IV dose once tolerated, with the dose guided by trough levels per local protocol (usually 100-300). It interacts with many drugs, so watch levels and kidney function closely; brands are not interchangeable. [S1]
