# Testicular and extragonadal germ cell tumours

## Recognise and diagnose

- **Testicular cancer is the commonest tumour in males aged 15–39.** Suspect it with any solid, painless testicular lump; about 10% present with metastatic symptoms (back pain, leg swelling, breathlessness, cough, neck mass, gynaecomastia). Investigate at once: delay worsens stage. [S1]
- **Work-up:** bilateral testicular ultrasound, bloods, tumour markers (AFP, beta-hCG, LDH) before and after orchiectomy, and CT chest, abdomen and pelvis. Brain imaging for more than 5 lung nodules, poor IGCCCG risk or beta-hCG above 5000 mIU/ml. FDG-PET has no staging role. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Radical inguinal orchiectomy is mandatory; never do a trans-scrotal biopsy or orchiectomy** (it alters lymphatic drainage). With life-threatening metastatic disease, start chemotherapy first and delay orchiectomy. [S1]
- **Normal markers do not exclude a germ cell tumour.** A raised AFP rules out pure seminoma. After orchiectomy, markers should fall with their half-lives (AFP 5–7 days, beta-hCG 1–3 days); failure to fall means residual disease. [S1]
- **Offer fertility counselling and sperm banking routinely before any treatment, ideally before orchiectomy.** [S1]

## Stage I disease

- **Stage I seminoma:** active surveillance is preferred for most; a single dose of carboplatin (AUC 7) is the alternative, especially with more than one risk factor. Use para-aortic radiotherapy only if chemotherapy is contraindicated. [S1]
- **Stage I non-seminoma:** orchiectomy alone cures about 75%. Lymphovascular invasion raises relapse risk to about 50%. Surveillance is excellent for all; alternatively one cycle of BEP cuts relapse below 5% in patients with lymphovascular invasion. [S1]

## Advanced disease

- ⚠️ **BEP (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin) is standard,** chosen by IGCCCG risk: three cycles for good risk, four for intermediate and poor risk. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Bleomycin may be unsuitable** over age 40, with lung disease, heavy smoking, jobs needing high lung capacity, mediastinal tumours or planned lung surgery or radiotherapy. Check spirometry and DLCO at baseline and during treatment; stop bleomycin if DLCO falls by more than 25%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Bleomycin toxicity presents as sudden cough or breathlessness.** Adjust the dose when creatinine clearance is below 50 ml/min, and keep the total cumulative dose within 360–400 units. [S1]
- **Without bleomycin:** four cycles of EP for good risk; VIP with G-CSF for intermediate or poor risk. Carboplatin combinations are inferior to cisplatin ones. [S1]
- **Give full doses on time with G-CSF support;** reduced dose intensity worsens outcomes. Check markers before each cycle: an inadequate fall after one or two cycles signals poorer prognosis. [S1]
- **Treat high-risk and relapsed patients in experienced multidisciplinary centres.** [S1]

## Residual masses and special situations

- **Non-seminoma with normal markers and residual retroperitoneal nodes of 1 cm or more: nerve-sparing RPLND** (PET is not useful). **Seminoma residuals under 3 cm: surveillance;** larger ones need PET at least 6 weeks after the last bleomycin. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Choriocarcinoma syndrome or high ARDS risk** (extensive lung metastases, hypoxaemia): start with a dose-reduced induction such as 2–3 day EP or baby-BOP, then full cycles once stable. Prevent tumour lysis when tumour volume is large. [S1]
- **Consider thromboprophylaxis in higher-risk patients** (retroperitoneal disease over 3.5 cm, stage III or poor risk) and avoid central venous devices where possible. [S1]

## Relapse and salvage

- **Relapse after first-line chemotherapy (18–20%) is still potentially curable.** Options are conventional-dose TIP (four cycles with G-CSF) or high-dose chemotherapy with stem cell support; resect all residual lesions afterwards. Late relapse beyond 2 years: early complete surgery. [S1]

## Follow-up and survivorship

- **Stage I seminoma on surveillance:** abdomen and pelvis imaging every 6 months for 3 years, then yearly in years 4 and 5. Chest imaging is no longer routine. Include testicular self-examination and ultrasound in follow-up. [S1]
- **Survivors carry late risks:** second cancers, cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, hypogonadism (11–35%), neuropathy, hearing loss and tinnitus. Counsel on lifestyle and smoking cessation, and monitor blood pressure, cholesterol and testosterone. [S1]
