# Cytomegalovirus retinitis

## Who is at risk

- **CMV retinitis is the commonest eye manifestation of CMV in immunocompromised people,** affecting about 20–40% of people with HIV. HTLV-1, common among Aboriginal adults in Australia, also predisposes to it. [S1]
- **In HIV, a CD4 count below 50 cells/µL should prompt early assessment;** CD4 can also guide screening. Non-HIV patients (transplant, autoimmune disease, lymphoma or chemotherapy) are a growing at-risk group. [S1]

## Recognise

- **Early retinitis may be symptom-free; symptoms are non-specific** (floaters, flashes, blurred vision, blind spots). Lesions start peripherally and advance toward the posterior pole; untreated, retinitis causes retinal detachment and irreversible vision loss. [S1]
- **Typical fundus appearances:** wedge-shaped, haemorrhagic or granular retinitis with little or no vitritis; central necrosis with haemorrhage gives the 'pizza pie' look. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Zone 1 (within 1500 µm of the optic disc or 3000 µm of the fovea) threatens central vision:** involvement of the macula or optic nerve causes major visual loss. [S1]

## Diagnose

- **The diagnosis is made on fundus examination,** supported by aqueous humour PCR for active CMV replication. Blood or urine CMV tests can help, but a negative result does not exclude retinitis. [S1]
- **Exclude the mimics:** progressive outer retinal necrosis, acute retinal necrosis, syphilitic retinitis, tuberculosis and toxoplasmosis. [S1]

## Treat

- **First-line antivirals are ganciclovir, valganciclovir, cidofovir and foscarnet,** given systemically (IV or oral) and/or locally (intravitreal injection or implant). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Intravenous ganciclovir 5 mg/kg over 1 hour every 12 hours for 7 to 14 days, then 5 mg/kg once daily 7 days a week (or 6 mg/kg once daily 5 days a week).** Watch for myelosuppression and liver toxicity. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Oral valganciclovir 900 mg twice daily for induction is as effective and safe as IV ganciclovir** and easier to take; it can cause neutropenia, anaemia and thrombocytopenia. [S1]
- **Zone 1 disease: consider intravitreal ganciclovir injections or a slow-release ganciclovir implant**; intravitreal therapy carries risks of vitreous haemorrhage and retinal detachment. [S1]
- **Ganciclovir-resistant or intolerant disease:** foscarnet 60 mg/kg IV every 12 hours for 14 days (nephrotoxic; monitor renal function and electrolytes), or cidofovir 5 mg/kg IV weekly for 2 weeks then every 2 weeks (proteinuria, renal failure, neutropenia). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Maribavir penetrates the retina poorly** (vitreous-to-plasma ratio up to 0.28), so it is a poor choice for retinitis despite its activity against resistant CMV. [S1]
- **Antiretroviral therapy has greatly reduced CMV retinitis in HIV,** but people with pre-existing retinitis who start it can develop immune recovery uveitis, a common cause of new vision loss. [S1]
- **Retinal detachment from CMV retinitis is repaired by vitrectomy** (silicone oil repair succeeds in 56–94% of HIV patients). [S1]

## Monitor

- **During treatment monitor renal function, full blood count, liver function, CD4 count and viral load,** and arrange regular ophthalmology reviews to track the retinitis. A final CD4 above 50 cells/mm³ is associated with better survival in AIDS. [S1]
