# Carbamazepine poisoning in children

## Key points

- ⚠️ **Onset can be prolonged or delayed — children need observation for clinical features to develop.** [S1]
- **Serum concentrations correlate reasonably with clinical effects** and are particularly helpful in large ingestions. [S1]
- **Consider decontamination for large ingestions, with a toxicologist.** 24-hour advice: Poisons Information Centre 13 11 26. [S1]

## Clinical course

- **Onset: 1–2 hours for immediate-release, 4–8 hours for sustained-release.** Features: nausea and vomiting, drowsiness, anticholinergic effects, seizures, coma and life-threatening arrhythmias. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Large ingestions can give prolonged or delayed (over 48 hours) symptoms** — slow, erratic absorption from anticholinergic ileus. [S1]

## Who needs assessment

- **Assess:** any deliberate self-poisoning · accidental ingestion over 20 mg/kg (or over 20 mg/kg above the usual daily dose if on carbamazepine) · any symptomatic child · unknown quantity. **Consider non-accidental poisoning** if developmental age doesn't fit. [S1]
- ⚠️ **50 mg/kg (or more than 3 g) is associated with significant toxicity.** Calculate the maximum possible dose per kg and note the formulation. [S1]
- **Examine for:** ataxia, nystagmus, drowsiness, coma, seizures · tachycardia, hypotension, ⚠️ **heart block, QRS widening, VF** · urinary retention, reduced bowel motility, dry mouth. [S1]

## Investigations

- **ECG at presentation and repeat at 6 hours until normal.** Paracetamol level in all intentional overdoses. [S1]
- **Carbamazepine levels:** 5–12 mg/L (20–50 micromol/L) therapeutic · 10–20 mg/L (40–85) nystagmus, sedation, ataxia · 20–40 mg/L (85–170) horizontal and vertical nystagmus, coma. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Above 170 micromol/L (40 mg/L): respiratory depression, seizures, arrhythmia.** Severe features can appear in children at lower concentrations than in adults. [S1]

## Treatment

- **Treat:** all symptomatic children · unknown quantity · over 20 mg/kg if not usually on carbamazepine · over 20 mg/kg above the usual daily dose if on it. [S1]
- **Support blood pressure and electrolytes; IV fluid 20 mL/kg for hypovolaemia or hypotension.** [S1]
- **Decontamination only with a toxicologist.** Consider **activated charcoal for massive ingestion (over 50 mg/kg) within 24 hours**, or after a large controlled-release ingestion. ⚠️ **With CNS toxicity, give charcoal only once the airway is secured.** [S1]
- **For large ingestions with bowel sounds present, multidose activated charcoal may be indicated** — discuss it with a toxicologist. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Seizures: benzodiazepines for any that don't self-resolve. Sodium-channel-blocking anticonvulsants (e.g. phenytoin) are relatively contraindicated.** [S1]

## Observation and disposition

- **Mild symptoms (ataxia, blurred vision): observe at least 8 hours after ingestion, longer for controlled-release;** discharge once symptom-free. [S1]
- **Moderate–severe or persisting after 8 hours (depressed conscious state, arrhythmia): admit** for observation and supportive care; discuss level monitoring with a toxicologist and severe cases with PICU. [S1]
- **Consider admission for every intentional overdose** and any child still symptomatic after 8 hours. [S1]
- **Consider transfer** for severe symptoms that may need intensive care, or care beyond the hospital's comfort level. [S1]
- **Discharge when:** normal GCS, normal ECG, and symptom-free after 8 hours of observation. [S1]
- **Adolescents with deliberate ingestion need a self-harm risk assessment and drug and alcohol assessment before discharge**, with links to services if needs are found. [S1]
