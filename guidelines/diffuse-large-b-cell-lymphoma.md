# Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (large B-cell lymphoma)

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, staging, first-line treatment by risk group, special subtypes (double/triple-hit, primary mediastinal, testicular, intravascular), older and unfit patients, CNS prophylaxis, relapsed/refractory disease and survivorship for adults with large B-cell lymphoma. ## Diagnosis and staging

- **Have the biopsy reported by a reference haematopathology laboratory.** Immunohistochemistry misclassifies cell of origin in up to 30% of cases, so do not withhold an active therapy on that basis alone. [S1]
- **Test for MYC rearrangement by FISH, and if present test for BCL2 and BCL6,** to separate DLBCL NOS from lymphoma with MYC and BCL2 and/or BCL6 rearrangement. [S1]
- **Stage with PET-CT; calculate the IPI (mandatory) and CNS-IPI (strongly advised) before treatment.** Use MRI for brain or leptomeningeal disease, add MRI and CSF analysis for patients at high CNS risk, and reserve marrow biopsy for a suspected second blood disorder. [S1]
- **An end-of-treatment PET is required for everyone.** Deauville 1–3 means complete metabolic response; because a positive scan is right only about 60% of the time, biopsy or re-scan residual uptake before acting on it. [S1]

## First-line treatment (under 80)

- **Stage I/II, no risk factors, non-bulky: four cycles of R-CHOP21** (IB). Up to age 80, a PET after two cycles showing complete metabolic response supports stopping at four. [S1]
- **Bulky disease (largest mass 7.5 cm or more): consider six cycles of R-CHOP21** (IA). [S1]
- **One risk factor (stage III/IV, or stage I/II with poor performance status or high LDH): six cycles of R-CHOP21** (I, A). In early-stage disease, radiotherapy is usually unnecessary once six full-dose cycles achieve complete metabolic response. [S1]
- **IPI 2–5: six cycles of Pola-R-CHP are preferred** (I, B), after a 6.5% gain in 2-year PFS over R-CHOP in POLARIX; R-CHOP is an acceptable fallback. Two-weekly R-CHOP is no better than three-weekly, and upfront high-dose therapy with autograft gives no survival gain. [S1]
- **In advanced disease, radiotherapy is only for a single PET-positive residual site;** PET-negative patients do not routinely need it. (II, B) [S1]
- **Consider primary G-CSF and antimicrobial prophylaxis for all curative chemo-immunotherapy,** especially in patients over 60 or on more intensive regimens. Calcium channel blockers may blunt anti-CD20 antibodies, so consider switching them. [S1]

## Australian regimens and supportive care (S2, eviQ)

- **R-CHOP21, every 21 days for 6 to 8 cycles:** day 1 rituximab 375 mg/m2, doxorubicin 50 mg/m2, vincristine 1.4 mg/m2 (capped at 2 mg) and cyclophosphamide 750 mg/m2; prednisolone 100 mg by mouth on days 1 to 5. All drugs are on the PBS general schedule. [S2]
- **Where the sources differ, favourable early-stage disease:** S2 suggests considering 4 cycles of R-CHOP21 plus 2 further doses of rituximab for patients aged 60 or under with favourable DLBCL (age-adjusted IPI 0, no mass of 7.5 cm or more); S1 gives four cycles of R-CHOP21 alone, with an interim PET guiding the stop. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Pola-R-CHP, every 21 days:** cycles 1 to 6 give polatuzumab vedotin 1.8 mg/kg with rituximab 375 mg/m2, doxorubicin 50 mg/m2 and cyclophosphamide 750 mg/m2 on day 1 and prednisolone 100 mg on days 1 to 5, plus filgrastim 5 micrograms/kg daily from day 2 until neutrophil recovery; cycles 7 and 8 are rituximab alone. Polatuzumab is TGA-registered but not PBS-listed. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Anthracycline heart monitoring (S2):** count lifetime anthracycline dose; baseline echocardiogram (or gated heart pool scan) and ECG. With normal baseline LVEF (above 50%) and low risk, recheck LVEF once over 70% of the anthracycline threshold or if symptoms appear; in high-risk patients, echo every 6 weeks and within 3 months of finishing; in all adults, an echo within 12 months after anthracycline treatment ends. [S2]
- **Supportive care with R-CHOP-type therapy (S2):** screen for hepatitis B (HBsAg, anti-HBc, anti-HBs) before starting; tumour lysis risk is high, so give prophylaxis; consider withholding antihypertensives for 12 hours before and during each rituximab infusion (hypotension risk); give laxatives with vincristine and assess neuropathy before each cycle; G-CSF is PBS-available by febrile neutropenia risk; no live vaccines. [S2]

## Special situations

- ⚠️ **High CNS relapse risk:** CNS-IPI 4–6 (10–12% risk), two or more extranodal sites, or testis, breast, epidural, orbit, kidney or adrenal involvement. Get MRI and CSF (cytology and flow cytometry) as early as possible, ideally before cycle 1. (III, A) [S1]
- **CNS prophylaxis: intrathecal methotrexate is not recommended.** Two cycles of high-dose IV methotrexate (at least 3 g/m²) may be considered (III, B) for high-risk patients who reach complete metabolic response with Pola-R-CHP or R-CHOP. [S1]
- **Double- or triple-hit lymphoma:** consider an intensive regimen (R-CODOX-M/R-IVAC, DA-EPOCH-R, R-ACVBP or R-CHOEP14) for selected patients (III, B); keep R-CHOP21 for the unfit. Pola-R-CHP does not seem better than R-CHOP here, and autograft consolidation is not recommended. [S1]
- **Primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma:** use R-CHOP14 or a more intensive regimen (R-M/VACOP-B, R-ACVBP, DA-EPOCH-R) and avoid R-CHOP21. Skip mediastinal radiotherapy after complete metabolic response; a DS5 result (Deauville) needs salvage therapy. [S1]
- **Primary testicular lymphoma:** six cycles of R-CHOP, radiotherapy to the other testis (strongly advised) and CNS prophylaxis. [S1]
- **Age 80 or over, or unfit:** use a geriatric assessment, then R-miniCHOP for six cycles in fit patients (I, B), which reduces doxorubicin to 25 mg/m², cyclophosphamide to 400 mg/m² and vincristine to 1 mg. A corticosteroid prephase, optionally with one 1 mg IV dose of vincristine, improves performance status. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Heart failure or LVEF below 50%: use a regimen without conventional doxorubicin** (R-CEOP, R-GCVP, R-COMP with non-pegylated liposomal doxorubicin, or R-GEMOX). After earlier anthracycline, work within the 450 mg/m² lifetime limit. [S1]

## Relapsed or refractory disease

- **Biopsy before second-line treatment** (III, B), to confirm relapse, exclude another diagnosis and check CD19 and CD20 expression; biopsy again before third line because target antigen can be lost. [S1]
- **Refractory disease or relapse within 12 months: offer CAR T-cells (axi-cel or liso-cel).** (I, A) Axi-cel improved 4-year overall survival (55% vs 46%). CAR T-cells may not suit patients with performance status 2 or worse, a large tumour burden or fast-rising LDH. [S1]
- **Relapse more than 1 year after first-line therapy in a fit patient: salvage immunochemotherapy** (R-DHAX, R-ICE, R-GDP or R-ESHAP), then high-dose therapy (such as BEAM) and autograft if chemosensitive (I, B). Long-term PFS is about 40% in late relapse but under 15–20% in early relapse or primary refractory disease. [S1]
- **Not a candidate for CAR T-cells or transplant: R-GemOx or Pola-BR** (III, B); consider glofitamab with GemOx (I, A), which improved median OS (25.5 vs 12.9 months) over R-GemOx, or tafasitamab-lenalidomide if the disease is not refractory. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Bendamustine may weaken T-cell fitness** and so reduce the effect of later bispecific antibodies (epcoritamab, glofitamab, odronextamab). [S1]
- **Third line and beyond:** if CAR T-cells were not used in second line, offer them (III, A); if already CAR T-exposed, offer a CD20xCD3 bispecific (epcoritamab, glofitamab, odronextamab), loncastuximab tesirine, tafasitamab-lenalidomide or immunochemotherapy, and consider a trial. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Glofitamab (PBS authority, relapsed or refractory DLBCL after two or more lines), every 21 days for 12 cycles:** obinutuzumab 1,000 mg on cycle 1 day 1 to deplete B cells, then glofitamab step-up 2.5 mg on day 8 and 10 mg on day 15, then 30 mg on day 1 of cycles 2 to 12; dexamethasone 20 mg IV before each of the first doses. Consider a 24-hour admission after the first glofitamab dose (or at least 10 hours' observation) and after grade 2 or higher CRS. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Epcoritamab (PBS authority, after two or more lines), 28-day cycles until progression:** subcutaneous step-up 0.16 mg on day 1, 0.8 mg on day 8, then 48 mg on days 15 and 22; 48 mg weekly in cycles 2 and 3, fortnightly in cycles 4 to 9, then 4-weekly from cycle 10. Dexamethasone 16 mg on the dosing day and the 3 days after each cycle 1 dose. Consider a 24-hour admission after the first full dose (cycle 1 day 15), or keep the patient near a centre that can manage CRS and ICANS for 24 hours. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Bispecific antibody toxicities (S2):** cytokine release syndrome (fever, chills, hypotension, tachycardia, hypoxia) can be fatal and may need interruption, steroids or tocilizumab, so confirm tocilizumab is on hand before starting; ICANS can come with, after or without CRS; tumour flare (pain, swelling) in early cycles can mimic progression and threaten airways when bulky; think of HLH when CRS is atypical or unresponsive. [S2]

## Follow-up and survivorship

- **Do not scan asymptomatic patients routinely** (3B). Review history, examination and blood count for at least 2 years. Being event-free at 2 years means survival matches the general population, and 5 relapse-free years means cure. [S1]
- **Late effects:** cardiology review and echocardiography within 1 year of finishing, then at 3 and 5 years and every 5 years; breast screening from 8 years after mediastinal radiotherapy; TSH at 1, 2 and 5 years after neck radiotherapy. [S1]
