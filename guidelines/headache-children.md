# Headache in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** red flags, investigation threshold, and acute treatment of tension-type, migraine and cluster headache in children, including ED migraine therapy. The adult counterpart is `migraine`; related: `head-injury-children`, `meningitis-and-encephalitis-children`.

> ✅ **PARAPHRASED, HASH-ANCHORED.** 20 of 20 claims paraphrased from the **RCH Melbourne Clinical Practice Guideline *Headache*** (Last updated December 2022); **24 anchors re-checkable by machine; 7 doses.** The RCH text is not reproduced: its terms licence personal use only.

> ⚠️ **Look for red flags first. No red flags: no investigations.** Simple analgesia is the mainstay; **opioids are not recommended.**

---

## Red flags

- ⚠️ **History red flags:** age under 4 · early-morning headache · waking from sleep · triggered by coughing, sneezing or position change · sudden severe onset · occipital · vomiting without another cause · a changed or progressively worsening pattern. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Also:** focal neurological symptoms, new seizures, developmental regression, meningitis/encephalitis features, significant head injury, relevant systemic disorder, anticoagulant or antiplatelet use, **VP shunt.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Examination red flags:** altered conscious state, new focal signs, abnormal head position, head circumference crossing centiles upward, **raised ICP (papilloedema, ataxia, bradycardia with hypertension)**, meningism. [S1]
- **Examine** BP and conscious state, a full neurological examination including fundoscopy, and look for neurocutaneous stigmata. [S1]
- **Most common causes:** migraine and tension-type (primary); viral illness (secondary). Serious secondary causes include CNS infection, raised ICP and haemorrhage. [S1]

## Investigate

- **No red flags → no routine investigations.** **Red flags → senior discussion, and think about intracranial imaging.** [S1]

## General advice

- ⚠️ **Prevent medication-overuse headache: simple analgesics under 15 days a month, triptans under 10 days a month.** Hydration, sleep hygiene, exercise, trigger avoidance, and address emotional stressors. [S1]

## Tension-type headache

- **Paracetamol 15 mg/kg (max 1 g) or ibuprofen 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg).** ⚠️ **Do not offer opioids.** [S1]

## Migraine

- **Early in the attack, usually at home: ibuprofen 10 mg/kg (max 400 mg) or paracetamol 15 mg/kg (max 1 g)**, fluids, and rest in a quiet dark room. [S1]
- **Rizatriptan wafer if oral analgesics fail:** over 6 years and under 40 kg 5 mg; over 40 kg 10 mg orally; **may repeat once after 2 hours (max 2 doses in 24 hours).** Sumatriptan nasal spray is no longer available. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Propranolol raises rizatriptan levels** — avoid or use a lower dose with specialist advice. ⚠️ **Avoid opioids in migraine.** [S1]
- **In ED:** give ibuprofen and paracetamol if they haven't been given yet; **for vomiting, ondansetron 0.15 mg/kg oral/IV (max 8 mg).** [S1]
- **8 years and over: IV chlorpromazine 0.25 mg/kg, up to 12.5 mg**, diluted in 10–20 mL/kg 0.9% sodium chloride (max 1 L) and run over 30–60 minutes, **or IV prochlorperazine 0.15 mg/kg, up to 12.5 mg**, in the same fluid over 30–60 minutes (a single 12.5 mg dose may be given over 12 years and 50 kg). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Both can cause hypotension: monitor BP and give with IV fluid**, plus more fluid if needed. [S1]
- **Therapeutic nerve blocks** are an option, done by anaesthetic/pain specialists or experienced ED clinicians. [S1]
- **Aura:** focal neurological symptoms (most often visual, sensory or speech) that precede the headache and last under an hour. [S1]

## Cluster headache

- **Simple analgesia has not been shown to work. Give 100% oxygen at 12 L/min or more via non-rebreather with reservoir.** **Rizatriptan:** over 6 years and under 40 kg 5 mg, over 40 kg 10 mg orally; may repeat once after at least 2 hours (max 2 doses in 24 hours). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Cluster headache may have a secondary cause — low threshold for neuroimaging.** [S1]

## Escalation and discharge

- **Involve the paediatric team for any red flag.** Consider transfer if neurosurgery or care beyond the local facility is needed. [S1]
- **Discharge** when clinically stable with no red flags and follow-up arranged. A migraine is unlikely to fully resolve in ED; continue rest at home, with GP or paediatrician follow-up for ongoing care and preventives. [S1]
