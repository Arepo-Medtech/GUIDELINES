# Lupus nephritis and ANCA-associated vasculitis

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** kidney-related care of lupus nephritis (including classes III–V) and ANCA-associated vasculitis in adults, from the CARI (Australia and New Zealand) commentary on the KDIGO 2021 glomerular disease guideline, plus the general glomerular-disease points that apply to both. Extra-renal SLE is not covered. Other glomerular diseases (IgA nephropathy, membranous, FSGS, children's nephrotic syndrome): see `nephrotic-syndrome-children`; general CKD care: see `chronic-kidney-disease`. Edition 2.0 adds S2 (EULAR 2023) for lupus nephritis and for its general SLE drug points (hydroxychloroquine, glucocorticoid limits, belimumab or anifrolumab); organ-specific extra-renal care stays out of scope.

> ⚠️ **Several KDIGO lupus-nephritis options are not available in Australia or New Zealand:** belimumab (for lupus nephritis) and voclosporin. The commentary predates the PBS listing of obinutuzumab for lupus nephritis and does not discuss it.

> 🇦🇺 **S1 is the Australian and New Zealand reference (CARI 2025)** for both conditions. **S2** (EULAR 2023 SLE recommendations) adds lupus nephritis response targets, drug doses and the place of add-on biologics, plus general SLE drug points. S2 covers lupus only, not ANCA vasculitis.

## Before treatment

- **Report kidney biopsies with a validated international scoring system** (for lupus nephritis, the ISN/RPS classification). Biopsy remains key in glomerular disease when the cause is not clear. [S1]
- **With marked proteinuria in adults, prefer a timed urine collection** over a random spot sample to measure protein: it is more accurate and varies less. [S1]
- **Creatinine-based eGFR overestimates true GFR** in nephrotic syndrome with low albumin, and CKD-EPI is unvalidated in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander and Māori people. [S1]
- **Give people starting immunosuppression a third primary COVID vaccine dose** (ATAGI), ideally more than 2 weeks before treatment starts. This covers high-dose steroids (> 20 mg/day for > 14 days), mycophenolate, azathioprine, calcineurin inhibitors, cyclophosphamide and anti-CD20 agents. [S1]
- **Australian and NZ adjustments to KDIGO (CARI):** depending on the setting, serology or genetic tests may stand in for, or accompany, a kidney biopsy; and for patients under 25, CARI backs the 2021 CKiD U25 eGFR equation. For ANCA vasculitis, CARI endorses KDIGO unchanged. [S1]

## ANCA-associated vasculitis: diagnosis and monitoring

- ⚠️ **Do not wait for a biopsy or positive serology before treating AAV,** especially when kidney function is falling fast. [S1]
- **Do not base treatment decisions on ANCA titres alone:** positivity or rising titres do not reliably predict relapse. Loss of PR3-ANCA after rituximab goes with longer remission; turning from negative to positive goes with higher relapse risk. [S1]

## ANCA-associated vasculitis: treatment

- **Induce remission with a glucocorticoid plus cyclophosphamide or rituximab.** Prefer rituximab in children and adolescents, pre-menopausal women, men worried about fertility, older adults, relapsing disease and PR3-ANCA positive disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **With severe disease (serum creatinine > 354 μmol/L), prefer glucocorticoid plus cyclophosphamide.** IV cyclophosphamide suits a moderate cumulative dose, low white cell count or poor adherence, where an infusion centre is close. [S1]
- **Give pneumocystis prophylaxis for the whole cyclophosphamide course, or for 6 months after rituximab,** and monitor IgG for hypogammaglobulinaemia. [S1]
- **Use reduced-dose steroids:** with cyclophosphamide, bring prednisolone down to 5 mg/day within 6 months; with rituximab, stop it within 6 months. **Avacopan** can spare or replace glucocorticoids and has been PBS-listed for ANCA vasculitis since October 2024. [S1]
- **Reserve plasma exchange for specific cases:** diffuse alveolar haemorrhage, creatinine > 5.7 mg/dL (500 μmol/L) needing dialysis, rapidly rising creatinine, or combined AAV and anti-GBM disease. [S1]
- **In anti-GBM plus ANCA double-positive disease, maintain as for ANCA vasculitis.** [S1]

## Lupus nephritis: diagnosis

- **Proteinuria does not reliably track how severe the nephritis is,** so assess clinical and laboratory findings together. Base the decision to treat class III/IV disease on the biopsy. [S1]

## Lupus nephritis: treatment

- **Hydroxychloroquine for every patient.** First-line induction is a corticosteroid with a mycophenolic acid analogue (MPAA) or low-dose IV cyclophosphamide, starting with IV glucocorticoid pulses then a lower starting dose tapered quickly. [S1]
- **Choose between MPAA and low-dose cyclophosphamide on availability, adherence, age, severity, ethnicity and past treatment:** efficacy is similar and low-dose cyclophosphamide has fewer adverse events. Standard-dose IV cyclophosphamide may help severe disease (AKI, cellular crescents, fibrinoid necrosis). [S1]
- **Alternatives:** MPAA with a calcineurin inhibitor. ⚠️ Belimumab (for lupus nephritis) and voclosporin (which helps when eGFR > 45 mL/min/1.73 m2, with MPAA) are **not available in Australia or New Zealand**. [S1]
- **Maintain with low-dose glucocorticoid plus MPAA;** azathioprine is second line, then a calcineurin inhibitor or mizoribine. Continue for at least 36 months, stopping glucocorticoids after 12 months in complete remission. [S1]
- **Rituximab is an option for refractory disease or when steroids must be minimised.** Class V treatment depends on proteinuria, with RAAS blockade plus immunosuppression for nephrotic syndrome. [S1]
- ⚠️ **EULAR: for active proliferative lupus nephritis, give low-dose (Euro-Lupus) IV cyclophosphamide or mycophenolate with glucocorticoids (IV methylprednisolone pulses, then lower oral doses), and consider adding belimumab or a calcineurin inhibitor (voclosporin or tacrolimus with mycophenolate) from the start.** The add-on is left to the treating doctor; if it is not used first, add belimumab or voclosporin when response is inadequate at 3–6 months. [S2]
- **Choosing the add-on (EULAR):** belimumab worked better when baseline proteinuria was under 3 g/day; voclosporin lowers proteinuria quickly, which may suit nephrotic-range disease, but its safety at GFR 30–45 mL/min is unknown because AURORA-1 excluded GFR below 45. In the small pure class V subgroups, neither beat placebo on renal response. [S2]
- **Class V (EULAR): the proteinuria threshold for starting immunosuppression is 1 g/day,** unchanged from the 2019 EULAR/ERA-EDTA advice. [S2]
- **Patients at high risk of kidney failure** (reduced GFR, cellular crescents, fibrinoid necrosis or severe interstitial inflammation) can have high-dose (NIH) IV cyclophosphamide, 0.5–0.75 g/m2 once a month for 6 months, with IV methylprednisolone pulses. Such patients were under-represented or excluded in the recent trials. [S2]

## Lupus nephritis: response and maintenance

- **Response targets (EULAR/ERA-EDTA 2019, now validated):** proteinuria down by at least 25% at 3 months and 50% at 6 months, and under 500–700 mg a day by month 12, with GFR staying within 10% of baseline. [S2]
- **EULAR maintenance:** keep treating for at least 3 years after response. Stay on a mycophenolate-based regimen (with belimumab or a calcineurin inhibitor if those were used); after cyclophosphamide, switch to mycophenolate or azathioprine and keep belimumab if it was part of the first regimen. [S2]
- **In sustained remission, taper gradually, stopping glucocorticoids first.** In the WIN-LUPUS trial, withdrawing mycophenolate or azathioprine after 2–3 years of lupus nephritis treatment was not shown to be non-inferior: those who stopped had more renal relapses and extra-renal flares. [S2]

## Pregnancy in glomerular disease (CARI)

- **Before conception:** discuss the risks to the mother, the baby and the kidney disease; avoid teratogenic drugs (renin–angiotensin system inhibitors, most immunosuppressants) or use safe contraception while taking them; and weigh the total cyclophosphamide dose, as it can leave permanent infertility. [S1]
- ⚠️ **During pregnancy:** care from a team of nephrologist, obstetrician and maternal–fetal medicine specialist. Do not use RAS blockers or certain immunosuppressants, for example mycophenolate. Consider low-dose aspirin with calcium and vitamin D, aim for blood pressure under 140/90 mmHg, and consider prophylactic anticoagulation (heparin or a heparin derivative preferred) at high thrombosis risk: nephrotic syndrome, earlier VTE, high BMI. [S1]
- **After the birth:** bring in a paediatric nephrologist if the baby may have an inherited kidney disease, and a pharmacist if the mother wants to breastfeed on drugs that reach breast milk. Rarely, antibody-mediated disease (such as FSGS or membranous nephropathy) crosses the placenta. [S1]

## Supportive care in glomerular disease (KDIGO 2021, as tabled by CARI)

- **Lifestyle:** sodium below 2 g (90 mmol) a day; moderate, preferably plant-based, protein restriction; carbohydrate no more than 35 kcal/kg of ideal body weight, and fewer calories with a high BMI; a heart-healthy diet, reaching ideal weight, more exercise, stopping smoking and less alcohol. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Blood pressure: aim for systolic under 120 mmHg** using RAAS blockade, plus further blood-pressure drugs as needed. [S1]
- **Oedema:** start with a loop diuretic, add a thiazide if needed, and consider amiloride or spironolactone when loop diuretics fail. **Lipids:** a statin when cardiovascular risk is high, as with diabetes or hypertension. [S1]
- **Prophylactic anticoagulation:** decide on histology, amount of proteinuria and serum albumin, helped by an online bleeding-risk tool; heparin, a heparin derivative or warfarin is preferred, and direct oral anticoagulants have not been studied. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Infection:** every patient gets pneumococcal and influenza vaccines and screening for TB, syphilis, hepatitis and HIV; high-dose steroids or other immunosuppression call for pneumocystis prophylaxis. [S1]

## SLE drug principles (EULAR)

- **Hydroxychloroquine for every patient unless contraindicated, aiming at 5 mg/kg of actual body weight a day,** adjusted to flare and retinal-toxicity risk. A higher start (not above 400 mg/day) can be used in moderate or severe disease, then lowered. Kidney disease raises retinal risk, so eye follow-up should be closer. [S2]
- **Glucocorticoids: dose by organ and severity, taper to 5 mg/day or less (prednisone-equivalent) for maintenance, and stop when possible.** In moderate-to-severe disease, IV methylprednisolone pulses of 125–1000 mg a day for 1–3 days can be used. The maintenance ceiling was lowered from 7.5 mg/day in 2019. [S2]
- **If SLE does not respond to hydroxychloroquine (with or without steroids), or steroids cannot be cut to an acceptable long-term dose, add an immunosuppressant (methotrexate, azathioprine or mycophenolate) and/or a biologic (belimumab or anifrolumab).** EULAR sets no order between the two biologics, and a conventional immunosuppressant need not be tried first. [S2]
