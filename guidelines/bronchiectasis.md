# Bronchiectasis and chronic suppurative lung disease

## Recognise

- **Bronchiectasis means chronic cough and sputum, recurrent chest infections and CT evidence of bronchial dilatation.** It remains under-recognised and undertreated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Indigenous Australians carry the highest burden:** about 1470 per 100 000 children under 14 years in remote northern communities, and up to 23% of Indigenous adults having chest CT. They die about 20 years earlier than non-Indigenous adults with bronchiectasis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Diagnose it early in children, where mild disease can reverse.** Over 60% of referred adults had symptoms since childhood and a worse prognosis. Use paediatric radiographic criteria when reading a child's chest CT. [S1]
- **In children, CSLD is the same clinical picture without CT signs of bronchiectasis.** Refer a child to a specialist for more than 3 episodes a year of wet or productive cough lasting over 4 weeks, wet cough that has not settled after 4 weeks of antibiotics, more than 2 pneumonias, severe asthma with chronic wet cough, or chest X-ray changes persisting beyond 6 weeks. [S2]

## Assess

- **The diagnosis starts the work-up:** look for an underlying cause, comorbid conditions and treatable traits, and grade extent and severity. Baseline and extended investigations differ for children and adults. [S1]
- **Confirm with chest CT** (multidetector, with high-resolution reconstruction), which also grades severity and extent; in children, seek specialist advice before ordering it. Consider CT in adults with asthma or COPD who also have chronic cough, sputum and recurrent exacerbations, especially if their airway disease is more severe. [S2]
- **Minimum baseline tests (S2).** Children: full blood count; IgG, IgA, IgM and IgE; a sweat chloride test; airway cultures including mycobacteria; and spirometry, usually from age 6. Adults: full blood count; immunoglobulins; sputum culture including fungi and mycobacteria; spirometry; and Aspergillus serology. Further tests (for example ciliary studies, CFTR genetics, bronchoscopy, aspiration studies, extra immune tests, HIV, echocardiogram) depend on the picture. [S2]
- **In adults, grade severity with a score such as the Bronchiectasis Severity Index or FACED plus quality-of-life measures,** and look for treatable comorbidities (COPD, asthma, sinus disease, heart disease, low mood or anxiety, stress incontinence, low BMI). Refer severe disease, and people with rheumatoid arthritis, other autoimmune disease or IBD or on steroids, immunosuppressants or biologics, to a specialist bronchiectasis service. [S2]

## Treat infection

- ⚠️ **Offer eradication therapy for newly detected *P. aeruginosa*** (the 2023 statement changed "consider" to "offer"), since *P. aeruginosa* predicts worse outcomes. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Treat an exacerbation with antibiotics for at least 14 days** (up from ten days in 2015). In children with non-severe exacerbations, oral amoxycillin–clavulanate beat placebo. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Exacerbation escalation (S2):** choose the antibiotic from lower-airway cultures, local susceptibility, severity and tolerance or allergy, and follow up closely. If response is poor, repeat cultures (including for NTM). If oral treatment fails, give intensive airway clearance and IV antibiotics: in children, supervised for at least 14 days; in adults, IV for at least 5 days, moving to oral only once clearly improving, and at least 14 days in total. [S2]
- **Long-term antibiotics (> 2 months) aim to cut bacterial load and airway inflammation** when eradication is not possible. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Consider a long-term oral macrolide in frequent exacerbators (≥ 3 exacerbations a year)**: a trial of at least six months, reviewed regularly for benefit, with monitoring for rare but severe adverse effects. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Before a long-term macrolide:** get specialist advice; exclude NTM in anyone who can give sputum; do an ECG for the QTc in adults (and in children with cardiac events, a family history of long QT or arrhythmia, or other QT-prolonging drugs); and warn adults that hearing loss means stopping it. Review regularly for adherence, gut and hearing effects, and macrolide-resistant bacteria. [S2]
- **Not for routine use (S2):** inhaled or oral corticosteroids (unless asthma or eosinophilic airway inflammation coexists; in adults, inhaled steroids may help if blood eosinophils exceed 3%), inhaled bronchodilators (case by case, for example before mucoactives, inhaled antibiotics or airway clearance), and mucoactives such as isotonic or hypertonic saline and mannitol (a trial only if frequent exacerbations continue; give a bronchodilator first if the airways are hyper-responsive). ⚠️ **Recombinant human DNase is contraindicated.** [S2]
- **Do not prescribe inhaled antibiotics routinely.** Consider them in adults chronically infected with *P. aeruginosa* with frequent exacerbations when macrolides are contraindicated, not tolerated or fail. [S1]
- **In children, long-term azithromycin reduced exacerbations most between 17 and 62 weeks after starting** (a newer report, after the statement). [S1]

## Prevent and plan

- **Advise avoiding e-cigarettes** (now named among products to avoid). The statement also details surgery indications and pre-operative work-up, and equitable access to specialist care, including telehealth for rural and remote areas. [S1]
- **Airway clearance techniques are recommended,** individualised and reviewed, ideally twice a year, by a respiratory physiotherapist. Offer pulmonary rehabilitation to adults, especially with poor exercise tolerance or frequent exacerbations; encourage regular activity at every age; optimise nutrition; help stop smoking, vaping, cannabis and second-hand smoke; and avoid air pollution. [S2]
- **Review at least yearly in adults and 6-monthly in children,** ideally with a multidisciplinary team: severity (oximetry, spirometry), sputum culture (routine bacteria, and mycobacteria yearly), treatable traits and complications (such as reflux or aspiration, asthma, ABPA, P. aeruginosa or NTM, dental and ENT problems, incontinence, mental health), and education. [S2]
- **Vaccinate to the National Immunisation Program,** including pneumococcal and influenza vaccines for high-risk chronic lung disease; give influenza vaccine every year, follow national COVID-19 policy, and consider yearly influenza vaccine for household contacts. [S2]
- **Everyone with CSLD or bronchiectasis should be under a specialist respiratory service.** Review more often with moderate disability, worsening disease, haemoptysis, pulmonary hypertension, 3 or more exacerbations a year, P. aeruginosa or NTM, severe asthma symptoms, an underlying cause, advanced disease or transplant assessment. For First Nations, Māori and Pacific Islander people, keep a high index of suspicion, investigate early, and provide interpreters and local health workers. [S2]
- **Plan transition from paediatric to adult care:** documented, developmentally appropriate and involving both teams. [S1]
