# Convulsive status epilepticus, cluster and prolonged seizures: first-, second- and third-line treatment

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation

## Recognise and start

- ⚠️ **A convulsive seizure lasting 5 minutes or more is convulsive status epilepticus:** resuscitate and treat as an emergency, in adults and children alike. [S1]
- **If the person has an individual emergency management plan to hand, give the medicine it specifies.** [S1]
- **Without a plan: in the community, give a benzodiazepine straight away (buccal midazolam, or rectal diazepam); where IV access and resuscitation are already available, use IV lorazepam.** [S1]
- **Speed matters more than which benzodiazepine.** IV lorazepam is preferred in hospital because it acts fast and sedates and depresses breathing less; buccal midazolam stays first choice in the community, with rectal diazepam as the alternative. [S1]
- **Look for a treatable cause:** hypoglycaemia, eclampsia and alcohol withdrawal may need their own treatment, and missed antiseizure medication can trigger status. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Dissociative (non-epileptic) seizures can look like convulsive status epilepticus;** keep the distinction in mind. [S1]

## If the first benzodiazepine fails

- **No response to the first dose: call an ambulance in the community, or get expert help in hospital.** [S1]
- **Give a second benzodiazepine dose if the seizure has not stopped 5 to 10 minutes after the first** (or keep following the person's plan). [S1]
- ⚠️ **After 2 benzodiazepine doses, give a second-line drug intravenously: levetiracetam, phenytoin or sodium valproate.** Levetiracetam may be faster to give with fewer adverse effects; this use of levetiracetam was off-label in the UK in April 2022. Valproate carries the MHRA safety measures. [S1]
- **The evidence found all three second-line drugs beneficial with no efficacy difference,** so phenytoin or valproate remain acceptable; the committee expects levetiracetam to become the usual choice because it is quicker to prepare and easier to give. [S1]

## AES timing and evidence (S2)

- ⚠️ **AES phases, one scheme for all ages beyond the newborn:** stabilise from 0 to 5 minutes; start initial therapy at 5 minutes and judge it by 20; start second therapy at 20 minutes and judge it by 40; third therapy from 40 minutes. Very sick or ICU patients may move faster or skip straight to the third phase. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Initial therapy is a benzodiazepine: IM midazolam, IV lorazepam or IV diazepam (Level A).** Give one full adequate dose rather than several small ones; only IV lorazepam and IV diazepam may be repeated, once, at full dose. Prehospital, or when those three are unavailable, rectal diazepam or intranasal or buccal midazolam are reasonable (Level B). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Adult doses from the trials behind the AES algorithm:** IM midazolam 10 mg or IV lorazepam 4 mg (RAMPART; 5 mg and 2 mg in children of 13–40 kg); IV lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (VA trial); paramedic IV lorazepam 2 mg or diazepam 5 mg, repeatable after 4 minutes to totals of 4 mg and 10 mg. AES notes consensus protocols often use phenobarbital 20 mg/kg. [S2]
- **Without IV access, IM midazolam outperformed IV lorazepam (Level A):** seizures had stopped by hospital arrival in 73% vs 63%. Lorazepam worked faster once in (1.6 vs 3.3 minutes), but midazolam went in much sooner. [S2]
- **IV phenobarbital works as initial therapy (Level A) but is slower to give,** so it is an alternative rather than the first choice; it is also a second-phase fallback (Level B) when none of the three preferred second-phase drugs is available. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Second phase: fosphenytoin (Level U), valproic acid (Level B) or levetiracetam (Level U).** AES found no clear evidence that any is better, and pointed to the then-running ESETT trial for the answer. [S2]
- **Prefer fosphenytoin to phenytoin when both are available (Level B):** it caused less infusion-site pain and no significant arrhythmias in the tolerability studies; phenytoin remains acceptable, and their relative efficacy is unknown. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Each further drug works less often (Level A):** in the VA trial the first drug stopped overt status in 55.5%, a second drug in a further 7.0%, and a third in only 2.3%. [S2]
- **Third phase: no clear evidence (Level U).** Options are repeating a second-line drug, or anaesthetic doses of thiopental, midazolam, pentobarbital or propofol, each with continuous EEG monitoring. [S2]
- **Expect breathing, blood-pressure and rhythm problems with any IV antiseizure drug (Level A),** yet benzodiazepine-treated patients had less respiratory depression than those given placebo: untreated status itself compromises breathing. [S2]
- **Death rates: below 3% for children, as high as 30% for adults;** outcome depends mainly on the cause, how long status lasts and the patient's age. [S2]
- **Common reasons treatment falls short:** aiming to reduce rather than stop seizures, relying on sedatives or paralysing agents, and giving too little anticonvulsant. [S2]

## Refractory status

- **If the first second-line drug fails, consider another second-line drug, with expert guidance.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Third line, under expert guidance: phenobarbital or general anaesthesia.** Evidence for both is small, and they need someone experienced in giving them. [S1]

## Aftercare and plans

- **After an episode, agree an emergency management plan if the person lacks one and recurrence is a concern.** The plan should name the emergency medicine, who is trained to give it, and when. [S1]

## Cluster and prolonged seizures

- **Seizure clusters, usually 3 or more that stop on their own within 24 hours, are a medical emergency.** Follow the person's plan, or without one consider a benzodiazepine such as clobazam or midazolam now; seek expert advice if clusters recur. [S1]
- **A prolonged convulsive seizure (more than 2 minutes longer than the person's usual seizure) is also an emergency:** follow the plan or consider midazolam or clobazam immediately. Any convulsive seizure reaching 5 minutes is managed as status epilepticus. [S1]
