# Convulsive status epilepticus, cluster and prolonged seizures: first-, second- and third-line treatment

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation

## Recognise and start

- ⚠️ **A convulsive seizure lasting 5 minutes or more is convulsive status epilepticus:** resuscitate and treat as an emergency, in adults and children alike. [S1]
- **If the person has an individual emergency management plan to hand, give the medicine it specifies.** [S1]
- **Without a plan: in the community, give a benzodiazepine straight away (buccal midazolam, or rectal diazepam); where IV access and resuscitation are already available, use IV lorazepam.** [S1]
- **Speed matters more than which benzodiazepine.** IV lorazepam is preferred in hospital because it acts fast and sedates and depresses breathing less; buccal midazolam stays first choice in the community, with rectal diazepam as the alternative. [S1]
- **Look for a treatable cause:** hypoglycaemia, eclampsia and alcohol withdrawal may need their own treatment, and missed antiseizure medication can trigger status. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Dissociative (non-epileptic) seizures can look like convulsive status epilepticus;** keep the distinction in mind. [S1]

## If the first benzodiazepine fails

- **No response to the first dose: call an ambulance in the community, or get expert help in hospital.** [S1]
- **Give a second benzodiazepine dose if the seizure has not stopped 5 to 10 minutes after the first** (or keep following the person's plan). [S1]
- ⚠️ **After 2 benzodiazepine doses, give a second-line drug intravenously: levetiracetam, phenytoin or sodium valproate.** Levetiracetam may be faster to give with fewer adverse effects; this use of levetiracetam was off-label in the UK in April 2022. Valproate carries the MHRA safety measures. [S1]
- **The evidence found all three second-line drugs beneficial with no efficacy difference,** so phenytoin or valproate remain acceptable; the committee expects levetiracetam to become the usual choice because it is quicker to prepare and easier to give. [S1]

## Refractory status

- **If the first second-line drug fails, consider another second-line drug, with expert guidance.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Third line, under expert guidance: phenobarbital or general anaesthesia.** Evidence for both is small, and they need someone experienced in giving them. [S1]

## Aftercare and plans

- **After an episode, agree an emergency management plan if the person lacks one and recurrence is a concern.** The plan should name the emergency medicine, who is trained to give it, and when. [S1]

## Cluster and prolonged seizures

- **Seizure clusters, usually 3 or more that stop on their own within 24 hours, are a medical emergency.** Follow the person's plan, or without one consider a benzodiazepine such as clobazam or midazolam now; seek expert advice if clusters recur. [S1]
- **A prolonged convulsive seizure (more than 2 minutes longer than the person's usual seizure) is also an emergency:** follow the plan or consider midazolam or clobazam immediately. Any convulsive seizure reaching 5 minutes is managed as status epilepticus. [S1]
