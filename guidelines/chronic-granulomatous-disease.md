# Chronic granulomatous disease: diagnosis, prophylaxis, infections, inflammation and definitive therapy

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** children and adults with chronic granulomatous disease (CGD) and symptomatic female carriers of X-linked CGD: DHR and genetic diagnosis, antibacterial, antifungal and interferon-γ prophylaxis, work-up and empiric treatment of infection, inflammatory complications (including CGD colitis), environmental precautions, vaccines, and referral for haematopoietic cell transplant. Recognising inborn errors of immunity in general: see `primary-immunodeficiencies`.

> ⚠️ **S1 is expert opinion, not a formal society guideline** (Journal of Human Immunity 'How I Treat' review, 2026): diagnosis, prophylaxis, infection, inflammation, prevention, carriers and the definitive-therapy referral rest on S1. **S2 is an Australasian society consensus** (ASCIA, 2026) on genetic testing, and **S3 an international society guideline** (EBMT/ESID Inborn Errors Working Party, 2026) on transplant; each covers inborn errors of immunity in general, with CGD named. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> The PBS lists only **interferon gamma-1b** under this condition. Co-trimoxazole and itraconazole prophylaxis are prescribed under their general PBS listings.

## Recognise

- **Suspect CGD with recurrent, severe invasive bacterial or fungal infections plus inflammatory disease.** Typical organisms: *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Burkholderia cepacia*, *Serratia marcescens*, *Nocardia*, *Klebsiella*, mycobacteria and *Aspergillus*. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In an infant, *Serratia marcescens* infection (often of bone) almost proves CGD.** An obstructing pyloric granuloma can mimic pyloric stenosis and be the presenting sign. [S1]

## Diagnose

- **The dihydrorhodamine (DHR) assay is the standard test; always review the histograms**, not just the percentage of DHR-positive cells. The NBT test is largely outdated. [S1]
- **Know the DHR pitfalls:** eosinophilia, myeloperoxidase deficiency, paracetamol and sepsis can make it falsely abnormal; NCF4 CGD is usually missed by a standard PMA-stimulated DHR. [S1]
- **Confirm every suspected case with genetic testing alongside the DHR.** Many CGD gene panels leave out NCF1, so request dedicated NCF1 analysis if the panel is negative. [S1]

## Genetic testing in Australia (ASCIA)

- 🇦🇺 **ASCIA recommends genomic testing for every patient with a suspected monogenic phagocytic defect, including CGD.** The gene and inheritance pattern matter for testing relatives, family planning and choosing a stem cell donor. [S2]
- ⚠️ **There is no federal Medicare funding for IEI genomic testing in Australia;** the patient pays privately or the state Hospital and Health Service covers it. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Do not hold back supportive treatment while genomic results are pending:** a genetic diagnosis is not needed before supportive therapy starts. [S2]
- **Check which genes the ordered panel contains** (curated lists such as PanelApp Australia help): laboratories' panels do not all contain the same genes. [S2]
- **A negative genomic result does not exclude an inborn error of immunity;** reanalysis raises the yield, and a variant of uncertain significance must not drive treatment on its own. [S2]
- **Give genetic counselling before and after testing,** ideally from an immunologist and a genetic counsellor with IEI expertise, using the Australian Genomics consent forms and information sheets; cover at-risk relatives, reproductive decisions and cascade testing. [S2]

## Prophylaxis

- ⚠️ **Co-trimoxazole is the core antibacterial prophylaxis: 5 mg/kg of the trimethoprim component, up to 160 mg (one double-strength tablet), daily.** The intermittent schedules used for *Pneumocystis* are not enough. [S1]
- **If co-trimoxazole cannot be taken:** cefpodoxime or levofloxacin are alternatives (fluoroquinolones are less desirable). Refer for allergy testing, as desensitisation may be possible. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Itraconazole is the antifungal of choice for prophylaxis.** It does not cover *Aspergillus nidulans* or Mucorales. Capsules and oral liquid are not interchangeable; the liquid is absorbed better. Check CYP3A4 drug interactions. [S1]
- **Consider itraconazole trough monitoring, aiming for at least 0.5 mcg/ml** (parent drug plus active metabolite). In one centre 70% of children under 12 were below target on standard doses. [S1]
- **If itraconazole fails or is not tolerated, posaconazole is preferred** (voriconazole is less suitable long term because of phototoxicity and skin cancer). Aim for a level of at least 0.7 mcg/ml and monitor liver function. [S1]
- **Interferon-γ (subcutaneous, three times a week) reduced serious infections in the 1991 trial of 128 patients**, but that was before azole prophylaxis. Its added benefit on top of an azole is uncertain, and it has no role in treating acute infection. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Decide on interferon-γ case by case**, after discussing risks, benefits and cost. It must never replace reliable antibacterial and antifungal prophylaxis. Fever, myalgia, fatigue, headache and rash are common. [S1]

## Infection: assess and treat

- ⚠️ **Fever of 101°F or more for over an hour, more than one such reading in 24 h, or prolonged low-grade fever needs a doctor's review.** Patients can look well even with serious, advanced infection. [S1]
- **Work-up:** FBC with differential, CRP or procalcitonin, liver function, aerobic and anaerobic blood cultures and nasal MRSA PCR, taken before antimicrobials where possible. Galactomannan and β-D-glucan are less sensitive in CGD. [S1]
- **Image to find abscesses** (CT/MRI of chest, abdomen and pelvis if fever persists without a source; low-dose chest CT). If no pathogen is found, do bronchoalveolar lavage or biopsy/drainage. [S1]
- **Empiric regimens:** sepsis: meropenem or cefepime + co-trimoxazole + posaconazole or voriconazole, ± vancomycin. Liver abscess: meropenem or cefepime + metronidazole. Lymphadenitis: cefepime + co-trimoxazole or vancomycin. Narrow once the organism is known. [S1]
- **Adding corticosteroids has helped some staphylococcal liver abscesses, severe *Nocardia* pneumonia and other refractory infections;** decide jointly with immunology and infectious diseases. [S1]

## Inflammatory complications

- **Granulomas respond quickly to corticosteroids** (with antimicrobials for any active infection). CGD colitis: corticosteroids, mesalazine and azathioprine are first line. **Avoid TNF inhibitors** because of the infection risk; ustekinumab has had the most success. [S1]
- **Monitor routinely:** haemoglobin, MCV, iron studies, liver enzymes, faecal calprotectin, CRP and ESR, plus lung imaging and pulmonary function tests. Vision changes need urgent ophthalmology review. [S1]

## Prevention

- **Avoid compost, hay, mulch and construction dust (*Aspergillus*), and swimming in fresh or brackish water.** Well-chlorinated pools are safe. Use bottled or boiled water in endemic regions. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Give all routine vaccines except live bacterial ones: no BCG and no oral typhoid.** Live viral vaccines (MMR, varicella) are safe. The source refers to the US (AAP) schedule. [S1]

## Definitive therapy

- ⚠️ **Refer early for haematopoietic cell transplant (HCT):** it is standard for X-linked CGD or little or no residual oxidase activity, and should be strongly considered with a matched related donor, severe or refractory infection, or severe or steroid-dependent inflammation. Survival with a fully matched donor now exceeds 90%. [S1]
- **Do not give granulocyte infusions** (sensitisation can compromise a later transplant). Gene therapy remains experimental. [S1]

## Transplant: EBMT/ESID guidance

- **EBMT/ESID: growing international experience supports HSCT as standard of care in CGD, often pre-emptively.** [S3]
- **Adolescents and adults (over 15 years) are increasingly transplanted;** patients with phagocyte disorders such as CGD do better than those with combined immunodeficiency or antibody deficiency, and ongoing complications argue for transplanting pre-emptively. [S3]
- ⚠️ **Treat all active infections before transplant where possible, and identify antimicrobial resistance:** active infection at transplant worsens outcomes. [S3]
- **With severe permanent lung or liver damage, treosulfan-based conditioning may be preferred** to lower the risk of veno-occlusive disease and lung toxicity. [S3]
- **Donors:** matched unrelated donors now give survival similar to matched siblings, though an HLA-identical sibling is still first choice in most cases. Screen family donors for being affected by, or a carrier of, the same defect. [S3]

## Female carriers of X-linked CGD

- **Carriers with under 20% normal oxidase-producing neutrophils are at risk of CGD-type infections** and may need prophylaxis. Repeat the DHR periodically. Carriers should not donate marrow to affected male relatives. [S1]
