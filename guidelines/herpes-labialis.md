# Oral and labial herpes (cold sores)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition and primary-care management of herpes simplex cold sores and oral herpes in adults and children, with the mouth-ulcer red flags that apply. Primary herpetic gingivostomatitis in children is in `hsv-gingivostomatitis` (RCH); general herpes stomatitis is in `mouth-and-throat-conditions`; genital HSV is in `genital-herpes`. Herpes simplex keratitis is not covered.

## Recognise

- **Cold sores are caused by herpes simplex virus,** the same virus that causes genital herpes. [S1]
- **Typical picture:** clusters of small fluid-filled blisters, usually around the mouth or on the face. **The first infection can be severe.** [S1]
- **Oral herpes** shows as blisters and ulcers on the lips, gums, tongue and palate, often with fever. [S1]
- **Rule out look-alikes:** impetigo (school sores), and hand, foot and mouth disease, which gives small blisters in the mouth plus on the hands and feet. [S1]
- **What antivirals can and cannot do:** they cut the chance of passing the virus on and make episodes shorter and milder, but they do not cure it; recurrences and viral shedding remain possible for life. [S1]

## Treat

- **Relieve pain on the skin:** ice, or lidocaine (lignocaine) gel. [S1]
- **Keep the person well hydrated;** a severe case may need IV fluids. [S1]
- **Wash the sores with normal saline** to stop them becoming secondarily infected. [S1]
- **Topical antiviral option:** apply aciclovir 5% cream 5 times daily for 5 days, starting the moment symptoms begin, ahead of the blister. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe or recurrent cold sores need a medical consult;** oral antiviral treatment may be required. [S1]
- **With kidney disease, discuss with a doctor first** — antiviral doses may need to be reduced. [S1]

## Painful mouth ulcers

- **For sore mouth ulcers:** paracetamol and lidocaine gel for pain, plus a saltwater or 0.2% chlorhexidine mouthwash (10 mL held in the mouth for 1 minute, used 3 times daily) to keep the mouth clean. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags for urgent medical consult:** a mouth ulcer lasting over 2 weeks (possible cancer), ulcers that keep coming back, non-traumatic ulcers in a child (possible systemic disease), or severe ulcers in a young child. [S1]
- ⚠️ **A young child with severe mouth ulcers:** talk to a doctor about antiviral treatment, check for dehydration because a sore mouth stops drinking, and send to hospital if the child is not eating. [S1]
