# Suspected metabolic disorders in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognising a possible inborn error of metabolism, first-line samples, and initial anti-catabolic management in neonates and children. > ⚠️ **Once a metabolic disorder is suspected, consult the metabolic team urgently.** Take samples during the acute illness, but **never delay treatment** for them. **Hyperammonaemia is a medical emergency.**

---

## Key points

- **Metabolic disorders mimic common childhood illness at any age**; a high index of suspicion is needed. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Any newborn with progressive illness or unexplained encephalopathy should prompt consideration of a metabolic disorder**, especially after an uncomplicated pregnancy and birth — **and consult the metabolic team urgently.** [S1]
- **Blood and urine collected during the acute illness are the key to diagnosis, but must not delay management.** [S1]
- **Newborn screening misses some disorders**, and a newborn may become unwell before results are back. [S1]

## When to suspect it

- ⚠️ **Acute red flags:** confusion or lethargy progressing to coma without injury, intoxication or other neurological cause · newborn who, often after a well period, has poor feeding, weak suck or hypotonia · recurrent seizures · **presumed sepsis responding poorly to treatment** · rapid deep breathing ± apnoea · unexplained shock or cardiac failure · BRUE (especially recurrent) · unexpected hypoglycaemia, metabolic acidosis or respiratory alkalosis · liver dysfunction or organomegaly · multisystem involvement. [S1]
- **Family history:** consanguinity, relatives with a similar illness or unexplained neurodevelopmental disorders, unexplained paediatric death (especially of neonatal males) — though a known family history is uncommon. [S1]
- **Pregnancy clues:** HELLP syndrome (the fetus may have a fatty acid oxidation disorder) and acute fatty liver of pregnancy. [S1]
- **At any age in childhood:** recurrent unexplained vomiting, developmental delay or regression, **illness triggered by catabolic stress (fasting, infection, exercise) or by certain foods or medicines**, aversion to protein or fruit, unexplained neurological symptoms or exercise intolerance, acquired organomegaly. [S1]
- **Unusual body odour** can point to a diagnosis — MSUD smells of maple syrup, isovaleric aciduria of sweaty feet. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Metabolites are best detected at the acute presentation.** Careful sampling matters but **must not delay management of the unwell child.** [S1]
- **First-line blood:** venous or capillary gas · glucose (point-of-care is fine) · venous lactate · **ammonia (transfer the sample urgently)** · FBC, UEC, LFT, CRP, cultures as indicated. **Urine:** ketones, glucose, pH, and a metabolic screen including amino and organic acids. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hyperammonaemia is a medical emergency.** Interpret results in context — unwell children without a metabolic disorder often have deranged gases — and **discuss abnormal results with the metabolic team urgently.** [S1]

## Treat

- **Resuscitate first:** recognise and manage the unwell child; treat hypoglycaemia, seizures or altered consciousness and the precipitating illness (e.g. sepsis, gastroenteritis). [S1]
- **Known disorder: use the child's emergency management plan** and contact the metabolic team as soon as a disorder is suspected. [S1]
- **Stop any triggering intake (e.g. stop feeds)** and correct dehydration and ongoing losses with IV or nasogastric fluids. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Prevent catabolism: glucose 10% with sodium chloride 0.9% IV is standard first-line — except mitochondrial conditions, which need glucose 5% with sodium chloride 0.9%.** Special formula or IV lipids only after metabolic-team discussion. [S1]
- **Toxic metabolites:** IV chelating/diverting drugs under metabolic team direction; with rapid deterioration, **ICU haemofiltration** may be needed. [S1]
- **Peri-operative care: plan early with the metabolic team and avoid prolonged fasting**, giving enough calories as IV glucose-containing fluid to prevent catabolism. [S1]

## Disposition

- **Involve the local paediatric team for any suspected metabolic disease**; transfer if care is beyond the hospital's comfort level; **discharge only after discussion with the metabolic service.** [S1]

## Pattern of first-line results (guide only)

- **The pattern table is a guide only**, most relevant to neonates and small infants; lactic acidosis, respiratory alkalosis, hypoglycaemia and ketonaemia can all occur in an unwell child who has no metabolic disorder. [S1]
- **Anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO₃); normal 8 to 16 mmol/L.** [S1]
