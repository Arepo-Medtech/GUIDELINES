# Chronic abdominal pain in children

> **Functional abdominal pain is a positive diagnosis**, not a diagnosis of exclusion. Most children need no investigations; red flags drive testing.

---

## Recognise it

- **Chronic abdominal pain = intermittent or constant pain present for at least two months.** Usually, no organic cause turns up. [S1]
- **Organic and functional causes are not mutually exclusive** — they can coexist and interact. [S1]
- **Comorbid anxiety and depression are very common**, and may both result from and contribute to chronic pain. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Ask about associated symptoms:** unintentional weight loss or stunting · unexplained fever · change in bowel habit, chronic diarrhoea or blood in stools · nocturnal stooling · appetite change · dysphagia or odynophagia · persistent (especially bilious) vomiting · urinary symptoms · extraintestinal features of IBD. [S1]
- **Also ask** about reflux symptoms, diet (including excluded foods), menstrual and sexual history where relevant, any IBD or coeliac disease in the family, a HEADSSS screen and the **functional impact on school and activities**. [S1]
- **Examine:** hydration, pubertal status, weight and height (ideally serial), abdomen for palpable faeces or mass, and extraintestinal signs of IBD. [S1]

## Differential diagnosis

- **Organic causes are more likely if there are red flags.** [S1]
- **Coeliac disease:** bloating, a change in bowel habit, slow growth or poor weight gain, iron deficiency resistant to treatment, family history. [S1]
- **Irritable bowel syndrome:** pain at least 4 days per month with a change in bowel habit, or related to defecation. [S1]
- **Abdominal migraine:** stereotyped paroxysmal periumbilical or diffuse pain with few or no GI symptoms between attacks; migraine in the child or family. [S1]
- **Functional dyspepsia:** postprandial fullness, early satiety, epigastric pain or burning unrelated to bowel habit. [S1]

## Investigate only when indicated

- **Many children need no investigations**; target any testing to the likely diagnosis. [S1]
- **Faecal calprotectin for children over 4 years with IBD features.** Do not test under 4 years without discussing with a paediatrician or paediatric gastroenterologist. [S1]
- **Bloods, if indicated:** UEC, LFTs, CMP, lipase, TSH, glucose, coeliac serology with total IgA, ESR/CRP, FBE and ferritin. [S1]
- **Routine imaging isn't needed**; the yield of abdominal ultrasound is very low, though it may exclude a mass when examination is abnormal or there are significant constitutional symptoms. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not perform abdominal X-rays for non-specific abdominal pain** — they are rarely helpful even for constipation. [S1]
- **Test for *H. pylori* only with peptic-ulcer red flags**, by stool antigen or urease breath test. **Do not use *H. pylori* serology.** [S1]

## Treat

- **Reflux symptoms in older children and adolescents: a 4-week PPI trial before referral may help.** Refer if not responding or unable to wean. [S1]
- **Suspected IBD: get specialist advice and a paediatric gastroenterology referral.** [S1]
- **Functional pain:** educate and reassure (no physical damage; most resolve with time and support); further tests and medicines are usually not needed; address psychosocial stressors. [S1]
- **Screen for comorbid anxiety or depression** and consider mental health referral; **encourage return to function** (school, social, physical activity) and provide follow-up. [S1]
- **IBS, abdominal migraine, functional dyspepsia: refer to a specialist** for CBT/psychology, specialist dietitian input, gut-directed hypnotherapy, or medicines such as SSRI/low-dose TCA and antispasmodics. **No diet changes without professional advice.** [S1]

## Refer and discharge

- **Significant functional impairment** (poor school attendance, sleep disturbance, depression or anxiety): think about referring to adolescent medicine, a paediatrician or a paediatric chronic pain service. [S1]
- **Consult the local paediatric team** if the child needs admission, has red flags or abnormal investigations. [S1]
- **Discharge** once red flags have been thought through, nothing concerning is found **and** clear follow-up is in place. [S1]
