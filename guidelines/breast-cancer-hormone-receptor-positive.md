# Breast cancer — hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative advanced disease: ribociclib with endocrine therapy

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** ribociclib, a CDK4/6 inhibitor, combined with an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant for adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer: eligibility, dosing, monitoring, toxicity and the MONALEESA trial evidence. It is authored from **one eviQ protocol page (ID 3379)**, because eviQ publishes regimens, not a treatment-sequence overview. **Early breast cancer is not covered** (adjuvant ribociclib, eviQ ID 4455; adjuvant abemaciclib, ID 4168), nor are palbociclib, abemaciclib, capivasertib, everolimus or endocrine therapy on its own. HER2-positive disease: see `breast-cancer-her2-positive`; medication to lower breast cancer risk: see `breast-cancer-risk-reduction`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`. Edition 2.0 adds, from S2 (SEOM-GEICAM-SOLTI 2025), biomarker testing and an international sequence of lines of therapy around ribociclib; those other drugs' doses are still not covered.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 30 claims; **50 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 7 doses.** **S1** eviQ Cancer Treatments Online (ID 3379 v.6; first approved 27 November 2018, last reviewed 24 October 2024, review due 31 December 2026; AU): 20 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored · **S2** Spanish Society of Medical Oncology (SEOM) (published online 3 November 2025; international): 10 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ **Advanced disease only.** This guideline is written from the eviQ metastatic ribociclib protocol. It does not cover the adjuvant (early breast cancer) use of CDK4/6 inhibitors.

> ⚠️ International guideline (SEOM-GEICAM-SOLTI, 2025) as S2. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ: several drugs S2 places in the sequence (for example inavolisib, capivasertib, elacestrant) were not checked against the PBS here.

> ⚠️ **PBS difference:** eviQ notes that ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor **after prior endocrine therapy** is TGA-registered but **not PBS-listed**. Check the PBS table below before prescribing.

## Who it is for

- **Ribociclib is for HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer and is always paired with endocrine therapy:** either fulvestrant or a non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor, as the first endocrine-based treatment or after earlier endocrine therapy. [S1]
- **Patients must be postmenopausal, or have ovarian suppression:** pre- and perimenopausal women add a GnRH agonist such as goserelin, started 4 weeks before ribociclib. ECOG 0 to 2. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not use** when QTcF is above 450 ms before treatment, with a history of ventricular arrhythmia or long QT syndrome, or with a significant risk of QTc prolongation (recent MI, heart failure, unstable angina, bradyarrhythmia, uncorrected electrolytes). [S1]

## Dose

- **Ribociclib 600 mg by mouth once daily, preferably in the morning, on days 1 to 21 of each 28-day cycle** (3 weeks on, 1 week off), until progression or unacceptable toxicity. It is PBS authority-listed, except with an aromatase inhibitor after prior endocrine therapy; tablets are 200 mg. [S1]
- **Dose levels step down from 600 to 400 to 200 mg daily;** if even less than 200 mg would be needed, stop ribociclib. [S1]
- **With a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor** (azoles, macrolides, ritonavir, verapamil, amiodarone, grapefruit juice and others), lower ribociclib to 400 mg daily; go back to the previous dose once at least 5 half-lives have passed after the inhibitor is stopped. [S1]
- **Kidney:** no change if eGFR is 30 or above; below 30, start at 200 mg daily with caution. **Liver:** with moderate or severe impairment at baseline, start at 400 mg daily with caution. [S1]

## Monitoring

- ⚠️ **QT monitoring:** correct electrolytes and avoid other QT-prolonging drugs before starting. Check magnesium, potassium, calcium and phosphate at baseline and before cycles 2 to 6, and do an ECG before starting, at day 14 of the first cycle, then as needed. [S1]
- **Bloods:** LFTs, FBC, EUC/eGFR plus calcium, magnesium and phosphate at baseline and before cycles 2 to 6; consider repeating FBC and LFTs at day 15 in each of the first 2 cycles. [S1]
- **A rising creatinine is often not true kidney damage,** because ribociclib blocks renal transporters, but assess further to exclude kidney impairment. [S1]
- **Radiotherapy and surgery:** consider pausing ribociclib from 1 day before palliative radiotherapy until 1 week after it finishes, and around surgery until the wound has healed. [S1]

## Toxicity and red flags

- **Neutropenia:** for ANC 0.5 to under 1.0, withhold until 1.0 or more and reduce only if it recurs; for ANC under 0.5 or febrile neutropenia, withhold and restart one dose level lower. **Platelets** 25 to under 75: withhold until 75 or more; under 25: stop. [S1]
- ⚠️ **QTcF grade 2:** withhold until below 481 ms, then restart one dose level lower. **Grade 3** (501 ms or more): repeat the ECG within 1 hour, involve a cardiologist, withhold until below 481 ms, restart lower and stop if it happens again. **Grade 4:** stop permanently. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hepatotoxicity:** for ALT/AST more than 5 to 20 times ULN with bilirubin up to 2 times ULN, withhold until back to baseline grade and restart one level lower, stopping if it recurs. Stop ribociclib for ALT/AST above 20 times ULN, or above 3 times ULN with bilirubin above 2 times ULN. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pneumonitis and skin:** grade 2 ILD/pneumonitis means withholding until grade 1 or better, considering a lower dose, and stopping if it recurs; grade 3 or 4 means stopping. Toxic epidermal necrolysis has occurred: stop permanently for any severe cutaneous reaction. [S1]
- **Interactions:** other QT-prolonging drugs (tamoxifen, azole antifungals, tricyclics, antiarrhythmics) should be avoided, or ribociclib reduced, with ECG monitoring. CYP3A4 inducers such as carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampicin or St John's wort can blunt its effect. [S1]

## Where ribociclib sits: testing and treatment sequence (S2)

- **At first diagnosis of advanced disease, biopsy to confirm ER, PR and HER2** [I, A], and rebiopsy new metastases where possible because receptor status can change [II, A]. For HR-positive, HER2-negative disease also test germline BRCA1/2 [I, A], PIK3CA [I, A], other PI3K–AKT pathway changes (AKT1, PTEN) [I, B] and ESR1 [I, A], and identify HER2-low tumours. [S2]
- **Start with endocrine therapy, alone or with a targeted drug, and use it in sequence** [I, A]; chemotherapy first only for visceral crisis (life-threatening disease with imminent organ failure). Pre- and perimenopausal women have ovarian suppression or ablation and are then treated as postmenopausal [I, A]. [S2]
- **Endocrine resistance defines the line.** First-line (endocrine-sensitive): no prior endocrine therapy, or relapse at least 12 months after finishing adjuvant therapy. Primary resistance: relapse in the first 2 years of adjuvant therapy, or progression within 6 months of endocrine therapy for advanced disease. [S2]
- ⚠️ **First line: endocrine therapy plus a CDK4/6 inhibitor is standard** [I, A], used as early as possible after diagnosis [II, C]. All three improve PFS; ribociclib improved overall survival in MONALEESA-2, 3 and 7, abemaciclib in endocrine-refractory disease (MONARCH-2), and palbociclib did not significantly. No trial compares them directly. [S2]
- **Endocrine therapy alone (an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant) is for patients whose comorbidities rule out a CDK4/6 inhibitor** [I, A]. After chemotherapy, maintenance is usually endocrine therapy alone. [S2]
- **Second line:** progression on an aromatase inhibitor, or within a year of finishing adjuvant endocrine therapy, calls for a CDK4/6 inhibitor with fulvestrant [I, A]. Test ESR1 [II, B] and the PI3K–AKT pathway [I, A] to choose the next endocrine partner: each is altered in roughly 40% of patients. [S2]
- **Targeted endocrine combinations by mutation:** PIK3CA-mutated disease progressing during adjuvant therapy: add inavolisib to first-line palbociclib and fulvestrant (PFS and OS gains). PI3K–AKT-altered disease after an aromatase inhibitor with or without a CDK4/6 inhibitor: capivasertib with fulvestrant. Alpelisib with fulvestrant [I, B] and everolimus combinations [I, B] were tested before CDK4/6 inhibitors, so data after them are limited. ESR1-mutated disease after an aromatase inhibitor with a CDK4/6 inhibitor: elacestrant [I, A], more so after a first-line PFS over 12 months. [S2]
- **When endocrine options are exhausted:** trastuzumab deruxtecan is the most effective choice for HER2-low or HER2-ultralow disease [I, A]; sacituzumab govitecan improved PFS and OS in later lines. Otherwise use single-agent chemotherapy in sequence, combining only for high tumour burden or when a fast response is needed [II, A]. Check DPYD genotype before capecitabine to avoid severe or fatal toxicity. [S2]
- **Germline BRCA-mutated, HR-positive disease:** consider olaparib or talazoparib after first-line endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor [I, C]; both improved PFS over chemotherapy, though talazoparib did not improve OS. [S2]
- **Staging and response:** CT of chest and abdomen plus a bone scan [II, A], or FDG PET-CT instead [II, B]. Rising tumour markers alone should not trigger a change of line [II, C], and routine brain imaging without symptoms is not advised [V, C]. [S2]

## Evidence

- **MONALEESA-2** (first line, postmenopausal, with letrozole; 668 women): median PFS 25.3 against 16.0 months, and median overall survival 63.9 against 51.4 months (HR 0.76). [S1]
- **MONALEESA-3** (with fulvestrant, first or second line; 726 women): median overall survival 67.6 against 51.8 months (HR 0.67), and 5-year survival 56.5% against 42.1%. [S1]
- **MONALEESA-7** (pre- or perimenopausal, goserelin plus an aromatase inhibitor or tamoxifen): median overall survival 58.7 against 48.0 months (HR 0.76) and median PFS 23.8 against 13.0 months. [S1]
- **Toxicity in MONALEESA-2:** grade 3-4 events in 66.2% with ribociclib against 32.7% with placebo, mainly neutropenia (59.3%); febrile neutropenia 1.5%; QTcF above 480 ms in 3.6%; and 53.9% needed a dose reduction. [S1]
