## ⚠️ The four rules that reduce prescribing

> **1. "USE ANTI-INFECTIVES ONLY WHERE THERE IS CLEAR EVIDENCE OR STRONG SUSPICION OF AN INFECTION THAT
> WILL NOT RESOLVE UNAIDED. Many common infections, SUCH AS OTITIS MEDIA AND SINUSITIS, FREQUENTLY RESOLVE
> SPONTANEOUSLY"** [N1].
>
> **2. ⚠️ "NEVER TREAT VIRAL INFECTIONS WITH ANTIBACTERIALS; THERE IS NO EVIDENCE THAT ANTIBACTERIALS
> PREVENT SECONDARY BACTERIAL INFECTIONS, eg in viral upper respiratory tract infection"** [N1].
>
> **3. "CHOOSE A DRUG WITH PROVEN EFFICACY, NARROW SPECTRUM AND FEW ADVERSE EFFECTS, THAT IS THE CHEAPEST
> OF THE AVAILABLE ALTERNATIVES."** ⚠️ **"NEWER, BROAD-SPECTRUM OR MORE EXPENSIVE DRUGS ARE NOT NECESSARILY
> BETTER"** [N1].
>
> **4. "BEFORE STARTING AN ANTI-INFECTIVE, TAKE A SPECIMEN FOR CULTURE AND SUSCEPTIBILITY TESTING IF
> APPROPRIATE"** [N1].

⚠️ **This compendium's `cough` guideline carries the same conclusion from an open source: Australia
prescribes antibiotics for acute respiratory infections at four times the rate Therapeutic Guidelines
recommends, and discoloured sputum correlates poorly with bacterial infection.**

**Note also: AMH's own indications "are NOT COMPREHENSIVE and are often MUCH MORE LIMITED THAN THE MARKETED
INDICATIONS" — based on clinical practice and evidence, and MAY INCLUDE NON-MARKETED USES** [N1].

## Empirical treatment [N1]

> **Base treatment on knowledge of LOCAL patterns of likely pathogens and LOCAL susceptibility data.**
> **Consider coverage of infrequent or resistant pathogens ONLY if clinical and other factors suggest they
> may be present** [N1].

---

## Prophylaxis [N1]

**Restrict to certain clinical conditions, surgical procedures with high risk of infection, or where the
consequences of infection are severe — e.g. a prosthesis** [N1].

### Non-surgical [N1]
**Contacts of meningococcal or *H. influenzae* type b meningitis · prevention of recurrent RHEUMATIC FEVER
· frequent recurrent UTIs · ENDOCARDITIS prevention in selected instances · selected contacts of active TB
· POST-SPLENECTOMY · primary and secondary prophylaxis of opportunistic infections in HIV · POST-NEEDLE
STICK INJURY.**

### ⚠️ Surgical [N1]

> **Cover is NOT required for all possible pathogens.** **The aim is HIGH DRUG TISSUE CONCENTRATIONS AT
> THE TIME OF THE PROCEDURE.** ⚠️ **SINGLE DOSES ARE USUALLY SUFFICIENT** [N1].

**A SECOND dose may be required when:**
- **procedures are DELAYED**
- **a SHORT-ACTING antibacterial (e.g. CEFAZOLIN) is used and the procedure lasts MORE THAN 3–4 HOURS**
- **there is EXCESSIVE BLOOD LOSS**

**Up to 24 hours of prophylaxis may be required for certain indications, e.g. some cardiac surgery** [N1].

## Combinations and topicals [N1]

**Combination treatment is indicated for: proven or suspected MIXED infections where one drug cannot give
the required spectrum (e.g. skin and soft tissue infections in diabetics); where SYNERGY is required (e.g.
enterococcal endocarditis); and TO PREVENT DEVELOPMENT OF RESISTANCE (e.g. TB)** [N1].

> ⚠️ **AVOID TOPICAL ANTI-INFECTIVES — EXCEPT FOR EYE INFECTIONS.** **Sensitisation and/or selection of
> resistant organisms can occur, with the consequence that SYSTEMIC ANTI-INFECTIVES OF THE SAME CLASS
> CANNOT BE USED IN SERIOUS INFECTIONS** [N1].
>
> ✅ **When topical treatment is needed, PREFER agents not used systemically — MUPIROCIN — or ANTISEPTICS
> such as CHLORHEXIDINE and POVIDONE-IODINE**: wide spectrum, less sensitisation, and ⚠️ **they DO NOT
> PROMOTE MICROBIAL RESISTANCE** [N1].

## Route and dose [N1]

**ORAL OR RECTAL administration is adequate for most infections.** Exceptions: the oral route is
unavailable (unconscious), absorption is unreliable (vomiting), or inadequate drug concentration at the
site would result [N1].

> ⚠️ **"USUAL DOSE SCHEDULES ARE ADEQUATE FOR MOST INFECTIONS. IN GENERAL, SICKER PEOPLE DO NOT REQUIRE
> HIGHER DOSES THAN PEOPLE WITH LESS SEVERE INFECTION"** [N1]. **Severity is not a dosing input.**

**HIGHER doses may be necessary when** [N1]:
- **the organism is proven or suspected to have REDUCED SUSCEPTIBILITY**
- **altered kinetics are likely — crush injuries, serious burns**
- **distribution to the site is compromised: ABSCESS (usually requires surgery or drainage) · BONE ·
  SUBARACHNOID SPACE · BRAIN · INTRACELLULAR (e.g. TB) · NECROTIC TISSUE · PROSTATE · SKIN · NAILS ·
  reduced perfusion (peripheral vascular disease) · CARDIAC VEGETATIONS · VITREOUS HUMOUR**

**AMBULATORY IV treatment** lets patients complete prolonged IV therapy at home, usually selected and
monitored by infectious diseases specialists; suitable for soft tissue and bone infections, endocarditis,
bacteraemia and intra-abdominal infections, with continuous infusion by elastomeric pump where multiple
daily doses would be needed [N1].

## ⚠️ Duration [N1]

> **"THERE ARE FEW INDICATIONS FOR PROLONGED TREATMENT. Generally treat until major signs and symptoms of
> infection have resolved, clear clinical improvement is evident and laboratory indices show a trend
> towards normal"** [N1].
>
> ⚠️ **"IF CLINICAL RESPONSE IS SLOWER THAN EXPECTED, REVIEW THE INITIAL DIAGNOSIS AND/OR TREATMENT
> CHOICE"** — not the dose [N1].

| Band | Conditions [N1] |
|---|---|
| **SINGLE DOSE** | **candidal vulvovaginitis · intestinal worms · uncomplicated gonorrhoea** |
| **SHORT, <5 days** | **bacterial gastroenteritis · malaria · uncomplicated UTIs** |
| **LONGER, 5–10 days** | **bacteraemia · cellulitis · chlamydial infection · intestinal amoebiasis · meningitis · peritonitis · pneumonia (Gram-negative, pneumococcal, *Mycoplasma*) · sinusitis · streptococcal pharyngitis · tonsillitis** |
| **EXTENDED, >10 days** | **bacterial endocarditis · deep-seated staphylococcal infection including bacteraemia · INFECTIONS IN IMMUNOCOMPROMISED PEOPLE · *Legionella* pneumonia · osteomyelitis · septic arthritis · PID · prostatitis · late syphilis · TB · NEUTROPENIC FEVER** |

⚠️ **These are BANDS, not regimens.** **No drug, dose or exact duration is attached to any of them in this
guideline.**

## ⚠️ Resistance [N1]

> **"ANY USE OF AN ANTI-INFECTIVE ALLOWS THE DEVELOPMENT OF RESISTANCE TO IT"**, and depending on the
> mechanism, resistance to other anti-infectives may also occur. **"MULTIDRUG RESISTANCE IN COMMON
> CLINICAL PATHOGENS IS A GROWING PROBLEM AND WIDESPREAD AND INDISCRIMINATE USE OF BROAD-SPECTRUM
> ANTI-INFECTIVES IS A MAJOR CONTRIBUTOR"** [N1].

**BROAD-SPECTRUM drugs affect bacterial flora MORE than narrow-spectrum drugs and are MORE LIKELY to cause
GI adverse effects and SUPER-INFECTION WITH RESISTANT ORGANISMS** [N1].

> ⚠️ **THE RESERVE LIST.** **"Drugs that remain the LAST DEFENCE against multi-resistant strains should be
> used ONLY UNDER EXPERT DIRECTION"** [N1]:
>
> **VANCOMYCIN · TEICOPLANIN · RIFAMPICIN · IMIPENEM · MEROPENEM · EXTENDED-SPECTRUM CEPHALOSPORINS ·
> QUINOLONES**

### Additional therapies in eTG

- Surgical prophylaxis in obesity: cefazolin 3 g IV if weight over 120 kg and GFR 40 mL/min or more. [T1]
- Penicillin hypersensitivity: use cefazolin for nonsevere reactions and (in hospital) even for severe immediate reactions; use a non-beta-lactam (eg vancomycin 15 mg/kg up to 2 g, or teicoplanin 15 mg/kg up to 800 mg IV within 120 minutes pre-incision) only after severe delayed reactions. [T1]
- Gentamicin or tobramycin prophylaxis (adults, lean body weight): 3 mg/kg up to 280 mg for procedures of 4 hours or less (CrCl 20 or more); 5 mg/kg up to 480 mg for 4-6 hour procedures (CrCl 40 or more); avoid if CrCl below 20 mL/min. [T1]
- S. aureus decolonisation before arthroplasty or sternotomy: mupirocin 2% nasal ointment twice daily for 5 days, with or without daily chlorhexidine 2-4% washes or dilute bleach baths for 5 days; add vancomycin or teicoplanin if MRSA-colonised. [T1]
- Switch IV to oral once clinically stable with functioning gut, improving fever, no unexplained haemodynamic instability and a suitable oral agent (not for meningitis-type infections); review empirical therapy daily in hospital. [T2]
