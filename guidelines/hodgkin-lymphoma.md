# Hodgkin lymphoma (classic and nodular lymphocyte-predominant)

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, staging, first-line treatment of early and advanced classic Hodgkin lymphoma in younger and older adults, relapsed/refractory disease (salvage, autograft, brentuximab maintenance, PD-1 inhibitors, allograft), survivorship, and nodular lymphocyte-predominant HL. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> 🇦🇺 **S2 is Australian (eviQ protocols, Cancer Institute NSW)** and gives the regimen doses and Australian funding status. ⚠️ Per S2, brentuximab is **not PBS-reimbursed within BrECADD** (S1's preferred regimen for advanced disease), nivolumab is a **PBS authority** item within N-AVD, and dacarbazine is not TGA-approved or PBS-listed for these combinations. ## Diagnosis and staging

- **Diagnose from an excisional node biopsy where possible,** a core biopsy otherwise; fine-needle aspiration is not enough. [S1]
- **Allocate risk group from PET/CT staging plus clinical risk factors;** skip marrow biopsy when a baseline PET/CT is done, and do an interim PET/CT whenever the chosen pathway uses it. Offer fertility counselling before treatment to anyone who may want children. [S1]

## Early-stage classic HL

- **Early-stage favourable: two cycles of ABVD, then 20 Gy involved-site radiotherapy** (1A), whatever the interim PET shows. [S1]
- **Dropping radiotherapy costs some disease control** even when the interim PET is negative. It can still be discussed after complete metabolic response (Deauville 3 or less) if late radiation risks outweigh the benefit, judged by a radiation oncologist; then follow the chemotherapy-only schedules from RAPID, H10F or HD16. [S1]
- **Early-stage unfavourable, age 60 or under: '2 + 2'** (two cycles of escalated BEACOPP then two of ABVD), with 30 Gy involved-site radiotherapy only if the end-of-chemotherapy PET is positive (1A). Swapping in eBEACOPDac or another procarbazine-free variant is an alternative (4A). [S1]
- **If a less intensive route without escalated BEACOPP is preferred:** four ABVD cycles, then involved-site radiotherapy to 30 Gy (1A). Escalating to escalated BEACOPP for a positive PET after two ABVD cycles is for selected patients only, as its advantage had gone by 10 years. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Bleomycin lung toxicity:** over 60, give bleomycin in two cycles at most; over 70, consider leaving it out (3A). Dropping bleomycin after cycle two reduces short-term lung toxicity (RATHL). [S1]
- **ABVD (early stage), 28-day cycles, 2 to 4 cycles (S2):** doxorubicin 25 mg/m2, vinblastine 6 mg/m2, dacarbazine 375 mg/m2 and bleomycin 10,000 IU/m2, each on days 1 and 15. Keep to schedule whatever the blood count, since dose intensity matters; G-CSF is not routine (it may add to bleomycin lung toxicity) unless delays occur. S2 also allows a switch to escalated BEACOPP after two ABVD cycles if the PET is positive. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Bleomycin limits (S2):** never exceed a lifetime 400,000 IU (risk rises above 300,000 IU) and check the running total before each dose; do baseline lung function tests; stop bleomycin for clinical lung toxicity (cough, breathlessness, basal crackles) or a DLCO below 30 to 35% of baseline. [S2]

## Advanced classic HL

- **Age 60 or under: BrECADD for four cycles if the PET after two cycles is negative, six if positive,** then PET-guided radiotherapy (1A). It beat escalated BEACOPP on 5-year PFS (93.6% vs 90.6%) with less toxicity, neuropathy and infertility. [S1]
- **Where an ABVD-type approach is preferred, six cycles of nivolumab-AVD** are the alternative for younger patients (1A); its 3-year PFS was 91% against 82% for brentuximab-AVD. Without targeted agents, interim-PET-guided eBEACOPDac is an option (4A). [S1]
- ⚠️ **BrECADD, escalated BEACOPP, eBEACOPDac and brentuximab-AVD cause frequent neutropenia and febrile neutropenia:** primary G-CSF and cotrimoxazole prophylaxis are mandatory. [S1]
- **Partial remission on PET after systemic therapy:** consider 30 Gy involved-site radiotherapy with modern planning. [S1]
- **Over 60 and fit for multi-agent chemotherapy: six cycles of nivolumab-AVD** (2A). In this group it gave 3-year PFS of 82% versus 58% with brentuximab-AVD. [S1]
- **Older patients without access to targeted agents:** up to two cycles of A(B)VD, then four of AVD (3A). More than two cycles with bleomycin cause unacceptable lung toxicity at this age. [S1]
- **Older patients unfit for multi-agent chemotherapy:** options are limited and cure is unlikely; consider brentuximab paired with dacarbazine or with PD-1 blockade, or one conventional cytotoxic drug (3B). [S1]
- ⚠️ **BrECADD, every 21 days for 4 to 6 cycles (S2):** brentuximab vedotin 1.8 mg/kg (maximum 180 mg) on day 1; dexamethasone 40 mg on days 2 to 5; doxorubicin 40 mg/m2 and cyclophosphamide 1,250 mg/m2 on day 2; etoposide 150 mg/m2 on days 2 to 4; dacarbazine 250 mg/m2 on days 3 and 4; filgrastim 5 micrograms/kg from day 5 until recovery (or pegfilgrastim). Dexamethasone 40 mg can be given for 4 days before cycle 1 in those over 40. [S2]
- **Where the sources differ, BrECADD eligibility and funding:** S2 lists BrECADD for patients aged 60 or under or fit patients aged 61-75 with advanced disease (stage 3 or 4, or 2B with a large mediastinal mass or extranodal lesions), whereas S1 recommends it for age 60 or under. ⚠️ S2 notes that, within BrECADD, brentuximab has TGA registration yet no PBS funding. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Nivolumab-AVD, every 28 days for 6 cycles (S2):** nivolumab 240 mg with doxorubicin 25 mg/m2, vinblastine 6 mg/m2 and dacarbazine 375 mg/m2, all on days 1 and 15, for untreated stage III or IV classic HL; nivolumab is a PBS authority item. Discuss with an expert first if there is significant autoimmune disease, an organ transplant, past viral hepatitis or previous lung radiotherapy. Immune-related adverse events can start early, escalate fast or appear after stopping; consider baseline troponin and urinalysis. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Brentuximab-AVD, every 28 days for 6 cycles (S2):** brentuximab vedotin 1.2 mg/kg (maximum 120 mg) with doxorubicin 25 mg/m2, vinblastine 6 mg/m2 and dacarbazine 375 mg/m2 on days 1 and 15, for untreated CD30-positive stage III or IV HL, with primary G-CSF prophylaxis; brentuximab is a PBS authority item here. Never combine brentuximab with bleomycin (contraindicated: lung toxicity). [S2]
- **Supportive care with BrECADD (S2):** PJP prophylaxis, for example trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole 160/800 mg one tablet twice daily on two days a week or once daily three days a week; irradiated blood products. [S2]

## Relapsed or refractory classic HL

- **Definitions:** refractory means progression on treatment or relapse within 3 months of finishing; early relapse is within 12 months; late relapse is 12 months or more. Confirm relapse by biopsy where possible (3A). [S1]
- **High-dose chemotherapy with autograft is standard for most eligible patients** (2A); age alone does not exclude, and selected fit patients over 65 qualify. Aim for complete metabolic response first: 5-year PFS is about 75% with it versus 25% to 30% without. [S1]
- **Salvage before autograft:** where PD-1 inhibitors are available and funded, combine one with chemotherapy, together or in sequence (3B). Otherwise, brentuximab-naive patients get brentuximab plus chemotherapy (2A). BEAM is the standard conditioning; collect stem cells early. [S1]
- **Brentuximab maintenance after autograft for high-risk patients** (1A), up to 16 doses. High risk means primary refractory disease, early relapse or extranodal disease at relapse; 5-year PFS rose to 59% from 41%. [S1]
- **Relapse after autograft, PD-1-naive: give PD-1 blockade, for a maximum of 2 years.** Judge first response after four cycles using LYRIC criteria so pseudo-progression does not end treatment early. [S1]
- **Discuss allogeneic transplant** with eligible responders (3B) and with fit patients refractory to PD-1 therapy (3A). ⚠️ Stop the PD-1 inhibitor at least 6 weeks before the allograft. [S1]
- **Not eligible for autograft: single-agent PD-1 inhibitor is preferred** (2A). If PD-1 inhibitors are unsuitable or unfunded, give brentuximab alone for up to 16 cycles, especially if brentuximab-naive (2A). [S1]

## Follow-up and survivorship

- **After end-of-treatment imaging, follow clinically;** routine surveillance scans are not indicated without symptoms. Check TSH yearly after neck radiotherapy, and blood pressure and lipids yearly for everyone. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Breast screening after chest radiotherapy before age 36:** yearly mammography and/or breast MRI from 8 years after radiotherapy, but not before age 25. [S1]

## Nodular lymphocyte-predominant HL

- **NLPHL stage IA without risk factors: 30 Gy involved-site radiotherapy alone** (2A). Brentuximab and PD-1 inhibitors have no role in NLPHL (5A), because the tumour cells lack CD30. [S1]
