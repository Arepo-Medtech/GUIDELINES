# Intralesional corticosteroids in dermatology

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults receiving intralesional triamcinolone (or betamethasone) for localised skin disease: keloid and hypertrophic scar, hypertrophic lichen planus, granuloma annulare and other granulomatous disorders, lichen simplex chronicus and necrobiosis lipoidica. ## What it is and when to use it

- **Intralesional therapy means injecting a corticosteroid, usually triamcinolone acetonide, straight into a lesion** in or just under the skin. [S1]
- **Products:** triamcinolone comes in 2 strengths, 10 mg/mL and 40 mg/mL (Kenacort-A 10 and Kenacort-A 40); betamethasone injection is Celestone Chronodose (1 mL). [S1]
- **Indications include** keloids and hypertrophic scars; hypertrophic lichen planus; lichen simplex chronicus; necrobiosis lipoidica; granuloma annulare and other granulomatous disease (cutaneous sarcoidosis, granuloma faciale); discoid lupus; alopecia areata; localised psoriasis; and inflamed acne or epidermoid cysts. [S1]
- **Why inject rather than apply?** It goes past a thick stratum corneum, gives a higher concentration where the dermal inflammation is, and is less likely than a topical steroid to thin the epidermis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not inject into actively infected skin** (such as impetigo or herpes simplex), or when the patient is allergic to triamcinolone. [S1]

## Technique and dose

- **Clean the site with alcohol or antiseptic and inject with a fine needle into the dermis, not the fat,** to avoid leaving a dent. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Volume: about 0.1–0.2 mL per cm² of affected skin, usually no more than 1–2 mL in total per session,** repeated every 4–8 weeks. [S1]
- **Use it at full strength or thinned** with saline or a local anaesthetic. [S1]
- **Typical triamcinolone strengths:** 40 mg/mL for a thick keloid; 10 mg/mL for a moderately thick hypertrophic scar; 10 mg/mL for discoid lupus or granuloma annulare. [S1]
- **Injections can be given each month, for several months, as long as the disease is active.** [S1]

## Adverse effects

- **Early effects usually settle:** pain, bleeding, bruising, infection, contact allergy to the benzyl alcohol preservative, poor wound healing and sterile abscess (sometimes needing drainage). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Warn about delayed effects:** fat atrophy with dimpling (the commonest, and possibly permanent), pale or dark marks that may persist, telangiectasia (treatable with laser or IPL), local hair growth and steroid acne. [S1]
- **Systemic effects are unlikely at intralesional doses,** which are very small. Allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, are very rare and not dose-related. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Large intramuscular doses of triamcinolone have caused serious systemic harm,** and periocular injection has rarely been linked with blindness, as well as glaucoma and cataract. [S1]

## Keloid and hypertrophic scar: the Japan Scar Workshop approach

- **Adults:** start straight away with a strong steroid plaster for 3 months (a weak tape if mild); add triamcinolone acetonide injections if plasters are not enough; refer to a specialised facility if plaster and injections fail. [S2]
- **Children:** steroid tape comes first, not injection: a weak tape for 3 months, then a stronger plaster for another 3 months, with referral if tape or plaster does not work. [S2]
- **Triamcinolone acetonide injection both treats an existing keloid or hypertrophic scar and prevents relapse after excision.** [S2]
- ⚠️ **Dose per injection: 2–5 ml made by diluting 5–10 mg of triamcinolone acetonide with local anaesthetic** (e.g. xylocaine 1% with epinephrine). Keeping the dose at or below 5 mg may mean fewer menstrual irregularities. [S2]
- **Limit the pain:** anaesthetic tapes or creams, local anaesthetic before the steroid, thin 30G or 27G needles on locking syringes, and no large volumes into hard areas (the pain arrives when the anaesthetic wears off). [S2]
- **Do not aim at the hard centre at first:** enter from the border with normal skin and target the deepest or most inflamed part of the scar; once it softens, the core can be injected. [S2]
- **Space repeat injections at 2-week intervals.** Small scars may improve after one or two injections, with steroid tape or plaster keeping the result. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Do not inject in pregnancy, and avoid injection in diabetes, glaucoma or cataract.** Inject fat cautiously (it atrophies), and take care around the face: a triamcinolone embolus has caused blindness. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Use lower doses in children and elderly people:** iatrogenic Cushing syndrome has followed injections in both, and high doses can upset menstruation and lower bone density in older patients. [S2]

## Intramuscular (systemic) use

- **Triamcinolone is sometimes given intramuscularly instead, as a systemic steroid:** typically 0.5–1 mg/kg (40–80 mg in an adult), repeatable every 30 days for 3–6 months. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid systemic-dose triamcinolone** in active TB or systemic fungal infection; extensive plaque, pustular or erythrodermic psoriasis (it may destabilise it); active peptic ulcer; uncontrolled diabetes, heart failure or severe hypertension; and severe depression or psychosis. [S1]
