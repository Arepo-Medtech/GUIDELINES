# Myelodysplastic neoplasms (MDS) in adults

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, diagnostic work-up, risk stratification (IPSS-R / IPSS-M) and treatment of lower-risk and higher-risk myelodysplastic neoplasms in adults, including supportive care, iron chelation, ESAs, lenalidomide for del(5q), hypomethylating agents and allogeneic transplant referral. Related: `myeloproliferative-neoplasms` (myelofibrosis), `acute-myeloid-leukaemia` (progression to AML), `iron-overload-and-chelation` (which left MDS iron overload out of scope). The imatinib rows for PDGFR-rearranged myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative disorders are in `hypereosinophilic-syndrome`.

## Recognise

- **MDS is a clonal stem-cell disorder**: dysplastic blood and marrow cells, failing haematopoiesis and a raised risk of turning into AML. About 10% follow earlier chemotherapy or radiotherapy. [S1]
- **It is a disease of older people:** median onset is about 75 years, and incidence climbs above 30/100,000 after age 70. [S1]
- **Anaemia is the usual first finding (roughly 70-80%)**, often picked up on a routine blood count. About a third of patients have recurrent infections; half have low platelets at diagnosis, but bleeding at presentation is uncommon. [S1]
- **Ask about family history:** germline predisposition (for example DDX41) can still present at 60-70 years. [S1]

## Assess and stratify

- **Diagnosis needs a blood count with differential and a bone marrow examination**, after other causes are excluded. Marrow histology is now mandatory at diagnosis, alongside cytology with iron staining. [S1]
- **Karyotype and somatic mutation testing are essential** for a firm diagnosis, prognosis and treatment choice. The blast cut-off separating MDS from acute leukaemia is 20%. [S1]
- **IPSS-R still sorts patients into lower- and higher-risk for treatment decisions**; the molecular IPSS-M reclassifies 46% of patients and improves prediction, and will take over once fully validated. [S1]
- **Pre-MDS states (ICUS, CCUS, CHIP) do not meet MDS criteria;** with CCUS, review about every 3-6 months, particularly when mutations or cytogenetic changes are present. [S1]

## Lower-risk MDS

- **Watchful waiting is often enough** when cytopenias are mild; anaemia is the commonest reason to start treatment. Where treatment is needed, good supportive care (transfusion, antibiotics, treating comorbidity) is the foundation. [S1]
- **Transfuse red cells by clinical need;** with severe coronary disease or other serious comorbidity, keeping Hb above 10 g/dl is appropriate. Give platelets for bleeding rather than prophylactically, except with fever or severe infection. [S1]
- **Treat infections early and generously, but do not give routine antibiotic prophylaxis.** Follow standard pneumococcal, influenza and SARS-CoV-2 vaccination advice. [S1]
- **Consider iron chelation** (deferasirox, desferrioxamine) when life expectancy exceeds 2 years and the patient has had at least 20 red-cell units or ferritin is above 1000 ng/ml; it matters most before allogeneic transplant. [S1]
- **Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents** (erythropoietin 40,000 IU weekly, up to 80,000 IU weekly) should be selected by the Nordic score. Best candidates have EPO below 200 (500) U/l, need at most 2 units in 8 weeks and have low-risk disease; up to 75% respond. Stop if there is no response by 6 months. [S1]
- **G-CSF on its own has no randomised evidence in MDS;** the accepted exception is giving it for repeated complicated infections in severe neutropenia. [S1]
- **Lenalidomide for transfusion-dependent lower-risk MDS with isolated del(5q):** about 60% respond. Start at 10 mg/day (5 mg/day may suit older patients), adjusting for platelets; stop after 4 months without benefit. Screen for TP53 mutation first. Benefit without chromosome 5 changes is low. [S1]
- **Immunosuppression (ATG plus ciclosporin)** suits a subgroup with hypocellular marrow, low risk and low transfusion need; about 30% become transfusion-free. Give it only in a haematology centre. [S1]

## Higher-risk MDS

- ⚠️ **Consider allogeneic stem cell transplant from the time of diagnosis in every higher-risk patient.** It is the only potentially curative option; refer suitable patients to a transplant centre at diagnosis. Selected patients over 70 can now be transplanted. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Azacitidine for transplant-ineligible higher-risk MDS** (evidence Ib, grade A): 75 mg/m² subcutaneously or IV for 7 days, repeated every 28 days. Give at least 4-6 cycles before judging response, and continue until response is lost. [S1]
- **Azacitidine prolonged survival by 6-9 months** over supportive care in randomised trials, and in the larger phase III trial beat low-dose cytarabine and intensive chemotherapy too. [S1]
- **After azacitidine failure** (progression or no response after 4-6 cycles), enrol in a clinical trial where possible. Decitabine can briefly restore counts; venetoclax combinations are unapproved in this setting. [S1]
- **AML-type intensive chemotherapy is not established for higher-risk MDS**; use it only case by case, for example as induction before a planned transplant. Patients with an unfavourable karyotype gain nothing unless transplant follows straight after. [S1]

## Monitor

- **Follow blood counts regularly; repeat the marrow if progression is suspected** or before curative therapy. Karyotype evolves in up to 30% and mutations in up to 70%, so plan sequential testing when the result would change management. [S1]
