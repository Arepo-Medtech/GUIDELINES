# Systemic mastocytosis in adults

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis (tryptase, marrow, KIT D816V), staging imaging, prognosis, symptom and anaphylaxis management, bone health, and cytoreductive treatment of indolent and advanced systemic mastocytosis in adults, including imatinib for KIT D816V-negative aggressive disease. Mastocytosis with eosinophilia carrying FIP1L1::PDGFRA is classed as a PDGFRA-rearranged myeloid neoplasm: see `hypereosinophilic-syndrome`. Anaphylaxis: see `acute-anaphylaxis`.

## Recognise and diagnose

- **Most adults with mastocytosis have the systemic form, and about 80% have skin lesions**, so investigate for SM in any adult with cutaneous mastocytosis. Some indolent SM involves only the marrow. [S1]
- **When to suspect mastocytosis in an adult and refer (S2 Table 1):** fixed pigmented maculopapules with Darier's sign; telangiectatic lesions on the trunk and upper limbs (TMEP); erythematous-violaceous lesions with Darier's sign; mast-cell activation symptoms in at least two organs with no other cause; repeated anaphylaxis after Hymenoptera stings, sometimes more than an hour later; anaphylaxis with no cause found; anaphylaxis with a known allergy but a raised basal tryptase away from the reaction; severe sting anaphylaxis with very low sensitisation (specific IgE sometimes under 1 kU/L, or negative skin tests); and early unexplained osteoporosis or repeated low-trauma vertebral fractures. Send suspicious skin lesions to a dermatologist for biopsy, start first-line symptom treatment, and refer to a mastocytosis centre. [S2]
- **Diagnose cutaneous-only mastocytosis only after dermatology review, skin histology, and exclusion of SM criteria.** (GRADE 1) [S1]
- **Adult skin forms (S2 Table 2):** urticaria pigmentosa (brownish maculopapules on thighs and trunk, itch as the first symptom, Darier's sign) and TMEP (telangiectatic lesions on the upper trunk and limbs, no itch, Darier's sign negative; 20% of the UP variant), or a mix. Confirm UP by skin biopsy with CD117 (KIT) immunostaining (more than 20 mast cells per window at ×40), test the skin for KIT D816V in every form, and screen for WHO extracutaneous criteria (the table gives 85% for UP and mixed forms and 50% for TMEP). [S2]
- **Serum tryptase is needed for diagnosis and follow-up, at least yearly.** A persistent level above 20 ng/ml is a minor WHO criterion; levels above 200 ng/ml are much more common in aggressive SM and SM with an associated haematological neoplasm. [S1]
- **A bone marrow biopsy, ideally reviewed by a pathologist experienced in mast cell disease, is mandatory**; dense aggregates of at least 15 mast cells are the major criterion, and CD25/CD2 expression marks abnormal mast cells. [S1]
- **WHO diagnostic criteria (S1 Table 1): SM needs the major criterion with at least one minor one, or three minor ones.** Major: multifocal dense mast-cell infiltrates (15 or more cells in aggregates) in marrow or another organ outside the skin. Minor: more than 25% of infiltrate mast cells spindle-shaped or atypical (or more than 25% immature or atypical in aspirate smears); an activating KIT codon 816 mutation in marrow, blood or another extracutaneous organ; mast cells expressing CD2 and/or CD25; and total tryptase persistently above 20 ng/mL, which does not count when there is an associated clonal myeloid disorder. [S1]
- **'B' findings (high burden, no organ failure):** marrow infiltration above 30% and/or total tryptase above 200 ng/mL; dysplasia or myeloproliferation in other lineages that falls short of a separate neoplasm, with near-normal counts; enlarged liver, spleen or nodes without organ dysfunction. **'C' findings (organ damage):** cytopenia (ANC below 1 × 10^9/L, Hb below 10 g/dL, or platelets, printed in the source as below 1 × 10^9/L) without another marrow cancer; hepatomegaly with impaired liver function, ascites or portal hypertension; large osteolytic lesions or pathological fractures; splenomegaly with hypersplenism; and malabsorption with weight loss from gut infiltration. [S1]
- **WHO classification (S1 Table 2):** cutaneous mastocytosis (maculopapular or urticaria pigmentosa, diffuse, or a skin mastocytoma); indolent SM (SM criteria, no C findings, no associated clonal non-mast-cell disease), with the provisional smouldering SM (2 or more B findings, no C findings) and isolated bone marrow mastocytosis (no skin lesions); SM with an associated clonal haematological disease; aggressive SM (one or more C findings, no mast cell leukaemia), which includes lymphadenopathic mastocytosis with eosinophilia; mast cell leukaemia (diffuse marrow infiltration, 20% or more mast cells on the aspirate and usually 10% or more of blood white cells; an aleukaemic variant is rare); mast cell sarcoma; and extracutaneous mastocytoma. [S1]
- **Test every SM patient for KIT D816V** (found in over 95% of adults; confers imatinib resistance) using a high-sensitivity method. With raised marrow mast cells plus eosinophilia, test for FIP1L1-PDGFRA. (GRADE 2) [S1]
- **Imaging:** Doppler abdominal ultrasound for all (liver size, portal hypertension, ascites); CT and MRI for 'C' findings. FDG-PET/CT has little role in initial assessment. (GRADE 3) [S1]
- **Estimate prognosis with the WHO subtype and the clinical or hybrid Mayo (MAPS) model.** (GRADE 4) [S1]
- **Test for KIT D816V by ASO-qPCR or droplet digital PCR on blood and marrow DNA** (detection threshold 0.01%; positive in more than 94% of SM). The allele burden also tracks cytoreductive treatment. If negative but suspicion stays high, sequence the whole KIT coding region from marrow RNA; other techniques are not advised. [S2]
- **Basal tryptase above 20 ng/mL is a minor criterion, but hereditary alpha-tryptasaemia (TPSAB1 duplication, in 5% of people) raises basal tryptase,** so this criterion will need re-evaluation. [S2]

## Symptoms and anaphylaxis

- **Individualise treatment**, from watchful waiting and symptom control up to cytoreduction for aggressive, advanced or refractory disease. Trigger avoidance and antihistamines (H1 for general, H2 for gut symptoms) are the basis of symptom control. (GRADE 2) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Anaphylaxis risk is about 50% in adults with indolent SM.** Every SM patient should always carry two epinephrine autoinjectors, even without prior anaphylaxis, and patient and family should be trained; IM adrenaline is the emergency treatment. [S1]
- **Patients with SM and Hymenoptera venom allergy need lifelong venom immunotherapy.** [S1]
- **Before procedures, tailor prophylaxis to the procedure and disease form** (GRADE 1): usually none before skin biopsy, but premedicate before marrow biopsy or endoscopy. Beta-lactams and local anaesthetics are safe (GRADE 1); decide NSAIDs case by case. [S1]
- **Drugs that directly activate mast cells, to use with caution and only when unavoidable (S1 Table 4):** opioids, neuromuscular blockers, quinolones and vancomycin, iodinated contrast, and NSAIDs. The NSAID risk is not very high: advice is individual, and people already taking them without reactions can carry on. [S1]
- **No pre-emptive food restrictions are needed:** food allergy is no commoner than in the general population. Vaccinate (including against COVID-19) after same-day antihistamine prophylaxis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Adrenaline autoinjector dose (S2):** 0.3 mg if under 60 kg, 0.5 mg if over 60 kg, repeated every 15 minutes if needed, up to two injections; carry two pens, which keep at room temperature. S2 suggests pens for patients with a history of severe anaphylaxis; S1 advises them for every SM patient (see Unresolved). [S2]
- **Symptomatic drugs (all off-label):** second-generation H1 antihistamines for flushing and itch, H2 blockers for gastroduodenal symptoms (a PPI if H2 blockers only partly work), montelukast, and oral sodium cromoglycate 100 or 200 mg 2 or 4 times a day, which helps gut symptoms after 2 to 3 weeks. Avoid medium- or long-term systemic steroids (rebound, bone harm); budesonide suits gut symptoms. [S2]
- **Omalizumab after first-line symptomatic failure:** 150 mg every 15 days, increasing to 300 mg every 2 weeks if needed; give the first two injections in hospital. [S2]

## Bone and skin

- **Osteoporosis affects 8-41% of SM patients** and fragility fractures are common. Give a bisphosphonate with vitamin D and calcium for osteopenia or osteoporosis (GRADE 4), and consider cytoreduction after pathological fractures (GRADE 5). [S1]
- **Examine the skin at least once a year** (melanoma incidence appears raised); emollients, sunscreen, antihistamines, ketotifen and cromoglycate are the basis of skin care. (GRADE 4) [S1]
- **Skin involvement, step by step (S1 Table 3):** for everyone, avoid triggers (mast-cell-activating drugs, allergens, Hymenoptera stings, sudden or extreme temperature changes, rubbing), keep the skin moisturised and protect it from the sun. For lesions, itch and flushing use H1 and H2 antihistamines; for continuous moderate symptoms, a scheduled non-sedating H1 antihistamine with a sedating one on demand, an H2 blocker such as famotidine, and a mast cell stabiliser (ketotifen, with or without cromolyn); for severe symptoms add a leukotriene antagonist such as montelukast. Alternatives: short courses of topical or systemic corticosteroids (skin atrophy, osteopaenia and adrenal suppression with repeated use), pimecrolimus 1% cream twice a day (little evidence), phototherapy as second line, dye or Nd:YAG lasers, and excision of a solitary mastocytoma as third line. Diffuse forms with blistering or life-threatening symptoms may need hospital care. [S1]
- **Calcium and vitamin D (S2):** supplement when dietary calcium is under 1000 mg/day or 25-OH vitamin D is 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L) or less, in every form of mastocytosis. If denosumab is used, expect a rebound fracture risk when it stops; zoledronic acid with bone-resorption marker monitoring is the current approach. [S2]
- ⚠️ **No phototherapy (PUVA) in adults with mastocytosis:** melanoma is more common in mastocytosis, and phototherapy adds to that risk. Topical steroids have no place in adults. [S2]
- **Monitoring (S2):** tryptase, albumin, blood count and liver tests at least yearly to catch progression; bone density every 24 to 36 months; review symptoms 3 to 6 months after starting treatment, since control often takes that long. [S2]

## Advanced SM: cytoreduction and transplant

- **Cytoreduction is indicated for advanced SM, and for indolent or smouldering SM when symptom treatment fails.** Choose the agent by clinical picture and molecular results (KIT status, high-risk mutations); cladribine, interferon-alpha and midostaurin are suitable first choices. (GRADE 5) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Imatinib works only without KIT D816V.** It is an option for newly diagnosed aggressive SM that tests negative for D816V or carries an imatinib-sensitive mutation, or where genetic testing is unavailable. (GRADE 4) [S1]
- **Midostaurin gave a 60% response rate in advanced SM** (116 patients) with median PFS 14.1 months and OS 33.1 months; dose reduction for toxicity was needed in 56%. [S1]
- **In pregnancy needing cytoreduction, consider interferon-alpha.** (GRADE 4) [S1]
- **Consider allogeneic transplant** for eligible advanced SM with expected survival under 5 years, or SM with an associated neoplasm that itself warrants transplant (GRADE 4). In SM-AHN, treat whichever component drives the illness (GRADE 5). [S1]
- **Avapritinib (a KIT D816V inhibitor) for indolent SM with disabling symptoms:** in PIONEER, 54% of 141 patients versus 0 of 71 on placebo halved their serum tryptase by week 24. It is the first TKI for indolent mastocytosis refractory to symptomatic treatment (EU authorisation 2023). [S2]
- **Non-advanced SM, cytoreduction (S2):** used empirically and only after a multidisciplinary meeting; interferon-alpha and cladribine work but are rarely used in non-advanced disease, discussed after omalizumab fails. Midostaurin is authorised for advanced SM; in indolent SM its gut toxicity is poor (nausea 80%, vomiting 35%). Imatinib does not work with KIT D816V. [S2]
