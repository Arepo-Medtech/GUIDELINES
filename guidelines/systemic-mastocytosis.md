# Systemic mastocytosis in adults

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis (tryptase, marrow, KIT D816V), staging imaging, prognosis, symptom and anaphylaxis management, bone health, and cytoreductive treatment of indolent and advanced systemic mastocytosis in adults, including imatinib for KIT D816V-negative aggressive disease. Mastocytosis with eosinophilia carrying FIP1L1::PDGFRA is classed as a PDGFRA-rearranged myeloid neoplasm: see `hypereosinophilic-syndrome`. Anaphylaxis: see `acute-anaphylaxis`.

## Recognise and diagnose

- **Most adults with mastocytosis have the systemic form, and about 80% have skin lesions**, so investigate for SM in any adult with cutaneous mastocytosis. Some indolent SM involves only the marrow. [S1]
- **Diagnose cutaneous-only mastocytosis only after dermatology review, skin histology, and exclusion of SM criteria.** (GRADE 1) [S1]
- **Serum tryptase is needed for diagnosis and follow-up, at least yearly.** A persistent level above 20 ng/ml is a minor WHO criterion; levels above 200 ng/ml are much more common in aggressive SM and SM with an associated haematological neoplasm. [S1]
- **A bone marrow biopsy, ideally reviewed by a pathologist experienced in mast cell disease, is mandatory**; dense aggregates of at least 15 mast cells are the major criterion, and CD25/CD2 expression marks abnormal mast cells. [S1]
- **Test every SM patient for KIT D816V** (found in over 95% of adults; confers imatinib resistance) using a high-sensitivity method. With raised marrow mast cells plus eosinophilia, test for FIP1L1-PDGFRA. (GRADE 2) [S1]
- **Imaging:** Doppler abdominal ultrasound for all (liver size, portal hypertension, ascites); CT and MRI for 'C' findings. FDG-PET/CT has little role in initial assessment. (GRADE 3) [S1]
- **Estimate prognosis with the WHO subtype and the clinical or hybrid Mayo (MAPS) model.** (GRADE 4) [S1]

## Symptoms and anaphylaxis

- **Individualise treatment**, from watchful waiting and symptom control up to cytoreduction for aggressive, advanced or refractory disease. Trigger avoidance and antihistamines (H1 for general, H2 for gut symptoms) are the basis of symptom control. (GRADE 2) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Anaphylaxis risk is about 50% in adults with indolent SM.** Every SM patient should always carry two epinephrine autoinjectors, even without prior anaphylaxis, and patient and family should be trained; IM adrenaline is the emergency treatment. [S1]
- **Patients with SM and Hymenoptera venom allergy need lifelong venom immunotherapy.** [S1]
- **Before procedures, tailor prophylaxis to the procedure and disease form** (GRADE 1): usually none before skin biopsy, but premedicate before marrow biopsy or endoscopy. Beta-lactams and local anaesthetics are safe (GRADE 1); decide NSAIDs case by case. [S1]
- **No pre-emptive food restrictions are needed:** food allergy is no commoner than in the general population. Vaccinate (including against COVID-19) after same-day antihistamine prophylaxis. [S1]

## Bone and skin

- **Osteoporosis affects 8-41% of SM patients** and fragility fractures are common. Give a bisphosphonate with vitamin D and calcium for osteopenia or osteoporosis (GRADE 4), and consider cytoreduction after pathological fractures (GRADE 5). [S1]
- **Examine the skin at least once a year** (melanoma incidence appears raised); emollients, sunscreen, antihistamines, ketotifen and cromoglycate are the basis of skin care. (GRADE 4) [S1]

## Advanced SM: cytoreduction and transplant

- **Cytoreduction is indicated for advanced SM, and for indolent or smouldering SM when symptom treatment fails.** Choose the agent by clinical picture and molecular results (KIT status, high-risk mutations); cladribine, interferon-alpha and midostaurin are suitable first choices. (GRADE 5) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Imatinib works only without KIT D816V.** It is an option for newly diagnosed aggressive SM that tests negative for D816V or carries an imatinib-sensitive mutation, or where genetic testing is unavailable. (GRADE 4) [S1]
- **Midostaurin gave a 60% response rate in advanced SM** (116 patients) with median PFS 14.1 months and OS 33.1 months; dose reduction for toxicity was needed in 56%. [S1]
- **In pregnancy needing cytoreduction, consider interferon-alpha.** (GRADE 4) [S1]
- **Consider allogeneic transplant** for eligible advanced SM with expected survival under 5 years, or SM with an associated neoplasm that itself warrants transplant (GRADE 4). In SM-AHN, treat whichever component drives the illness (GRADE 5). [S1]
