# Peripheral T-cell lymphomas (nodal PTCL and systemic ALCL) in adults

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, staging, first-line treatment, transplant consolidation, relapsed/refractory treatment and follow-up of nodal peripheral T-cell lymphomas (PTCL-NOS, follicular helper T-cell lymphoma) and systemic anaplastic large-cell lymphoma (ALCL) in adults. Extranodal and leukaemic subtypes (ENKTCL, EATL, HSTCL, T-PLL, ATLL, LGL) are in the source but not summarised. Cutaneous T-cell lymphoma: see `cutaneous-t-cell-lymphoma`. Related B-cell counterparts: `diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma`, `hodgkin-lymphoma`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 33 claims; **77 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 9 doses.** **S1** ESMO and EHA Guidelines Committees (d'Amore F (published 7 May 2025; international): 22 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored · **S2** eviQ Cancer Treatments Online (retrieved 24 September 2026 (review due: 4038 30 June 2030; 69 and 630 as required; 1665 and 3455 30 June 2027; 3507 31 December 2027); AU): 11 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ International guideline (ESMO-EHA, 2025). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **Australia funds drugs Europe does not.** The PBS lists romidepsin and pralatrexate for chemotherapy-refractory PTCL; the source notes these single agents have only conditional US (FDA) approval and no EMA approval, and places them below salvage chemotherapy and transplant.

> 🇦🇺 **S2 is Australian: six eviQ treatment protocols, not guidelines** (CHOP21, CHOEP21, BV-CHP, brentuximab vedotin, romidepsin, pralatrexate). They add the doses, cycle numbers, PBS status and supportive care S1 does not give. Where they differ from S1, both are shown.

## Diagnose and stage

- **Get a surgical (excisional or incisional) biopsy where possible**; core biopsies are much less accurate for diagnosis and subtyping. (II, A) [S1]
- **PTCL is rare and hard to diagnose**, so an expert haematopathologist with all slides and at least 1 tissue block should make or confirm the diagnosis. Treatment differs by entity. [S1]
- **Discuss every patient in a PTCL-experienced multidisciplinary team.** (I, A) [S1]
- **Baseline tests:** full blood count, LDH, uric acid, renal and liver function, immunoglobulins, and HIV, hepatitis B and C and EBV serology (HTLV-1 in ATLL). [S1]
- **PET-CT is the preferred imaging** at diagnosis and end of treatment (I, B), with CT if PET is unavailable. A bone marrow biopsy is recommended in every case (I, A), and re-biopsy at relapse (I, A). [S1]

## First line: PTCL-NOS and follicular helper T-cell lymphoma

- **Offer a clinical trial whenever possible.** (II, A) [S1]
- **CHOP (or CHOP-like, such as CHOEP) is standard** for non-ALCL nodal types despite poor outcomes; nothing has beaten it. (II, B) [S1]
- **Brentuximab-CHP did not improve outcomes in PTCL-NOS or TFHL** in the randomised trial (few such patients were enrolled), and is not EU-approved for them. [S1]
- **Limited-stage, non-bulky, favourable-risk disease:** a shortened course of three or four cycles (CHOP or CHOEP) can suffice (IV, B), optionally followed by involved-site radiotherapy of 30-40 Gy in 15-20 fractions (IV, B). [S1]
- **Autologous transplant consolidation can be considered** for responders whose disease is advanced, or limited but high-risk (III, B); it is acceptable in chemosensitive disease. [S1]
- 🇦🇺 **CHOP21 as given in Australia (eviQ ID 69):** prednisolone 100 mg by mouth on days 1 to 5, with doxorubicin 50 mg/m², vincristine 1.4 mg/m² (capped at 2 mg) and cyclophosphamide 750 mg/m², all IV on day 1; repeat every 21 days for 6 to 8 cycles. Every drug is on the PBS general schedule, and eviQ suggests considering a 5 to 7 day prednisolone 100 mg prephase before cycle 1. [S2]
- 🇦🇺 **CHOEP21 (eviQ ID 630), for mature T-cell or NK-cell lymphoma, nodal or extranodal:** the same prednisolone, doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide doses, a flat vincristine 2 mg, and etoposide 100 mg/m² IV on days 1 to 3; every 21 days for 6 to 8 cycles, all on the PBS general schedule. [S2]
- **Anthracycline regimens (eviQ):** count every anthracycline the patient has ever had, do a baseline echocardiogram (or gated heart pool scan) and ECG, and repeat the echocardiogram within 12 months of finishing; tumour lysis risk is high, so give prophylaxis. [S2]

## First line: systemic ALCL

- ⚠️ **Six cycles of brentuximab vedotin with CHP are recommended for chemotherapy-eligible ALCL, ALK-positive or ALK-negative** (I, A); CHO(E)P for six cycles is an alternative (III, B). [S1]
- **In the randomised trial, BV-CHP beat CHOP in ALCL:** 5-year PFS 51.4% versus 43.0%, and 5-year OS 70.1% versus 61.0%. [S1]
- **Limited-stage, non-bulky ALCL with IPI 0-1:** a shortened course of three or four cycles (BV-CHP or CHOEP) is an option. (III, B) [S1]
- **Transplant in first CR:** consider autologous transplant for eligible, chemosensitive ALK-negative ALCL (II, B). Generally not for ALK-positive ALCL (III, D), unless high-risk (IPI 2 or more, bulky) without CR after three cycles (III, B). [S1]
- 🇦🇺 **BV-CHP as given in Australia (eviQ ID 4038):** brentuximab vedotin 1.8 mg/kg (capped at 180 mg), cyclophosphamide 750 mg/m² and doxorubicin 50 mg/m², all IV on day 1, with prednisolone 100 mg by mouth on days 1 to 5; every 21 days for 6 to 8 cycles. Brentuximab is PBS Authority; G-CSF is at the clinician's discretion. [S2]
- **eviQ's BV-CHP protocol is indicated for any previously untreated CD30-positive PTCL,** not only systemic ALCL (compare S1 above, and Unresolved). [S2]
- ⚠️ **Brentuximab vedotin safety (eviQ):** infusion reactions are a high risk (premedicate after any earlier reaction); assess neuropathy before every dose, since grade 2 or worse may call for a lower dose, a delay or omission; investigate unexplained abdominal pain with amylase and lipase; and watch for new cough or breathlessness, PML and severe skin reactions (SJS, TEN, DRESS). [S2]

## Relapsed or refractory disease

- ⚠️ **Prognosis is poor** (median PFS 3.1 months and OS 5.5 months in one study), so enter a clinical trial whenever possible. (II, A) [S1]
- **Outside a trial, use a platinum-based regimen not cross-resistant with first-line anthracycline chemotherapy** (for example ICE, DHAP, GDP). Frailer patients can have lenalidomide, bendamustine or ciclosporin. (III, B) [S1]
- **Transplant responders:** for eligible patients who respond to salvage, allogeneic transplant is preferred if not used first line; it is the best curative option, with about 50% 5-year survival in retrospective series. (II, B) [S1]
- **Romidepsin and pralatrexate as single agents** have conditional FDA approval only (not EMA), and romidepsin, like several other single agents, gives response rates under 40% with few lasting responses. [S1]
- **Relapsed ALK-negative ALCL:** salvage chemotherapy (ICE, DHAP, GDP, IVAC-MTX) or brentuximab monotherapy (III, B). [S1]
- **Relapsed ALK-positive ALCL:** brentuximab if not used first line or after a late relapse (III, A); if refractory to it, an ALK inhibitor such as crizotinib (III, A). Consolidate responders with allogeneic transplant (III, A). [S1]
- 🇦🇺 **Brentuximab alone (eviQ ID 1665) for relapsed or refractory CD30-positive systemic ALCL after suitable front-line curative chemotherapy:** 1.8 mg/kg IV (capped at 180 mg) every 21 days for up to 16 cycles; PBS Authority. [S2]
- 🇦🇺 **Romidepsin (eviQ ID 3507), for PTCL after at least one earlier therapy:** 14 mg/m² IV on days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle, until progression or unacceptable toxicity; PBS Authority. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Romidepsin can lengthen the QT interval:** correct electrolyte problems before starting, avoid other QT-prolonging drugs, and consider baseline and periodic ECGs and potassium, magnesium and calcium in high-risk patients; check FBC, EUC, eGFR, calcium, magnesium and phosphate before every dose. eviQ recommends Pneumocystis prophylaxis (for example trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 160/800 mg twice a day on two days each week, or once a day on three days each week) and antifungal prophylaxis. [S2]
- 🇦🇺 **Pralatrexate (eviQ ID 3455), for relapsed or chemotherapy-refractory PTCL:** 30 mg/m² by IV bolus once a week for six weeks, then a one-week break (a 49-day cycle), until progression; leucovorin (calcium folinate) 30 mg by mouth, taken thrice daily on the 2 days after each dose, is added to reduce mucositis. PBS Authority. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Pralatrexate needs vitamin B12 and folate cover:** hydroxocobalamin 1000 micrograms IM, first given within 10 weeks before the first dose and then every 8-10 weeks, and folic acid 1 mg by mouth daily from within 10 days before the first dose until 30 days after the last. Check for mucositis weekly and omit or reduce the dose at grade 2 or worse; serious, sometimes fatal skin reactions tend to appear early, usually after the first dose. [S2]

## Follow-up

- **Review with history and examination every 3 months for 1 year, then every 6 months for 2 more years** (4 more after autologous transplant) (IV, C). Routine surveillance scans in CR are not recommended (III, D). [S1]
- **Follow-up can stop** after 3 years if not transplanted and asymptomatic, or after 5 years after autologous transplant, if still in CR. (III, B) [S1]
