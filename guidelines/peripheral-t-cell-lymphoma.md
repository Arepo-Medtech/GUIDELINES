# Peripheral T-cell lymphomas (nodal PTCL and systemic ALCL) in adults

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, staging, first-line treatment, transplant consolidation, relapsed/refractory treatment and follow-up of nodal peripheral T-cell lymphomas (PTCL-NOS, follicular helper T-cell lymphoma) and systemic anaplastic large-cell lymphoma (ALCL) in adults. Extranodal and leukaemic subtypes (ENKTCL, EATL, HSTCL, T-PLL, ATLL, LGL) are in the source but not summarised. Cutaneous T-cell lymphoma: see `cutaneous-t-cell-lymphoma`. Related B-cell counterparts: `diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma`, `hodgkin-lymphoma`.

## Diagnose and stage

- **Get a surgical (excisional or incisional) biopsy where possible**; core biopsies are much less accurate for diagnosis and subtyping. (II, A) [S1]
- **PTCL is rare and hard to diagnose**, so an expert haematopathologist with all slides and at least 1 tissue block should make or confirm the diagnosis. Treatment differs by entity. [S1]
- **Discuss every patient in a PTCL-experienced multidisciplinary team.** (I, A) [S1]
- **Baseline tests:** full blood count, LDH, uric acid, renal and liver function, immunoglobulins, and HIV, hepatitis B and C and EBV serology (HTLV-1 in ATLL). [S1]
- **PET-CT is the preferred imaging** at diagnosis and end of treatment (I, B), with CT if PET is unavailable. A bone marrow biopsy is recommended in every case (I, A), and re-biopsy at relapse (I, A). [S1]

## First line: PTCL-NOS and follicular helper T-cell lymphoma

- **Offer a clinical trial whenever possible.** (II, A) [S1]
- **CHOP (or CHOP-like, such as CHOEP) is standard** for non-ALCL nodal types despite poor outcomes; nothing has beaten it. (II, B) [S1]
- **Brentuximab-CHP did not improve outcomes in PTCL-NOS or TFHL** in the randomised trial (few such patients were enrolled), and is not EU-approved for them. [S1]
- **Limited-stage, non-bulky, favourable-risk disease:** a shortened course of three or four cycles (CHOP or CHOEP) can suffice (IV, B), optionally followed by involved-site radiotherapy of 30-40 Gy in 15-20 fractions (IV, B). [S1]
- **Autologous transplant consolidation can be considered** for responders whose disease is advanced, or limited but high-risk (III, B); it is acceptable in chemosensitive disease. [S1]

## First line: systemic ALCL

- ⚠️ **Six cycles of brentuximab vedotin with CHP are recommended for chemotherapy-eligible ALCL, ALK-positive or ALK-negative** (I, A); CHO(E)P for six cycles is an alternative (III, B). [S1]
- **In the randomised trial, BV-CHP beat CHOP in ALCL:** 5-year PFS 51.4% versus 43.0%, and 5-year OS 70.1% versus 61.0%. [S1]
- **Limited-stage, non-bulky ALCL with IPI 0-1:** a shortened course of three or four cycles (BV-CHP or CHOEP) is an option. (III, B) [S1]
- **Transplant in first CR:** consider autologous transplant for eligible, chemosensitive ALK-negative ALCL (II, B). Generally not for ALK-positive ALCL (III, D), unless high-risk (IPI 2 or more, bulky) without CR after three cycles (III, B). [S1]

## Relapsed or refractory disease

- ⚠️ **Prognosis is poor** (median PFS 3.1 months and OS 5.5 months in one study), so enter a clinical trial whenever possible. (II, A) [S1]
- **Outside a trial, use a platinum-based regimen not cross-resistant with first-line anthracycline chemotherapy** (for example ICE, DHAP, GDP). Frailer patients can have lenalidomide, bendamustine or ciclosporin. (III, B) [S1]
- **Transplant responders:** for eligible patients who respond to salvage, allogeneic transplant is preferred if not used first line; it is the best curative option, with about 50% 5-year survival in retrospective series. (II, B) [S1]
- **Romidepsin and pralatrexate as single agents** have conditional FDA approval only (not EMA), and romidepsin, like several other single agents, gives response rates under 40% with few lasting responses. [S1]
- **Relapsed ALK-negative ALCL:** salvage chemotherapy (ICE, DHAP, GDP, IVAC-MTX) or brentuximab monotherapy (III, B). [S1]
- **Relapsed ALK-positive ALCL:** brentuximab if not used first line or after a late relapse (III, A); if refractory to it, an ALK inhibitor such as crizotinib (III, A). Consolidate responders with allogeneic transplant (III, A). [S1]

## Follow-up

- **Review with history and examination every 3 months for 1 year, then every 6 months for 2 more years** (4 more after autologous transplant) (IV, C). Routine surveillance scans in CR are not recommended (III, D). [S1]
- **Follow-up can stop** after 3 years if not transplanted and asymptomatic, or after 5 years after autologous transplant, if still in CR. (III, B) [S1]
