# Progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis (PFIC)

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** children and adults with PFIC types 1–6 and benign recurrent intrahepatic cholestasis (BRIC): recognition by subtype, nutrition, pruritus treatment including ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitors, cancer surveillance, biliary diversion and liver transplant. Alagille syndrome is in the source but not summarised here. Adult cholestatic disease: see `primary-biliary-cholangitis`.

## Recognise

- PFIC is a group of single-gene disorders of canalicular bile acid transport or secretion; the intermittent form is BRIC and the chronic form is PFIC. [S1]
- PFIC disorders are autosomal recessive. Pruritus is the most significant clinical feature and can affect school, sleep and quality of life. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Serum GGT separates the types:** PFIC1 and PFIC2 have low or normal GGT; PFIC3 (ABCB4/MDR3) is distinguished by raised GGT. [S1]
- **PFIC1 (ATP8B1)** starts in infancy and typically progresses to cirrhosis by the second decade, with extrahepatic features (pancreatitis, diarrhoea, sensorineural hearing loss) that can persist or worsen after liver transplant. [S1]
- **PFIC2 (ABCB11/BSEP)** is the most common form, usually presenting in the neonatal period; without treatment it progresses rapidly to cirrhosis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption can cause catastrophic bleeding (vitamin K), rickets (vitamin D) and neuromuscular dysfunction (vitamin E).** [S1]

## Nutrition and supportive care

- Failure to thrive and malnutrition are common and need close monitoring and supplementation, especially with **formula containing medium-chain triglycerides**, plus fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E and K). Some children need nasogastric or gastrostomy feeds. [S1]
- Skin emollients, trimmed fingernails and avoiding hot baths help minimise itching and excoriation. [S1]

## Pruritus: first-line medicines

- Antihistamine and ursodeoxycholic acid can be trialled but are typically not very effective alone in PFIC. **Ursodeoxycholic acid 10–20 mg/kg daily** may improve liver biochemistry but helps little in severe pruritus with PFIC1 or PFIC2; in PFIC3 it has normalised liver tests in half of patients. [S1]
- Other medicines to consider include rifampicin, cholestyramine or antihistamines. Cholestyramine must be given at least 2 hours apart from other medicines and increases the risk of fat-soluble vitamin deficiency. [S1]

## Pruritus: IBAT inhibitors (odevixibat)

- Ileal bile acid transporter inhibitors have shown efficacy for refractory pruritus in PFIC; two are available, maralixibat and odevixibat. [S1]
- Odevixibat is approved (in the source's jurisdiction) for pruritus in all types of PFIC from 3 months of age. [S1]
- In the phase 3 PEDFIC trial (PFIC1 or PFIC2), odevixibat improved pruritus by week 4, with sustained improvement, lower aminotransferases and better growth and sleep; no serious adverse events were reported. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Odevixibat dosing: start at 40 µg/kg once daily; if pruritus has not improved after 3 months, increase in 40 µg/kg steps up to 120 µg/kg once daily.** [S1]
- Maralixibat in PFIC1 and PFIC2 (INDIGO) gave a response that varied by subtype; BSEP deficiency with residual function did best. [S1]

## Monitor

- ⚠️ **PFIC2 carries a risk of hepatocellular carcinoma and cholangiocarcinoma, as early as 10 months of age**: screen with tumour markers (including alpha-fetoprotein) and imaging. [S1]

## Surgery and transplant

- For pruritus refractory to medicines, surgical biliary diversion can be considered. After partial external biliary diversion one study reported improved pruritus in 81% with PFIC1 and 76% with PFIC2; no non-transplant operation has been shown superior to another. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hepatoportoenterostomy (Kasai) is not indicated in PFIC** and can increase liver injury. [S1]
- Liver transplant is considered for end-stage liver disease, HCC or refractory pruritus. With severe FIC1 or BSEP deficiency only 50% survive with their native liver at 10 years. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In PFIC1 without cirrhosis, strongly consider non-transplant surgery first**, because transplant can bring pancreatitis, allograft steatosis and diarrhoea. In PFIC2, anti-BSEP antibodies can cause recurrent disease after transplant. [S1]
