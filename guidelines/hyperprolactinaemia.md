# Pathological hyperprolactinaemia and prolactinoma

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognising and confirming hyperprolactinaemia, excluding secondary causes, and dopamine agonist therapy, surgery, withdrawal, pregnancy and special groups for adults and children with prolactinoma. Antipsychotic-related hyperprolactinaemia is also covered in `schizophrenia`; fertility work-up in `infertility`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise

- **Look for high prolactin** with galactorrhoea (unless pregnant or lactating), loss of libido or infertility, new menstrual irregularity or amenorrhoea, erectile dysfunction or low-gonadotrophin hypogonadism in men, or any sellar mass. No galactorrhoea does not rule it out. In children, delayed puberty is a trigger too. (strong) [S1]
- **Exclude other causes first:** review all medicines (dopamine antagonists such as antipsychotics, metoclopramide and domperidone), and test for pregnancy, primary hypothyroidism, renal insufficiency and liver failure. (strong) [S1]
- 🇦🇺 **Reading the level (Australian units, S2):** physiological rises (pregnancy, breastfeeding, exercise, sex, stress) are brief and usually no more than twice the upper limit; drug-induced rises usually run 500–4000 mIU/L; stalk compression usually stays below 2000 mIU/L; a level above 10 000 mIU/L almost always means a macroprolactinoma. [S2]
- **Drug-induced hyperprolactinaemia (S2):** it is seen with antipsychotics such as risperidone and, less often, some SSRIs, and usually resolves within 72 hours of stopping the drug. [S2]

## Confirm and image

- **If prolactin is under five times the upper limit of normal, repeat it;** use cannulated sampling when stress may be the cause. A lesion pressing on the stalk can raise prolactin up to six times ULN without a prolactinoma. (strong) [S1]
- **Check for macroprolactin when prolactin is only moderately raised (below 200 ng/ml)**, at least when symptoms or imaging do not fit. **With a giant adenoma but normal or only mildly high prolactin, re-measure at 1:100 dilution** to exclude the hook effect. (strong) [S1]
- **MRI once hyperprolactinaemia is confirmed** and no non-adenoma cause is evident. **Screen IGF1 at baseline** for hidden GH co-secretion (acromegaly), and test other pituitary axes in macroadenoma. (strong) [S1]
- **S2: one raised prolactin can be enough for the diagnosis, provided blood was taken without undue stress** (S1 advises repeating results below five times the upper limit). [S2]
- 🇦🇺 **Macroprolactin accounts for at least 10% of hyperprolactinaemia** and is mostly inactive. Many Australian laboratories screen for it routinely with polyethylene glycol precipitation, which also gives the monomeric prolactin, usually normal in macroprolactinaemia. [S2]
- **Work-up once confirmed (S2):** gonadotrophins with oestradiol in women or morning testosterone in men; thyroid and kidney function; a pregnancy test in women who could be pregnant. Pituitary MRI unless another clear cause is found; for a mass over 1 cm, test other pituitary hormones and visual fields. Check bone density if hypogonadal. [S2]

## Treat

- ⚠️ **Cabergoline is the preferred dopamine agonist** (long half-life, most effective, well tolerated), and patients on other agonists who are resistant or intolerant should switch to it. Knosp grade 2 or higher: treat with cabergoline. (strong) [S1]
- **Usual doses:** cabergoline 0.5 to 3.5 mg per week, bromocriptine 2.5 to 15 mg per day. Start low and increase slowly; taking it at bedtime or with food may help tolerance. (weak) [S1]
- **Offer surgery as a first-line option alongside drugs for microprolactinomas and well-contained macroprolactinomas (Knosp 0–1)**, done by an expert pituitary surgeon; also consider it for intolerance or resistance, or rapidly worsening vision. (strong / weak) [S1]
- **A premenopausal woman with a microprolactinoma who does not want pregnancy can simply take sex hormone replacement.** Men still hypogonadal after more than 6 months of treatment should be considered for testosterone. (strong / weak) [S1]
- **Resistance means no normal prolactin, or under 30% shrinkage, after at least 6 months on standard doses** (cabergoline 2.0 mg per week, bromocriptine 7.5–10 mg per day). For aggressive tumours that keep growing after all other options, temozolomide is recommended. [S1]
- **Treatment is usually unnecessary** for physiological or drug-induced hyperprolactinaemia, macroprolactinaemia or an asymptomatic microprolactinoma; hypothyroidism-related hyperprolactinaemia should settle on thyroxine. (S2) [S2]
- **Symptomatic drug-induced hyperprolactinaemia (S2):** first stop the drug or switch to one that spares prolactin. If stopping is riskier, treat hypogonadism with sex hormone replacement. A dopamine agonist is only occasionally used for galactorrhoea, because it can worsen the psychiatric illness in someone on an antipsychotic. [S2]
- 🇦🇺 **In Australia, cabergoline and bromocriptine are the dopamine agonists mostly used, and quinagolide (non-ergot) is also available;** start low and titrate to limit nausea and postural hypotension. Cabergoline normalises prolactin in up to 95% and shrinks the tumour in about 90%. S2 names the agonist as first-line for every macroprolactinoma and most symptomatic microprolactinomas. [S2]
- **S2 defines resistance as under 50% shrinkage or a prolactin that does not normalise** (10% of patients on cabergoline, 25% on bromocriptine); try another agonist or higher doses. Surgery, radiotherapy or both are often needed for persistent field loss, resistance or apoplexy; after surgery prolactin normalises in about 90% of microadenomas but under 50% of macroadenomas. [S2]

## Safety and monitoring

- 🚩 **Warn about impulse-control disorders** (gambling, compulsive buying, hypersexuality, aggression, mood change), more often in men; stop or reduce the drug if they occur, and involve the partner or family. (strong) [S1]
- **Echocardiogram for cardiac valve disease:** baseline if long-term cabergoline above 2.0 mg per week is expected, then every 2–3 years on that dose; on 2.0 mg per week or less, after 5–6 years. A new murmur also needs one. (weak) [S1]
- **MRI 3–6 months after starting a dopamine agonist for a macroprolactinoma.** If there is no good response by 3–6 months, adequate response is unlikely. [S1]
- **Consider stopping cabergoline at intervals:** about one in five stay in remission. Best candidates: low maintenance dose, more than 2 years of treatment, clear shrinkage. After stopping, check prolactin every 3 months for a year, then yearly for life. Recurrence usually responds to restarting. (strong) [S1]
- 🚩 **CSF rhinorrhoea after a shrinking invasive macroprolactinoma:** test nasal fluid for β2-transferrin or β-trace protein; confirmed leaks need surgical repair. (strong) [S1]
- **Valve risk from cabergoline (S2) is mainly with daily doses above 3 mg (as in Parkinson's disease);** prolactinoma doses, usually under 2 mg a week, appear not to cause valvulopathy, and bromocriptine does not. [S2]
- **Monitoring and stopping (S2):** check prolactin one month after starting, then periodically; MRI after about a year for a microprolactinoma, earlier for a macroprolactinoma, new symptoms or rising prolactin. The agonist can often be tapered off after 2–3 years if prolactin stays normal, especially with no visible adenoma, but hyperprolactinaemia returns in 26–69%, mostly within 12 months, so keep testing. [S2]

## Pregnancy and antipsychotics

- **Stop the dopamine agonist once pregnancy is confirmed** (keeping it is an option for large macroprolactinomas). Do not use prolactin to follow the tumour in pregnancy. With a macroprolactinoma, review monthly and check visual fields every 3 months; MRI without gadolinium if growth is suspected. (strong) [S1]
- **Measure prolactin before starting an antipsychotic.** On an antipsychotic, prolactin above ten times ULN is unusual and suggests a prolactinoma; reducing the dose or switching to a prolactin-sparing drug such as aripiprazole usually tells the two apart. (strong) [S1]
- **Pregnancy raises growth risk mainly in macroprolactinomas:** clinically significant growth in 20–25%, against about 3% for microprolactinomas. (S2) [S2]
