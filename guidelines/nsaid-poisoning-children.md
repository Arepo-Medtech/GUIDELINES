# NSAID poisoning in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** risk assessment by ibuprofen dose, investigations, supportive care and disposition for non-aspirin NSAID poisoning in children. Aspirin is covered separately in `salicylate-poisoning-children`; general principles are in `poisoning-initial-management-children`; seizure management is in `seizures-acute-management-children`.

## Key points

- **At lower doses most NSAID overdoses are benign.** ⚠️ **A large overdose can lead to coma, shock, seizures and acute renal failure.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Mefenamic acid overdose is associated with seizures — over 3.5 g can cause them.** [S1]
- **Covers** ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, celecoxib, meloxicam, indomethacin, ketorolac, piroxicam, etoricoxib, parecoxib, mefenamic acid, ketoprofen, sulindac, tiaprofenic acid — **not aspirin** (see salicylate poisoning). [S1]

## Assessment

- **Assess:** any symptomatic child · any significant accidental ingestion or deliberate self-poisoning. **Consider non-accidental poisoning** if developmental age doesn't fit. [S1]
- **Examine for:** epigastric pain, nausea, vomiting · drowsiness, lethargy, seizures, coma · acute renal failure · shock. [S1]
- **Ibuprofen severity:** under 200 mg/kg no symptoms · 200–400 mg/kg mild CNS and GI effects · ⚠️ **over 400 mg/kg multi-organ dysfunction becomes a risk.** [S1]
- **Symptomatic or concurrently ill children: FBC, UEC, LFTs.** Consider paracetamol in all intentional overdoses. [S1]

## Management

- **Care is supportive. After a large overdose, keep the child well hydrated** to lower the risk of acute renal failure. [S1]
- **Activated charcoal is not indicated.** [S1]
- **Discuss with a toxicologist**, particularly for symptomatic children and abnormal pathology. [S1]
- **Epigastric pain → proton pump inhibitor; seizures → manage as afebrile seizures.** [S1]

## Disposition

- **Admit for observation:** all symptomatic children · ⚠️ **ibuprofen 200 mg/kg or more** · all intentional overdoses. Poisons Information Centre 131126. [S1]
- **Transfer** when care exceeds the local centre's capacity. PIPER: 1300 137 650. [S1]
- **A child who is symptom-free with normal vital signs can go home 4 hours after ingestion.** [S1]
- **Adolescents with deliberate ingestion need a self-harm risk assessment and drug and alcohol assessment before discharge**, with links to services if needs are found. [S1]
