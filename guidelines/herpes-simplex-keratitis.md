# Herpes simplex keratitis: treatment by corneal layer, and prophylaxis

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** specialist diagnosis and treatment of herpes simplex keratitis by the corneal layer involved (epithelial, stromal, endothelial), long-term oral prophylaxis, perioperative and post-keratoplasty prophylaxis, and antiviral resistance, in adults and children. Primary-care recognition, same-day referral and the 'no steroid for a dendrite' rule are in `conjunctivitis-and-eye-infections` (adults) and `acute-red-eye-children`; neonatal and systemic HSV are out of scope. ## Classify by corneal layer

- **Name the disease by the layer involved, because it sets the treatment:** epithelial (dendritic or geographic ulcer), stromal (with or without ulceration) or endothelial (formerly 'disciform'). Fluorescein shows whether a stromal keratitis is ulcerated, which decides the antiviral–steroid balance. [S1]
- **Epithelial disease is active viral infection and needs antivirals; stromal disease is mainly immune** and usually needs an antiviral plus a topical steroid. [S1]
- **A typical dendrite is a slit-lamp diagnosis;** laboratory tests are rarely needed and do not help in stromal disease, where virus usually cannot be grown. Keep testing for atypical cases. [S1]

## Who is at higher risk

- **Children get worse disease:** more often bilateral, combined epithelial and stromal, recurring within the first year, and misdiagnosed. Scarring can cause amblyopia, so watch them more closely than adults. (Strong, moderate quality) [S1]
- **Ask about atopy** in severe, bilateral or treatment-resistant disease; bilateral cases are often misdiagnosed, and atopic patients tend to respond better to oral than topical antivirals. (Strong, moderate quality) [S1]
- ⚠️ **Steroids by any route (drops, intravitreal or systemic) can provoke severe epithelial keratitis,** and prostaglandin-analogue glaucoma drops are best avoided in patients with a history of HSV. [S1]
- **Any eye surgery in a patient with past ocular HSV:** strongly consider antiviral cover around the operation, particularly while steroid drops are in use. [S1]

## Epithelial keratitis

- ⚠️ **Treat with an antiviral alone, and keep topical steroids out of the initial management.** (Strong, good quality) [S1]
- **Oral aciclovir 400 mg five times daily did at least as well as topical aciclovir ointment five times daily** in two randomised trials. Topical aciclovir is not an FDA-approved eye product but is widely used outside the US. [S1]
- **Two antivirals at once do not speed healing, and debridement alone is not adequate treatment** (use it only if antivirals are contraindicated or unavailable). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Use oral antivirals cautiously over age 65 and in renal impairment,** as all three can be nephrotoxic; reduce doses and lengthen intervals in renal insufficiency, planned with a nephrologist. [S1]

## Stromal keratitis

- ⚠️ **Treat with a topical steroid plus an oral antiviral for at least ten weeks,** adjusting the balance according to whether the epithelium is ulcerated. (Strong, good quality) [S1]
- **Why longer than ten weeks:** in HEDS a ten-week prednisolone taper cut treatment failure to 26% from 73% on placebo, but half the steroid group failed within six weeks of stopping. The steroid taper should therefore run beyond ten weeks, titrated to response. [S1]
- **Prefer oral over topical antivirals here:** the US topical agents penetrate the stroma poorly, and long-term trifluridine is toxic to the ocular surface. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Stromal keratitis with epithelial ulceration is the form most often mistaken for microbial keratitis.** It is under-studied; the evidence favours therapeutic-dose oral antiviral with careful topical steroid. [S1]

## Endothelial keratitis

- **Treat with a topical steroid plus an oral antiviral.** (Strong, good quality) Mean healing in trials took 21 to 25 days, faster than stromal disease, so prolonged treatment may not be needed. [S1]

## Prevent recurrence

- ⚠️ **Recurrent stromal keratitis: give long-term (one year or more) low-dose oral prophylaxis** — aciclovir 400 mg twice daily or valaciclovir 500 mg once daily, the only regimens proven to cut recurrence; famciclovir 250 mg twice daily is a reasonable alternative. (Strong, good quality) [S1]
- **In HEDS, a year of prophylaxis roughly halved recurrences, with no lasting effect once it stopped;** the benefit was greatest after stromal keratitis or multiple recurrences. [S1]
- ⚠️ **After penetrating keratoplasty for HSV: oral aciclovir 800 mg three times a day, later reduced to 400 mg twice a day; keep it going a year or longer,** and for as long as topical steroid continues. (Strong, good quality) [S1]
- **Suspect resistance with frequent recurrence despite prophylaxis, or treatment failure in an immunocompromised patient,** and consider susceptibility testing: aciclovir resistance reaches 6.4% in immunocompetent patients. (Strong, moderate quality) [S1]
- **Diagnosis and management belong with an ophthalmologist** because of the risk of blindness; teach patients the warning signs of recurrence and the need to return promptly. [S1]
