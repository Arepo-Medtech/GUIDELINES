## Definitions

- **Most children have isolated asymptomatic microscopic haematuria**: they need follow-up with repeat urinalysis, not immediate investigation. [S1]
- **Microscopic haematuria = more than 10 RBC/microlitre**, detectable only on urinalysis. [S1]
- **Persistent microscopic haematuria = 3 positive samples at least 1 week apart**, not after exercise or during menstruation. [S1]
- **Macroscopic haematuria** means visible blood; its source is more often the urethra or bladder than the kidney. [S1]
- **Red or brown urine is not always blood** — haemoglobin, myoglobin, medication or food can cause it; microscopy differentiates. [S1]
- **Newborn 'brick dust' in the nappy is usually urate crystals**, sometimes mistaken for blood. Blood may also come from outside the urinary tract (vaginal bleeding, rectal fissure). [S1]
- **Common causes:** isolated asymptomatic microscopic haematuria, viral infection, UTI, trauma, HSP, hypercalciuria, inherited glomerular basement membrane disorders. [S1]

## Assess

- **History:** previous haematuria, UTI symptoms, systemic symptoms (fatigue, oedema, rash, arthralgia, coryza), **recent trauma (non-accidental injury included) or surgery**, family history of hearing loss, renal disease/stones or haematuria, bleeding disorder or immunodeficiency. [S1]
- **Ask about medicines** (e.g. NSAIDs, rifampicin, metronidazole, nitrofurantoin, cyclophosphamide), **food** (beetroot and berries colour urine) and **exercise**. [S1]
- **Examine:** urinalysis, weight, eyes, skin, genitalia, joints, oedema, organomegaly, and **blood pressure**. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags — haematuria plus at least one of:** hypertension · proteinuria · flank/abdominal pain · fluid overload (ascites, oedema) · immunocompromise. [S1]

## Investigate

- **Isolated asymptomatic microscopic haematuria with no other abnormality: GP review, repeating urinalysis twice more with at least 1 week between samples** (not during menstruation, no exercise that day). No other interim tests; it commonly resolves. [S1]
- **Persistent isolated microscopic haematuria:** urine microscopy, urinary calcium:creatinine and protein:creatinine ratios, and urinalysis of immediate family. [S1]
- **Macroscopic haematuria:** urine microscopy and culture, calcium:creatinine and protein:creatinine ratios, FBE, UEC, LFT, CMP, coagulation profile, renal ultrasound; **glomerulonephritis screen if painless**. [S1]

## Escalate and discharge

- ⚠️ **Consult the local paediatric team** if the child is systemically unwell, has any red flag, or **microscopic haematuria has not resolved after three consecutive tests**. [S1]
- **Nephritis or haemolytic uraemic syndrome: consider nephrology. Abdominal mass: consider surgery or urology.** [S1]
- **Discharge once suitable follow-up has been planned.** [S1]
