# Endogenous Cushing's syndrome: medical treatment

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with endogenous Cushing's syndrome (pituitary, ectopic or adrenal) who need cortisol-lowering drugs: when medical therapy is used, osilodrostat and other steroidogenesis inhibitors, titration versus block-and-replace, monitoring, the adrenal-insufficiency risk, thromboprophylaxis and pregnancy. Diagnosis and surgery are not covered. Adrenal crisis in children: `adrenal-crisis-children`.

## When drugs are used

- **Surgery is first line for every cause of Cushing's syndrome.** [S1]
- **Medical therapy is used when surgery is impossible or unsuitable, to prepare severely hypercortisolaemic patients for surgery, long term after failed surgery or recurrence, or as a bridge while pituitary radiotherapy takes effect.** [S1]
- **Cushing's disease:** drugs are mainly for those not in remission after surgery or the 20% to 30% who relapse later. Other options are repeat surgery (remission up to 75% to 80% in expert centres), bilateral adrenalectomy (almost 100% remission, but permanent adrenal insufficiency), or radiotherapy, which takes 1 to 10 years and needs medical bridging. [S1]
- **Recurrence markers:** mean of at least two 24-hour UFCs, average of at least 3 late-night salivary cortisols, and cortisol after a 1-mg overnight dexamethasone test. Patients with at least 2 of the 3 raised are more likely to benefit from treatment. [S1]
- **Routine preoperative medical treatment is not supported,** except in poor general health, contraindication to surgery, or refusal. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Very severe hypercortisolism (e.g. ectopic ACTH, UFC more than 10 times ULN) is treated medically first,** with rapid dose escalation, to get the patient fit for tumour surgery or bilateral adrenalectomy. [S1]

## Osilodrostat

- **Osilodrostat inhibits 11β-hydroxylase and aldosterone synthase.** It is approved (FDA, EMA) for adults not cured by surgery or for whom surgery is unsuitable. [S1]
- **Efficacy in Cushing's disease:** in the LINC3 randomised-withdrawal phase, 31 (86%) of 36 kept normal UFC at week 34 on osilodrostat versus 10 (29%) of 35 on placebo; in the upfront placebo-controlled LINC4 trial in milder disease, UFC normalised in 77% versus 8%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Dosing (non-severe):** oral, 2 to 60 mg/day in total; usually start 2 mg twice daily and titrate by 1 to 2 mg every 2 to 3 weeks on cortisol and clinical response. Very mild disease: 1 mg/day in the evening. [S1]
- **Allow for a second, delayed effect about 3 weeks in:** wait 3 weeks before raising the osilodrostat dose in moderate disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severe hypercortisolism (authors' suggestion):** start osilodrostat at 10 to 20 mg/day and raise by 10 mg/day every 72 hours, guided by morning cortisol (for adrenal insufficiency) and UFC (for efficacy); doses up to 100 mg/day have been used. [S1]
- **Adverse effects:** fatigue, nausea, headache, diarrhoea and adrenal insufficiency (hypoadrenalism-related events in 51.1% in LINC3 and 25% in LINC-4). Hirsutism in women; precursor excess can cause hypokalaemia, oedema and hypertension; QTc prolongation in 4% and raised ALT or AST in 4%. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Osilodrostat is contraindicated in pregnancy, children and adolescents** (lack of data). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Adrenal insufficiency can persist long after osilodrostat is stopped,** so keep checking symptoms and morning cortisol after discontinuation. [S1]
- **On metyrapone or osilodrostat, measure cortisol by tandem mass spectrometry or a highly specific immunoassay:** 11-deoxycortisol cross-reacts in some immunoassays and overestimates cortisol. [S1]

## Other agents

- **Metyrapone for severe disease:** start 1500 mg/day, increase by 750 mg every 72 hours to a maximum of 3750 mg/day, given 3 or 4 times a day. Adding ketoconazole (800 mg/day, rising quickly to 1200 mg/day) works when metyrapone alone is too slow. [S1]
- **Moderate disease (UFC usually under 3- to 5-fold ULN, comorbidities controlled):** lowest starting doses are osilodrostat 2 mg (or 1 mg) twice daily, metyrapone 250 mg 3 times daily, ketoconazole 200 mg twice daily, or cabergoline 0.5 mg 3 times weekly. [S1]
- **Sex can guide the choice outside severe disease:** ketoconazole lowers androgens and may suit women; metyrapone or osilodrostat raise them and may suit men. [S1]

## Titration or block-and-replace

- **Two strategies:** titrate the dose to normal cortisol, or block fully and replace with glucocorticoid. Block-and-replace suits severe or cyclical disease, or when monitoring is difficult. [S1]
- **Choose by severity:** mean UFC below 5×ULN, titrate slowly with close monitoring; UFC 5 to 10×ULN without life-threatening features, start higher doses and check cortisol or UFC every 5 to 7 days; UFC above 10×ULN with serious complications, very high doses of fast-acting inhibitors in block-and-replace, uptitrating every 2 to 3 days, often in hospital. [S1]
- **Replacement in block-and-replace:** hydrocortisone 15 to 20 mg/day in 2 to 3 doses, or prednisolone about 3 to 5 mg daily. [S1]

## Monitor

- **Combine markers:** UFC for overall secretion, late-night salivary cortisol for the nadir (both detect under-dosing), and morning serum cortisol to detect over-dosing. Morning cortisol below 5 µg/dL indicates adrenal insufficiency; 8 to 10 µg/dL may indicate good control. In moderate disease check every 2 to 3 weeks. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Teach every patient about adrenal insufficiency when starting:** its signs (nausea, weight loss, marked fatigue, postural hypotension), how to start glucocorticoid at home, an emergency injection pack, whom to call, and written sick-day rules. With moderate-to-severe symptoms, the patient starts glucocorticoid before contacting the team. [S1]

## Thrombosis and pregnancy

- ⚠️ **Consider thromboprophylaxis at diagnosis in every patient and continue for 3 months after biochemical remission,** unless contraindicated; standard weight-based prophylactic low-molecular-weight heparin is preferred (2025 international Delphi consensus). [S1]
- **Pregnancy:** women with active Cushing's need contraception, preferably non-oestrogen. If a drug is needed in pregnancy, metyrapone and cabergoline are the best options; none is approved for pregnancy. [S1]
