# Advanced urothelial carcinoma: first-line enfortumab vedotin with pembrolizumab

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** first-line treatment of previously untreated locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma with enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab: eligibility, dosing and dose levels, the skin, glucose, lung, nerve and eye toxicities that need active monitoring, and the trial evidence. Non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (intravesical BCG), perioperative and adjuvant treatment, avelumab maintenance and later-line enfortumab vedotin alone are separate eviQ protocols; **S2 (SEOM–SOGUG 2025)** adds where they, platinum chemotherapy, nivolumab with cisplatin-gemcitabine and erdafitinib fit in the sequence, without doses. Non-muscle-invasive, perioperative and adjuvant treatment are not covered. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 33 claims; **43 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 4 doses.** **S1** eviQ Cancer Treatments Online (first approved 5 November 2025; review due 31 December 2026; AU): 23 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored · **S2** Spanish Society of Medical Oncology (SEOM) and Spanish Genitourinary Oncology Group (SOGUG) (published 17 September 2025; international): 10 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ **S1 is a single eviQ treatment protocol (enfortumab vedotin + pembrolizumab); S2 (SEOM–SOGUG 2025) adds the rest of the advanced-disease sequence.** Doses for the other regimens are not given; it is still not a full bladder cancer pathway.

> ⚠️ International guideline (SEOM–SOGUG, Spain, 2025) for S2. Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ; S2 flags several drugs as EMA-approved but not reimbursed in Spain.

## Who is eligible

- **For locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma that has not been treated before**, in patients with ECOG performance status 0 to 2. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not use with** existing neuropathy of grade 2 or worse, uncontrolled diabetes, or active keratitis or corneal ulcers. [S1]
- **Discuss with an expert first if the patient has** significant autoimmune disease, an organ transplant, past viral hepatitis or previous lung irradiation. [S1]
- **Start only under specialists experienced with immunotherapy** who are confident they can manage immune-related adverse events. [S1]
- **The pivotal trial enrolled only patients who could have had platinum chemotherapy and immunotherapy.** [S1]

## Treatment and dosing

- ⚠️ **Regimen:** enfortumab vedotin 1.25 mg/kg IV on days 1 and 8 (capped at 125 mg, the maximum starting dose for patients of 100 kg or more) with pembrolizumab 200 mg IV on day 1, every 21 days. Continue until progression or unacceptable toxicity; pembrolizumab stops after at most 35 cycles, while enfortumab vedotin has no cycle limit. [S1]
- **Pembrolizumab may instead be given as 400 mg every 42 days.** [S1]
- **Infusion order:** in cycle 1, leave 30 minutes between finishing enfortumab vedotin and starting pembrolizumab; if cycle 1 goes well, the gap can drop to 15 minutes. [S1]
- **Enfortumab vedotin dose levels:** start 1.25 mg/kg (to 125 mg); first reduction 1.0 mg/kg (to 100 mg); second 0.75 mg/kg (to 75 mg); third 0.5 mg/kg (to 50 mg). [S1]
- **Pembrolizumab dose reduction is not recommended;** toxicity is managed by holding or stopping it. Moderate to severe hepatic impairment has not been studied. [S1]

## Red flags and monitoring

- ⚠️ **Enfortumab vedotin has caused severe and fatal skin toxicity and hyperglycaemia.** [S1]
- **Skin reactions are more frequent with the combination than with enfortumab vedotin alone.** Watch closely during the first 2 cycles and throughout, and refer to dermatology early. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Suspected SJS/TEN, blistering, recurrent grade 3 or any grade 4 skin reaction: hold treatment at once and refer to a specialist; stop permanently if confirmed.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Hyperglycaemia and diabetic ketoacidosis, sometimes fatal, occur even without known diabetes**; risk is higher with prior hyperglycaemia or BMI of 30 or more. Refer anyone with baseline HbA1c above 6.5% for glucose optimisation before cycle 1. [S1]
- **Hold treatment until blood glucose is below 13.9 mmol/L.** Ketoacidosis needs urgent inpatient care. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pneumonitis/ILD, including deaths, is more common with the combination** (median onset 4 months). Grade 2: hold until grade 1 or less, reduce if it persists or recurs. Grade 3 or 4: stop. [S1]
- **Check for neuropathy before every dose.** Grade 2: pause until grade 1 or less, resume at the same dose the first time and one level lower the second. Grade 3 or 4: stop. [S1]
- **Eyes:** dry eye is the usual problem. Consider prophylactic artificial tears, refer to ophthalmology if symptoms persist or worsen, and note that contact lenses raise keratopathy risk. [S1]
- **Bloods:** FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs, cortisol, thyroid function, glucose and HbA1c at baseline; FBC, EUC, eGFR, LFTs and glucose before every cycle; thyroid function every 4–6 weeks on treatment and 12-weekly afterwards. [S1]
- **Screen for hepatitis before starting a checkpoint inhibitor**; chronic hepatitis B or C or uncontrolled HIV needs close monitoring and antivirals where indicated. [S1]

## Evidence

- **Phase III evidence:** 886 patients with untreated advanced urothelial cancer were randomised to enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab or to gemcitabine with cisplatin or carboplatin. [S1]
- **Survival roughly doubled:** median overall survival 31.5 vs 16.1 months (HR 0.47) and median PFS 12.5 vs 6.3 months (HR 0.45), with benefit in every subgroup. [S1]
- **More treatment changes for toxicity with the combination:** dose reductions 40.7% vs 35.0% and discontinuations 37.9% vs 18.5%; treatment-related deaths were under 1.0% in both arms. [S1]
- **SEOM–SOGUG calls enfortumab vedotin with pembrolizumab the preferred first-line standard.** Its benefit held whatever the cisplatin eligibility, PD-L1 status, metastatic site or tumour location; 67.7% responded, 29.1% completely. It beat a control arm in which 30% had avelumab maintenance. [S2]
- **Longer follow-up (median 29.1 months):** at 24 months 37.1% against 12.6% were progression-free and 60.1% against 35.4% alive, and responses lasted a median of nearly 2 years. [S2]

## Other first-line options (SEOM–SOGUG)

- **Platinum chemotherapy:** cisplatin regimens (MVAC, dose-dense MVAC, cisplatin-gemcitabine) give median survival of 12–15 months. About 50% of patients cannot have cisplatin; carboplatin with gemcitabine is safer but less effective (median survival 9–10 months). [S2]
- **Avelumab maintenance after platinum:** for patients free of progression after 4–6 cycles, switching to avelumab lengthened median survival to 21.4 from 14.3 months (HR 0.69), with benefit across subgroups whether cisplatin or carboplatin had been used. [S2]
- **Nivolumab with cisplatin-gemcitabine (CheckMate 901):** for cisplatin-eligible patients, up to six cycles with nivolumab then nivolumab maintenance for at most 2 years raised median survival to 21.7 from 18.9 months (HR 0.78); 21.7% had a complete response. It is the only checkpoint inhibitor shown to add survival to first-line cisplatin chemotherapy. [S2]
- **A single checkpoint inhibitor** is an option only for frail, platinum-ineligible patients with PD-L1-positive, preferably low-volume disease; single-arm trials gave response rates of 23.5% (atezolizumab) and 28.6% (pembrolizumab). [S2]

## After first-line treatment (SEOM–SOGUG)

- ⚠️ **After enfortumab vedotin with pembrolizumab there is no evidence for the next step.** The preferred choice is platinum chemotherapy, with no avelumab maintenance to follow; erdafitinib is reasonable when FGFR2/3 is altered. [S2]
- **After platinum chemotherapy alone:** pembrolizumab improved survival over second-line chemotherapy (median 10.1 against 7.2 months, HR 0.71). [S2]
- **After both platinum and a checkpoint inhibitor:** enfortumab vedotin alone (median survival 12.88 against 8.97 months, HR 0.70) or, for FGFR3/2 mutations or fusions, erdafitinib (12.1 against 7.8 months, HR 0.64). The best order of the two is unknown; taxanes or vinflunine later are for selected patients only. [S2]
- **Upper tract urothelial carcinoma:** treat advanced disease as for bladder cancer; it carries FGFR mutations more often than bladder tumours do. [S2]
