# High-risk neuroblastoma — eflornithine maintenance

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** what high-risk neuroblastoma is, and the place, evidence, dosing, safety and monitoring of oral eflornithine (DFMO) maintenance after response to multimodality therapy. Induction, consolidation and immunotherapy regimens are not covered. Related: `febrile-neutropenia-children`, `anticancer-drugs-general-principles`. ## Recognise

- **Neuroblastoma is a cancer of the autonomic nervous system** that causes about 15% of childhood cancer deaths; it is rarer in adolescents and adults. [S1]
- **'High-risk' is assigned from imaging stage, age, chromosomal changes and pathology.** Treatment combines intensive chemotherapy, surgery, radiotherapy, autologous stem-cell transplant and immunotherapy. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Relapse is common: more than 50% relapse** even after intensive multimodality treatment. [S1]

## Eflornithine: who and why

- **Eflornithine is an oral maintenance drug to lower relapse risk** for patients whose disease has at least partly responded to earlier multiagent, multimodality treatment. [S1]
- **It works by irreversibly blocking ornithine decarboxylase,** the key enzyme in polyamine synthesis. [S1]
- **Evidence: a matched comparison of 90 treated patients with 270 historical controls,** all under 21 with at least a partial response. Event-free survival HR 0.48 and overall survival HR 0.32 favoured eflornithine; four-year survival was 96% versus 84%. [S1]
- **The trial was externally controlled, not randomised,** so the result is uncertain; sensitivity analyses gave similar answers. [S1]
- **Place in therapy:** an added option that may improve survival after at least partial response to induction and consolidation (including immunotherapy), with toxicity that needs ongoing monitoring. [S1]

## Dose

- **Oral, twice a day, dosed by body surface area:** from 192 mg (1 tablet) up to 768 mg (4 tablets) per dose. Continue for 2 years, or until recurrence or unacceptable toxicity. Lower doses can be used for adverse effects. [S1]
- **Reduce the dose in severe renal impairment** (eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m²). [S1]
- **Tablets can be chewed, or crushed into a little liquid,** for children who cannot swallow them. [S1]

## Safety and monitoring

- ⚠️ **Main serious harms: liver toxicity, neutropenia, anaemia and hearing loss.** Ear, chest and skin infections, conjunctivitis, cough, diarrhoea and vomiting are also common. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Check full blood count, liver function and hearing before starting and periodically during treatment.** [S1]
- **Pregnancy category B3.** Patients (or partners) who could conceive need effective contraception while on treatment and until 1 week after the final dose; there are no lactation data. [S1]
