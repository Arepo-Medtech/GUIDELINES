# High-risk neuroblastoma — eflornithine maintenance

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** what high-risk neuroblastoma is, and the place, evidence, dosing, safety and monitoring of oral eflornithine (DFMO) maintenance after response to multimodality therapy; plus, from S2, the European (SIOPEN) dinutuximab beta immunotherapy schedule and the management of its adverse effects. Induction and consolidation regimens are not covered. Related: `febrile-neutropenia-children`, `anticancer-drugs-general-principles`. PBS access is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise

- **Neuroblastoma is a cancer of the autonomic nervous system** that causes about 15% of childhood cancer deaths; it is rarer in adolescents and adults. [S1]
- **'High-risk' is assigned from imaging stage, age, chromosomal changes and pathology.** Treatment combines intensive chemotherapy, surgery, radiotherapy, autologous stem-cell transplant and immunotherapy. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Relapse is common: more than 50% relapse** even after intensive multimodality treatment. [S1]
- **Risk group (S2):** set by age at diagnosis (cut-off 18 months), the tumour's histology and its molecular features: MYCN amplification, segmental chromosome changes and ploidy. [S2]
- **About 50% of neuroblastomas are high-risk, with survival below 50%** despite multimodal treatment (induction chemotherapy and surgery, then high-dose chemotherapy and an autologous stem-cell transplant, radiotherapy and differentiation therapy). Adding an anti-GD2 antibody (dinutuximab, COG ANBL0032 trial) lifted survival above 60% (S2). [S2]

## Anti-GD2 immunotherapy before maintenance (S2)

- ⚠️ **SIOPEN standard of care: dinutuximab beta by long-term continuous infusion (100 mg/m² over 10 days), without IL-2,** for patients who responded to induction and consolidation. The slower infusion caused less pain, allergy and hypotension than the 8-hour, 5-day schedule; adding IL-2 raised toxicity and did not improve survival. [S2]
- **Most dinutuximab beta adverse effects occur during the infusion and settle when it ends.** Pausing the infusion to give supportive care does not appear harmful; treat with supportive measures first, before reducing or interrupting doses, and prepare parents for what to expect. [S2]
- ⚠️ **No corticosteroids from 2 weeks before the first dose to 1 week after the last,** except in life-threatening situations (the experts also allow them for adrenal insufficiency and asthma). For anaphylaxis or transverse myelitis, stop the infusion at once and discontinue permanently. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Pain: give triple therapy** (a non-opioid such as paracetamol or ibuprofen throughout, gabapentin, and morphine). Gabapentin is built up before the infusion: 10 mg/kg/day starting 3 days ahead; on the following day, 10 mg/kg twice (2 × 10); from the eve of the infusion, 10 mg/kg three times (3 × 10), kept until the cycle ends. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Morphine:** start a 0.02–0.05 mg/kg/h IV infusion before the antibody, or a 0.05–0.1 mg/kg bolus 2 h before it; then about 0.03 mg/kg/h during the infusion. Wean over no more than 5 days, finishing by the end of the first cycle's infusion and within 3–5 days of starting the second. Aim for the lowest dose for the shortest time; IV ketamine can be added for uncontrolled pain. [S2]
- **Fever is expected:** paracetamol can be given to prevent it. Take blood cultures when fever occurs, but empirical antibiotics are not advised for fever alone, even with a mildly raised CRP, unless the child is neutropenic or infection is clinically suspected. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Allergy:** premedicate with an IV antihistamine about 20 min before each infusion, repeated every 4–6 h if needed (or regular oral antihistamine from the day before). Grade 1–2 reactions: pause, treat and restart. Grade 3–4 anaphylaxis: stop permanently and give IV antihistamine, adrenaline and steroids. [S2]
- **Capillary leak:** watch blood pressure and weight; mild leak may need no treatment, otherwise manage fluids by weight, blood pressure and local protocol. Use diuretics cautiously because of hypotension; furosemide suits marked weight gain with pulmonary oedema or ascites when blood pressure is normal. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Transverse myelitis: stop the infusion immediately and never restart dinutuximab beta;** give IV immunoglobulin and high-dose steroids. Children with severe neuropathy should not be rechallenged even after full recovery. [S2]
- **Raised liver enzymes without symptoms need no action:** below 20 times the upper limit of normal, monitor closely and keep the dose; avoid hepatotoxic drugs. [S2]

## Eflornithine: who and why

- **Eflornithine is an oral maintenance drug to lower relapse risk** for patients whose disease has at least partly responded to earlier multiagent, multimodality treatment. [S1]
- **It works by irreversibly blocking ornithine decarboxylase,** the key enzyme in polyamine synthesis. [S1]
- **Evidence: a matched comparison of 90 treated patients with 270 historical controls,** all under 21 with at least a partial response. Event-free survival HR 0.48 and overall survival HR 0.32 favoured eflornithine; four-year survival was 96% versus 84%. [S1]
- **The trial was externally controlled, not randomised,** so the result is uncertain; sensitivity analyses gave similar answers. [S1]
- **Place in therapy:** an added option that may improve survival after at least partial response to induction and consolidation (including immunotherapy), with toxicity that needs ongoing monitoring. [S1]

## Dose

- **Oral, twice a day, dosed by body surface area:** from 192 mg (1 tablet) up to 768 mg (4 tablets) per dose. Continue for 2 years, or until recurrence or unacceptable toxicity. Lower doses can be used for adverse effects. [S1]
- **Reduce the dose in severe renal impairment** (eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m²). [S1]
- **Tablets can be chewed, or crushed into a little liquid,** for children who cannot swallow them. [S1]

## Safety and monitoring

- ⚠️ **Main serious harms: liver toxicity, neutropenia, anaemia and hearing loss.** Ear, chest and skin infections, conjunctivitis, cough, diarrhoea and vomiting are also common. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Check full blood count, liver function and hearing before starting and periodically during treatment.** [S1]
- **Pregnancy category B3.** Patients (or partners) who could conceive need effective contraception while on treatment and until 1 week after the final dose; there are no lactation data. [S1]
