# Drug choice for selected infections

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-22 · **Status:** drafted and source-verified; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** empirical drug choice, route and duration for the infections AMH tabulates —
respiratory · skin and soft tissue · urinary · gastrointestinal · genital · community-acquired
*S. aureus* · bacterial meningitis · endocarditis prophylaxis · melioidosis.

> ### ⚠️ AMH'S OWN FIRST SENTENCE, CARRIED UNCHANGED

> microbiologists or infectious diseases specialists"** [D0].
>
> They are a starting point for empirical therapy, not an authority that displaces the person who knows
> the local antibiogram.

## How to read AMH's tables

- A drug of choice is listed for each indication when appropriate, and **the route is given unless it is
  oral**; alternatives cover special situations such as drug resistance or penicillin hypersensitivity [D0].
- **In general, ampicillin is interchangeable with amoxicillin, flucloxacillin with dicloxacillin, and
  clindamycin with lincomycin** [D0].
- ⚠️ **Seventeen infections are deliberately absent** because AMH discusses them elsewhere: acne, anogenital
  warts, bacterial vaginosis, cutaneous candidiasis, eye infections, gingivitis, *H. pylori*-induced ulcers,
  nail infections, oral candidiasis, **otitis media**, prostatitis, **rhinosinusitis**, **sore throat**,
  tinea, trichomoniasis, vulvovaginal candidiasis and worm infections [D0]. **Three of the commonest
  reasons an Australian GP writes an antibiotic script are therefore not on this page.**

## Respiratory infections [D1]

| Condition | First choice | Alternative | Duration and qualifiers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bronchiolitis**, mild (usually RSV) | — | — | ⚠️ **Self-limiting; antibacterials, bronchodilators and corticosteroids NOT recommended.** Risk factors for severe illness: prematurity, cardiopulmonary disease, immunodeficiency |
| **Acute bronchitis** | — | — | ⚠️ **Usually viral with secondary bacterial infection — antibacterials generally NOT indicated** |
| **Acute exacerbation of COPD** | amoxicillin **or** doxycycline | — | ⚠️ **Only if there is fever AND increased sputum volume AND purulence.** **5 days** |
| **Croup**, moderate-to-severe | dexamethasone **or** prednisolone | inhaled budesonide | **Antibacterials not indicated.** Humidification widely used, little evidence for efficacy. Severe illness may need inhaled adrenaline and oxygen |
| **Influenza** — treatment | oseltamivir, zanamivir **or** baloxavir | oseltamivir or peramivir if severe | ⚠️ **Treat symptomatically. Antivirals NOT recommended unless influenza is circulating in the community** — then consider **only** for those at risk of complications |
| **Influenza** — prophylaxis | oseltamivir, zanamivir or baloxavir | — | ⚠️ **The best method is vaccination with the current strain.** Antivirals for institutional prophylaxis, pandemics |
| **Pertussis** | azithromycin **or** clarithromycin | trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole | **Treatment and prophylaxis are identical**; length depends on the agent. **Treat early** to improve symptoms and shorten infectiousness; **give prophylaxis to close contacts**, especially around young children. ⚠️ **Best prevention is vaccination** |
| **Community-acquired or aspiration pneumonia, adults — LOW severity** | **amoxicillin** | doxycycline **or** clarithromycin | **5 days depending on progress** |

**Low-severity pneumonia — the qualifiers matter more than the drug** [D1]:
- "Low severity" means **mild infection that can be safely managed at home**, ⚠️ **checking for worsening
  after 24 to 48 hours**.
- Pathogens: viral, *S. pneumoniae*, *M. pneumoniae*, *Chlamydophila pneumoniae*, *Legionella* spp.,
  *H. influenzae*, *S. aureus*.
- Use **doxycycline or clarithromycin** in penicillin hypersensitivity, or if *M. pneumoniae*,
  *Chlamydophila pneumoniae* or *Legionella* is suspected.
- **If combination treatment is required, ADD** doxycycline or clarithromycin **TO** amoxicillin.
- In **non-severe** penicillin hypersensitivity, **replace amoxicillin with cefuroxime**.
- ⚠️ **Be aware of local susceptibility patterns.**

## Urinary infections [D3]

| Condition | First choice | Alternative | Duration and qualifiers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute cystitis** | **nitrofurantoin** | trimethoprim **or** cefalexin | **Females 3–5 days; 7 days if pregnant. Males 7 days. Children 3–5 days.** For cases resistant to empirical therapy, **choose the antibiotic with the narrowest spectrum to which the pathogen is sensitive** |
| **Acute pyelonephritis**, mild | amoxicillin + clavulanic acid | **ciprofloxacin if penicillin allergy** | **Usually 7–10 days.** Change to amoxicillin, trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole or cefalexin when susceptibility is known |
| **Acute pyelonephritis**, severe (eg sepsis) | **IV gentamicin** | IV ceftriaxone **or** ciprofloxacin | Start IV, change to oral when possible. ⚠️ **Ceftriaxone gives NO enterococci or *P. aeruginosa* cover.** **Use ceftriaxone if gentamicin is contraindicated or if IV treatment will continue beyond 72 hours.** Usually 7–10 days |
| **Recurrent UTI** — treatment | as for acute cystitis or pyelonephritis | — | ⚠️ **Asymptomatic bacteriuria in elderly women does not usually need treatment.** ⚠️ **In males, chronic prostatitis is usually present** |
| **Recurrent UTI** — prophylaxis | trimethoprim **or** cefalexin | nitrofurantoin | **At night for 3–6 months or longer.** **Prophylaxis may not be useful for children.** In women, **postcoital single-dose** trimethoprim, cefalexin or nitrofurantoin may be an alternative to long-term prophylaxis |

Pathogens for cystitis: *E. coli*, *Staphylococcus saprophyticus*, *Proteus*, *Klebsiella*,
*Enterococcus* spp. [D3]

## Gastrointestinal infections [D4]

### *Clostridioides difficile*-associated disease

- **Initial non-severe episode: oral vancomycin is first choice**, with **oral metronidazole only if oral
  vancomycin is not available**. **Treat 10 days**, longer for recurrences [D4]. AMH cites Australasian
  guidelines in *Intern Med J* 2025; 55(3): 503–13 [D4]. ⚠️ **That citation was checked against the
  public PubMed record on 2026-09-22 and is correct (PMID 40035163); its abstract independently confirms
  "the replacement of oral metronidazole with vancomycin for initial CDI and the emerging role for
  fidaxomicin and faecal-microbiota transplant". ⚠️ THE FULL TEXT IS CLOSED ACCESS AND WAS NOT RETRIEVED,
  so the vancomycin dose, the 10-day course and the recurrence algorithm below rest on AMH alone** —
  see [`sti-syndromes-and-screening`](sti-syndromes-and-screening.md) for the access check.
- ⚠️ **Avoid proton pump inhibitors, which may worsen outcome, and antidiarrhoeals such as loperamide,
  because toxin may be retained and worsen colitis** [D4].
- ⚠️ **Risk factors are increasing age, severe disease, long antibacterial course and long hospital stay —
  but community-acquired infection also occurs and patients may be young with no exposure to antibacterials
  or hospitals** [D4]. Illness ranges from mild diarrhoea to colitis and **toxic megacolon**, which may be
  life-threatening [D4].
- **First recurrence, non-severe:** oral vancomycin, **fidaxomicin** or **faecal microbiota
  transplantation**, depending on what was used initially and whether a trigger is present. **Severe or
  refractory: seek specialist advice** [D4].

*Campylobacter jejuni* **especially in Asia**, norovirus, and parasites such as *Giardia duodenalis* [D4].

## Community-acquired *Staphylococcus aureus* infection [D6]

| Situation | Choice |
|---|---|
| Mild-to-moderate, **assume methicillin-susceptible** | **flucloxacillin**; alternative cefalexin **or** clindamycin |
| **Patient or family history of community-acquired MRSA** | **clindamycin or trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole**, otherwise **seek specialist advice** |
| **Severe**, community-acquired MRSA suspected | **IV vancomycin**, otherwise **seek specialist advice** |

⚠️ **Consider community-acquired MRSA**, which may be associated with **abscesses, bacteraemia and
necrotising pneumonia**. ⚠️ **Be aware of local susceptibility patterns — health care-acquired and
community-acquired MRSA patterns differ** — and modify treatment according to susceptibility [D6].

## Bacterial meningitis [D7]

Pathogens: *N. meningitidis*, *S. pneumoniae*, *L. monocytogenes*, *H. influenzae* [D7].

| Stage | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Before hospitalisation** | **IV or IM benzylpenicillin, or IV/IM ceftriaxone** |
| **On admission to hospital** | **IV cefotaxime or IV ceftriaxone**; alternative **IV moxifloxacin** |

- ⚠️ **Start dexamethasone with or before the first antibacterial dose if possible** — at both stages [D7].
- **Add vancomycin** if there are **Gram-positive cocci in the CSF**, or known penicillin-resistant
  pneumococcus [D7].
- **Add IV benzylpenicillin** — or IV trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole — **if at high risk of *Listeria***
  (eg over 50 years, immunocompromised, or *Listeria* suspected) [D7].
- **Continue dexamethasone if the infection is due to *S. pneumoniae* in adults or *H. influenzae* in
  children** [D7].

**Prophylaxis** [D7]:

| Organism | First choice | Alternative | Qualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| *H. influenzae* | rifampicin | IV or IM ceftriaxone | **Use ceftriaxone in pregnancy** |
| *N. meningitidis* | ciprofloxacin **or** rifampicin | IM ceftriaxone | **Use ceftriaxone in pregnancy; use ciprofloxacin if the contact is taking oral contraceptives** |

## Endocarditis prophylaxis [D8]

**High-risk patients only**, **single dose before the procedure** [D8].

| Procedure | First choice | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Certain **dental** procedures or **upper respiratory tract** interventions (viridans streptococcus group) | oral, IV or IM **amoxicillin**, or IV/IM **ampicillin** | oral or IV **clindamycin** |
| Certain **genitourinary or GI** procedures (*Enterococcus* spp.) | IV or IM **amoxicillin** or **ampicillin** | IV **vancomycin** or IV **teicoplanin** |

- If oral administration is not possible, give IV or IM [D8].
- ⚠️ **Use clindamycin in penicillin hypersensitivity OR if a beta-lactam has been used more than once
  within the previous month** [D8] — the second condition is easily missed.

## Melioidosis (*Burkholderia pseudomallei*) [D9]

| Phase | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Acute** | **IV ceftazidime or meropenem**, otherwise seek specialist advice. **At least 14 days, which may need to extend up to 8 weeks** |
| **Maintenance** | **trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole for at least 3 months** |

- ⚠️ **Use meropenem for neurological disease.**
- **Add trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole for complicated disease, including neurological.**
- ⚠️ **Compliance is very important** [D9].

### Additional therapies in eTG

- COPD exacerbation not improving after 48-72 h in severe COPD: amoxicillin-clavulanate 875+125 mg 12-hourly for 5 days; if Pseudomonas identified, ciprofloxacin 750 mg 12-hourly for 5 days. [T1]
- Moderate-severity CAP (oral): amoxicillin 1 g 8-hourly plus doxycycline 100 mg 12-hourly, azithromycin 500 mg daily for 3 days, or clarithromycin 500 mg 12-hourly; severe penicillin allergy moxifloxacin 400 mg daily; usually 5 days. [T2]
- Recurrent cellulitis (2 or more episodes): phenoxymethylpenicillin 250 mg orally 12-hourly, reviewed 6-monthly; compression therapy for leg oedema; stand-by oral prescription. [T3]
- Cellulitis with systemic features: flucloxacillin 2 g IV 6-hourly (benzylpenicillin 1.2 g if streptococcal; cefazolin 2 g 8-hourly for nonsevere allergy; vancomycin for MRSA), total up to 14 days. [T3]
- Acute cystitis in nonpregnant women under 65 with mild symptoms: consider ibuprofen 400 mg 8-hourly for up to 3 days with or without delayed antibiotic script. [T10]
- C. difficile first recurrence: vancomycin 125 mg 6-hourly for 10 days, or fidaxomicin 200 mg 12-hourly for 10 days if the first episode resolved on vancomycin without a clear trigger; second or later recurrence: faecal microbiota transplantation preferred; severe complicated disease: vancomycin 250-500 mg 6-hourly plus IV metronidazole 500 mg 8-hourly. [T13]
- Acute bacterial prostatitis: ciprofloxacin 500 mg 12-hourly for 14 days first line; trimethoprim 300 mg daily or TMP-SMX 160+800 mg 12-hourly for 14 days second line. [T18]
- Genital herpes suppression from 36 weeks' gestation until delivery: aciclovir 400 mg 8-hourly or valaciclovir 500 mg 12-hourly. [T19]
- Severe PID or pregnancy: ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily plus azithromycin 500 mg IV daily plus metronidazole 500 mg IV 12-hourly, stepping to oral to complete 14 days. [T20]
- Melioidosis durations by focus: skin or bacteraemia 2 weeks, pneumonia 2-4 weeks, deep collection 4 weeks, osteomyelitis 6 weeks, neurological 8 weeks of IV therapy; TMP-SMX can be introduced by graded escalation. [T24]
- Endocarditis prophylaxis for tonsillectomy/adenoidectomy or drainage of established respiratory/ENT infection in high-risk cardiac patients, using the dental regimens or treatment active against viridans streptococci. [T23]
- Shingles is an HIV indicator condition: offer HIV testing. [T9]
