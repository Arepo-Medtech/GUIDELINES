# Severe dry eye disease with keratitis: anti-inflammatory treatment and topical ciclosporin

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** specialist management of inflammation in moderate-to-severe dry eye disease in adults: grading severity, telling severe dry-eye keratitis from other keratitis, when to start topical ciclosporin, bridging with corticosteroids, monitoring, stopping and patient counselling. ## Recognise and grade

- **Dry eye disease is common (about 11–30% of Europeans)** and harms vision, quality of life and work, with a physical and psychological toll. [S1]
- **Tear hyperosmolarity is taken to be the starting event,** from excess evaporation or low tear production; it damages the surface directly and by setting off inflammation, which keeps the cycle going. [S1]
- **Grade severity on five things** (unanimous panel view): symptom burden, staining of cornea and conjunctiva, reduced tear secretion, a fast tear break-up time, and meibomian gland disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Is it dry-eye keratitis at all?** New vessels, anterior-chamber cells and flare, or stromal oedema point to another keratitis or keratopathy (interstitial, neurotrophic, bullous). Ciclosporin trials selected severe dry-eye keratitis on fluorescein staining, symptoms and Schirmer testing. [S1]
- **Look for systemic disease:** Sjögren's syndrome carries the strongest link, then other immune and rheumatic conditions (rheumatoid arthritis, lupus), small-fibre neuropathy, chronic pain states and chronic graft-versus-host disease. [S1]

## Step up treatment

- **Escalate step by step** — environmental measures, artificial tears, lid hygiene, then topical steroid and/or topical ciclosporin — whether or not there is systemic disease. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Ciclosporin, like topical steroid, is second-line:** for when tear substitutes, lid hygiene and environmental changes have failed. [S1]
- **Base the decision to start ciclosporin on** how much the cornea and conjunctiva stain, immune-related systemic disease, a quick response to steroid, and poor control on standard care. Other risk factors can justify it even without severe corneal staining. [S1]
- **Its licensed use is severe keratitis only;** earlier use is off-label, which the panel found the most contested point (79.3% agreement). [S1]

## Corticosteroids and bridging

- **Topical steroids break the inflammatory cycle quickly but only for short courses,** because of raised IOP, cataract and opportunistic infection. Fluorometholone, loteprednol and hydrocortisone are less likely to raise IOP or cause cataract. [S1]
- **Ciclosporin, unlike steroid, is safe to use long term.** [S1]
- **Bridge with a steroid:** start ciclosporin together with the steroid or up to a week after it, and taper the steroid on the signs and symptoms of inflammation. In trials the steroid ran 4–8 weeks before tapering; the fast steroid covers ciclosporin's slow onset and improves how well it is tolerated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Watch the pressure on steroid:** check IOP after 1–2 weeks. There is no set review interval on combined treatment; base it on the steroid's type and strength, how often it is used, and comorbidities. [S1]

## Monitor and continue

- **Review response at 1 month, then every 3 months.** Improvement may start in the first month but is usually seen at 6–8 weeks. [S1]
- **Even with an early response, keep going;** discourage stopping early. [S1]
- **Keep using artificial tears alongside ciclosporin;** a lubricant 15–20 min before and/or after the dose markedly reduces burning and stinging. [S1]
- **Continue indefinitely while it works and is tolerated;** tapering or stopping is an option after sustained improvement (61% had not relapsed at 24 months in SANSIKA follow-up). Stop for poor tolerance or no effect. [S1]
- **No need to stop ciclosporin for eye surgery:** a healthy ocular surface before cataract or glaucoma surgery improves results. [S1]

## Counsel for adherence

- **Warn about stinging at instillation,** the commonest side effect; it is usually mild to moderate and few patients stop because of it. Systemic absorption is negligible. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Tell patients the benefit is slow** (possibly several weeks), since people often stop once symptoms first ease or when nothing seems to happen. Storage: do not freeze, keep in the original pack away from light. [S1]
