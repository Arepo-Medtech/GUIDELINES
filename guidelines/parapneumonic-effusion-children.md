# Parapneumonic effusion in children

**Edition:** 1.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, investigation, antibiotics, supportive care and referral/transfer/discharge for parapneumonic effusion and empyema in children. The underlying pneumonia is covered in `community-acquired-pneumonia-children`; empiric antibiotic doses are in `empiric-antimicrobials-children`.

> ⚠️ **Severity is clinical, not radiological.** An unwell child with a complicated effusion needs respiratory and/or surgical referral for consideration of drainage.

## Recognise it

- **Suspect an effusion in any unwell child with pneumonia, or when fever persists despite 48 hours of appropriate antibiotics.** [S1]
- **Simple** effusion = sterile, non-loculated transudate. **Complicated** = loculated effusion or empyema, from bacterial invasion of the pleural space. [S1]
- **Commonest empyema organisms:** *S. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus* and Group A Streptococcus. [S1]
- **On examination:** localised absent breath sounds with a dull percussion note, alongside the adjacent pneumonia's bronchial breathing and crackles. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Severity depends on the child's clinical state, not the size or look of the effusion.** Severe features: marked tachycardia, altered mental state, severe respiratory distress, cyanosis or severe hypoxaemia. [S1]
- **Other causes to consider:** TB or mycoplasma, malignancy, foreign body, congestive cardiac failure, rheumatological serositis. [S1]

## Investigate

- **No laboratory test is diagnostic.** Consider FBE, UEC (think SIADH and atypical HUS), CRP (tracks treatment success) and blood culture. [S1]
- **Severely unwell:** LFTs (albumin) and coagulation studies — consider toxic shock syndrome. [S1]
- **If a drain goes in,** think about sending the pleural fluid for MC&S, Gram stain and pneumococcal PCR. [S1]
- **Imaging:** AP CXR; **chest ultrasound distinguishes simple from complicated.** CT is not routine — only for failure to respond or suspected malignancy or foreign body. [S1]

## Treat

- **Outpatient management is possible** for a small simple effusion with no respiratory distress in a child tolerating oral antibiotics. [S1]
- **Admit** when respiratory support is needed (hypoxia, work of breathing moderate to severe), hydration support, IV antibiotics, or to assess the effusion's severity. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Restrict maintenance NG or IV fluids to 2/3rds of calculated requirement** to avoid fluid overload, with regular review of fluid status. [S1]
- **Supportive care:** oxygen if required, analgesia, encourage mobilisation. [S1]
- **Antibiotics: a 3rd generation cephalosporin first line; consider MSSA and MRSA cover.** Definitive therapy follows investigation results and local specialist advice. [S1]

## Refer, transfer, discharge

- **Discuss with the local paediatric team** any child with an effusion who needs admission. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Discuss with respiratory and/or surgical team:** complicated effusion · septic shock (a drain may be needed for source control) · severe respiratory distress · fever or respiratory distress persisting beyond 48hrs of IV antimicrobials. [S1]
- **Consider transfer:** complicated effusion where surgery is anticipated · cardiac, chronic respiratory, immunodeficiency or immunosuppression comorbidity · high-level respiratory support · care beyond the local hospital's comfort level. [S1]
- **Discharge when** tolerating oral antibiotics, afebrile, off oxygen and tolerating adequate oral intake. [S1]
