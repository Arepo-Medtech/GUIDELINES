# Oral mucositis and stomatitis from cancer treatment

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with oral mucositis from chemotherapy, radiation therapy or blood and marrow transplant, and stomatitis from targeted therapies (notably mTOR inhibitors): recognition, assessment, prevention, treatment and patient education. ## Recognise

- **Oral mucositis and stomatitis are different conditions** despite the terms being used loosely: their clinical picture and underlying mechanisms differ. [S1]
- **Oral mucositis** means red or ulcerated lesions of the mouth lining caused by anticancer drugs, by radiotherapy (above all to the oesophagus or the head and neck), or by high-dose chemotherapy before a blood and marrow transplant (BMT). [S1]
- **Stomatitis** follows targeted therapies such as tyrosine kinase and mTOR inhibitors; its ulcers look like aphthous ulcers. The agreed name for the mTOR form is mTOR inhibitor-associated stomatitis (mIAS). [S1]
- **How common:** every patient given head and neck radiotherapy, and over 96% of those given total body irradiation, gets some mucositis; up to 75% after BMT depending on conditioning; about 20-40% on conventional chemotherapy; 10-40% on targeted agents. In a review of 44 mTOR-inhibitor studies, 73.4% developed stomatitis. [S1]
- **Timing:** radiotherapy mucositis starts about two weeks in, peaks at the end of treatment, and can last up to eight weeks after; after BMT it appears around day 5 and lasts 2 to 3 weeks; with chemotherapy it usually starts 2 to 3 weeks in. mIAS appears within 4-5 days and mostly settles within 7-10 days even if the drug continues. [S1]

## Assess

- **Mucositis lesions are irregular in shape and depth with a fibrinous pseudomembrane,** often alongside damage elsewhere in the gut. **mIAS ulcers are small, distinct and ovoid** with a grey-white centre and red halo, and mIAS does not involve the rest of the gut. [S1]
- **Use a scale that captures symptoms, signs and function.** Check pain and swallowing (and response to analgesia), redness or ulcers, dry mouth, thick saliva, candida or other infection, aspiration, voice change, taste, eating, hydration, mouth-care adherence, and dentures or piercings. [S1]
- **Grade stomatitis with the mIAS scale, not a chemotherapy/radiotherapy scale** such as CTCAE, which can mismeasure targeted-therapy stomatitis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Mucositis is dangerous as well as painful:** it is a significant risk factor for febrile neutropenia, can threaten the airway, and can lead to sepsis during post-BMT aplasia. [S1]

## Prevent and treat: everyone

- **Good basic mouth care underpins prevention and limits severity of both conditions:** teach oral hygiene, remove trauma sources such as poorly fitting dentures, and avoid hard or spicy food, hot drinks, smoking and alcohol. Involve a multidisciplinary team. [S1]
- **Saline or bicarbonate rinses:** MASCC/ISOO makes no recommendation either way; plain water rinses are fine if those are not tolerated, to keep patients doing mouth care. [S1]

## Prevent and treat: oral mucositis

- **Recommended by MASCC/ISOO:** 30 minutes of oral cryotherapy (ice chips) with bolus fluorouracil or high-dose melphalan (with or without TBI) before BMT; and patient-controlled morphine analgesia for pain. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Do not use cryotherapy with oxaliplatin:** cold can worsen its throat and larynx dysaesthesia. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Benzydamine mouthwash (the PBS drug):** MASCC/ISOO recommends it for head and neck radiotherapy up to 50 Gy without chemotherapy, and suggests it with concurrent chemoradiotherapy. In Australia it is routinely available only in an alcohol-based form, is seldom used, and the eviQ head and neck radiation oncology committee advises against it. [S1]
- **Suggested for head and neck chemoradiotherapy:** oral glutamine 10-30 g/day throughout treatment for prevention; morphine 0.2% mouthwash for mucositis pain; and honey, topical and oral, may be used for prevention (with or without chemotherapy). [S1]
- **Recommended against:** intravenous glutamine to prevent mucositis in BMT, and sucralfate for mucositis pain in head and neck radiotherapy or solid-tumour chemotherapy. [S1]
- **Lower the oral-cavity radiation dose where technique allows,** since that dose drives mucositis risk. [S1]

## Prevent and treat: targeted-therapy stomatitis

- **Prevention:** use saline rather than plain-water rinses (higher infection risk on targeted agents, expert opinion); a steroid mouthwash lowers the incidence and severity of mIAS. [S1]
- **Treatment (expert opinion):** rinse more often, up to hourly; sugar-free gum or saliva substitutes for dryness; anaesthetic rinses such as viscous lidocaine 2% or coating agents for mild to moderate pain; topical or systemic analgesia (transdermal fentanyl plus short-acting opioid for breakthrough has worked); dexamethasone mouth rinse for lesions; and oral prednisolone or intralesional triamcinolone for very symptomatic ulcers. [S1]

## Patient education

- ⚠️ **Tell patients to report straight away** any pain, trouble swallowing, thrush, mouth ulcers, bleeding or difficulty speaking, and to check their mouth every day. [S1]
- **Daily mouth care:** see a dentist before treatment if possible; keep the mouth moist; use a water-based lip balm (not paraffin or petroleum jelly); brush with a soft brush four times a day with mild fluoride toothpaste, replacing it monthly; rinse with alcohol-free mouthwash; keep flossing only if already a habit; clean dentures separately; avoid smoking, alcohol, acidic, hot, spicy, rough or fizzy foods and drinks. [S1]
