# Neurofibromatosis type 1 — tumour surveillance and management

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** diagnosis, lifelong clinical and imaging surveillance, and management of NF1-associated tumours (plexiform neurofibromas, ANNUBP/MPNST, gliomas, breast cancer, phaeochromocytoma, GIST) in children and adults. Related: `soft-tissue-sarcoma` (MPNST), `phaeochromocytoma`, `gastrointestinal-stromal-tumour`, `glioblastoma`. PBS access (selumetinib) is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Recognise and diagnose

- **NF1 is autosomal dominant, with a birth incidence of about one in 2000–2500.** About half of patients inherit it; the other half carry a de novo NF1 variant. [S1]
- **Diagnosis needs two of the revised criteria:** six or more café-au-lait macules, skinfold freckling, neurofibromas, optic pathway glioma, Lisch nodules or choroidal abnormalities, a characteristic bone dysplasia, a heterozygous pathogenic NF1 variant, or a parent with NF1. [S1]
- **Lifetime cancer risk is about 59.6%, against 30.8% in the general population,** and life expectancy is shortened by 8–15 years, mostly through malignancy. [S1]
- **Management decisions belong with a multidisciplinary team that includes an NF1 expert.** [S1]

## Surveillance schedule

- **Children up to ten years: clinical assessment every year. Over ten: at least every two years.** [S1]
- **Eyes:** until age eight, children without a known optic pathway glioma should be examined by an ophthalmologist experienced in NF1; after eight, formal visual screening every year until adulthood. [S1]
- **Adults: clinical assessment at least every three years,** covering history, signs of brain tumour or MPNST, skin neurofibromas and plexiform neurofibromas. [S1]
- **Whole-body MRI at least once at the transition to adulthood** to measure internal plexiform tumour burden (a predictor of MPNST). If it shows no internal plexiform neurofibromas, repeat WB-MRI is not needed. [S1]
- **MRI is the preferred imaging; avoid CT in children where possible** to limit radiation in a population already at raised cancer risk. [S1]

## Plexiform neurofibromas

- **Plexiform neurofibromas occur in about 40–60% of people with NF1.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Surgery should be considered for a symptomatic plexiform neurofibroma, with closer monitoring; if it is symptomatic and inoperable, a MEK inhibitor may be considered.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **New pain, a change in texture or a sudden increase in growth of a plexiform neurofibroma means evaluate for malignant change.** [S1]

## MPNST and atypical neurofibroma (ANNUBP)

- **MPNST lifetime risk is 8–16%, usually diagnosed at age 20–40.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Red flags for MPNST:** rapid growth, new persistent substantial pain, new weakness, sensory deficit, sphincter disturbance, difficulty swallowing or breathing, or any change in tumour consistency. [S1]
- **If malignancy is suspected, image with 18FDG PET-MRI (or PET-CT if unavailable) plus regional MRI.** [S1]
- **Suspected ANNUBP or MPNST: resect primarily when safe;** otherwise take an image-guided biopsy, decided by a sarcoma MDT, because a biopsy can miss the malignant part. [S1]
- **MPNST treatment is complete excision with wide margins;** chemotherapy and radiotherapy only in specific situations. [S1]

## Gliomas

- **Optic pathway gliomas affect up to 15% of children with NF1;** refer every one, symptomatic or not, to an expert unit. [S1]
- **Low-grade glioma:** complete resection when feasible for symptomatic or progressive tumours; watch and wait if asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic; for inoperable tumours needing treatment use chemotherapy and avoid radiotherapy. [S1]

## Other NF1-associated tumours

- **Breast cancer: annual screening from age 30, preferably by breast MRI;** from 50, follow national population screening. [S1]
- **Phaeochromocytoma/paraganglioma: test biochemically for symptoms, in pregnancy and before elective surgery under general anaesthetic;** routine screening is not recommended. [S1]
- **GIST: resect an incidental asymptomatic GIST of 2 cm or more;** smaller ones can be watched with MRI. NF1 GISTs usually lack KIT/PDGFRA changes and do not respond to tyrosine kinase inhibitors. [S1]

## Psychosocial care

- **Screen regularly for psychosocial wellbeing and neuropsychological function,** with a psychologist in the multidisciplinary team. [S1]
