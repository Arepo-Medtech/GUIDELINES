# Locally advanced cervical cancer

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** recognition, staging, chemoradiation with brachytherapy, pembrolizumab with chemoradiation, induction and adjuvant chemotherapy, and follow-up for adults with locally advanced cervical cancer (FIGO IB2–IVA). Early-stage surgery, fertility-sparing treatment and recurrent or metastatic disease are mentioned only in passing. Screening and the first steps after an HPV result: see `cervical-cancer-screening`. HPV vaccination: see `human-papillomavirus-vaccination`. PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

> ✅ **COMPILED FROM 2 SOURCES.** 25 claims; **51 fragments or anchors re-checkable by machine; 6 doses.** **S1** Spanish Society of Medical Oncology (SEOM) and Spanish Ovarian Cancer Research Group (GEICO) (published 31 August 2024; international): 16 claims, quoted · **S2** eviQ Cancer Treatments Online (first approved 24 April 2026; review due 30 June 2027; AU): 9 claims, paraphrased, hash-anchored

> ⚠️ International guideline (SEOM-GEICO, 2023). Australian practice follows the PBS listings below and may differ.

> ⚠️ **The guideline was written before pembrolizumab with chemoradiation was approved in Europe** and calls cisplatin chemoradiation the standard 'until immunotherapy is approved'. In Australia, pembrolizumab is PBS-listed for high-risk locally advanced cervical cancer (see the PBS table).

> 🇦🇺 **S2 is the Australian eviQ protocol for chemoradiation with pembrolizumab (ID 4590, 2026).** It is a regimen protocol, not a guideline: it adds the pembrolizumab dose and schedule, eligibility, PBS status, kidney dosing and monitoring. Staging, surgery, induction chemotherapy, recurrence and follow-up rest on S1.

## Recognise

- **HPV is found in around 99% of cervical cancers.** Early disease is often silent, so screening matters; abnormal cytology or a positive high-risk HPV test leads to colposcopy and biopsy. [S1]
- **Locally advanced disease can cause abnormal bleeding or discharge, pelvic pain and dyspareunia,** easily mistaken for vaginitis or cervicitis. Bowel or urinary symptoms or fistula suggest advanced disease. [S1]
- **A visible or palpable lesion is diagnosed by biopsy;** examine under anaesthesia if pelvic examination is inadequate or vaginal or parametrial spread is uncertain. [S1]

## Stage

- **Stage with FIGO 2018, which now includes imaging and pathology.** Use pelvic MRI for local extent and PET/CT for stage IB3 and above (chest and abdominal CT if PET is unavailable). Cystoscopy or proctoscopy only if invasion is suspected. [S1]
- **Para-aortic node dissection may be considered when imaging shows no para-aortic disease,** to find occult metastases that would change the radiotherapy field. [S1]
- **Five-year survival is about 60% for locally advanced disease,** under 50% with pelvic node metastases and under 20–30% with para-aortic node metastases. [S1]

## Treat

- ⚠️ **Locally advanced means FIGO IB2, II, III and IVA. Standard treatment is external beam radiotherapy with weekly cisplatin, then brachytherapy;** it adds about 10% to overall survival and halves relapse risk. Weekly carboplatin AUC2 is the alternative in renal impairment. [S1]
- **Weekly cisplatin dose: 40 mg/m², capped at 70 mg a week.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pembrolizumab with chemoradiation (KEYNOTE-A18)** for high-risk disease (FIGO 2014 IB2–IIB node-positive, or III–IVA) improved progression-free survival (HR 0.70) and 24-month survival (87% vs 81%). The guideline expects it to become standard. [S1]
- **Induction chemotherapy (INTERLACE: 6 weekly carboplatin AUC 2 plus paclitaxel 80 mg/m² before chemoradiation)** is an option for selected patients. [S1]
- **Do not give adjuvant chemotherapy after chemoradiation, and do not use neoadjuvant chemotherapy before radical surgery** as a standard. [S1]
- **After radical surgery with high-risk findings** (positive nodes, margins or parametrium), give pelvic radiotherapy with concurrent platinum. [S1]

## Pembrolizumab with chemoradiation: the Australian protocol

- 🇦🇺 **Who qualifies (eviQ):** high-risk locally advanced cancer of squamous, adenocarcinoma or adenosquamous type, with ECOG 0–1. 'High risk' follows KEYNOTE-A18 and FIGO 2014: stage IB2–IIB with nodes involved (at least 2 pelvic or 1 para-aortic), or stage III–IVA regardless of nodes. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Schedule:** cycles 1 and 2 last 21 days, with pembrolizumab 200 mg IV on day 1 and cisplatin 40 mg/m² IV on days 1, 8 and 15 alongside radiotherapy. Cycles 3 to 5 give pembrolizumab 200 mg alone every 3 weeks, then cycles 6 to 20 give pembrolizumab 400 mg every 42 days (15 maintenance cycles). A sixth weekly cisplatin is optional if radiotherapy runs longer than 5 weeks. [S2]
- **Give cisplatin on radiotherapy days, before the fraction; if radiotherapy is postponed, postpone cisplatin too, but keep pembrolizumab on schedule.** [S2]
- ⚠️ **PBS status:** cisplatin is a general schedule item and pembrolizumab needs a PBS authority. Emetic risk is high, so an NK1 antagonist, palonosetron and dexamethasone are given with each cisplatin dose. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Kidney function (ADDIKD):** at eGFR 60 or above, full-dose cisplatin; at 45–59, full dose, a 25% cut or a different protocol; below 45, avoid cisplatin. Prefer a measured GFR when eGFR is under 60. **If cisplatin is dropped, eviQ finds no robust prospective evidence for pembrolizumab with radiotherapy alone,** and the alternative may be radiotherapy only. [S2]
- **Blood tests:** at baseline, FBC, kidney function (EUC, eGFR), LFTs, glucose, calcium, magnesium, phosphate, cortisol and TFTs. The same panel, minus TFTs and cortisol, is repeated before each dose; TFTs (and, if considered, cortisol) every 4–6 weeks on treatment and every 12 weeks as indicated afterwards. Screen every patient for hepatitis before a checkpoint inhibitor. [S2]
- **Correct anaemia during chemoradiation:** a low haemoglobin is linked with worse outcomes, because poorly oxygenated tumour responds less to radiation. Check iron and treat deficiency. [S2]
- **Cisplatin dose changes:** ANC 0.5 to under 1.0 or platelets 50 to under 75: delay until recovery; ANC under 0.5, platelets under 50 or febrile neutropenia: delay and consider 25% less cisplatin afterwards. Grade 2 neuropathy at the next dose: 25% less, then 50% less if it persists; grade 3 or 4: omit cisplatin. Pembrolizumab is never dose-reduced, and a grade 3 or 4 infusion reaction means stopping it for good. [S2]
- **Updated KEYNOTE-A18 results (1060 patients, median follow-up 29.9 months):** 36-month progression-free survival 69.3% against 56.9% (HR 0.68) and 36-month overall survival 82.6% against 74.8% (HR 0.67). Grade 3 or worse adverse events: 78% against 70%; possibly immune-mediated events: 39% against 17%. The CALLA trial of durvalumab with chemoradiation improved neither PFS nor OS. [S2]

## Recurrence

- **Up to 70% of locally advanced cancers recur, mostly within two years.** Confirm recurrence histologically. Central pelvic recurrence without sidewall, extrapelvic nodal or peritoneal disease: pelvic exenteration in a specialised centre. [S1]
- **Metastatic or recurrent disease:** platinum–paclitaxel plus pembrolizumab for fit patients with PD-L1 CPS 1 or more, with bevacizumab once fistula risk is discussed; cemiplimab after platinum if no prior immunotherapy. [S1]

## Follow-up

- **Image high-risk stage II+ patients 3–6 months after treatment** (PET/CT preferred, plus pelvic MRI). Then review every 3–6 months for two years and every 6–12 months in years 3–5, with specialist vaginal and pelvic-rectal examination; return to annual checks after five recurrence-free years. [S1]
- **Routine cytology after chemoradiation has low predictive value.** Teach patients recurrence symptoms, and start vaginal dilators with lubricant and topical oestrogen early for sexual rehabilitation. [S1]
