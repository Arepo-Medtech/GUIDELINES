# Alopecia areata — moderate to severe disease in adults

**Edition:** 2.1 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with moderate to very severe alopecia areata: natural history, severity scoring, systemic corticosteroids, JAK inhibitors (baricitinib, ritlecitinib), conventional immunosuppressants, and topical and intralesional therapy for limited disease. Children and adolescents are outside the source's scope. Androgenetic alopecia is covered in `androgenetic-alopecia`. PBS access is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`. Australian expert criteria for starting and continuing subsidised JAK inhibitors (S2) are in their own section.

> ⚠️ **The PBS items for this condition are topical and intralesional corticosteroids only** (betamethasone, triamcinolone). The consensus centres on JAK inhibitors, which are TGA-approved for severe alopecia areata; S2 (August 2025) reported no PBS subsidy for them in AA at publication, and none is listed in the table below. Check the PBS before prescribing.

> 🇦🇺 **Both sources are Australian expert consensus (2025).** S1 covers treatment; S2 sets proposed criteria for who should get subsidised JAK inhibitors, and when to call remission or failure. S2's criteria are proposals for reimbursement, not PBS restriction text, and its authors ask that they be tested before use.

## Natural history

- **About two in three people have an acute episode that resolves by itself; the other third develop chronic persistent AA (CPAA),** defined as an episode lasting over 12 months and seen in roughly 33% of patients. Episode length is the strongest predictor of outcome. [S1]
- **Around 40% of episodes are one patch that fully and lastingly regrows within 6 months;** another 27% gain further patches yet still remit within 12 months. Under 5% progress over weeks to months to alopecia totalis or universalis. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Once CPAA is established, spontaneous remission is rare and close to 50% end up with alopecia totalis or universalis.** [S1]
- **The psychological burden is large:** 17% of newly diagnosed patients were given antidepressants and 9% anxiolytics, over twice the national rate. The patient's own view of severity and loss of brows or lashes predict poor quality of life better than the SALT score. [S1]

## Grade severity

- **Score scalp loss with SALT (0 = none, 100 = total).** By SALT: 1–20 is limited, 21–49 moderate, 50–94 severe and 95–100 very severe disease. Experts agree moderate to very severe disease warrants systemic therapy. [S1]

## Systemic corticosteroids

- **For acute moderate to very severe AA, prednisolone (or a similar corticosteroid) is first line;** a trial is also an option in chronic disease. Toxicity rules out use beyond 3 months, and relapse on tapering is common. [S1]
- **Expert-preferred regimen: prednisolone once each morning at 0.4–0.6 mg/kg/day, tapered off over 6–12 weeks.** [S1]
- **Screen for untreated infection first, and during treatment watch sleep, mood, glucose, blood pressure, peptic ulcer, eyes and infection;** consider bone protection. [S1]

## JAK inhibitors

- ⚠️ **Consider a JAK inhibitor from SALT 21 upward (moderate to severe),** and at lower scores when brows or lashes are involved. Earlier treatment appears to respond better. [S1]
- **Baricitinib (JAK1/2; TGA-approved for severe and very severe AA in adults):** severe disease starts at 2 mg orally once daily, rising to 4 mg if response is inadequate; very severe disease starts at 4 mg, stepping down to 2 mg once response is adequate. [S1]
- **Ritlecitinib (JAK3/TEC; TGA-approved June 2024 for severe AA from age 12):** 50 mg orally once daily. [S1]
- **Efficacy:** in the phase 3 BRAVE trials, 36.8%–40.9% on baricitinib 4 mg reached SALT 20 or less at 52 weeks, and 89.1% of them held it to 152 weeks. Ritlecitinib 50 mg gave 45.1% at year 1, and a review judged the two drugs comparable. [S1]
- **Response is poorer with alopecia totalis and with episodes over 4 years,** though even long episodes can respond, and duration does not slow the response. [S1]
- **Target SALT 20 or below (secondary goal 10 or below),** but patients with totalis or universalis may count regrowth of brows, lashes or beard as success even with no SALT change. [S1]
- **No response to one JAK inhibitor does not predict failure of another;** switching is reasonable in non-responders. [S1]

## Safety and monitoring on JAK inhibitors

- **Before starting, assess risk factors for JAK-inhibitor harm.** The only labelled absolute contraindications are hypersensitivity and active serious or opportunistic infection; other conditions need an individual risk–benefit judgement. [S1]
- **Who is at higher risk of serious harm on a JAK inhibitor (S1 pre-treatment table):** people aged 50 or over with one or more cardiovascular risk factors; anyone with past pulmonary embolism, venous thromboembolism, retinal artery occlusion or stroke; current or long-term past smokers; people facing surgery or immobilisation; and those with past cancer (weigh its type, stage, duration and treatment). [S1]
- **S1's contraindication table lists:** serious active infection; hypersensitivity to the drug; active cancer (other than resolved non-melanoma skin cancer); platelets below 100,000 cells/mm3, haemoglobin below 8 g/dL, neutrophils below 1000 cells/mm3 or lymphocytes below 500 cells/mm3; an interacting medicine; past thromboembolism or pulmonary embolism; pregnancy or planned pregnancy; breastfeeding; eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m² (for baricitinib, eGFR between 30 and 60 needs a dose adjustment); severe hepatic impairment, Child-Pugh C (for ritlecitinib); and immunosuppression. The extracted table does not say which items are absolute. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Vaccinate first:** bring routine immunisation up to date and add recombinant zoster, influenza, pneumococcal and COVID-19 vaccines. Give any live vaccine at least 1 month before starting, and ideally the first Shingrix dose too. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Pause treatment until values recover** if lymphocytes fall below 500/mm3, haemoglobin below 8 g/dL or neutrophils below 1000 cells/mm3, with serious or opportunistic infection, or with liver enzymes at 3 times the upper limit or more (consider pausing for zoster). **Stop it** if platelets drop below 50,000/mm3. [S1]
- **For a flare of new patches, increase baricitinib from 2 mg to 4 mg daily;** use intralesional steroid for limited patches or a short tapering course of oral corticosteroid for extensive flares. [S1]
- **After 6–12 months at target response, stopping or tapering can be discussed:** about a fifth of patients stay in remission off treatment, and most who relapse respond again when restarted. [S1]

## Other systemic agents

- **Keep methotrexate, ciclosporin and azathioprine for patients who need systemic therapy but cannot take a JAK inhibitor,** given their weaker efficacy, higher cost and worse side effects. [S1]
- **Ciclosporin is not TGA-approved for AA and is not advised first line;** off label, experts dose 3–5 mg/kg/day for no more than 6–12 months, alone or as a steroid-sparing agent. Expert methotrexate dose: 15–20 mg once weekly. [S1]

## Who should get a subsidised JAK inhibitor (proposed Australian criteria)

- **Australian experts (S2) accepted the AA-IGA scale, where severe means 50% or more scalp loss.** They judged a JAK inhibitor nearly always the best choice at SALT 50 or above, usually best at 21–49, and sometimes best below 21. [S2]
- **Findings beyond scalp extent that raise eligibility:** an ophiasis pattern; ClinRo 2–3 loss of brows or lashes; marked beard loss in men; ClinRo 2–3 nail disease; and a strongly positive pull test. Adverse trichoscopy does not count. [S2]
- **A disease course that raises eligibility:** SALT climbing by more than 10 points within six weeks; earlier episodes or past totalis or universalis; a current episode lasting beyond one year; or more than 10 years since the first episode. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Refractory disease is the most heavily weighted factor.** It means no response to intralesional steroid (three or more rounds, 4–6 weeks apart), to a six-week oral steroid taper starting at 20 mg/day, or to azathioprine, methotrexate or ciclosporin at adequate dose for three months or more. A good response to earlier compassionate or private JAK-inhibitor supply also counts in favour. [S2]
- **How much each factor weighs, in points where one point equals 1% of scalp loss (S2 Table 3):** refractory disease 16.6; psychosocial harm 14.2; rapid progression 13.8; scalp loss that is hard to camouflage 13.6; DLQI above 11, 13; ophiasis pattern 13; ClinRO 2–3 brow loss 12.8; relapse 12.8; ClinRO 2–3 lash loss 11.2; strongly positive pull test 10.8; age 12–18, 9.6; large gaps in or total loss of beard in men 9.2; female sex 8.4; adverse trichoscopy 7; age 5–11, 6.8; ClinRO 2–3 nail change 6.8. [S2]
- **Psychosocial harm raises eligibility too:** a history of anxiety, depression, suicidal thoughts or social impairment made worse by AA; a DLQI of 11 or more; hair of cultural significance; and loss in places that are hard to hide. The experts note the DLQI is a poor fit for AA. [S2]
- **Children and adolescents:** experts judged 5–17-year-olds more likely than adults to benefit, and would make children from age five eligible once data support safety and efficacy. No consensus was reached on sex, failure of topical steroids, or coexisting atopic or autoimmune disease. [S2]
- **What counts as remission (S2):** SALT 0, regrown facial hair, a better DLQI, patient satisfaction, a negative pull test and less psychosocial impairment. SALT 10 or SALT 20 on its own was not accepted as remission. Review stopping or dose reduction 6 months after remission. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Treatment failure that should end subsidised therapy:** scalp hair loss less than 50% better than at baseline when judged 12 months after the JAK inhibitor was started. [S2]
- **Access at publication:** despite TGA approval there was no JAK-inhibitor subsidy for AA in Australia, and a private month of baricitinib cost over $1000. The UK NHS has funded ritlecitinib for severe AA since March 2024, and Canada and the UK reimburse JAK inhibitors for adults with more than 50% scalp loss. [S2]
- **For comparison, the British Association of Dermatologists also counts SALT 21–49 as severe** when there is psychosocial harm, noticeable brow or lash loss, too little response after 6 months or more of treatment, or a diffuse positive pull test. [S2]

## Limited disease: topical and intralesional

- **Topical corticosteroids suit limited disease, alone or as add-on; high-potency agents work better than weak ones.** Watch for folliculitis, atrophy, striae, telangiectasia and acneiform eruptions. [S1]
- **Intralesional triamcinolone acetonide is standard for limited AA (response 60% to 95% across 12 studies):** inject every 3–4 weeks for up to 6 months, at 2.5–10 mg/mL on the scalp and 2.5–5 mg/mL on the face. [S1]
- **Topical JAK inhibitors are not recommended for scalp AA, and topical calcineurin inhibitors have no proven benefit.** [S1]
