# Growth hormone and IGF-1 therapy in children — PBS eligibility and prescribing

**Edition:** 2.0 · 2026-09-24 · **Status:** drafted and source-checked; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** PBS eligibility, prescribing logistics, continuation rules and doses for somatropin (daily), somatrogon (weekly) and mecasermin (IGF-1) under the Section 100 Growth Hormone Program: all paediatric categories plus late-onset and mature-skeleton (adult) GH deficiency. It is an eligibility and prescribing summary, not a guideline on investigating short stature; GH stimulation testing protocols are in the ANZSPED HEDT-Paeds manual (not authored). Related: `hypoglycaemia-children` (GH deficiency as a cause of neonatal hypoglycaemia). PBS access for each drug is in `docs/no-guideline-pbs-listings.md`.

## Who can prescribe and how

- **Subsidised GH is not an ordinary PBS item:** it is supplied through a section 100 special arrangement, whose legal basis is the Growth Hormone Program Special Arrangement 2015. [S1]
- **For every paediatric category, the child must be under a paediatric endocrinologist, or a general paediatrician working with a named paediatric endocrinologist,** and may receive only one growth hormone at a time. [S1]
- **Apply through the Online PBS Authorities system (via HPOS) for an immediate decision** (written applications are also accepted). The first script covers 16 weeks plus 1 repeat of 16 weeks; later scripts cover 13 weeks plus 1 repeat. Scripts last 6 months, and ongoing approval is re-sought every 6 months (acceptable window 4-8 months). [S1]

## Rules that apply to all paediatric categories

- ⚠️ **Exclusions:** a condition carrying a known cancer risk (for example Down or Bloom syndrome), or any active tumour or sign of tumour activity. **Open epiphyses are required:** bone age under 15.5 years in boys and under 13.5 years in girls. [S1]
- **A child with diabetes** needs confirmation that poor glycaemic control is not the cause of the growth failure, that control is adequate, and that complication screening (especially for retinopathy) is happening. [S1]
- **First applications need recent auxology:** 12 months of height and weight data (2 measurements roughly a year apart), or 6 months (4-8 months apart) in an older child; the latest measurement no more than 3 months old; and a bone age from the past 12 months unless the child is 2.5 years or younger. [S1]

## Entry criteria by category

- **Biochemical GH deficiency (BGHD):** peak GH below 10 mU/L (3.3 µg/L) on 2 pharmacological stimulation tests, or on 1 pharmacological plus 1 physiological test, or on a single test backed by other evidence: a structural midline or pituitary abnormality, a genetic cause, or low IGF-1 or IGFBP-3. [S1]
- **BGHD category auxology:** height at or below the 1st percentile, or below the 25th percentile with slow growth (velocity under the 25th percentile, or up to 14 cm/year at age 2 or under, or up to 8 cm/year at 2.5 or under). GH deficiency caused by an intracranial lesion or cranial irradiation has its own category, where the lesion (or the irradiated child) must be under ongoing observation and management. [S1]
- **Short stature and slow growth (no GH deficiency needed):** height at or below the 1st percentile plus growth velocity under the 25th percentile for bone age (over 12 months, or 6 in an older child) or up to 8 cm/year at 2.5 years or under. Not eligible once height reaches 167.7 cm (boys) or 155.0 cm (girls), or with constitutional delay and a predicted adult height of at least 160.1 cm or 148.0 cm. [S1]
- **Infants at risk of hypoglycaemia from GH deficiency:** under 2 years, with a documented hypoglycaemia risk attributed to BGHD. At age 5 the child should be moved (reclassified) to the BGHD short-stature category. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Weekly (long-acting) GH is unsuitable for infants and young children prone to hypoglycaemia,** because GH levels may not stay high enough to support glucose. [S1]
- **BGHD with precocious puberty:** puberty starting before 9 years in boys (Tanner 2 or testes of 4 mL or more) or before 8 in girls, or menarche before 10, together with BGHD, and the child must be on a GnRH agonist to suppress puberty. [S1]
- **Hypothalamic obesity after a structural lesion:** a non-neoplastic lesion, or a neoplastic one after a 12 month observation period (after all treatment, or from diagnosis if treatment is judged unsafe); plus BGHD, other hypothalamic-pituitary deficits and hypothalamic obesity. Here growth velocity must be **above** the 25th percentile, since obesity-driven growth masks GH deficiency. [S1]
- **Turner syndrome:** genetically confirmed (45X in all cells, mosaicism, or loss or rearrangement of an X chromosome), raised as female; height below 155.0 cm and bone age below 13.5 years. **A higher dose is needed:** 9.5 mg/m²/week is recommended. [S1]
- **SHOX disorders:** a SHOX mutation or deletion on karyotype; if it comes with mixed gonadal dysgenesis (45X mosaic with Y material), a management plan for the raised gonadoblastoma risk must be in place. Height and growth criteria match the short-stature categories. [S1]
- **Chronic renal insufficiency:** eGFR under 30 mL/min/1.73 m², either without a transplant or at least 12 months after one. If a transplanted child's eGFR is 30 or more at 12 months, reclassify to short stature and slow growth. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Prader-Willi syndrome:** genetic or clinical-geneticist diagnosis; polysomnography within the previous 12 months, with any sleep-disordered breathing that needs treatment already treated, and repeated during the first 32 weeks; no uncontrolled morbid obesity (over 200% of ideal weight for height); younger than 18 years. [S1]

## Doses and continuing treatment

- **Daily somatropin: up to 7.5 mg/m²/week in most categories.** Older advice was 4.5-7.5 mg/m²/week; starting low and titrating is reasonable, but children without GH deficiency respond poorly at the low end, whereas truly GH-deficient children usually do well there. [S1]
- **Weekly somatrogon: 0.66 mg/kg once a week.** Check IGF-1 on day 4 after a dose, and cut the dose by 15% if IGF-1 is more than 2 SDS above the age-sex mean. If growth does not pick up in year one, look at adherence and other causes (hypothyroidism, undernutrition, advanced bone age) and consider stopping. [S1]
- **Continuing approval depends on response.** Each period (32 weeks initially, then 26 weeks) is renewed if the child was below the maximum dose (7.5 mg/m²/week); at the maximum, growth must reach the 50th velocity percentile, raise height SDS, reach at least 4 cm/year, or hold mid-parental height SDS. The ceiling is 9.5 mg/m²/week in the Turner, SHOX and renal categories. [S1]

## Weekly (long-acting) GH in GH deficiency: international consensus (S2)

- **Three weekly GH products are approved internationally for paediatric GH deficiency:** lonapegsomatropin, somapacitan and somatrogon. Each matched daily GH for 1-year height velocity in prepubertal children, but long-term and real-world data are still lacking. [S2]
- **Children who may gain most from weekly GH:** those at risk of poor adherence (such as teenagers), on multiple medicines, with neurodiversity or injection fear (theirs or a carer's), with more than one home or frequent travel, or from socioeconomically disadvantaged families. [S2]
- ⚠️ **Not yet studied in trials:** survivors of cancer or intracranial tumours (take special care with dosing and IGF-1 monitoring), Prader–Willi syndrome, very young children, and non-GH-deficient states such as SGA, Turner syndrome, SHOX deficiency and chronic renal insufficiency. Use shared decision-making in these groups. [S2]
- **Dosing is weight based and product specific,** so milligram doses cannot be compared between molecules or with daily GH. Consider a lower start (using ideal body weight) in obesity or hyperglycaemia risk, and a lower initial dose with raised intracranial pressure risk, severe GH deficiency, genetic or chromosomal abnormalities, renal failure or oestrogen therapy. [S2]
- **IGF-1 timing on weekly GH:** sample on day 4 after the dose (somapacitan, somatrogon) or day 4.5 (lonapegsomatropin) to estimate the average IGF-1; aim for an SDS between −2 and +2, preferably close to 0. Outside the 4- to 5-day window, apply product-specific correction factors. In the trials the dose was cut by 15–20% when average IGF-1 exceeded +2.0 SDS. [S2]
- **Switching without overlap:** from daily GH, give the first weekly dose the next day (at least 8 hours after the last daily dose); from one weekly product to another, give the new one 7 days after the last dose. [S2]
- **Missed doses:** pick a fixed injection day. A late dose can be given within ±2 days (lonapegsomatropin) or +3 days (somapacitan, somatrogon), then return to the usual day; missed doses matter for efficacy and for hypoglycaemia risk. If non-adherence persists, consider a school or community nurse giving the injection. [S2]
- **More than one injection a week is regularly needed** above about 45 kg (somatrogon), 50 kg (somapacitan) or 60.5 kg (lonapegsomatropin), when the dose exceeds one pen or cartridge. Record the time and site and rotate sites to avoid lipoatrophy. [S2]
- **Safety so far matches daily GH,** with no new signals over up to 5 years; somatrogon caused more injection-site reactions and pain than daily GH, and antidrug antibodies had no clinical effect. Only somapacitan is approved for adult GH deficiency, and oral oestrogen interactions in adolescents are unstudied. [S2]

## Mature skeleton and adult (late-onset) GH deficiency

- **Late-onset or mature-skeleton GH deficiency is managed by an endocrinologist and needs severe deficiency:** peak GH below 2.5 µg/L on insulin tolerance, below 0.4 on arginine, or below 3 on glucagon testing; or, without testing, age 18 or over with hypothalamic-pituitary disease, at least three pituitary hormone deficits and a subnormal IGF-1. Adults with Prader-Willi syndrome (18 or older) are not subsidised unless childhood-onset GH deficiency is documented. [S1]
- **Adult dosing is individual, typically 0.15 to 1 mg per day:** start at 0.15-0.3 mg/day and step up monthly, guided by response and IGF-1. [S1]

## Mecasermin (severe primary IGF-1 deficiency)

- **Mecasermin is for severe primary IGF-1 deficiency, ages 2 to 18:** basal IGF-1 below the 2.5th percentile, height at least 3 SD below the mean, slow growth, and normal GH secretion (latest GH above 6.6 mcg/L). Exclude GH deficiency and secondary causes first: malnutrition, hypopituitarism, hypothyroidism and medicines. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Mecasermin dose: begin at 0.04 mg/kg twice daily by subcutaneous injection,** and if tolerated for at least one week, step up by 0.04 mg/kg to a maximum of 0.12 mg/kg twice daily. Exceeding that ceiling may raise neoplasia risk, and higher doses cause more hypoglycaemia. Continue until the epiphyses fuse. [S1]
- **To continue mecasermin,** the child must show height-SDS catch-up on Laron syndrome charts and growth faster than 2 cm/year, unless the poor response is explained by under-dosing. [S1]
