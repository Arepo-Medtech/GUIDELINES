## Recognise

- **Discoid lupus (DLE) is a chronic, scarring skin disease and the commonest cutaneous lupus.** Long-lasting scaly plaques, mainly on the face, scalp and ears, can leave scars, atrophy, pigment change and permanent hair loss. [S1]
- **It mostly affects women in their 20s to 40s** and is rare in children. It is commoner in skin of colour; New Zealand data show a markedly higher relative risk for Māori and Pacific peoples than for people of European descent. [S1]
- **UV light can provoke DLE,** though lesions also occur on covered skin. Smoking is commoner in people with DLE and makes antimalarials work less well. [S1]
- **DLE is localised above the neck in 80% and generalised in 20%.** Early red patches become plaques with adherent scale and follicular plugs (the 'carpet-tack' sign when scale is lifted), with favourite sites being the nose, cheeks, ear lobes and the conchal bowl of the ear. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Some develop SLE:** about 5% with localised DLE and 15% with generalised DLE. [S1]

## Assess

- **Examine fully for other signs of lupus,** and confirm with a skin biopsy in most cases. [S1]
- **Baseline tests:** full blood count, renal function, ANA (often negative) and urinalysis. [S1]
- **Differentials include** lichen planus, actinic keratosis, polymorphic light eruption, drug-induced and chilblain lupus, squamous cell carcinoma, sarcoidosis and lupus vulgaris. [S1]

## General measures

- ⚠️ **Protect from the sun all year, with clothing plus generous high-SPF broad-spectrum sunscreen;** sunscreen on its own is not enough. Some people need to avoid window glass indoors or fit UV-blocking film. [S1]
- **Recommend vitamin D supplements for people who strictly avoid the sun, and help them stop smoking.** [S1]

## Treat

- **First treatment for localised DLE: intermittent courses of potent or ultrapotent topical corticosteroid for several weeks.** Intralesional corticosteroid injection is sometimes used. [S1]
- **Topical calcineurin inhibitors** (tacrolimus ointment, pimecrolimus cream) are an alternative. [S1]
- **Camouflage make-up and wigs** help confidence when scarring is extensive. [S1]
- **Oral prednisone or prednisolone** can give short-term control. [S1]
- **Longer-term systemic options, alone or combined,** include hydroxychloroquine, chloroquine, mepacrine, methotrexate, acitretin or isotretinoin, mycophenolate, dapsone, thalidomide and biologics (belimumab, rituximab), among others. [S1]

## Outlook

- **DLE is chronic and can last for years; treatment is often difficult.** ⚠️ Squamous cell carcinoma can rarely develop in a long-standing plaque on skin or mucosa. [S1]
