# Actinic (solar) keratosis

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** adults with actinic (solar) keratoses on sun-exposed skin: choosing field or lesion treatment, UV protection, topical 5-fluorouracil, imiquimod and diclofenac, cryosurgery, photodynamic therapy and combinations. Actinic cheilitis is out of scope. Suspected keratinocyte cancer (including an AK that persists after cryotherapy): see `keratinocyte-cancer`.

## Recognise

- **Actinic keratoses are keratinocyte neoplasms on skin with long-term UV exposure.** They may regress by themselves, persist, or, most importantly, progress to keratinocyte carcinoma if untreated. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Progression risk is higher with a past skin cancer, older age or immunosuppression.** Treating AKs is linked to fewer later skin cancers. [S1]
- **Look for the variants:** atrophic to markedly hyperkeratotic lesions (including cutaneous horns), pigmented and Bowenoid forms. [S1]

## Choosing treatment

- **Choose by lesion features (such as site), treatment factors (efficacy, tolerability, burden) and what the patient wants,** and decide together. [S1]
- **Not treating is reasonable** when life expectancy is limited or treatment harms would outweigh benefit. [S1]
- **Field therapy (topicals, photodynamic therapy) suits many AKs across an area** and may cut new and recurrent lesions; lesion-directed therapy suits a few isolated AKs. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Advise all patients to minimise UV exposure** (strong, good-practice recommendation). Sunscreen modestly reduces new AKs in trials. [S1]

## Field treatment

- ⚠️ **Topical 5-fluorouracil or imiquimod is strongly recommended for field treatment.** [S1]
- **5-fluorouracil works well (benefit judged moderate to large), but expect local irritation:** it is the main harm and the usual reason patients stop. [S1]
- **With imiquimod, the harms are mostly local irritation or flu-like symptoms.** [S1]
- **Topical diclofenac gets only a conditional recommendation:** its evidence is weaker, and both benefit and harm (mainly skin reactions) were judged small. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Topical diclofenac carries the NSAID boxed warning** for cardiovascular thrombotic events and gastrointestinal bleeding, ulceration and perforation; consider this in patients already taking NSAIDs. [S1]
- **Photodynamic therapy is conditionally recommended** because overall evidence is low quality; evidence for up to 2 ALA-PDT sessions is stronger, so a repeat session is reasonable. [S1]

## Lesion treatment and combinations

- ⚠️ **Cryosurgery is strongly recommended for individual AKs:** quick, available and effective, clearing 57% to 98.8% of lesions in follow-up of 3 months to 8.5 years. [S1]
- **Cryosurgery's main harms are pain during treatment and pigment change afterwards;** shorter freezes reduce these but may lower clearance. [S1]
- **Topical-plus-cryosurgery combinations:** of 5 recommendations, 3 conditionally support combining and 2 favour cryosurgery alone. Adding imiquimod after blue-light PDT is conditionally not recommended. [S1]

## Special groups and newer agents

- ⚠️ **Immunocompromised patients (such as transplant recipients or those with CLL) often have more complex disease and higher squamous cell carcinoma risk,** but evidence was too thin for specific recommendations. [S1]
- **Tirbanibulin 1% ointment (face and scalp) and 5-fluorouracil with calcipotriol** came after the evidence review, so the guideline makes no recommendation on them. [S1]
