# HIV — starting antiretroviral therapy in adults

**Edition:** 1.0 · 2026-09-23 · **Status:** drafted and source-anchored; awaiting clinical attestation
**Scope:** when to start ART, the checks before starting, and the choice of first regimen for adults with HIV, including darunavir- and abacavir-containing regimens (the PBS rows). ## When to start

- **Everyone with HIV should be offered ART,** both to cut HIV-related illness and death and to stop transmission to partners and infants (AI). **Start it as soon as possible after diagnosis** (AII). [S1]
- **(AU) Whether same-day starts improve long-term engagement and viral suppression is not known.** Timing is decided jointly by prescriber and patient; link a patient who is unsure to peer-based programmes. [S1]
- **Make clear that ART does not cure HIV** and has to be taken indefinitely without breaks to keep immune function and viral suppression. [S1]
- ⚠️ **Early ART matters most in AIDS-defining illness, acute or recent infection, and pregnancy,** where delay carries high risks of illness, death and transmission. [S1]
- **(AU) Late diagnosis is common: 48% of Australian HIV notifications in 2021 were late,** the highest share in 10 years. [S1]
- ⚠️ **With an acute opportunistic infection, start ART,** but for cryptococcal or TB meningitis a short delay may be justified because immediate ART raises the risk of serious IRIS. In non-meningeal TB, starting ART during TB treatment gives a clear survival benefit. [S1]
- **A cancer diagnosis should not hold up ART, and starting ART should not hold up cancer treatment.** [S1]

## Before the first prescription

- **Resistance genotype before treatment:** in people never exposed to ARVs, test reverse transcriptase and protease genes; add integrase testing when transmitted INSTI resistance is a concern. [S1]
- **Do a pregnancy test before starting ART,** and ask everyone who could become pregnant about their plans. [S1]
- ⚠️ **(AU) Screen for HLA-B*5701 before any abacavir-containing regimen (AI).** A positive result means no abacavir, recorded in the notes as an abacavir allergy, because the hypersensitivity risk is above 50%. The test is available across Australia as a targeted genetic test without full HLA typing. [S1]
- **HLA-B*5701 needs testing only once in a lifetime,** so record the result carefully. A negative result does not completely exclude abacavir hypersensitivity. [S1]

## Choosing the first regimen

- **For most people, start an oral second-generation INSTI with two NRTIs:** bictegravir/TAF/emtricitabine (AI), or dolutegravir with TAF/emtricitabine, TDF/emtricitabine or TDF/lamivudine (AI). Dolutegravir/lamivudine as a two-drug regimen suits some people (AI). [S1]
- ⚠️ **Avoid dolutegravir/lamivudine when the viral load is above 500,000 copies/mL, with hepatitis B co-infection, or before the genotype and HBV results are back.** [S1]
- ⚠️ **After long-acting cabotegravir PrEP, do not start an INSTI until an INSTI genotype shows no resistance;** if ART must start first, use boosted darunavir with TAF or TDF plus emtricitabine or lamivudine (AIII). **(AU)** Cabotegravir PrEP was TGA-approved in 2022 but its PBS listing process ceased by June 2024, though some patients may have used it overseas; request an integrase genotype. [S1]
- **If viral suppression has not been reached 8 to 12 weeks into an INSTI regimen, repeat resistance testing, including integrase.** [S1]
- **Dolutegravir/abacavir/lamivudine is now a second-tier option,** because abacavir needs an HLA-B*5701 test, carries a possible cardiovascular risk, and other choices exist. Use it (BI) when kidney or bone concerns rule out TAF and TDF, only in documented HLA-B*5701-negative people, and not in hepatitis B co-infection unless another HBV-active drug such as entecavir is added. [S1]
- **Boosted darunavir is the one protease inhibitor recommended to start with, and only in certain situations.** Transmitted PI resistance is uncommon, so it is favoured over an NNRTI when starting before resistance results, and its high resistance barrier suits people at risk of patchy adherence. [S1]
- ⚠️ **In pregnancy use ritonavir-boosted darunavir, not cobicistat-boosted:** cobicistat regimens give inadequate drug levels in pregnancy and should not be started. Dolutegravir is now the preferred INSTI in pregnancy, since updated data showed no significant excess of neural tube defects. [S1]
- **Kidney function limits cobicistat-boosted darunavir:** with TDF/emtricitabine it is not recommended below a creatinine clearance of 70 mL/min, and with TAF/emtricitabine not below 30 mL/min. [S1]
- **Darunavir contains a sulfonamide group; use it cautiously in severe sulfonamide allergy,** though most people with that allergy tolerate it. [S1]
- **Separate dolutegravir from cation antacids and laxatives:** give it 2 hours or more ahead of them, or wait 6 hours or more afterwards. [S1]
- **Tenofovir-free regimens should not be used with hepatitis B co-infection unless an HBV-active drug such as entecavir is added.** [S1]
- **Weight gain on INSTI- or TAF-based regimens is not a reason to withhold them.** [S1]
- **(AU) Doravirine/TDF/lamivudine is TGA-approved but not PBS-reimbursed.** [S1]
